# Testing a binary probability with two choices of echo phase

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A binary detector can respond nonmonotonically to the kinematic mass delivered to one port. A sequence of reversible echoes can still compare that mass with any programmed threshold if the detector responds reliably near the two endpoints. Randomizing the threshold gives a reference for the ordinary probability. Comparing the native read with that reference requires a separate test. In particular, two nonmonotone response curves can exactly reproduce every single-launch ordinary distribution for every pulse word.

## One source and two selective phases

Use the matched echo and blank-ray phase of the earlier binary return protocol. An input has kinematic nonblank mass $x=\|(I-P_0)V|0\rangle\|^2$, with $P_0=|0\rangle\langle0|$ on the complete system. At each level choose the output target $P$ as either $P_0$ or $I-P_0$, put $D_P=I+(e^{i\pi/3}-1)P$, and replace $V$ by $VD_{P_0}V^\dagger D_PV$. The mass outside the chosen target cubes. Choosing blank therefore maps nonblank mass by $d(x)=x^3$. Choosing nonblank maps it by $u(x)=1-(1-x)^3$. A length-$n$ list needs $3^n$ forward or inverse calls to the original $V$ and $3^n-1$ selective phases. It uses a single prepared register, a fixed realization within the trial and a complete binary terminal flag. [Circuit and single-target identity](A_binary_read_of_amplified_echoes.md) · [Grover's fixed-point construction](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.95.150501)

Choose a numerical threshold $0<t<1$. Starting from $t_0=t$, choose $u$ when $t_j\le1/2$ and $d$ when $t_j>1/2$, and set $t_{j+1}$ to the chosen map of $t_j$. Apply this same fixed list to the unknown $x$. All decisions can be computed from $t$ before a launch. Denote the resulting nonblank mass by $x_n$. Then
$$ x<t\Longrightarrow x_n\longrightarrow0,\qquad x>t\Longrightarrow x_n\longrightarrow1. $$
At $x=t$ the list gives $x_n=t_n$ and makes no endpoint prediction.

To prove this, $[1/8,7/8]$ is invariant for the threshold update, and every interior $t$ enters it in finitely many steps. Below $1/8$ the prescribed iterations increase toward one until they enter, and above $7/8$ the reflected argument applies. For $l(y)=\log(y/(1-y))$, the derivative of $l(d(y))$ with respect to $l(y)$ is $3/(1+y+y^2)>1$. For $u$ replace $y$ by $1-y$. Ordered inputs stay ordered. Once the threshold enters the strip, every subsequent step increases their absolute log-odds gap $g$ by at least
$$ (\lambda-1)\min(1,g),\qquad
\lambda=\frac3{1+z+z^2}>1,\qquad z=\frac{7e}{1+7e}. $$
Indeed the first $\min(1,g)$ log-odds units outward from the threshold stay within $[-\log 7-1,\log 7+1]$, where either derivative is at least $\lambda$. On the remainder it is at least one. The gap grows at first at least geometrically until it reaches one, then at least linearly. The threshold stays in the strip, proving both limits. The endpoints $x=0,1$ are fixed at every step. This proof also bounds the circuit size needed for a chosen *kinematic* endpoint tolerance once a lower bound on the starting log-odds gap is known. A finite observed-error bound also needs calibrated gate accuracy and detector behavior in those neighborhoods. Read-once Boolean reliability networks likewise amplify differences between input biases. The physical operations here are coherent single-register phase words. [Amplification by read-once formulas](https://doi.org/10.1137/S009753979223633X)

## A binary reference without monotonicity

Let a hidden type specify the prepared mass $x$ and the decoded binary response $r(s)$ to an output of mass $s$. The final instrument must pool the complete binary flags and use the same response at a given type across pulse lists. Assume $r(0)=0$, $r(1)=1$, and that the one-sided limits there equal those endpoint values. Its behavior elsewhere may be discontinuous and nonmonotone. A random physical port swap is possible using the common decoded curve of the earlier note. Source types may vary between launches, including in correlation with the response curve. Threshold and protocol choices must be assigned independently of them. Rates here count assigned trials with both outcomes accounted for.

With a fixed threshold, the limiting measured success rate lies between $\Pr(x>t)$ and $\Pr(x\ge t)$ in the sense of liminf and limsup. This follows by bounded convergence off the equality event and bounding the remaining reports by its mass. Thus scans at continuity points recover the distribution of kinematic masses in the specified incident ensemble.

With $N=2^m$ fair threshold choices, use $t_j=(j+1/2)/N$, $0\le j<N$, and call their mean report probability at type $A$ the reference $r_{n,N}(A)$. Off threshold each term converges to its comparison bit. At a tie its contribution remains between zero and $1/N$. Consequently
$$ \limsup_{n\to\infty}|r_{n,N}(A)-x_A|\le\frac1{2N}. $$
The worst case includes atoms exactly on a grid midpoint. With a continuum-uniform threshold independent of the launch, bounded convergence instead gives $r_{n,\infty}(A)\to x_A$ directly. The finite-grid bound is sufficient to take depth first and grid size second. It does not specify a common finite depth for an arbitrary family of detector curves. Exact endpoint inputs alone cannot verify the one-sided limits. For example $r(s)=1/2$ throughout the open interval and ideal predictions at both endpoints passes endpoint checks but remains fair on every pulse word starting at an interior mass.

## What additional paired records test

A reference whose limiting rate is $x$ supplies a programmable ordinary binary probability. An unmodified read can implement a different law. Prepare two copies with the same latent $x_A$ and response type $A$, with independent report draws conditional on that type and on their assigned designs. Run the native read $B$, of rate $r_A(x_A)$, or the independent random-threshold reference $T$, of rate $r_{n,N}(A)$, on each copy. The four incident-pair double-hit rates have contrast
$$ C_{n,N}=J_{BB}-J_{BT}-J_{TB}+J_{TT}
=\mathbb E\big[(r_A(x_A)-r_{n,N}(A))^2\big]. $$
The four assignments must share the pair population. Independent random choices of threshold on the two reference copies matter. Since all rates lie in $[0,1]$, changing the reference argument in the square by $a$ can change the square by at most $2|a|$. Boundedness and the grid estimate give
$$ \limsup_n\left|C_{n,N}-\mathbb E[(r_A(x_A)-x_A)^2]\right|\le 1/N. $$
Vanishing contrast in this iterated limit forces ordinary native response almost surely **on the sampled preparations**. An ordinary native response also gives vanishing contrast in that limit. Extending the result to every mass at a common type needs source coverage and response regularity. Reusing one detector across two trials is insufficient if the source varies within the pair or the two outcomes share additional noise. [Paired records and the conditional-product counterexample](Fluctuating_site_scores_and_paired_launches.md)

For a concrete single-read escape, take $h(s)=\max(0,1/16-2|s-1/4|)$ on the lower half, and extend it to the upper half by $h(s)=-h(1-s)$. Two equally likely curves $r_\pm(s)=s\pm h(s)$ take values in $[0,1]$ and agree with the identity near both endpoints. Each strictly decreases on one side of the lower tent. Each also obeys $r_\pm(1-s)=1-r_\pm(s)$, so the routing-bit swap preserves it. The single-launch rate averaged over freshly drawn signs is exactly $s$ for every mass and pulse program, regardless of depth. At $x=1/4$ a sign held over a conditionally independent pair gives limiting contrast $1/256$ against the ordinary reference. If the sign is freshly redrawn between the two acquisitions every paired law also agrees with the ordinary one. A finite set of known test masses leaves further alternatives because an undetected tent may fit any unprobed interior interval.

These comparisons assume coherent targeting of either output sector, repeatable inverses, independent settings and calibrated endpoint response. The paired conclusion additionally uses a source type shared across independent acquisitions. No measured binary calibration, unique law for arbitrary instruments or derivation of these apparatus assumptions follows here.
