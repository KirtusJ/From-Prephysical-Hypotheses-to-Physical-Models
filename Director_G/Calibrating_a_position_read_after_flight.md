# Reading the position of a fall with an uneven detector

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

The prepared staircase walk predicts equal probability near two positions at a fixed elapsed time. A detector can prefer one region of the apparatus, miss sites entirely, or measure a coherent superposition of sites. What can its reported positions establish? Randomizing the launch origin removes arbitrary spatial variation in an ordinary readout channel. For a fixed quantum detector, a random final sheet increment and rail switch reduce its response to that case in the target sectors. The resulting position error, including the missed fraction, can be measured with sharp localized launches. The analysis assumes the usual effect rule for a coherent detector; it addresses how a calculated position distribution can be compared with reported positions. ## A finite calibration at the terminal clock

Use the same $N=4M$ wells, departure staircase $g(s)=\lfloor s/2\rfloor\bmod M$, and conditional rail filter. Let $P(x)$ be the ideal terminal position distribution **relative to the recorded launch well** for one sector $k=+1$ or $-1$ after a successful preparation. Choose the launch well $a$ uniformly on the ring independently of the population and controls. Translation preserves the relative state up to a scalar phase. At a given departure tick the occupied relative coordinates $x$ have one fixed parity $p$. Then $g(x+a)-g(x)=\lfloor (a+p)/2\rfloor\bmod M$ for all such $x$, including crossings of the $4M$ seam. The product of these scalar factors pulls through the position-unresolved rail projections. Preparation yield is independent of $a$. Uniform translation of the launch must be available to the apparatus. Turning a software coordinate label without translating the actual launch does not implement it.

First suppose the readout is an arbitrary common classical channel $K(b\mid s)$, including a no-position report $\perp$ as a separate outcome. For each click record $d=b-a\bmod N$. Set

$$
h(e)=\frac1N\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}K(s+e\mid s),\qquad \alpha=\sum_e h(e).
$$

The per-prepared-launch law for the recorded displacement is the circular convolution $(P*h)(d)$. Indeed, put $s=a+x$ in the averaged rate $N^{-1}\sum_{a,x}P(x)K(a+d\mid a+x)$. The no-report probability is $1-\alpha$. A sharp reference prepared at $a$ measures $h$ itself. This identity allows an array with arbitrarily many blind sites. The exposure and source mixture must be stable across the translated starts; a shifted aperture or different clock in the reference requires its own calibration.

Now let the readout consist of fixed positive effects $E_b,E_\perp$ summing to identity on the terminal **site and rail** in a given sector. The readout may be common to both target sectors or be separately characterized for each. The detector must give the same response to the same terminal state for all choices of the random controls. In each fixed target sector do the following at the terminal surface, after the flight has taken place.

1. Draw $j$ uniformly in $\mathbb Z/M$ independently. Apply the sheet increment $j g(s)$ according to the **arrival** site $s$. It multiplies the terminal wavefunction by $\exp[-2\pi i k j g(s)/M]$. Where gains are independently programmable on the final arcs this is composed with the last tick, retaining the original departure field. This assumes that terminal gain control, or an independently calibrated equivalent operation. Extra unaccounted walk ticks would change the packet.
2. For rail-sensitive effects choose an independent uniform element of $I,X,Z,XZ$ on the output rails at fixed site. This requires the co-located sign and rail exchange after routing; if $E_b$ is already rail-insensitive this second switch is dispensable.

At same-parity $s,t$, the mean phase multiplier of the cross term vanishes unless $k(s-t)/2=0\bmod M$. For either unit winding and an accessible lifted footprint of diameter strictly less than $2M$, this leaves only $s=t$. In the rested flight after $2r+n$ ticks the diameter is at most $2(2r+n)$ and the hypothesis holds eventually because $2r+n=o(M)$. The four rail toggles replace each diagonal rail block of the density matrix by half its trace times identity. As a result every such effect gives **exactly** the classical channel above with

$$K(b\mid s)=\tfrac12\sum_{c=R,L}\langle s,c|E_b|s,c\rangle.$$

One sharp rail at each random reference well with the same toggles suffices. Calibration requires a sharp reference at the same clock and the same response during genuine flight; a clock-matched localized calibration source is an explicit premise. A spatially diagonal readout needs no spatial phase switch. The proof concerns terminal probabilities and does not license applying a dephasing operation partway through a flight intended to remain coherent. On the real line the related characterization of position readouts by convolution requires both translation covariance and momentum-boost invariance. Here the discrete phase average enforces the needed diagonal property on the reached footprint. [Carmeli, Heinonen and Toigo, §2.2, Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0403108)

### Combining separately calibrated sectors

Let $w_k$ be the fraction of prepared **target** launches in sector $k$ before the click, $P_k$ its ideal position law, and $h_k$ its unnormalized displacement-error law. Write $\alpha_k=\sum_e h_k(e)$. In the stipulated incoherent mixture, averaging the per-sector identity gives the unnormalized report law and its total mass,
$$D=\sum_k w_k(P_k*h_k),\qquad \alpha=\sum_k w_k\alpha_k.$$
This is a known forward model if the weights, sector position laws and sector responses are known. If the $h_k$ are common, $D=(\sum_k w_k P_k)*h$. If the $P_k$ are common, as in the real rested target flight, $D=P*h_{\rm eff}$ with $h_{\rm eff}=\sum_k w_k h_k$. A sharp, clock-matched reference with the **same prepared target weights** directly measures $h_{\rm eff}$ even when the weights are unknown. A common detector also suffices. Separate calibrations alone leave the effective kernel undetermined when the weights are unknown. In particular, normalize the mixture by $\alpha$ only after adding the per-launch click rates. At equal incoming weights, efficiencies $1/4$ and $1$ give clicked weights $1/5$ and $4/5$.

For a finite example, let the two efficient sector detectors report the true position with offsets $0$ and $-2$ on a 16-site ring. The folded winding score is zero for every mixture of the unit pair. On a common sharp launch at relative well zero, target weights $(3/4,1/4)$ give click probabilities $(3/4,1/4)$ at $0,-2$. A reference with equal weights gives $(1/2,1/2)$, a total variation error of $1/4$. Matching the reference weights removes it. For a different preparation, even a weight-matched reference can fail to supply a single convolution. Keep equal sector weights and the same offsets, and take true positions $0$ and $2$ in the respective sectors. Every click reports zero. Convolving the two separately averaged marginals puts mass $1/4$ at each of $-2$ and $2$, with total variation error $1/2$. The sector-resolved sum above retains this correlation.

## Resolution, missing reports and the Newtonian limit

Let $a_M$ denote the probability of the complete flight preparation in the actual source. Conditional on preparation, let $b_M$ be its mass outside the two target sectors. The [same-source witness](Phase_witness_and_flight.md) bounds $b_M$ and gives $b_M\to0$ when its original-launch score is $o(M^{-4})$ on the stated scaling. For the real rested preparation the two target sectors have the same ideal position law $P_M$. Use the common or target-weight-matched kernel $h_M$ just derived, with mass $\alpha_M>0$. The total click rate per original launch is $a_M[(1-b_M)\alpha_M+b_M\alpha_{\rm other}]$, with $0\le\alpha_{\rm other}\le1$. After conditioning on a click and the complete preparation, the reported law is within total variation

$$\frac{b_M}{(1-b_M)\alpha_M+b_M}$$

of $P_M*(h_M/\alpha_M)$ whenever the conditioning event has positive probability. Indeed, the posterior competing fraction is $b_M\alpha_{\rm other}/[(1-b_M)\alpha_M+b_M\alpha_{\rm other}]$, increasing in $\alpha_{\rm other}$ when the target click mass is positive. Thus $b_M=o(\alpha_M)$ suffices even if the calibrated efficiency tends to zero. A positive uniform efficiency floor is one sufficient special case. If $\alpha_M=b_M\to0$ and the competing population always reports, its posterior tends to $1/2$. Retain the per-original-launch preparation and click totals when reporting the conditional fall. The adverse yield example applies to an uncontrolled contaminant; independently measuring the competing yield can improve the bound.

Take site pitch $\Delta Y=\delta^2$, $\delta=(\pi/(MF_1))^{1/4}$, and a fixed elapsed time $T$ of the rested flight. Use centered representatives of the circular offsets. Since $N\Delta Y\to\infty$, sums of two tight physical displacements wrap with probability tending to zero. Let $H_M$ be the conditional reference law of $e\Delta Y$. If $H_M\Rightarrow H$, the clicked terminal reports converge to

$$\tfrac12\bigl(\delta_{-d}+\delta_{+d}\bigr)*H,\qquad d=F_1T^2/2,$$

under the above contamination condition. This follows from independence in the convolution and weak convergence of its factors. In particular, the raw two point masses are recovered if the measured error tends to zero in probability in physical units. A detector can blur a growing number of lattice sites and still satisfy this condition. With macroscopic but tight blur, divide the limiting characteristic function of the reported displacement by the measured characteristic function of $H$ near frequency zero; the denominator is nonzero in a neighborhood since it is continuous and equals one at zero. The ratio is $\cos(d\xi)$ there and identifies $d\ge0$ within the symmetric two-ray model even without a finite second moment of the blur. This implication describes limiting identifiable information. Finite-sample fits require uncertainty in both measurements and their ratio. Reporting a uniform random well makes every nonconstant finite-ring Fourier mode vanish and erases the signal. These single-time output laws do not determine unmeasured trajectories.

## Why sharp spots alone can give a false flight

Consider an efficient, translation-invariant detector that first applies the unitary $W=I-\Pi+T_\ell\Pi$, then reads sharp position. Here $\Pi$ projects onto a set of lattice momenta, $T_\ell$ translates by $\ell$ wells, and the two commute. It acts on both rails identically. For any single-site calibration launch the probability of an off-site report is bounded by $\|(W-I)|s\rangle\|^2\le4\,\operatorname{rank}(\Pi)/N$ with rank counted on sites only. For any flight spinor $\psi$ the distance from a translated sharp-read experiment is bounded in state norm by $2\|(I-\Pi)\psi\|$. Take the union of small arcs around $\pm\pi/2$ of angular width $\sqrt\delta$. The prepared flight's momentum is concentrated there: the unforced preparation peaks have width $O(r^{-1/2})$, their preparation displacement is $O(rh)$, the flight force adds $O(nh)=O(\delta)$, and the rail-unitary map on each shifted momentum characteristic preserves its norm. For both signs of the field these quantities are $o(\sqrt\delta)$ on the stated scaling. The arcs occupy $o(N)$ modes but capture $1-o(1)$ of the flight. Choose even $\ell$ with $\ell\Delta Y\to L\ne0$. Sharp calibration converges to a perfect spot at every site and randomizing origins preserves it, yet the reported fall converges to the *translated* two rays with added displacement $L$. This is an allowed coherent measurement under the enlarged detector class. Its nonlocal preprocessing has no asserted apparatus implementation. A verified locality or sharp-position-channel premise could exclude it directly. The terminal phase protocol excludes its coherent action on this flight.
