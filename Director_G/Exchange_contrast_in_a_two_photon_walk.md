# Exchange interference in a measured two-photon walk

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Defienne et al. sent two photons through a multimode fibre and changed their relative arrival time. At one chosen pair of output modes, temporal overlap raised the coincidence rate by a factor of 1.730 when both input photons could reach both outputs. With each photon focused separately onto its own output, the factor was 1.017. The ratio of those two changes is 1.700. This provides a physical comparison of exchange-sensitive and nearly exchange-insensitive routing on the same detected pair of ports. [Defienne et al., Figures 1–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4846436/) · [Original supplement including Table S1, via Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4846436/supplementaryFiles)

## Two routes to one coincidence

A type-II down-conversion source produced horizontally and vertically polarized photons. The apparatus controlled their spatial input modes separately, projected the output onto a common polarization, and detected one photon in each of the selected fibre modes \(X_2,Y_3\). Its 11 cm multimode fibre mixed about 380 modes during propagation. The researchers independently measured the single-photon transmission matrix with classical light to program the input fields. A balanced-beamsplitter Hong–Ou–Mandel scan of the source gave approximately 86% visibility. For the walk they compared zero path delay with a 0.4 mm difference, which distinguished the arrivals. This walk used interfering optical modes. Its dynamics did not implement an addressed two-site contact that swaps physical tag states. [Experimental setup and transmission-matrix measurement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4846436/) · [Source characterization, Supplementary Figure S1](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4846436/supplementaryFiles)

For a passive linear optical transfer with one photon in each of two inputs, let \(A\) be the product of transfer amplitudes for the assignment \((H\to X_2,V\to Y_3)\) and \(B\) the product for its reversal. For a separable pair of unmeasured internal states \(\alpha,\beta\), summing over final labels gives

\[
P_{\gamma}=|A|^2+|B|^2+2\gamma\operatorname{Re}(A\bar B),\qquad
\gamma=|\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle|^2 .
\]

This follows by taking the norm of \(A\,\alpha\otimes\beta+B\,\beta\otimes\alpha\) at the two distinct output ports. In a mixed spectral source a single visibility used as \(\gamma\) is an effective overlap parameter subject to independent calibration. Configuration I focuses the two photons separately and aims for \(B\simeq 0\). Configuration II makes both assignments possible and programs equal phases, aiming for constructive interference. Both measure exactly the same target port. The authors also scanned relative phase to obtain a peak, a dip and a nearly flat curve. [Target configuration and phase controls, Figures 3 and 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4846436/)

## Original target counts and uncertainty

Each of these four source-reported totals represents 900 seconds of acquisition with a 2.5 ns coincidence window. The supplement's Table S1 repeats the word “distinguishable” on the second delay row for each configuration. We assigned the cells using the explicit contrast definition \((R_0-R_{0.4})/R_{0.4}\) and the sign and magnitude given in Figure 3 and its discussion. The original time ordering and the 90 individual submeasurement counts are unavailable in Table S1. No output cells, photon singles, herald counts or acquisition windows have been added to this target table. With rows indexed by configuration and columns by delay, define \(Q=(R_{\mathrm{II},0}/R_{\mathrm{II},d})/(R_{\mathrm{I},0}/R_{\mathrm{I},d})\). The exact observed ratio is \(23998031/14117604\). Row-specific efficiencies and a common delay-dependent flux cancel when the expected rates factor into these nuisance terms times physical coincidence probabilities. For independent Poisson totals with fixed expectations, conditioning on both margins gives Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric law with odds parameter \(Q\). Inversion of its inclusive 2.5% upper and lower tails gives a 95% interval of **[1.6133, 1.7911]**, rounded outwards. Under the factorized no-interaction null \(Q=1\), the one-sided tail is \(7.91\times10^{-91}\). This calculation uses the target measurements defined by the focusing protocol. It does not test the largest fluctuation among arbitrary output speckles. [Count replay, tail and interval](results/fibre_exchange.json)

The authors report larger sampling errors than the simplest aggregate Poisson model admits for several cells. In the order II-zero, II-delayed, I-zero, I-delayed, their relative errors are 1.8%, 2.3%, 1.3%, 2.1%. Under first-order log propagation, independent cells give a log-ratio standard error of 0.03825. With arbitrary correlations, the triangle inequality bounds that linearized standard error by 0.075. A Gaussian sensitivity envelope using this larger value is about [1.467, 1.970]. This is conditional on the supplied errors covering run variation and on the normal approximation; no finite confidence bound follows for unmeasured systematic biases. Their monitored output speckle correlations stayed above 85%, which does not itself bound drift in a particular coincidence rate. The replay retains both the source-reported and direct-division contrasts. [Supplementary statistical analysis](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4846436/supplementaryFiles)

## Scope of the transfer to the contact walk

For negligible residual overlap at the delayed setting and unchanged complex route amplitudes, set \(v=2\operatorname{Re}(A\bar B)/(|A|^2+|B|^2)\), with \(|v|\le1\). If the directed comparison is exactly overlap-insensitive and delay-dependent gains are common, \(Q=1+\gamma v_{\mathrm{II}}\). The counts then give \(\gamma v_{\mathrm{II}}=0.700\), with the preceding Poisson interval shifted down by one. Adopting the independent source visibility 0.86 as the operative overlap would give \(v_{\mathrm{II}}\simeq0.814\). A remaining directed-path cross term gives instead \(Q=(1+\gamma v_{\mathrm{II}})/(1+\gamma v_{\mathrm{I}})\). A nonseparable setting-specific count efficiency multiplies \(Q\) by its own cross ratio and is observationally confounded with the interference in these four counts; a gain-only alternative would need a cross ratio of 1.700 at the point estimate. A finite accidental coincidence background can also change ratios. These data alone provide no absolute emitted-pair or whole-walk probability.

In the proposed three-gate occupation walk, the return coefficients \(A=36/125\) and \(B=-80/125\) give a **destructive** slope in the same overlap formula. Its ratio to the orthogonal-tag baseline at \(\gamma=1\) is \(1936/7696=121/481\). This value belongs to the proposed three-gate apparatus. The fibre has different transfer amplitudes and deliberately realizes constructive interference in configuration II. Its exchange term establishes experimentally accessible sensitivity to the distinction between output assignments after labels are ignored. It cannot tell which photon followed which assignment, certify a local contact SWAP, or supply original-launch yields for transferring an entire two-particle occupation record.
