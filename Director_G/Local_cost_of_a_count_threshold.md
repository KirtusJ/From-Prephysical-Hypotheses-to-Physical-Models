# How far and how much a count record must travel

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

The [count reference](One_threshold_on_several_walks.md) reads a single binary flag for “at least \(J\) hits” on \(m\) walk records. For three banks the independently chosen setting is \(J=1,2,3\). Two adjacent interactions suffice to deliver each of these records to the middle bank. A separate implementation that carries one head past the banks requires four head states for the OR setting, even with arbitrary complex head unitaries. These costs apply after the bank event bits are coherently available. The flight, the writing of those bits from physical sites, and transfer from the terminal station to a detector have their own costs.

## A local circuit with a central detector

Place one protected event coordinate \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) per site on a line. One edge layer allows a matching of disjoint nearest-neighbor reversible interactions. Within-site reversible operations and a final binary read at the central site are additional primitives. Allow each site a blank local counter modulo \(m+1\). The incoming state can be a superposition: the following permutation is specified on every basis word and preserves the input bits. This design sets no constant upper limit on the work performed inside an edge interaction.

Copy \(x_i\) into the local counter by controlled addition. From each end add its current counter into the neighboring counter towards the middle, keeping the source. Work from the two ends simultaneously on disjoint edges. With two groups remaining, merge them across the middle edge. With three groups remaining, merge one outer group into the middle, then the other. After the merges, the central counter holds \(K=\sum_i x_i\) exactly since \(0\le K\le m\). XOR \([K\ge J]\) into a clean local flag and read it while pooling the entire remaining state. Every step before the read is reversible. The count workspace can be cleared by reversing the sweeps after copying the flag, at the cost of another sweep; protected input coordinates remain available to reset their local counters.

For \(m\ge2\) the circuit uses **\(\lceil m/2\rceil\) edge layers** up to the central count. This is optimal when the detector is at the most favorable site. Its backward cone begins at one site. One matching expands the cone to at most two sites, and each further matching adds at most one site on either side of the bounding interval. The cone therefore contains at most \(2d\) sites after \(d\ge1\) layers, even with unlimited local registers. For each \(J\in[1,m]\) and each omitted site, put exactly \(J-1\) hits on the other sites and toggle the omitted bit. The two sharp inputs require opposite flags and agree on the entire cone. This also gives a two-input error lower bound of one half on at least one input for a stochastic read that obeys the same causal restriction. A single bank needs zero edge layers.

Three adjacent banks require two layers. Forty-eight require 24 with a middle detector. If the read is fixed at one end, its cone can gain only one site per layer and a single sweep attains the resulting \(m-1\) lower bound. Unlimited long-range gates, classical collection of several terminal records, or directed communication on *both* incident edges of a site in one layer would change the timing problem. The [earlier matching-layer bound](Phase_contact_and_shared_walks.md) concerned preparing an aligned state; here the pivotal pair witnesses a bound for one local threshold observable of arbitrary input words.

## A mobile reader with protected input

Consider a different constraint. One head in a fixed initial state passes each bit exactly once. At position \(i\) it undergoes \(U_{i,0}\) or \(U_{i,1}\) according to that bit. The controls preserve the bank and act unitarily on the head; they may vary with position and the preset threshold. There is no uncounted history register, intermediate measurement, reset, or later access to an old bit. At the end a single head read must put every false word wholly in a reject subspace and every true word wholly in its orthogonal complement. This is the exact, read-once unitary branching-program model. The lower bound is on the head dimension, not the total size of all protected banks. [Read-once quantum branching programs](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0302022)

For the OR setting \(J=1\), let \(v_i\) be the head vector after the first \(i\) zeroes. Let \(B_i\) be the span of vectors reached after \(i\) bits by prefixes containing at least one hit. Append zeroes to the end. All these states undergo the same unitary suffix, after which \(v_i\) must reject and \(B_i\) must accept. Thus \(v_i\perp B_i\). The next one maps the entire space \(B_i\oplus\operatorname{span}(v_i)\) isometrically into \(B_{i+1}\). Consequently \(\dim B_i\ge i\); at the end there must be space for \(B_m\) and \(v_m\). **The head needs at least \(m+1\) states.** Interchanging the input symbols yields the same obstruction for AND. Relaying the entire threshold menu requires this width even when the setting is known before the pass. Incrementing a head modulo \(m+1\) on a hit and accepting counter values \(J,\ldots,m\) attains it for the whole menu. Three banks require two head bits. This proof assumes exact discrimination on each sharp input. It supplies no width lower bound for approximate readers or a promise restricted to one source distribution.

A classical one-bit OR accumulator would send both old head states to one on a hit. That update cannot preserve the input and act reversibly on the head. A reversible implementation with fresh history could keep the old head rather than erase it, using a resource outside this one-pass model. A two-state head that toggles on each hit instead reads parity. At three independent, matched walk banks with site mass \(5/8\), its flagged kinematic mass is \(65/128\), while the intended majority mass is \(175/256\). Sharp words 100 and 110 already give opposite answers to the two tests. A one-bit *protected* output cannot distinguish how the flag was produced solely from its support dimension.

The [checker](check_local_threshold_reader.py) exhausts all words up to ten sites, compares the construction with every possible backward-cone expansion, and inverts each physical-edge schedule. For three banks it separately exhausts all time-dependent read-one permutations for widths one through three, up to a change of basis at each step making the read-zero permutation the identity. At each width and each of the three thresholds, zero programs separate the accepting and rejecting head states. A four-state counter separates all eight words for each threshold. This finite check concerns permutation heads; the span proof above also covers quantum heads for OR and AND. The [Lean file](lean/LocalThresholdReader.lean) checks the pivotal-word and dimension arithmetic and the consequence of the two boundary growth inequalities. The local compilation implements the Boolean projector on coherent protected records. It supplies no measured clock speed, physical bank placement, upper bound on correlations between banks, or detector rule. The empirical comparison still needs the terminal instrument to pool the workspace and the shot assignment to preserve the intended source population.
