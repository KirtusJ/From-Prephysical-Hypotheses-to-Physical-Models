Freeze the version and its commitments before judging a result. | Attack | Result at issue |
|---|---|
| An arbitrary finite signed mixer might visit only powers of two. | It does on two fixed wires. G1 requires the specified family of expanding, unwrapped light cones. Enumeration and induction exhibit simultaneous native \((n,1)\) and \((1,0)\) on that family. |
| Conservation could have smuggled in weights on unmeasured history subsets. | A single marker locally transported to one of two outgoing ports gives parent-group conservation directly. A fair marker is an explicit different model satisfying this local law. No value is assigned to an arbitrary subset of unrecorded histories. |
| A common score could already contain the Born rule. | \(f(k)=|k|\) scores signed totals with the same shape on all ports and gives dark cancellations. At tick two the source site 0 and the right front have raw weight ratio two. After splitting, their daughter groups have ratio one. Thus common-score sufficiency admits a competing law; physical marker transport excludes it. Conversely the fair marker preserves every such group but fails the positive equal-total comparison. Together the stipulations select the square, and evidence for each stipulation remains necessary. |
| A reversible hidden pointer could keep local odds but resolve two opposite paths. | Confirmed in the [parity-bit construction](../../z1_attacks/s3_reversible_tag.md). The proposed read is vulnerable to that apparatus. Compare same-time equal signed totals and vary which-path recording, holding the declared detector and its retained information fixed within each trial arm. |
| A passive marker read halfway through a run might be mistaken for a quantum projective measurement. | At tick two the rails at site 0 have totals \((1,1)\). A silent marker there must go right. A sharp quantum rail projection followed by the same splitter sends half right. G1’s specification covers terminal reads. Extending it with non-disturbing projective readout would contradict this calculation. |
| Could the full square-response law be inferred by observing any finite collection of fixed rings forever? | Every finite collection misses infinitely many positive powers of three. On the four- and eight-site rings together, doubling the score at the visited magnitude 32 also preserves every normalized panel. On sixteen sites at tick 13 the right port at site 7 separates the scores, with probabilities 1/8 and 1/5. [Proof and exact check](fixed_ring_calibration.md) |
| A localized source on a fixed ring could be treated as a continuum packet. | A sharp launch has uniform total quasimomentum. The packet result demands a different source and growing rings. Its preparation is an independent experimental obligation. |
