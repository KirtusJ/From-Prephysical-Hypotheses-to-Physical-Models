# What fixed rings can identify about a detector

This review concerns the sharp launch at `(0,R)` in G1 and a complete set of oriented terminal detectors. The selection theorem assumes rings of unbounded circumference and one response function shared by all of them. Restricting the experiment to finitely many fixed rings leaves infinitely many positive inputs to that function untested, even if one can read every port at every tick. Four- and eight-site rings have a second ambiguity that affects an input the experiment actually visits. [G1 specification](specification.md)

### A score ambiguity at an input that occurs

Compare the square response with a response that assigns twice the square when the signed total is +32 or −32 and assigns the square everywhere else. Both assign zero to zero and one to one. They produce identical complete terminal distributions on the four- and eight-site rings for all ticks. Therefore the same local marker coupling realizes both sets of marginals on these rings.

There is a simple criterion for such equality. Put a vertex at each nonzero signed total reached by the sharp launch. Join two totals if they occur in the same ring at the same tick. To match a square-response panel, the ratio of the alternative score to the squared total must equal the ratio of the two panel normalizers at every occupied port. Hence this ratio must be a single positive constant on each connected component. Conversely any such choice preserves every panel, since its constant cancels on normalization. The component containing +1 has constant one. Unvisited inputs are unconstrained by these panels.

On four sites, four steps translate the sharp source by two sites and multiply it by four. At each of the four intermediate depths there is only one nonzero magnitude. On eight sites, 24 steps return the initial sharp source multiplied by 4096. Integer propagation through ticks 0–23 encounters magnitude 32 only at ticks 12 and 13. At both ticks every occupied port has that magnitude. At every later tick a nonzero entry is a multiple of 4096. Linearity therefore turns these finite calculations into an all-time certificate. The periods of the normalized walk on these cycles also appear in the published literature. [Exact checker](check_fixed_ring_calibration.py) · [Recorded results](fixed_ring_calibration.json) · [Konno, Shimizu and Takei](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06396)

### A measurement that separates the responses

On 16 sites, launch at `(0,R)` and read the right rail at site 7 at tick 13. Its signed total is +32. Exactly two ports have magnitude 32, and other occupied ports have different magnitudes. The two scores give the following probabilities and normalizers.

| Readout | Square response | Doubled score at magnitude 32 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Panel normalizer at tick 13 | 8192 | 10240 |
| Probability at site 7, right rail | 1/8 | 1/5 |
| Probability at site 6, either rail, at tick 12 | 73/512 | 73/512 |
| Probability of its two outgoing ports at tick 13 | 73/512 | 137/640 |

The changed score consequently fails local conservation on the sixteen-site ring. The comparison uses unconditioned terminal readings on fresh launches with common preparation and detector efficiencies. The fractions are mathematical predictions. The checker obtains the tick-13 field both by local propagation and by enumerating all 8192 signed histories.

### Every finite ring collection misses infinitely many positive integers

Fix any finite list of ring sizes, with the same sharp launch on each. In fact only finitely many positive powers of three can ever occur as signed port totals on these rings.

For one ring let A be the raw integer step. Since A divided by the square root of two is orthogonal, A is normal and every complex eigenvalue has modulus √2. The spectral expansion of a fixed port after t ticks has the form $\sum_i c_i\lambda_i^t$, with distinct eigenvalues and nonzero coefficients after discarding terms that vanish identically. Suppose this total equals $3^j$. Take an inclusion-minimal nonempty subset I of terms whose sum is still $3^j$. No nonempty subsum in I can vanish, since deleting it would give a smaller subset with the same sum.

The vector with entries $\lambda_i^t/3^j$ for i in I belongs to the multiplicative subgroup generated by the eigenvalue vector and the constant vector with entries 1/3. This group has rank at most two. The nondegenerate unit-equation theorem of Evertse, Schlickewei and Schmidt gives finitely many solutions of $\sum_i c_i x_i=1$ in that group. For a singleton I there is only one possible coordinate. Each solution determines at most one pair (t,j). Equality at a coordinate for two pairs gives, on taking absolute values and squaring, $2^{t-s}=3^{2(j-k)}$, which forces t = s and j = k. There are finitely many subsets, ports and rings. This proves the claim without a cutoff on time. [Evertse, Schlickewei and Schmidt, Theorem 1.1](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p04)

Choose an absent positive power m greater than one. Adding one to the square score only at +m preserves every allowed reading on the chosen collection. On a sufficiently large ring the G1 edge at tick m+1 has incoming rail totals (m,1) at the same site. Their odds are m² to one under the square score and m²+1 to one under its rival. The general proof supplies existence. It need not locate a practically small m. For power-of-two rings there is also an elementary proof. Over the field of two elements with $X^N=1$, the two rails at tick $t\ge 1$ are represented by $X$ and $X^{-1}$ times $(X+X^{-1})^{t-1}$. For N a power of two the latter base to the power N/2 vanishes, so every total is even from tick N/2+1 onward. For the pair of sizes 4 and 8 the positive odd values reached before this cutoff are just 1 and 3. Thus +5 is one explicit unvisited input.

G1's stated selection theorem retains its unbounded family of rings. The proof for arbitrary finite collections settles the ambiguity at unvisited inputs. The example at magnitude 32 establishes the stronger ambiguity at a visited input for the specified two rings.
