# The edge-record walk and the seven Millennium problems

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

The candidate adopts six additional mathematical positions. Each stands or falls on a proof in its own domain. The walk theorem uses integer path counts and conservation of specified detector marginals. For example, append “RH is true” to its assumptions, or append “RH is false.” Both versions produce exactly the same three-tick table, because neither the transition nor the detector refers to a zero of the zeta function. This shows why that table cannot decide RH. It establishes no formal independence result about ZFC or about relations among the six conjectures. [Exact walk records](edge_records.json)

Clay currently lists five problems as **Unsolved**, Navier–Stokes as **Active**, and Poincaré as **Solved**. On 11 September 2026 Clay acknowledged an announcement of an apparent resolution of Navier–Stokes. Its status page still distinguishes that case from the accepted Poincaré theorem. [Clay’s Millennium register](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/) · [Navier–Stokes announcement](https://www.claymath.org/news/navier-stokes-announcement/)

1. **Riemann hypothesis — adopt RH.** Every zero $s$ of the analytically continued Riemann zeta function in $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1$ has $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$. A proof must cover the whole strip. A rigorous enclosure containing a zero and lying wholly off the critical line inside the strip would refute it. [Bombieri’s official description](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/riemann.pdf)

2. **Hodge conjecture — adopt the rational statement.** For every smooth complex projective variety $X$ and every $p$, each rational cohomology class in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)$ of Hodge type $(p,p)$ is a rational linear combination of classes of codimension-$p$ algebraic subvarieties. A proof must establish this spanning assertion in all degrees. A refutation must exhibit or establish the existence of a rational Hodge class outside that span. Integral counterexamples concern a different coefficient group. [Deligne’s official description](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/hodge.pdf)

3. **Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer — adopt the rank equality.** For each elliptic curve $E$ over $\mathbb Q$, the order of vanishing of its Hasse–Weil $L$-function at $s=1$ equals the rank of the finitely generated group $E(\mathbb Q)$. A proof must cover every such curve. A rigorously established mismatch for one curve suffices to refute it. Wiles distinguishes this prize statement from the refined formula for the leading coefficient. [Wiles’s official description](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/birchswin.pdf)

4. **Navier–Stokes — adopt global smooth existence for Clay’s unforced whole-space data (A).** Fix any viscosity $\nu>0$. In three space dimensions, every smooth divergence-free initial velocity on $\mathbb R^3$ whose spatial derivatives, including order zero, decay faster than every inverse power has a global smooth velocity and pressure solving the unforced incompressible equations, with kinetic energy uniformly bounded in time. One admissible initial field for which no such solution exists refutes (A). Clay also accepts the periodic existence alternative (B) and the breakdown alternatives (C) or (D), which permit prescribed smooth forcing. A forced breakdown alone would leave the unforced assertion open. [Fefferman’s four formulations](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/navierstokes.pdf)

5. **P versus NP — adopt $P\ne NP$.** At least one decision language with polynomial-length certificates verifiable in deterministic polynomial time has no deterministic polynomial-time decider. A proof must exclude every such decider for at least one language in NP. A deterministic algorithm for SAT with proved correctness on every input and a polynomial worst-case time bound would refute the position by NP-completeness. [Cook’s official description](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/pvsnp.pdf)

6. **Yang–Mills existence and mass gap — adopt both claims.** For every compact simple gauge group $G$, a nontrivial continuum quantum Yang–Mills theory on $\mathbb R^4$ exists with the axiomatic properties specified by Jaffe and Witten. Its Hamiltonian, with vacuum energy zero, has no spectrum in $(0,\Delta)$ for some $\Delta>0$. A proof must establish both the continuum theory and the gap for every such $G$. Refutation requires an admissible group for which no theory meets both requirements. A gap in a single finite lattice Hamiltonian addresses an approximation. [Jaffe and Witten’s official description](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf)

7. **Poincaré — accepted theorem.** Every closed, simply connected three-manifold is homeomorphic to the three-sphere. Here “closed” means compact without boundary. Perelman proved it using Ricci flow with surgery, developing Hamilton’s program. His proof appeared in 2002–2003 and Clay awarded the prize in 2010. A walk construction purporting to violate the theorem would have to produce a closed simply connected three-manifold and prove that it is not homeomorphic to $S^3$, thereby confronting the existing proof. [Milnor’s official description](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/poincare.pdf) · [Clay’s account of the resolution](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/poincare-conjecture/)
