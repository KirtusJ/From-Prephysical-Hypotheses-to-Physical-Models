# Specification and selection theorem

**G1 v1.0.** The claims apply to the following ensemble of experiments.

1. **Carrier.** Take rings of arbitrarily large circumference \(N\ge3\) with sites \(x\) and incoming rails \(R,L\). The reference launch is one positive route in \((0,R)\). For signed counts \(u=D_t(x,R),v=D_t(x,L)\), the next tick sends \(u+v\) to \((x+1,R)\) and \(u-v\) to \((x-1,L)\). Each output has one parent site. The only negative arc is \(L\to L\). Integer totals can be evaluated by local additions or by counting path signs; these are the same evolution.
2. **Marker.** Each run contains exactly one marker. Each native tick moves it from its current site to one of that site's two outgoing oriented ports. The marker leaves the tally dynamics intact. The launch distribution and the dynamics before the read are invariant under changing the planned terminal time. A complete ideal terminal read reports the marker’s actual port. These are physical commitments. They imply that the combined frequency of a parent’s two rails equals the combined frequency of that parent’s two daughters in a separate run one tick longer.
3. **Score.** On the complete set of \(2N\) detectors, including the unused ones, \(p_t(j)=f(D_t(j))/Z_t\), with \(Z_t=\sum_k f(D_t(k))\). The score shape \(f:\mathbb Z\to[0,\infty)\) is common across ports, times and ring sizes. A common multiplicative factor per panel has no observable effect. An unreachable port is dark; the common response then assigns \(f(0)=0\) also at exact cancellations. The sharp source has \(f(1)>0\); choose units \(f(1)=1\). This stipulates physical sufficiency of the local signed total, including equivalence of apparatuses with that total. A memory of which route arrived can violate it.
4. **Source extension.** For the mechanical application allow preparation of finite real signed arrays by approximating them by integer arrays up to an overall scale, and the same detector law for these launches. Approximation requires source and instrument stability in the stated limit. The edge theorem uses only the one localized launch. Preparation of the extended arrays and the physical clock, length and action calibrations remain separate hypotheses. [Mechanical specification](mechanics.md)

**Theorem.** On these rings, 1–3 imply \(f(k)=k^2\) for every integer. From the localized reference launch this gives \(p_t(j)=D_t(j)^2/2^t\).

**Proof.** Choose a ring large enough that the line light cone through depth \(t+1\) does not wrap, for instance \(N>2(t+1)\). At the right front \(x=t\) the incoming totals are \((1,0)\) and the two daughters have totals \((1,1)\). Marker conservation there implies \(Z_{t+1}/Z_t=2\). An odds comparison of another parent against this reference yields the same raw identity without ever measuring a normalizer. Normalize over every detector, retaining interior counts.

At \(t=n+1\), \(x=t-2\), the incoming totals are \((n,1)\). A right-ending route there contains one left step in its first \(n\) positions, and a left-ending route has taken the left step last. All have positive sign. Its daughters yield
\[
 f(n+1)+f(n-1)=2[f(n)+1].
\]
For \(n\ge1\), the value at \(n+1\) is determined successively from \(f(0)=0,f(1)=1\), giving \(f(n)=n^2\) on the nonnegative integers. At \(t=1\) the opposite front has totals \((0,1)\), so comparison with the right front gives \(f(-1)=1\).

For \(t\ge3\) the site \(x=-t+2\) has precisely one right step in every incoming history. The right-ending history has \(t-2\) left-to-left arcs. Among the left-ending histories, the one with the right step first has \(t-2\) such arcs and the other \(t-2\) histories have \(t-3\). The incoming totals are thus \(((-1)^{t-2},(t-3)(-1)^{t-3})\). Write \(m=t-3\). For even \(m\ge2\) the pair \((-1,m)\) gives \(f(m-1)+f(-m-1)=2(1+m^2)\), determining the odd negative values. For odd \(m\ge1\) the pair \((1,-m)\) gives \(f(1-m)+(m+1)^2=2(1+f(-m))\), determining the even negative values. No sign-reversal symmetry was needed. Conversely the integer identity \((u+v)^2+(u-v)^2=2(u^2+v^2)\) gives the global mass \(2^t\). A local stochastic marker realizing the marginals chooses the right daughter with probability \((u+v)^2/[2(u^2+v^2)]\) when \(u^2+v^2>0\), otherwise that parent has zero marker mass. Existence of this coupling proves consistency. Its transition formula was derived after selection. The axioms do not determine every conditional trajectory coupling.

**Physical checks on one ring.** At \(N=16,t=3\), the port \((-1,L)\) has signed total zero and two opposite-sign histories, hence predicted probability zero versus \(1/4\) for a marker choosing daughters with equal probabilities. At \(t=4\), ports \((0,L)\) and \((2,L)\) both have signed total \(+1\). The model gives \(1/16\) to each. Counting gives \(3/16\) and \(1/16\), respectively. The latter test compares two positive rates on the same rail at the same clock time. If the declared apparatus under controlled efficiencies and dark counts gives reproducibly unequal rates, the common-score commitment fails. If conserved markers demonstrably leave both successor ports, local marker transport fails. In either case the candidate ends for that apparatus. No rates have been measured here.
