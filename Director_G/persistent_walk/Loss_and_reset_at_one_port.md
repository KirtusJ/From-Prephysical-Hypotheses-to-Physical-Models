# What happens to the five-site return record under loss or random reset?

Director G research note for Kirtus Justus · 15 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

A fixed finite stochastic predictor has return probability q(t) = αPᵗb, with one launch distribution, one row-stochastic transition matrix and one terminal response vector in [0,1]. The target p(t) is the sharp return to site zero on the right rail of the five-site Hadamard walk. A fresh launch is read once. The ideal probabilities are dense in [0,1] on every progression of multiples of an even integer. This gives a sharp asymptotic worst-case error of 1/2. The following results distinguish two specified changes to the record. Neither change has been fitted to experimental observations. ## Independent loss at each tick

Suppose a launch survives each tick independently with probability 0 < r < 1, survival has no effect on the surviving walk, and a lost launch gives zero clicks. The unconditional return probability is y(t) = rᵗp(t). Predicting zero has vanishing absolute error at late times. For any ε > 0, a finite counter reproduces all ticks before T and then predicts zero, giving uniform absolute error at most rᵀ ≤ ε for sufficiently large T.

Exact finite-state representation still fails. In fact the minimum over finite predictors of limsup |y(t) − q(t)| / rᵗ is **1/2**. The normalization divides by the known launch survival probability. It does not divide by a possibly zero return probability.

To prove the lower bound one can assume q(t)/rᵗ is bounded, since otherwise the normalized error is unbounded. Restrict P to the states on paths from the support of α to the support of b. Paths outside this set contribute zero. Each communicating class of the restricted nonnegative matrix has spectral radius at most r. Indeed, append fixed positive-probability entry and exit paths to loops at a chosen state of the class. On sufficiently large multiples of its period d, its loop probabilities are bounded below by a positive constant times ρᵏ, by Perron–Frobenius applied to the primitive cyclic block. A productive class of radius ρ > r would therefore make q(t)/rᵗ unbounded.

Every eigenvalue of modulus r in the restricted matrix is r times a root of unity, by the peripheral spectrum of each irreducible class. Take D a common multiple of the relevant periods, or D = 1 if there are none. Along t = 2Dn, Jordan expansion makes q(t)/rᵗ a polynomial in n plus terms tending to zero. Boundedness forces the polynomial to be constant. Along that same progression p(t) approaches both endpoints arbitrarily late, proving the lower bound. A two-state chain which survives with probability r, with readout 1/2 while live and zero when lost, attains it. Consequently exact representation would contradict the normalized bound even though absolute error can be made uniformly arbitrarily small.

## A random reset to an interior probability

Now suppose instead that the target record is y(t) = c + rᵗ[p(t) − c], with 0 < c < 1. For c = 1/10 this is the sharp-port record when, at each tick, the walk undergoes the ideal step with probability r and otherwise resets to the maximally mixed state on its ten modes. The mixed state stays fixed under subsequent ideal steps. For general c this section concerns the specified scalar curve; a particular physical reset map would need separate justification.

**For every 0 < r < 1, this entire scalar record has an exact finite stochastic realization.** The return amplitude is a finite spectral sum with nonnegative weights adding to one. Its squared modulus gives p(t) = Σⱼ aⱼ cos(ωⱼt), with aⱼ ≥ 0 and Σⱼ aⱼ = 1, by grouping conjugate eigenvalue ratios. Include a term of weight c, zero frequency and phase π to represent −c. The total absolute weight W is now 1 + c. Choose L ≥ 1 so Wrᴸ ≤ min(c, 1−c), and choose N ≥ 3 with cos(π/N) ≥ r.

For each frequency, the point r exp(iωⱼ) lies in the regular N-gon with vertices exp(2πik/N). Choose a probability law on its vertices with that mean. Starting at zero, add independent increments with this law on the cyclic group of N sites. At time s the mean of exp(2πiX/N) equals [r exp(iωⱼ)]ˢ. Use L deterministic prefix states with the required tick-0 through tick-(L−1) readouts. On leaving the prefix, select a frequency block in proportion to its weight and enter vertex zero. At vertex k give that block the fixed response c + Wrᴸ cos(ωⱼL + φⱼ + 2πk/N), where φⱼ is zero for the original terms and π for the added term. The margin on L puts every response in [0,1]. Taking the weighted average of the block responses at time s gives y(L+s) exactly.

**An explicit example fits within the 55-state budget.** Take c = 1/10 and r = 1/2. The pinned minimal polynomial of degree 26 has 26 distinct unit-circle modes including +1 and −1. Pairing the 24 nonreal modes leaves 14 cosines. The constant coefficient is 15/116 > 1/10, so subtract c from that single weight. All weights stay nonnegative and their sum is W = 9/10. Set L = 4 and N = 3. The readout variation is W/16 = 9/160 < 1/10. For each frequency use increment probabilities [1 + cos(ωⱼ − 2πk/3)]/3, whose complex mean is exp(iωⱼ)/2. This uses 4 + 14 × 3 = **46 states** and reproduces every tick. The accompanying standard-library checker builds the transition matrix and compares it with direct ten-port amplitude propagation. The maximum discrepancy over ticks 0 through 120 was 8.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ in floating point. The polygon argument supplies exactness for all ticks.

There is also a high-coherence exclusion at the same fixed budget. If a 55-state model reproduces the reset record exactly, its transition matrix must have eigenvalue rζ, because the frequency ζ selected in the finite-duration test has a nonzero coefficient and remains visible after damping. For a unit sup-norm eigenvector v, ‖(ζI−P)v‖∞ = 1−r. The finite-duration note proves ‖(ζI−P)v‖∞ ≥ d₅₅²/(8·55), with d₅₅ > 0.10043. Hence any exact 55-state model requires 1−r > 0.0000229231475. For example, r ≥ 0.99997708 rules one out. The precise cutoff and the minimum number of states at r = 1/2 are open here. This is an exact-representation result. A sampled-count test would need its own power calculation under the calibrated noise law.
