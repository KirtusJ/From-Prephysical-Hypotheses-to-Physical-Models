# Certifying a position measurement with three forward ticks

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A position detector on a reflecting line reports which side of a cut the walker occupies. Does it also preserve the relative amplitudes in that region? Three ticks of the same forward walk can test this using an output where exactly two paths cancel. Changing when the detector acts tests either the two rails of one well or modes at two different wells. Taken together, the tests identify the action of the detector on the whole input space. They replace the inverse flight in the [two-tick return certificate](Certifying_a_coherent_half_interval_read.md). Cancellation in the presence of a path marker is the usual two-path interference effect. The placement of the markers and the connectivity at the walls are specific to this walk. [Schwindt, Kwiat and Englert, quantitative wave-particle duality](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9908072)

## Preparations and observations

Use \(L\ge4\) wells numbered from zero, the real Hadamard coin \(H\), and the reflecting shift \(S\) with positive wall signs. A right or left proposal at the corresponding outer wall stays on the well and reverses its rail. One tick is \(U=SH\). Choose any single cut between adjacent wells and let \(b(i)\) denote the side of directed mode \(i\). The target measurement outputs the classical label \(b\) and leaves the unnormalized branch \(P_b\rho P_b\). Assume a fixed linear isometric dilation \(W\) of the actual instrument, with orthogonal classical outcome sectors, the same initial apparatus on all preparations, and separately trusted source, forward ticks, timing, and final read. The complete read includes loss and failure.

1. Prepare each of the \(2L\) individual modes \(i\). Apply the unknown instrument and independently measure the output mode. Require that the joint result is always \((b(i),i)\). This test still needs a directed-mode read.
2. Launch from \((x,R)\) and run three ticks. Insert the instrument just after tick 2. At the end monitor the directed port \(d_x=S(x,L')\), where \(L'\) is the left rail. It is one well left of \(x\) on rail \(L'\), except at \(x=0\), where it is \((0,R)\). For every \(x\) require a dark port at the label \(b(x,R)\).
3. Repeat with the instrument just after tick 1. Check a dark port at the common label whenever \(S(x,R)\) and \(S(x,L')\) belong to the same bin. When the cut separates them the two paths carry different labels. Record both split outputs; each has ordinary joint mass \(1/8\) at \(d_x\). They supply no dark constraint on their relative phase.

These are at most \(4L\) settings including the localized checks. No reversed tick, rail flip, extra copy of the input or injected phase is needed.

## Why a dark port identifies the tags

First consider only support, the set of terminal outcomes that can occur. Assume that a tested label-and-mode event has zero population frequency exactly when its output vector vanishes, and that this applies to the full observed alphabet. This support property holds for positive finite powers of nonzero masses, including the convention of assigning weight one to every nonempty cell at power zero. An arbitrary reporting rule can hide an occupied cell or produce spurious counts. Support faithfulness is therefore a physical assumption. Frequencies and support here refer to the population. A finite run with zero counts provides a statistical bound under additional sampling assumptions.

The deterministic basis checks force \(W|i\rangle=|i\rangle e_i\) with a unit environment tag in the required label sector. Now consider the paths to \(d_x\). Since \(S\) is a permutation, each had to be at \(x\) before the third coin and make a left proposal. Of the four two-tick paths, exactly two return to \(x\). Each first proposal leads to one of the two rails there on its return. The rail-\(R\) and rail-\(L'\) amplitudes are both \(+1/2\). A left first proposal that holds at the left wall leaves rail \(R\); a second left proposal holds again with positive amplitude. The corresponding two holds at the right wall also have positive amplitude. The two remaining paths miss \(x\), including at a wall. The last coin gives opposite signs to the two returning paths. Their amplitudes at \(d_x\) are \(+1/\sqrt8\) and \(-1/\sqrt8\).

At either read clock let \(i,j\) be the intermediate modes of these two paths. If the modes belong to one bin, the returning vector at the monitored label and port is \(\pm(e_i-e_j)/\sqrt8\). A zero enforces \(e_i=e_j\), including their relative phase. At tick 2 the two modes are \((x,R),(x,L')\), so the tags coincide within each well. At tick 1 they are \(S(x,R),S(x,L')\). For interior \(x\) their sites differ by two, which joins each site parity within its bin. At each wall the two first destinations are that endpoint and its neighbor. This wall link joins the two parities of each end-touching bin of size at least two. A bin of one well has its rails joined already. Thus each side of any cut has a single vector \(e_b\). Linearity fixes \(W\) on every superposition in the input space, including one entangled with a spectator. Conversely such a dilation passes all the support tests.

This establishes the form of the **amplitude transformation** using a faithful support read. Relative weights of different nonempty events remain free. For example, square the ordinary masses and renormalize over the output cells. This gives exactly the same ideal deterministic outcomes and zeros, while changing the probabilities of other outputs with unequal positive masses. When ordinary probabilities and trace over unobserved coordinates are supplied, the identified dilation implements the full Lüders instrument.

## Position-only observation and finite departures

For the three-tick part alone, an ordinary calibrated **site** read also works. At the site of \(d_x\), the opposite terminal rail is fed by exactly one path. For \(2\le x\le L-1\) that path takes two left proposals followed by a right proposal. For \(x=1\) it takes three left proposals (the last two hold at the wall). For \(x=0\) it takes left, right, left. These cases follow by taking the unique inverse shift of the companion mode and listing the two-tick arrivals at that well. Thus, under exact basis checks, its contribution is \(1/8\) at the label of that path's intermediate mode and zero elsewhere. It adds without interference to \(d_x\). For a same-bin edge at label \(b\), let \(B\) denote this known background at that label. Then the joint site probability is
\[
  B+\|e_i-e_j\|^2/8.
\]
This site-only read uses the ordinary sum of squared rail amplitudes and the size of a nonzero background. The directed-port support test above requires only the absence of an outcome.

For imperfect basis checks use ordinary probabilities and their calibrated bounds. Write \(a_i\) for the joint correct-label and correct-mode probability, \(r_i=\sqrt{2-2\sqrt{a_i}}\) and \(r=(\sum_i r_i^2)^{1/2}\). As in the [two-tick comparison](Certifying_a_coherent_half_interval_read.md), projection on each correct output column gives a diagonal comparison dilation \(W_0|i\rangle=|i\rangle e_i\) with \(\|W-W_0\|\le r\). On a particular normalized preparation \(u=U^k|x,R\rangle\), \(k=1,2\), take \(\eta=\min(r,\sum_i|u_i|r_i)\). The remaining forward ticks and the terminal event projection are contractions. If \(h\) is the actual joint dark-port probability, the predicted norm for \(W_0\) is at most \(\sqrt h+\eta\). Give that edge length
\[
  \ell=\min\{2,\sqrt8(\sqrt h+\eta)\}.
\]
For a site observation with joint probability \(h_{\rm site}\), use instead
\[
  \ell=\min\{2,\sqrt{8[(\sqrt{h_{\rm site}}+\eta)^2-B]}\}.
\]
The radicand is nonnegative under the stated model and valid bounds. Use upper bounds for the observed unwanted probabilities and lower bounds for the basis checks in these estimates. On each side use shortest paths along the measured edges. Choose a root minimizing the largest distance to a mode of that side and call the larger of the two radii \(D\). Replacing each tag by the root tag changes \(W_0\) in operator norm by at most \(D\), by orthogonality of distinct visible output modes. Consequently half the diamond distance between the actual and ideal instruments is bounded by \(\min(1,r+D)\), with an arbitrary untouched spectator. This applies to transferring a calibrated operation to later uses when its state of readiness is reproduced. Source, propagation, and terminal measurement errors require their own bounds.

## Controls and minimal flight length

On eight wells with the cut between 3 and 4, launch at \(0,R\) and read after tick 1. A minus sign only at \(1,R\) preserves every localized test. It raises the ordinary left-label probability at the dark port from 0 to \(1/2\); orthogonal tags on \(0,R\) and \(1,R\) give \(1/4\). Using the whole terminal site 0 instead gives \(1/8,5/8,3/8\) respectively. With a cut between 0 and 1 the same first split lies across labels. Requiring darkness there would reject an ideal measurement.

For at most two total forward ticks, every word from a sharp directed start reaches its own terminal mode. Indeed its first step goes to one of two distinct sites even at a wall, and each step-2 mode has a unique pre-shift site. An intermediate site-bin read with any diagonal phase error inside a bin has exactly the same joint outcome probabilities as the ideal read in all of these experiments. Pointwise changes of phase multiply individual path amplitudes without creating any meeting at the same output. This holds for either launch rail, every label and either terminal rail, including reads before the first or after the last tick. Three is therefore the least number of total ticks sufficient with sharp single-mode starts, a single inserted instrument, unmodified forward ticks and terminal directed-mode counting.

The [integer replay](check_forward_cut.py) compares all contributing signed histories with a separately masked site recurrence, checks both walls and every cut for lengths 3 through 40, and includes canceled histories. The theorem above only needs lengths of at least four. The [result](results/forward_cut.json) contains all tested lengths and the eight-well path pairs, including pairs split by the middle cut. A [Lean 4.19 replay](lean/ForwardCut.lean) checks all eight-well launches for the two path signs, the intermediate modes at both clocks, the unique companion, and the distinct two-tick endpoints.
