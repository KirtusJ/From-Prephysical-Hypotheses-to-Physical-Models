# Which probability rules do optical walk records distinguish?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Mathematical development and reanalysis by Director G (OpenAI Codex). The experimental observations are those of the cited authors.

An adjustable phase plate gives an empirical test of two ways to assign a probability to the same algebraically labeled walk. At the published 135° setting, the ten-step return probability was $0.660\pm0.012$. The two ideal squared predictions are $0.667394$ and $0.004481$. A comparison with a second setting preserves the separation for arbitrary fixed site efficiencies when the centre is detected and each setting has positive detected off-centre mass, so both centre odds are defined and nonzero. The physical input to this inference is the identification of an optical delay and a polarization preparation with particular algebraic phases. The walk constructed from localized sheets and synchronized signed steps still lacks an internal test for this identification. [Xue, Qin and Tang, Figs. 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4001091/) · [Native transcript theorem](Native_preparations_and_probability_embeddings.md)

## A fixed optical phase label

Set $z=\exp(i\pi/4)$. The photon starts at the origin with coin $(H-iV)/\sqrt2$. At each tick the coin is Hadamard, the origin applies $z^j$ to both departures, and H and V stream left and right. The ten-tick run with $j=3$ represents the authors' 135° setting. For the norm law under $\sigma_k(z)=z^k$, $k=1,3,5,7$, apply the same field map to the plate, the launch quadrature, and the entire coherent walk. All paths have eleven inverse-square-root factors including preparation. Any automorphic sign on that common denominator vanishes on squaring. Horizontally and vertically polarized outputs at the observed position are summed as distinguishable modes. [Experimental coin and phase conventions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4001091/) · [Path enumeration](check_optical_walk_records.py)

For the ordinary labels, the raw signed amplitudes at the origin in the basis $(1,z,z^2,z^3)$ are $(-12,-10,0,10)$ and $(0,10,12,10)$. Taking the two squared moduli and dividing by 2048 gives the exact return weights below. The sign of $\sqrt2=z-z^3$ separates the two conjugate pairs. Whole-apparatus complex conjugation preserves the read. The four null comparisons in the phase sweep enter the same calculation.

There is a detector check within this same graph. Let $p,q$ be the predictions at 135° and 180° respectively. Suppose the detection efficiency $\eta_x$ is the same for both settings at each site and depends only on position. Write the observed odds for detecting the photon at the origin versus elsewhere as $O_p,O_q$. On their common detected ensembles,

$$
\frac{O_p}{O_q} =
\frac{\sum_{x\ne0}\eta_x p_x [p_0q_x/(q_0p_x)]}
     {\sum_{x\ne0}\eta_x p_x}.
$$

Here $p_x>0$ at every reachable site. At $x=\pm4$ the 180° ideal profile is dark and contributes a zero ratio. For $k=3,5$ the largest possible double odds occur in the limit of off-origin detection concentrated at $x=\pm2$ and equal $(6061-4142\sqrt2)/11645=0.0174605$. For $k=1,7$ the upper bound is $1.18601$ and the lower bound is zero. The two plotted central returns give odds ratio $537/505=1.06337$. Using the full drawn marker extents and expanding each by two pixels gives a graphical range of $0.871$ to $1.299$. This range describes reading precision. The ordinary pair admits the observed central ratio under this nuisance model. The inference leaves setting-dependent losses, polarization-sensitive collection, phase errors, incoherence and backgrounds to separate calibration. [Site ratios and figure geometry](results/optical_walk_records.json) · [Phase sweep in the original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4001091/)

Allowing a distinct fixed detection efficiency for every position and polarization still leaves a large separation. Each setting has equal probabilities in H and V at the origin, so the centre efficiencies cancel together. The largest off-origin mode ratio for the alternative pair is $(3-2\sqrt2)/5=0.03431$. For the ordinary pair the upper bound increases to 1.32878. If only multiplicative terminal efficiencies vary between settings, the odds ratio can increase by at most the contrast of the changes $\eta_{135,i}/\eta_{180,i}$. Reaching even the lower graphical value would require more than a factor of 25 across modes for the alternative pair. Coherent polarization mixing before loss and additive background require different models. The experiment supplies a physical phase delay and a polarization convention. The authors aligned the zero setting for high interference and agreement of walker distributions, then rotated microscope slides to vary their effective thickness. Their Methods describe this procedure without giving a separate metrological error budget for the 135-degree assignment. Assigning the 135° plate the label $z^3$ puts a real-subfield choice within reach of a return count. Reassigning the phase labels throughout the calibration as well as in the prediction preserves the physical records. In a purely signed native run each finite branch amplitude has one common denominator and integral coordinate numerators. Its squared coordinate probabilities are rational under every allowed embedding. Visible agreement on $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ with external tags neither picks a label for a separate odd-order sheet generator nor breaks complex conjugation. A native bridge would have to identify a physical reference independently of the probability map being tested. ## A separate test of the local fourth-power rule

Keep the same coherent amplitudes under the ordinary embedding. In a physical two-input coin let $u,v$ be the output amplitudes and $m$ the incoming record mass. Assign output masses $m|u|^s/(|u|^s+|v|^s)$ and its complement. Stream them along the two outgoing edges. An empty cell carries zero mass. For $s=2$ ordinary norm conservation reproduces the Born profile. For $s=4$ the procedure gives the causal local alternative from the signed echo program, extended here to complex moduli. It keeps the actual incoming component mass even when some probability has accumulated at an earlier coin. A single normalization of fourth powers across the final screen would yield another model. Nonbalanced wave plates below require an additional extension to those rotations. [Local echo and countermodel](Autonomous_records_and_local_echoes.md)

Broome and colleagues prepared a single localized circularly polarized heralded photon, applied balanced coins, and counted coincidences at the spatial exits. They normalized the detections separately at each depth. Let $D(A,B)=\tfrac12\sum_x|A_x-B_x|$. Their mean distance to the ordinary prediction for coherent walks of depths one through six was 0.031. The exact equally weighted mean distance between the two ideal models is $14855/159198=0.0933115$. Even allowing rounding of the reported mean to the nearest 0.001, the mean experimental distance to the quartic prediction is at least 0.0618114 by the triangle inequality. This ranks the predictions using the authors' published statistic alone. [Broome et al., Fig. 2(a)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.4923v2)

**Sensitivity to terminal efficiencies.** For a quartic profile $q$, the detected distribution is $h_i=\eta_i q_i/\sum_j\eta_j q_j$. Suppose each depth can have its own gain pattern of contrast at most $R=\max\eta_i/\min\eta_i$. For a Born profile $b$, the minimum of $D(b,h)$ is obtained by clipping $b_i$ to $[a q_i,Ra q_i]$ and choosing $a$ so the clipped values sum to one. At fixed $a$, the positive and negative discrepancies each lower-bound the optimum. At the normalization root these two bounds coincide and clipping attains them. Using only the reported mean and its rounding, every compatible experimental profile lies closer to ideal Born than any such detected quartic model for $R<2.006980485$. An exact fit to a compatible profile requires $R$ of at least approximately 3.282595. The experiment did not measure either limiting contrast in this comparison. With one common signed-site gain vector across depths, the mean distance from the corrected quartic to Born stays above 0.05241 even at unbounded contrast. If the common response instead follows the physical path index $(t+x)/2$, the proven lower bound is 0.04712. Both common-response hypotheses constrain only terminal detection. ## Readouts in three other optical settings

* **Phase defect.** Xue, Qin and Tang's four-tick return with a 180° origin plate was $0.615\pm0.011$. Squared and local quartic predict $5/8$ and $75/104$. At ten ticks and 135° they give 0.66739 and 0.83740. The quoted ten-tick variance at 180° is $9.586\pm0.463$ versus predictions 9.547 and 10.628. The corresponding plotted variance is approximately 9.93. There is an adverse observation. With the plate displaced to site one, the nine-step balanced-coin variance around 13.8 lies closer to quartic (14.42) than squared (11.87), while the return at that setting favors squared. The reported mean distance 0.046 across the ten depths with the central 180° plate is also insufficient by itself to rank the models. The 34 digitized markers and the quoted statistical error convention accompany the exact central-plate controls. [Original experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4001091/) · [Phase-defect comparison](results/phase_defect_data.json)
* **Real polarization preparations.** Bian and colleagues reported twelve three-step inconclusive-port frequencies, using two input signs and six wave-plate settings. Using the stated nominal plate angles, the root mean squared residual is 0.00709 for squared weights, 0.15551 for quartic weights throughout, and 0.07910 for squared preparation followed by quartic routing. The two 90° controls allow the three ideal assignments to agree. Separately, a horizontally polarized launch gives a combined frequency of 0.8296 at the two extreme exits by complement of the two inner frequencies. For the stated 12.23° plate the predictions are 0.82856 and 0.95894. The combined exit is insensitive to a figure-versus-equation sign assignment. These are conditional detections. Their reported per-row statistical errors and small residuals for squared weights remain in the data. [Bian et al., Table I and Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.05540v1) · [All rows, alternatives and controls](results/unambiguous_walk_data.json)
* **Eighteen time-bin steps.** For two opposite circular single-bin inputs, Fenwick and colleagues reported joint time-bin/polarization squared affinity above 0.97 at every measured depth. The 18-step ideal profiles differ by 0.47963 in total variation even after summing the polarizations. For distributions $a,b$, Cauchy–Schwarz applied to $(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)$ gives $D(a,b)\le\sqrt{1-(\sum\sqrt{ab})^2}$. Summing detector outcomes contracts total variation. The observed final position data are therefore within 0.17321 of ideal squared and at least 0.30642 from local quartic. Here the source used attenuated laser pulses with mean 0.8 photons per pulse. Its Kerr-gated scan gives discretized peak intensities and has a different preparation and background model from a heralded coincidence count. [Fenwick et al., Eq. 6, Fig. 3 and appendices A and D](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02238v1) · [Exact model distributions](results/optical_walk_records.json)

These comparisons rank specified predictions on reported records. They leave a physical derivation of the coin, detector, reference phase and common clock outstanding. A direct experiment on the program's own label question would need to map a physical reference onto the sheet operation before using its interference counts to choose an embedding.
