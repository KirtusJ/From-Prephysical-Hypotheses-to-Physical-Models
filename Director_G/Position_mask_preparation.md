# Preparing arbitrary signed history counts with position filters

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex), answering Director Z's product-event question.

One sharp right-moving launch suffices to prepare any prescribed positive and negative history counts on both rails at one named well, with every other endpoint empty. The masks accept whole positions; the required clock depends on the target counts and well. Suppose scores add over terminal ports, vanish on an empty multiset, are independent of position and horizon, and double on extending any admissible event by a free tick. The two initially distinct rail scores then coincide and are linear combinations of path count and squared signed count. Nonnegative nontrivial scores give nonnegative coefficients and fix an initially unknown common factor at two. With factor two supplied, the spatial variant below derives position independence for nonnegative line scores and for real scores on the stated finite rings. A three-tick experiment distinguishes the two terms. ## The walk and the event

At each tick a word appends R or L and steps one site in that direction. An L after L reverses its sign. All other transitions preserve it. Start at position zero in R with positive sign. A sequence of sets of positions defines an event: a word survives if its position belongs to the corresponding set at every tick. Each endpoint has a multiset `m=(p,q)` of positive and negative words. Write `J(p,q)=(q,p)`. Counts include opposite-sign words even when their signed sum vanishes.

The score of an event is the sum over its terminal oriented ports of real functions of their multisets, each zero at the empty multiset. Unless indicated otherwise, the functions are stationary across horizons and may differ between rails. Appending a free, unrestricted tick to any allowed product event multiplies its unnormalized score by two. This is a premise about comparisons across horizons. A physical implementation of the event would have to preserve coherence among all the positions accepted by a filter. Reading the route and pooling the resulting counts is a different instrument. History measures and interference sum rules have a longstanding general formulation. Single-photon quantum walks have experimentally tested how decoherence changes absorption at boundaries. The construction here specifies particular time-dependent apertures and their exact accepted words. [Sorkin, 1994](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9401003) · [Broome et al., 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.4923)

## A preparation by whole positions

At tick $t$ label a site by its deficit $d=(t-x)/2$. R leaves $d$ unchanged and L increases it by one. A fixed deficit in the table below denotes the moving physical site $x=t-2d$, with both rails admitted.

First retain the union of the positions of the two words `RRRRRRRR` and `LLLRLLLR` at each of their eight ticks. Their first arrivals separate. By the second tick the moving branch has deficit two and no further emission from deficit zero can catch it in one step. It never comes back. Exactly one positive R word therefore occupies each of deficits zero and six at tick eight. The second word has four L-after-L transitions.

During accumulation retain deficits 0, 4, 6 and 10. In either corridor starting at $b=0$ or $6$, the position $b$ carries a positive singleton and $b+4$ stores its accumulated multiset on R. To add one word, enable the following extra positions on successive ticks. A dash admits only the four persistent positions.

| Word added to the accumulator | Extra deficits, relative to $b$ | Unique route from the singleton to the accumulator |
|---|---|---|
| Negative | $1,2,3,$ —, — | LLLLR |
| Positive | $1,2,2,3,$ —, — | LLRLLR |

The first and last intermediate sites, 1 and 3, open once each. Only the singleton can enter at 1. A pause at 2 is two sites from either wall. At the first wall-only tick the traveling word arrives on L at 4 while the stored words arrive on R. The second wall-only tick sends both to R there. Every attempt to leave 4 to the left increases the deficit to 5 and is rejected. No history crosses between the two corridors because their nearest occupied walls differ by two deficits. This proves the stated update for arbitrary stored counts. [Lean proof of both pulses](lean/PositionMaskPulse.lean)

For desired output $U=(p_R,q_R)$ and $V=(p_L,q_L)$, complete all pulses from source 0 into stock 4 to store $JV$, then all pulses from source 6 into stock 10 to store $U$. Run one pulse at a time. Keep only 4 and 10 for one tick to delete the sources. On successive ticks $i=1,\ldots,6$ keep only $4+i$ and 10. The moving words take six L steps, reversing sign five times. The stationary words stay on R. The two meet for the first time at the final well. Its R and L multisets are exactly $U,V$, and every other well is empty. This includes either or both inputs empty. The depth and position before any translation are

$$T=15+6(p_R+q_L)+5(q_R+p_L),\qquad x=T-20.$$

At most five positions are kept in one layer. To realize the same pair at any prescribed $x_*$, first move a single word by $h=x_*-x$ and finish on R. Use $h$ right steps for $h\ge0$ and $1-h$ left steps followed by one right step otherwise. If the word is negative, append the zero-displacement word RLRLLR, whose sole L-after-L restores positive sign. Keep its one position at each prefix tick, then start the translated schedule. The construction works on a ring of circumference $N$ whenever $N/\gcd(N,2)>11$. At each tick reduction modulo that period is injective on the possible outgoing deficits 0 through 11. A one-site prefix also has a unique successor in its chosen direction on these rings. Smaller rings require separate analysis.

The same ordering also proves preparation at deficit period 11, namely on rings of 11 and 22 wells. The only new allowed arrow from wrapping is $10\to11\equiv0$. During the first corridor's pulses stock 10 is empty, so this arrow carries no words. During the second corridor's pulses the only retained successor of a word at 0 is 0: no further pulse opens deficit 1. Leaks from 10 can therefore accumulate at 0, but can never enter either output stock. On the source-deletion tick the allowed set is $\{4,10\}$, which discards both that surplus and any new seam leak. The six final layers exclude 0. The initial eight-tick split and one-site prefix have distinct successors as on the line. This proves full terminal equality for arbitrary counts, with unchanged depth. Other pulse orders can open deficit 1 after a leak and change the output. In Director Z's 256-target census on each of these two rings, the prescribed order passed all 256, reversing the corridor order passed 31, and the seeded shuffle passed 54. At smaller periods this construction has no general guarantee. [Cross-review, order tests and first failures](../Director_Z/cross_review/subagent_xr39_position_masks/FINDINGS.md)

The bound of five is a bound on positions simultaneously admitted at a tick. Total depth grows with the requested multiplicities, and a site-translation prefix costs additional ticks. The Lean proof verifies the isolated five-site pulse with incoming reservoir and stock on R; the two-corridor assembly, ring and prefix are justified by the path argument above. ## What continuation then fixes

Write $F_R,F_L$ for position-independent scores. A terminal pair $U,V$ localized at one site has unrestricted daughters $U+V$ on R and $U+JV$ on L. The construction realizes every pair. Consequently

$$F_R(U+V)+F_L(U+JV)=2F_R(U)+2F_L(V).$$

Setting $V=0$ makes the rail functions equal everywhere, with common value $F$. Setting $U=0$ gives $F(JV)=F(V)$. With $C=(1,1)$ one obtains $F(U+C)=F(U)+F(C)$. Subtract $a(p+q)$ with $a=F(C)/2$. Removing cancelling pairs then leaves an even function $g(n)$ of $n=p-q$, with $g(0)=0$. The singleton $V=(1,0)$ gives $g(n+1)+g(n-1)=2g(n)+2g(1)$. Its first differences increase by $2g(1)$, hence $g(n)=c n^2$ for all integers, with $c=g(1)$. Conversely, additivity of count and the square identity for sum and difference give continuation at each site and hence for every position event. Thus

$$F(p,q)=a(p+q)+c(p-q)^2.$$

If the rail scores are nonnegative and nontrivial, even an initially unknown **common** factor $k$ is fixed in this position-independent version. Sum the four single-input equations for $(m,0),(Jm,0),(0,m),(0,Jm)$. Their left side is twice the sum of both rail scores at $m,Jm$ and their right side is $k$ times that sum. Choose $m$ with positive sum. Nonnegativity at $(n,n)$ gives $a\ge0$ and at $(n,0)$ for unbounded $n$ gives $c\ge0$.

**Spatially varying scores.** At supplied factor two the same classification holds on the whole line for nonnegative, site-specific stationary scores, and on the rings above even for real scores of either sign. To see this fix $m$. At each site $x$, let $A_x$ and $B_x$ be its R and L scores at $m$. Split each into its even and odd parts under $m\leftrightarrow Jm$ by taking half the sum and half the difference. Isolated R or L inputs at *each* site are available by the preparation and prefix above. In their even equations both inputs have the same daughter sum, so $A_x^+=B_x^+=C_x$ and $2C_x=C_{x+1}+C_{x-1}$. Such a function is affine. On the line nonnegativity at all sites makes it constant, and on a ring periodicity does so. For the odd parts the single-input equations give

$$A^-_{x+1}=A^-_x+B^-_x,\qquad B^-_{x+1}=A^-_x.$$

The transfer matrix has eigenvalues $(1\pm\sqrt5)/2$. Neither has modulus one, so its only bounded two-sided orbit is zero. Positivity bounds both odd parts in absolute value by the common even part on the line. On a finite ring the orbit is periodic. The scores are therefore independent of site and rail and the preceding argument applies. Signed unbounded scores on the line give explicit exceptions in Director Z's Proposition 2. This spatial extension uses the factor two as a hypothesis.

## A three-tick read of the free coefficient

Keep both wells at tick one, keep only position zero at tick two, then open both outgoing wells at tick three. The paths LRL and RLL reach $(-1,L)$ with opposite signs. LRR and RLR reach $(+1,R)$ with equal positive signs. Let $\alpha=a/(a+c)$ for nonnegative, nonzero scores. With the unit launch calibrated to one, each tick divides the unnormalized score by two. The two per-launch weights are therefore

$$P(-1,L)=\frac{\alpha}{4},\qquad P(+1,R)=\frac12-\frac{\alpha}{4}.$$

The total admitted weight is $1/2$ at every $\alpha$. An exact dark left port with a functioning detector would select $a=0$ within this family. A measured upper bound $u$ on the *calibrated conditional left fraction among accepted launches* gives $\alpha\le2u$. Selecting either single first arm predicts $1/8$ per launch at each outgoing well. With a common positive efficiency per detector across settings, let $Y_{s,d}$ be the expected signal yield for setting $s$ and detector $d$. The ratio $C=Y_{\rm both,L}Y_{\rm single,R}/(Y_{\rm both,R}Y_{\rm single,L})=\alpha/(2-\alpha)$ cancels both efficiencies and the exposure of each setting, giving $\alpha=2C/(1+C)$. Background, crosstalk, setting-dependent loss and loss of coherence can alter the comparison. No frequency for this experiment has been supplied. The signed two-rail classification also leaves the physical character of a larger gain alphabet open.

The executable checks exact signed-word counts before scoring. In one deliberately changed aperture, adding well $-4$ at tick ten changes the final target $(1,0)$ to $(2,1)$ on the same rail. Both have signed difference one. A check using squared amplitudes alone would have missed those two extra paths. The file retains all 78 mismatches encountered in 192 small-ring trials, including each affected target. The 256 line targets, one 371-tick asymmetric target and prescribed-site prefix checks supplement the arbitrary-count proof. The bounded-final-read conjecture remains open.
