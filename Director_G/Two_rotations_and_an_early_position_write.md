# Two rail rotations and an early position write determine a common site response

Can position records select the ordinary probability law when a detector can use any continuous function of a well's squared amplitude? On the eight-site Hadamard walk, two comparisons suffice on a dense family of real rail preparations. One compares two randomized rotations, with a single adjustable mixing fraction. The other inserts the first position write into a two-tick flight. The comparisons determine both the mixing fraction and the detector law. In particular the resulting score assigns the four-step ring read its usual two informative rates and two null rates, under the same site response. [Four-step instrument](A_rail_measurement_from_four_free_steps.md)

These are proposed measurements. The signed step alone does not imply their observed equalities.

## Two comparisons on the same ring

At a known site, order the rails as $(R,L)$. One tick applies the real Hadamard and streams the two outputs in opposite directions. Let $e=(R+L)/\sqrt2$, so an input $e$ goes right with unit squared amplitude. Measure angles from $e$ and write $w=\cos^2\theta$ for the rightward mass. The stipulated detector pools the squared rail coordinates **within each well**, scores the result by one stable finite nonnegative function $f$, and divides by the sum of the eight site scores. Assume a calibrated dark response $f(0)=0$ and positive normalizers on all panels in use. A one-tick read then reports right with probability
$$g(w)=\frac{f(w)}{f(w)+f(1-w)}.$$
Sharp launches give $g(1)=1$, and $c=f(1/2)>0$ is required for a balanced panel. The same response must hold at each origin, at the chosen clocks and on a freshly prepared or conditionally restored rail.

Let $J$ rotate the rail by $\pi/2$. The six- and eighteen-tick flights have antipodal matrices $(3I-J)/4$ and $(3I+J)/4$. A successful position write there supplies an opposite pair of rotations $B^{-1},B$, where $B=(3I+J)/\sqrt{10}$ and $\alpha=\arctan(1/3)$ is its angle. Either clock has the same four site masses $(1,1,1,5)/8$ up to ordering on every rail. Two six-tick non-antipodal heralds give $J$ up to sign. The twelve-tick flight has four equal-mass positions, each preserving the rail up to sign. It can provide an identity reference. Recenter at the written site. These heralds must occur with positive probability, in particular $f(1/8),f(5/8)>0$. The input $e$ is accessible from $R$ on a six-tick non-antipodal herald. Clock matching, isolation from old records and acceptance of the prepared populations are apparatus assumptions. [Matrices and timed position feedback](Restoring_a_rail_with_position_records.md)

The last four alternatives can all take eighteen free ticks. Pad the six-tick kick with a twelve-tick identity. Pad the twelve-tick $I$ or $J$ branch with a center write after two ticks, followed by one four-tick null. Their raw rail matrices are $(I+J)/2$ and $\pm(I-J)/4$, with product $\pm I/4$. The conditional acceptance of this padding can depend on the input for a nonlinear detector. The duration of each write and routing of its classical controller must also be accounted for. The underlying 24-tick ring period was previously established by [Tregenna et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0304204).

Prepare $B^n e$ for integral $n$ and set $\gamma=2\alpha$, $w_n=(1+\cos n\gamma)/2$. In the first comparison choose $B$ or $B^{-1}$ with equal classical probability and read one tick later. In the reference arm choose $I$ or $J$ with one classical probability $\lambda$ for $I$. Use the same $\lambda$ for every $n$. It can be determined at $n=0$ and tested at the remaining preparations. The comparison asks whether the rightward rates agree:
$$\frac{g(w_{n+1})+g(w_{n-1})}{2}
=\lambda g(w_n)+(1-\lambda)[1-g(w_n)]. \tag{1}$$
There is an independent kinematic reason to try this comparison. For any input density matrix $\rho$, averaging $B\rho B^T$ and $B^{-1}\rho B^{-T}$ gives $(9\rho+J\rho J^T)/10$ by cancellation of the cross terms. Turning that matrix identity into probabilities for both randomized preparations would already assume a rule for equivalent mixtures. Equation (1) treats rate agreement as data and leaves the bias free.

For the second comparison, take two ticks from the same prepared input. At the first clock either leave the flight coherent or write position, preserve the outgoing rail and pool the two written labels only **after** measuring the final position. In the coherent flight the right edge, center and left edge have masses $w/2,1/2,(1-w)/2$. Each edge comes from a single first position. The two arrivals at the center occupy orthogonal rails. A written first position leaves a sharp rail, whose next two exits have equal rates. Thus the written experiment gives incident rates $g(w)/2,1/2,[1-g(w)]/2$. Test equality of the complete final site rates with and without the write. Dividing the right-edge probability by the center probability on the coherent side gives
$$f(w_n/2)=c\,g(w_n). \tag{2}$$
The left edge gives the complementary equation. The contrast uses a change of when position is recorded. A write which changes the surviving rail or an efficiency depending on the first outcome can change it.

Classical setting assignments must be independent of the incident population. For probabilistically prepared kicks, tabulate each assigned arm's launch count, heralds, failures, timeouts and successful final outcomes separately. Estimate the conditional rate for each arm, then combine those rates with the assigned weights in (1). Pooling successes directly gives weights proportional to assignment probability times acceptance. Equal acceptance totals cannot establish transfer of a hidden population. For example, take two equally frequent types that always report different terminal bits. If one herald accepts only the first and another only the second, both accept half the launches and their retained bits are opposite. A single stable score and rail-preserving preparation make the conditional rates used in (1) meaningful. With hidden types one needs a preparation or separate evidence that the selected types have the same relevant response across source, kicks, reference and read. The six- and eighteen-tick acceptances in the scalar model coincide automatically. Their numerical value and the exposure needed for long words still depend on $f$.

## Identification

Write $h_n=2g(w_n)-1$ and $\cos\beta=2\lambda-1$, $0\le\beta\le\pi$. Equation (1) gives $h_{n+1}+h_{n-1}=2\cos\beta\,h_n$. With $h_{-n}=h_n$ and $h_0=1$, induction gives $h_n=\cos n\beta$, including the endpoints. Since $e^{i\gamma}=(4+3i)/5$ has infinite order, its powers are dense on the circle. Indeed a root of unity would make $2\cos\gamma=8/5$ a rational algebraic integer, hence an integer.

Continuity of $f$ **at half occupancy** already restricts the possible frequencies. Whenever $e^{in_j\gamma}\to1$, (2) gives $g(w_{n_j})\to1$ and therefore $e^{in_j\beta}\to1$. The homomorphism $e^{in\gamma}\mapsto e^{in\beta}$ is continuous at identity, hence uniformly continuous on the subgroup. It extends uniquely to a continuous circle homomorphism. Lifting its argument to the real line gives an additive continuous argument, and periodicity gives an integer multiple of the angle. Thus for some $k\ge0$,
$$g(w_n)=G_k(w_n),\qquad G_k(w)=\frac{1+T_k(2w-1)}2,$$
where $T_k(\cos t)=\cos kt$. Half-occupancy continuity in the binary odds gives $G_k(1/2)=1/2$ because the $w_n$ are dense. Hence $k$ is odd.

**A continuous score.** By density and continuity, (2) gives $f(w/2)=cG_k(w)$ throughout $[0,1]$. The binary identity $G_k(w)[f(w)+f(1-w)]=f(w)$ extends there as well, by cross multiplication. For odd $k\ge5$, the two smallest positive roots of $G_k$ are $x=\sin^2(\pi/k)<1/2$ and $y=\sin^2(2\pi/k)=4x(1-x)>2x$. The binary identity gives $f(x)=0$, so the half-mass identity gives $G_k(2x)=0$, impossible between these two roots. For $k=3$, $G_3(3/4)=0$ would give $f(3/8)=0$. Since $G_3(3/8)=27/32>0$, the binary identity would then force $f(5/8)=0$, extinguishing the observed antipodal herald. This argument uses positive heralds on the apparatus and cross multiplication even where a hypothetical off-orbit binary normalizer vanishes. Thus $k=1$, $g(w)=w$, and $\lambda=(1+\cos\gamma)/2=9/10$. Equation (2) gives $f(w)=2cw$ below half. The binary odds determine $f(w)=2cw$ above half and below unity, and continuity fixes the endpoint. On any normalized site table this common score yields the squared site masses. This applies also to a longer flight when the same detector law transfers there.

**Regularity at one preparation.** Alternatively suppose $f$ is differentiable just at $1/2$. Let $w_n$ approach 1 through distinct values. The left derivative of $f$ in (2) gives $f'(1/2)=2c G'_k(1)=2ck^2$. The binary odds at $1/2$ give $g'(1/2)=f'(1/2)/(2c)$. Since $g$ coincides with the polynomial on a dense set, $g'(1/2)=G'_k(1/2)=k\sin(k\pi/2)$. These yield $k=1$ and the same $\lambda$. This weaker premise determines the binary rates on the tested orbit and the score on its half-masses. It permits changing $f(5/8)$ alone, for instance. Orbit masses have reduced denominator dividing $2\cdot5^{|n|}$, and their half-masses have denominator dividing $4\cdot5^{|n|}$. The change can alter the six-tick herald rate even when the comparisons remain exact.

## Competing predictions and finite exposure

For the binary read alone the continuous score $f=G_3$ gives $g=G_3$, and (1) holds with a different bias $\lambda=81/250$. At $w_1=9/10$ it requires $f(9/20)=81/500$ under (2), whereas its score is $81/125$. The directly observable coherent right-edge/center ratio is $162/125$ against the written ratio $81/250$. In general arbitrarily high odd harmonics can approximate the ordinary predictions on any fixed finite menu with nonzero tolerance. For the finitely many angles $t_j=\arccos(2w_j-1)$ in the menu, simultaneous recurrence yields arbitrarily large multiples $m$ of four with every $mt_j$ as close to zero modulo $2\pi$ as desired. Take $k=1+m$ and include $\gamma$ among the angles. Then $G_k(w_j)\to w_j$, the fitted biases approach $9/10$, and all panel probabilities converge where the ordinary normalizers are positive. Global identification uses the family of comparisons and a regularity premise. No finite-precision exclusion of every continuous rival follows.

The prior [dyadic calibration](Local_writes_and_dyadic_site_scores.md) instead uses programmable labels and a remote spectator. Both results require stable whole-site scoring. The eight-site test adds no evidence that an actual detector satisfies this premise or that differing launch histories always have the same response.

## A separate calibration test on a cyclic angle grid

Consider an alternative experiment with \(N\ge 6\) even, at the real rail angles \(\theta_j=\pi j/N\). It requires a calibrated relative-angle preparation and analyzer, a common selection rule across settings, and a binary detector. Define \(C_j=2p_j-1\) from its right-click rate. Assume the read depends only on the relative setting, \(C_{-j}=C_j\), exact agreement at zero (\(C_0=1\)), and exact reversal at the half cycle (\(C_{N/2}=-1\)). Impose a further test: the real matrix with entries \(C_{a-b}\) is positive semidefinite. This condition can be checked on the rate table by diagonalizing it. Positivity of each individual click rate alone would not imply it.

Then \(p_1\le\cos^2(\pi/N)\). **Equality at that one neighboring setting fixes every grid rate** to \(p_j=\cos^2(\pi j/N)\). To prove this, diagonalize the circulant in the discrete Fourier basis. Its normalized eigenvalues \(w_k\) are nonnegative, sum to one, and \(C_j=\sum_k w_k\cos(2\pi kj/N)\) by symmetry. The half-cycle read forces all even weights to vanish. The largest possible value of \(C_1\) on the remaining modes is \(\cos(2\pi/N)\), attained only at \(k=\pm1\). The entire grid response then follows by substitution. This is a finite version of the [positive-definite Fourier representation](https://www.tropp.caltech.edu/notes/Tro22-Matrix-Analysis-LN.pdf).

For a shortfall \(\epsilon=\cos(2\pi/N)-C_1\), the total weight outside the two fundamental modes is at most \(\epsilon/[\cos(2\pi/N)-\cos(6\pi/N)]\), since \(k=\pm3\) give the next largest cosine among odd modes. The same bound applies to the maximum absolute **probability** error on the grid. This statement assumes exact positivity, endpoint calibration and setting symmetry. Finer grids narrow the spectral gap.

On twelve settings a concrete adverse table retains all ordinary \(C_j\) except \(C_2,C_{10},C_4,C_8\), which are set to zero. Every probability remains in range, the half-cycle complement holds at every setting, and the neighboring read is unchanged. Its normalized fifth Fourier eigenvalue is \(-1/12\), so it fails precisely the positive-semidefinite test among the stated hypotheses. Conversely a genuine higher odd cosine alone passes positivity and endpoint reversal but misses the neighboring maximum unless it aliases the fundamental. A continuous response \(\cos(2(N+1)\theta)\) aliases the fundamental at every sampled angle; it differs between samples. Exact grid calibration therefore selects a binary response on those preparations. Its positivity, extremal neighboring rate and physical realization still require independent justification before using it to claim an emergent probability law.
## Reusing the walk's own rotation

The eighteen-tick antipodal herald above supplies an angle \(\alpha=\arctan(1/3)\). Prepare \(e\), retain the successful branch at each repetition, and let \(C_n=2p_n-1\) be the signed one-tick right read after \(n\) successes. Require stable, relative-angle registration \(C_{-n}=C_n,\ C_0=1\), including when comparing different preparation histories. For each \(j\ge1\) test the matrix \((C_{a-b})\) restricted to indices \(0,j-1,j,j+1\) for positive semidefiniteness. Only rates up to repetition \(j+1\) occur in that test.

Put \(c=C_1\) and \(\eta=1+C_2-2c^2\). Each passing matrix is a Gram matrix of real unit vectors. The vector \(v_{j+1}+v_{j-1}-2c v_j\) has squared norm \(2\eta\); taking its inner product with \(v_0\) bounds \(|C_{j+1}+C_{j-1}-2c C_j|\le\sqrt{2\eta}\). Thus when the first two measured means are \(4/5,7/25\), the recurrence is exact and forces \(C_n=\cos(2n\alpha)\) throughout the tested range. For \(|c|<1\) and nonzero residual, iteration bounds \(|C_n-\cos(n\arccos c)|\le(n-1)\sqrt{2\eta}/\sqrt{1-c^2}\): the response to a unit residual at step \(j\) is \(\sin((n-j)\arccos c)/\sin(\arccos c)\). A finite test establishes only its finite range.

If the conditions hold for every repetition and the same probability depends continuously on rail angle modulo \(\pi\), the result determines the entire real-angle binary read. Indeed \(2\cos(2\alpha)=8/5\) cannot be the sum of a root of unity and its inverse, as it is rational and nonintegral. Thus \(\alpha/\pi\) is irrational and the orbit is dense. This uses the walk's available herald and its specified state update. Neither selecting successful preparations nor normalizing their reads explains why an actual detector should pass the Gram tests or return the two calibrated probabilities \(9/10,16/25\). The inference concerns binary outcomes on real rays; it does not supply joint probabilities for other instruments.
### Can the Gram table come from one binary history?

A positive-semidefinite table can be a Gram table of real vectors. Interpreting its entries as correlations of jointly defined **binary** records is a stronger physical claim. For the first three orientations suppose there are signs \(X_0,X_1,X_2\) on a common launch population, and agreement of a chosen pair represents its right read. For each launch, differing endpoints require a change on at least one neighboring pair. With complete acceptance this implies \(d_{02}\le d_{01}+d_{12}\) for mismatch rates. The calibrated table gives \(d_{01}=d_{12}=1/10,\ d_{02}=9/25\), exceeding the bound by \(4/25\). Thus assigning simultaneous signs to these settings cannot justify their proposed Gram table on a common undisturbed cohort. The pair interpretation is an additional hypothesis about the detector. [Leggett and Garg on joint histories and noninvasive measurability](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.54.857)

Count failures per original launch. If panel \(ij\) accepts fraction \(q_{ij}>0\) and \(d_{ij}\) is its mismatch fraction among acceptances, its underlying mismatch probability lies between \(q_{ij}d_{ij}\) and \(q_{ij}d_{ij}+1-q_{ij}\), even when the acceptance depends on the hidden signs. With random panel assignment independent of the common input population, a necessary condition is therefore

\[
q_{02}d_{02}\le q_{01}d_{01}+1-q_{01}+q_{12}d_{12}+1-q_{12}.
\]

At equal acceptance \(q\) the three rates rule out such a model precisely when \(q>25/27\). The boundary is attainable. In 54 equally weighted records take 9 sign triples \(+--\), 9 triples \(++-\), and 36 triples \(+++\). Each adjacent panel rejects four records on which its own pair differs. The endpoint panel rejects four equal records. The three panels each retain 50 records, with 5, 5 and 18 mismatches respectively. A shared random overall sign can make each unselected sign unbiased without changing any comparison. Further independent thinning within each panel preserves its conditional mismatch rate.

With ideal squared selection, one successful antipodal gate costs a factor \(5/8\) in yield and two cost \(25/64\), conditional on a prepared input. The witness can match yields \(5/8,5/8,25/64\) by further panel-specific thinning. **Those three success totals and conditional binary rates alone leave the joint-sign rival viable.** This construction matches the three summaries; a required common herald, nested selection, a record of failed outcomes, or further settings can impose additional constraints. No hardware rates are claimed.
### Recover the preparation on the same launch

The eight-site walk has a way to cross the preceding loss threshold. It uses whole-position writes and feedback on their outcomes. Conditional on a common prepared input, keep every launch in the denominator, including timed-out launches. For a positive attempt use eighteen ticks, for a negative attempt six ticks. Write and recenter. With sign \(s=\pm1\) the antipodal outcome applies rotation \(s\alpha\), and the other three outcomes all apply \(-s\pi/4\) up to global sign. These are the signed physical columns above. Under ideal squared registration the respective probabilities are \(5/8,3/8\) on *every* incoming ray. The latter probability pools the three distinct recorded wells.

Let \(t\) be the signed sum of attempts and \(r\) the signed sum of antipodal hits. The current angle relative to the input is \(r\alpha-(t-r)\pi/4\) modulo \(\pi\). Start with one positive attempt. At subsequent checkpoints, if \(r<1\), make four positive attempts; if \(r>1\), make four negative attempts. Check only at the end of each group. Then \(t\equiv1\bmod4\), so \(r=1\) delivers precisely the desired ray at every successful checkpoint, including the initial one. On failure the exponent lies in \(\{-2,-1,0,2,3,4\}\). A negative group takes 24 ticks and can be padded to 72 with four twelve-tick identity flights, retaining all wells and recentering each time. Once successful, fill remaining groups with the same identity instrument. All launches of one bounded gate use the same clock.

Start the unabsorbed distribution with mass \(3/8\) at zero. A group moves \(r\) toward one by a signed \({\rm Binomial}(4,5/8)\) increment, possibly overshooting. Absorb exactly at one. The [exact rational replay](check_rotation_site_score.py) gives a two-gate yield \(q^2=0.938702\) (rounded) with ten groups per gate, compared with \(0.921060\) with nine. Each gate has a budget of \(18+10(72)=738\) ticks excluding apparatus latencies. Two gates use the same walker. Their yields multiply by the state-independent conditional rates. In the three panels the one-gate acceptance is \(q\), the two-gate acceptance \(q^2\), and the right reads on success remain \(9/10,9/10,16/25\). Even counting all timeouts, \(q^2(9/25)>2[1-q+q/10]\). The predicted data thus exclude the specified common undisturbed sign triples **without discarding the preparation losses from the inequality**.

This is an instrument prediction under the squared registration and conditional update, not an observation. Assign panels after the common input herald. To interpret an experimental violation one still needs relative-setting registration across panels, the same incoming population, and the stated pair interpretation; an invasive or setting-dependent record remains a distinct possibility. The feedback removes the particular loss explanation and does not establish positivity or a universal Born rule. For the circuit principle of retaining and correcting failed unitary branches, see [Paetznick and Svore](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.1074).
### A test scored per original prepared launch

After the common input herald, assign one of \(01,12,02\) uniformly with fresh randomness independent of the apparatus, including its hidden state and past record. Record the assignment, the feedback history, and a terminal outcome or timeout. A launch scores one only for a completed neighboring-panel agreement or a completed endpoint-panel mismatch. Count every other assigned launch as zero. With right interpreted as agreement, this scoring uses a single terminal bit and its completion flag. The interpretation of the bit as comparing a common triple is the hypothesis under test.

For any three pre-existing signs, at most two of \(X_0=X_1,\ X_1=X_2,\ X_0\ne X_2\) are true. Rejecting any subset cannot increase this number. Thus the conditional win probability is at most \(2/3\), even if the signs and the rejection policy change after every launch in response to all earlier records. The bound needs neither a squared readout nor estimates of conditional rates among successes. It does need the same triple to govern all three possible assignments on that launch. If the least likely assignment conditional on the hidden state is only guaranteed to have probability \(l\), the bound weakens to \(1-l\).

For the recovered gate the ideal squared-readout prediction is \(w=[2q(9/10)+q^2(9/25)]/3\). The first group budget to beat \(2/3\) in the exact calculation is eight. At ten groups \(w=0.6939640973\). A model allowing an arbitrary response on a fraction \(f\) of launches and a common triple on the rest can reach at most \((1-f)2/3+f\), with assignment independent of class. Thus reproducing the predicted mean would require \(f\ge 3w-2\), about 8.19 percent. This concerns a decomposition of response models, not a measured fraction of physical systems.

For a fixed trial count \(n\), the upper tail of \({\rm Binomial}(n,2/3)\) bounds the chance of at least the observed win count under the common-triple model. One proof samples successive outcomes with fresh uniforms against their conditional success thresholds, all at most \(2/3\). For inspection during the run, choose a fixed alternative rate \(r\in(2/3,1)\) before collecting data. Starting at one, multiply by \(r/(2/3)\) after each win and \((1-r)/(1/3)\) after each loss. Conditional expected multipliers are at most one, so the probability of ever crossing \(1/\alpha\) is at most \(\alpha\) by the nonnegative supermartingale maximal inequality. A missing terminal read at the assigned deadline is a loss. A missing assignment log prevents auditing the denominator. A rejection of the common-triple model on this instrument would still allow an invasive detector. [Elkouss and Wehner on memory-robust win/loss tests](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.07233)
### A rival that passes the entire three-panel game

Within the paper's normalized site-score family keep the signed walk and its conditional ray update fixed. Change only the score: take \(f(w)=w\) outside \([1/4,9/25]\), and interpolate linearly through \((1/4,1/4),(81/250,7/20),(9/25,9/25)\) inside it. This continuous, strictly increasing score preserves darkness, sharp reads and normalized probabilities. Every feedback gate or padding write has masses drawn from \(0,1/8,1/4,5/8\), on which the score agrees with the identity. Thus for **each preceding record** the next feedback outcome has the ordinary probability and the same conditional update. Induction gives identical laws for all gate histories, including the names of the ordinary wells, timeouts and padding outcomes. The terminal read after one or two successful rotations has right mass \(9/10\) or \(16/25\); both masses and their complements retain their scores. Even keeping every intermediate write and every assigned trial in the three-panel game cannot distinguish this detector from the ordinary one.

Add a panel with **three** recovered positive rotations and the same terminal read. Its right mass from the signed rail is \(81/250\); its complement is \(169/250\). The two detectors now give right probabilities \(81/250\) and \(175/513\) among completions. Multiply each by the same \(q^3\) to obtain right completions per original prepared launch. This separation follows from the circuit and the specified rival. It has not been measured.

In the four-setting Gram test the first two means still fix \(c=4/5\) and \(\eta=0\). The third mean of the rival violates \(C_3-2cC_2+C_1=0\) by \(2(175/513-81/250)\). The putative vector \(v_1+v_3-2c v_2\) would therefore have zero squared length and a nonzero inner product with \(v_0\). This identifies exactly which proposed positivity test the rival fails. The three-panel game can test its own common-sign hypothesis; observing its predicted violation would leave this alternative to the ordinary binary law intact. For the distinction between equivalence on a set of experiments and equivalence on all available tests, see [Spekkens](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406166).
### A finite calibration of the unsampled real rays

Within the stable normalized site-score family add the assumption that \(f\) is nondecreasing. Then \(g(w)=f(w)/(f(w)+f(1-w))\) is nondecreasing wherever defined and \(g(1-w)=1-g(w)\). This gives an alternative to passing infinitely many Gram tests. For a finite ordered grid including 0 and 1, let its largest consecutive gap be \(d\). If every grid value obeys \(|g(w_j)-w_j|\le\eta\), then **every** binary rate satisfies \(|g(w)-w|\le d+\eta\): sandwich \(g(w)\) between the two neighboring measured rates. This uses a single response across preparations and angles; finite agreement alone cannot establish monotonicity or stability.

Two sources already in the walk shorten the required menu. Launch from \(e\) or \(R\), apply \(n\) recovered positive rotations, then read one tick. Their right masses are \((1+\cos(2n\alpha))/2\) and \((1+\sin(2n\alpha))/2\) respectively. Complementary masses are covered by the same binary counts. Include the calibrated endpoints and midpoint. The exact rational replay enumerates all \(n=1,\ldots,41\) from both signed rails. The largest gap first falls below \(1/100\) at depth 41 and is \(0.009928074\) rounded. Thus an empirical simultaneous error bound \(\eta\) at those settings would give a uniform absolute probability error below \(0.009929+\eta\), conditional on the stated response class. This bounds the binary read, not the unnormalized score on a many-well field.

For an exposure estimate under ordinary squared registration, give each recovered gate 23 groups, padding completed ones as above. The longest panel then takes \(41(18+23\cdot72)=68{,}634\) flight ticks before the terminal read and succeeds with probability \(0.95612\) per prepared launch. The calculation includes recovery timeouts and assumes the specified conditional ray update, state-independent herald rates and no extra read duration. Actual calibration requires recorded acceptance at each depth and an uncertainty interval for the conditional read. These numerical guarantees concern the sampling grid and predicted yield; no empirical value of \(\eta\) has been supplied.
### Extending the finite calibration to several wells

Even **exact** agreement on every binary ray leaves a many-well ambiguity. The increasing score \(f(u)=u[1+u(1-u)]\) gives \(g(w)=w\) for every binary input, because complementary masses acquire the same multiplier. On the native two-tick table \((1/2,1/4,1/4)\) its center probability is \(20/39\), while the ordinary read gives \(1/2\). This can be tested from the sharp R source without preparing any rotation.

Use the same finite grid as above and add a two-tick terminal read in a separate panel at each setting. Its center has mass \(1/2\); the two edges have masses \(w/2,(1-w)/2\). The edge-to-center **rate ratio** for the edge of mass \(w/2\) is \(R(w)=f(w/2)/f(1/2)\), independently of the normalizer. Both edges supply data. Suppose simultaneous bounds on the grid give \(|R(w_j)-w_j|\le\rho\) and \(|g(w_j)-w_j|\le\eta\), with positive center rates. Put \(h(u)=f(u)/[2f(1/2)]\). By monotonicity and the mesh gap \(d\),
\[
|h(u)-u|\le\delta=(d+\rho)/2\quad(0\le u\le1/2),\qquad
|g(w)-w|\le\beta=d+\eta .
\]
Now let \(m\) be the number of occupied sites in **any** normalized terminal mass table for which the same scoring and pooling rule holds. Its predicted site distribution differs from the ordinary one in total variation by at most \(\min(1,\beta+2m\delta)\). If all masses are at most one half the sharper bound is \(\min(1,m\delta)\).

For the latter case the unnormalized vectors \(h(u_i)\) and \(u_i\) differ in \(\ell^1\) by at most \(m\delta\). For nonnegative vectors with positive sums, total variation after normalization is at most their unnormalized \(\ell^1\) distance divided by the larger sum (expand using that denominator and bound the difference of sums). For one large mass \(x>1/2\), put \(y=1-x\). A singleton is exact by darkness. Otherwise compare the unnormalized score vector with the vector that keeps \(h(x)\) and assigns each smaller site \(i\) mass \(h(y)u_i/y\). Their \(\ell^1\) discrepancy is at most \((m-1)\delta+|h(y)-y|\le m\delta\), and the comparison vector sums to at least \(h(x)\ge1/2\). Its normalized large-site probability is \(g(x)\), and its remaining mass has exactly the ordinary proportions. This costs at most a further \(\beta\) in total variation and avoids division of the error bound by a nearly dark tail.

For a sharp launch on the eight-well ring a clock layer occupies at most four wells by parity. If the two kinds of calibration agree exactly at the 41-depth grid settings, the conservative uniform bound there is \(5d<0.04965\). Measured error adds \(\eta+4\rho\). This is conditional on the common monotone site-score family, dark empty sites, positive normalizers and transfer to the target clock and preparation. No data yet bound the two calibration errors. Adaptive histories require an additional argument about their conditional updates.
### Testing the rail left by a position write

Copy the well label, recenter, then let the localized rail fly freely for three ticks and read the final well. This probe requires no intervening writes or successful rotations. Within the two-rail quantum model put \(X=2\operatorname{Re}\rho_{RL}\), \(Z=\rho_{RR}-\rho_{LL}\) for the normalized post-write state. Its ordinary probabilities at offsets \(+3,-3,+1,-1\) on eight wells are
\[
\tfrac18(1+X,\ 1-X,\ 3+X+2Z,\ 3-X-2Z).
\]
These follow by propagating each incident rail column and summing the two terminal rail squares at each well. Thus \(X=4(P_{+3}-P_{-3})\) and \(Z=2[(P_{+1}-P_{-1})-(P_{+3}-P_{-3})]\). The formula applies on a specified recorded branch; multiplying by its frequency gives joint rates per prepared launch.

For the positive antipodal gate on \(e\) the target rail is proportional to \((1,2)\). It predicts \((9,1,13,17)/40\). An instrument that produces exactly the same position-write probabilities but completely dephases the rail in the \(R,L\) basis **after that write** gives \((5,5,9,21)/40\) in the otherwise calibrated probe, a total variation difference of \(1/5\). At ideal single-attempt herald yield \(5/8\) that branch contributes \(1/8\) to the joint total variation. This comparison isolates a change at the write while keeping the pre-write preparation and the final detector fixed.

There is a quantitative guarantee for a real pure target with Bloch coordinates \(u=(u_X,0,u_Z)\). If the observed conditional histogram is within \(\eta\) of the target prediction and terminal readout calibration is within \(\gamma\) of the ordinary rule on that branch, the ordinary histogram for the actual state is within \(\tau=\eta+\gamma\). The inversion above expresses \(u_X X+u_Z Z\) as a histogram mean with scores \(\pm(4u_X-2u_Z),\ \pm2u_Z\). An error \(\tau\) in total variation changes that mean by at most \(2L\tau\), where \(L=\max(|4u_X-2u_Z|,|2u_Z|)\). Since \(|r|\le1\) for a Bloch vector, the qubit trace distance to the target is \(|r-u|/2\le\sqrt{(1-r\cdot u)/2}\le\sqrt{L\tau}\), including a possible imaginary rail coherence. Purity of the target matters: opposite \(Y\)-axis pure states give identical histograms to each other and to the maximally mixed state. This test certifies the specified output on the tested branch under a localized qubit description and calibrated free propagation; it does not establish absence of hidden memory affecting later operations. [State and measurement calibration with incomplete measurements](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11500458/)
### Four real inputs test a complex branch map

For one eighteen-tick position write, keep its four labels separately. Model a branch \(j\) by a fixed completely positive trace-nonincreasing qubit map \(\mathcal E_j\), with the rail localized at the recorded well. Its signed-column target is \(p_jU_j(\cdot)U_j^\dagger\), where \(p_j=5/8\) or \(1/8\) and the real rotation \(U_j\) is given by the corresponding column matrix. Prepare \(R,L\) and the diagonal rails \((R\pm L)/\sqrt2\) in separate trials. For each input \(\psi\), count the branch yield and the three-tick probe wells **per prepared input**. The previous inversion gives the unnormalized overlap \(\langle U_j\psi|\mathcal E_j(\psi)|U_j\psi\rangle\) for a calibrated probe: use half the branch yield plus half the recovered unnormalized Bloch projection along the target. Here a ray in a map argument denotes its projector. Average the two overlaps within each basis to obtain \(S_Z,S_X\). Both bases have the same mean branch yield \(q=\operatorname{tr}\mathcal E_j(I/2)\) for a fixed linear map. Equality of those observed means is itself a check.

The branch's normalized-input Choi matrix \(J=(I\otimes\mathcal E_j)(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|)\), with \(\Phi=(RR+LL)/\sqrt2\), has trace \(q\). Write \(\Phi_U=(I\otimes U_j)\Phi\). Then
\[
\langle\Phi_U|J|\Phi_U\rangle\ge S_Z+S_X-q .
\]
Indeed each basis score is the trace of \(J\) against the projector onto the two matching input/output basis pairs (conjugate the input basis). After undoing \(U_j\), the two projectors are \((I+Z\otimes Z)/2\) and \((I+X\otimes X)/2\). They commute and their common range is the line of \(\Phi\). Their product dominates their sum minus identity. This is the [complementary-fidelity bound of Hofmann](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.94.160504) written for an unnormalized position branch. Input-dependent yields must remain in the scores.

In particular \(S_Z=S_X=q=p_j\) forces \(J=p_j|\Phi_U\rangle\langle\Phi_U|\), hence the entire target map on complex inputs, including inputs entangled with a reference. This inference uses complete positivity: transposing the input in the real rail basis before applying \(U_j\) gives the same four real-input tables, yet reverses the imaginary coherence. Transposition fails complete positivity (the antisymmetric vector has eigenvalue \(-1/2\) in its normalized-input Choi matrix). A state-dependent acceptance rule or hidden memory need not define the fixed map assumed here. The test provides a conditional bridge from the position records to the branch dynamics, not an independent derivation of quantum channels.
### What remains if only one-rail positivity is assumed?

Assume exact target yields and overlaps on the four input rays, \(p_j>0\), and a fixed positive **linear** branch map on qubit matrices. Positivity and unit conditional overlap with a pure target force the whole output on each tested input to equal its target. Divide out \(p_j\) and undo \(U_j\) mathematically, calling the corrected map \(A\). The four outputs give \(A(I)=I,\ A(X)=X,\ A(Z)=Z\). Even allowing input-dependent trace on untested states, the **entire** residual freedom is one number \(c\in[-1,1]\):
\[
A(Y)=cY,\qquad A(\rho)=\tfrac{1+c}{2}\rho+\tfrac{1-c}{2}\rho^{\mathsf T}.
\]

To see necessity, write \(A(Y)=aI+bX+cY+dZ\). For each unit \(u=(u_X,u_Z)\) in the real plane, test positivity on the pure input with Bloch coordinates \((\sqrt{1-s^2}u_X,s,\sqrt{1-s^2}u_Z)\) against the orthogonal real ray with coordinates \((-u_X,0,-u_Z)\). Twice the resulting expectation is \(1-\sqrt{1-s^2}+s(a-bu_X-du_Z)\). Both signs of arbitrarily small \(s\) force its linear coefficient to vanish. Varying \(u\) gives \(a=b=d=0\). The two \(Y\)-axis inputs then force \(|c|\le1\). Conversely each displayed mixture preserves positivity on single qubits.

Two possible extra calibrations now have quantitative targets. With a reference rail in \(\Phi\), assume the branch acts locally as identity on the reference tensored with the given map. Projection onto the antisymmetric ray after undoing \(U_j\) would have unnormalized weight \(p_j(c-1)/4\), since the partial transpose of \(\Phi\)'s projector is half the swap. Nonnegativity of this **one joint event** already forces \(c=1\) in the surviving family. It requires an entangled preparation, a suitable joint read and the locality-of-composition premise. [Choi's matrix criterion](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(75)90075-0)

Alternatively, a calibrated non-real preparation and analyzer on one rail can measure \(c\) directly. Use the same pure Bloch axis \(v\) for preparation and the final yes outcome after calibrated undoing of the target rotation. The predicted conditional yes probability is \(1-(1-c)v_Y^2/2\). The [three-sheet analyzer](Operational_coin_qubit.md) has an axis \((0,\sqrt3/2,1/2)\); matched preparation and analysis on this axis would give \((5+3c)/8\), ranging from \(1/4\) for transposition to one for the ordinary branch. Simultaneously conjugating the preparation and analyzer leaves this test unchanged. The three-sheet instrument and the eight-well branch require a demonstrated common encoding, calibrated inverse and accounted heralds before treating this as one implemented circuit. A three-tick read with real matrices alone has zero sensitivity to \(c\).

### One classical sheet query versus six hidden permutations

To test the wider class of definite-sheet models, secretly draw \(k\) uniformly from \(\{0,1,2\}\) and intervene **once**, between ticks two and three. In one arm apply \(y\mapsto y+k\), in the other \(y\mapsto -y+k\). Randomize the arm independently of the launch. A classical walker can bring any hidden state and correlated sheet into the intervention, retain its entire past, and use any shared downstream position detector. Assume the intervention updates only the one definite sheet by the specified permutation and conveys no other information about its choice. ### Can waiting reach the optimal joint measurement?

For each finite clock t after the intervention, every signed word contributes plus or minus the same amplitude of magnitude 2^(-t/2). ### Which local measurements can read the arm?

Immediately after the randomized operation, each arm is the uniform mixture over k of the vectors (a|k> + b|k+m>)/sqrt(2), with m=1 for translation and m=-1 for reflection. Define S|k> = |k+1> modulo three, K = |a><b| - |b><a| and L = S-transpose minus S. Subtracting the mixtures cancels their diagonal walker blocks and gives the exact difference K tensor L divided by six. Both factors are real skew-symmetric. The trace of either factor against any real symmetric matrix vanishes by transposition. Consequently every measurement whose effects are sums of products of real local effects on walker and sheet gives identical outcome probabilities in both arms. This includes local real basis changes followed by separate readings and classical comparison. The later well-click signal evades this restriction because subsequent ticks couple well and sheet before the read.

A pair of complex local measurements suffices to attain the full ceiling. Measure iK on the walker and -iL on the sheet. Their product observable is six times the state difference. On the occupied walker subspace the eigenvectors are (a - i s b)/sqrt(2), with outcome s = +1 or -1. In the sheet Fourier basis with coefficients exp(2 pi i j k/3)/sqrt(3) the outcomes are q = 2 sin(2 pi j/3). Guess translation for s q positive, reflection for s q negative, and either for zero. The two positive differences are each sqrt(3)/6, giving equal-prior success (1 + 1/sqrt(3))/2. These measurement bases specify concrete targets for calibration. The calculation assumes their availability as abstract quantum measurements. Implementing both with walk controls, or synthesizing an equivalent joint real read, remains open. For related real versus complex two-system structure see [Caves, Fuchs and Rungta](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0009063).

### Can the walk supply its own phase reference?

Consider independent launches with fresh hidden uniform offsets and a common unknown arm. Permit a collective real measurement on all walkers, another on all sheets, and classical comparison. Transposing the sheet indices alone interchanges the two arm states: the diagonal blocks are each I/6 and the off-diagonal shift blocks exchange. For n copies, transposing the entire sheet register still interchanges the two n-copy states. Every effect that is a sum of products of real symmetric walker and sheet effects is invariant under that transpose. Moving the transpose across the trace proves exact equality of every outcome probability for every finite n. Pooling unknown-arm launches under these controls gives equal-prior accuracy 1/2.

Now prepare one target and one independently randomized **known translation** reference with the same walk. Number the target zero and the reference one. Put A = iK and B = -iL/sqrt(3), using the preceding definitions. Each has outcomes in {-1,0,1}. Directly on the arm-m source, the mean of A times B is m/sqrt(3): each vector in the offset mixture contributes m to the expectation of K tensor L. Measure A_0 A_1 across the walkers and B_0 B_1 across the sheets. Both observables are real symmetric because each contains two purely imaginary factors. Multiply the two recorded outcomes and guess translation for positive sign, reflection for negative sign, tossing a fair coin at zero. Independence gives mean product m/3, hence **2/3 success in either arm**. This uses two coherent launches with a joint operation on each side. Keeping only ordinary single-launch records would lose the needed correlations.

With N independent known translation references, measure all A_0 A_j on the walker side and all B_0 B_j on the sheet side. The families commute and admit real joint spectral measurements. Their outcome law is the coarse-graining of the common complex product eigenbasis law. In that calculation write Z_j for the product of the A and B eigenvalues on copy j. The recorded comparison for reference j is Z_0 Z_j. The reference variables are independent and bounded with positive mean 1/sqrt(3), so the sign of their sum converges almost surely to positive. Guess by the sign of the sum of the recorded comparisons, randomizing ties. The result converges to the optimal single-target rule based on Z_0, including its zero tie, and therefore to success (1 + 1/sqrt(3))/2. A walk implementation of the coherent inter-launch comparisons, and calibration independent of squared readout, are still required.

### Sharp one-reference bound and a swap calibration

Write D for the target difference and T for transposing all sheet indices. Let R be an independent reference state. An allowed binary decision effect E is invariant under T, so its bias depends only on the transpose-even part of D tensor R. Since T(D) = -D, that part equals D tensor (R - T(R))/2. This operator is traceless. The largest bias Tr(E X) for a traceless Hermitian X and any effect between zero and identity is half its trace norm, by its positive and negative spectral decomposition. Equal priors supply another factor of one half. Thus the success probability is at most 1/2 + ||D||_1 ||R - T(R)||_1 / 8, using multiplication of singular values under tensor products. For the known translation R, R - T(R) = D and ||D||_1 = 2/sqrt(3). The bound is **2/3**, attained above. It applies also to adaptive classical communication whose final effects are sums of real local products.

For a specific model of coherence loss, multiply the reference off-diagonal walker blocks by v between zero and one and preserve its diagonal blocks. This is a convex mixture of the intact reference and a complete unread branch measurement. Now R - T(R) = v D, so the sharp success rate becomes **1/2 + v/6**. The same comparison attains it by linearity, with chance accuracy on the dephased component. This formula presumes the stated channel and an intact target.

A lower-demand algebraic probe measures exchange symmetry separately on the two walkers and on the two sheets, then multiplies the two signs. This measures full-copy swap. Its mean is the overlap Tr(target R). The nine pairs of offset vectors give overlap 1/3 for identical arms and 1/6 for opposite arms: their inner product is half the sum of the two branch-label matches. Guess translation for positive parity and reflection for negative. The equal-prior success is 13/24. A parity measurement requires interference between exchanged copies; simply exchanging their labels and reading them does not measure it. This specifies a second calibration target with exact rates and uses the established [swap-overlap identity](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.88.217901). Neither comparison has yet been implemented with the stipulated walk controls.

### Balanced pair comparisons for a single reference

Coherently relabel the two sheet trits by u = y0 + y1 and w = y0 - y1 modulo three. This permutation has an inverse since two is invertible modulo three. In the new coordinates the desired sheet observable B_0 B_1 is P_w - P_u, where P projects the indicated coordinate onto the normalized uniform trit and preserves the other. Indeed in the original coordinates three times this difference has matrix entry 1 for equal sums minus 1 for equal differences (both contributions count). This is precisely the entry of -L tensor L. The [exact checker](check_pair_sheet_comparison.py) verifies all 81 entries.

For each coordinate independently, choose one of the three unordered pairs uniformly. Test the antisymmetric difference mode of that pair with a balanced mixer. On a miss, undo the mixer and preserve the entire surviving subspace coherently. On a hit, record failure for that coordinate. Repeat on misses up to n rounds; record pass if all miss. In particular, a miss reports only one bit and must preserve coherence with the third label. For pair ij the miss projector is Q = I - (e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^T/2. Every Q fixes the uniform vector, and the average Q is P + (I-P)/2. The all-miss effect obeys E_0=I and E_(n+1)=average(Q E_n Q). Substitution gives E_n = P + 2^(-n)(I-P). The checker also evaluates the recurrence directly in rational arithmetic. This uses the projective state update on each miss; outcome probabilities alone would not specify the instrument. Subtract the two pass bits in the order w minus u. Their difference is in {-1,0,1}, and its mean observable is exactly (1-2^(-n)) B_0 B_1. On the walkers, A_0 A_1 exchanges aa with bb with a minus sign and ab with ba with a plus sign. Balanced sum/difference measurements on these two disjoint mode pairs therefore give its sign. Multiply the walker sign by the sheet difference and randomize zero decisions, retaining every launch. The resulting success is **1/2 + (1-2^(-n))/6** for an intact reference. Four rounds per coordinate give 21/32. This circuit reduces the comparison to coherent label routing, balanced pair mixers and one-bit projective records. Coherent access to two launches and routing the branch modes a,b to those mixers are additional controls whose realization in the original fixed walk remains open.
