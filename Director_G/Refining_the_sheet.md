# Which probability rule survives refinement of a cyclic sheet?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

The signed staircase at any fixed sheet order admits counting and a square law for each cyclic character. On independently prepared copies, sufficiency of the signed summed residue forces a common pure law. Every pure label passes that test. Comparing **different orders** adds a restriction because a coarse stroke now has to give the same joint records as a specified number of fine strokes. Under this restriction we give necessary and sufficient walk records for the ordinary folded character at every order. The result allows no uniform cutoff for the approach to fine resolution and assumes no calibrated complex angle. It does require a physical identification between the apparatuses at different orders. ## Comparing a coarse preparation with a fine preparation

At each order $m\ge3$ launch on R at a named site of a $4m$-site ring. Both daughters of a departure from $x$ gain $\lfloor x/2\rfloor\bmod m$. Only L following L changes sign. Use whole-position masks, unconditioned probabilities and the stationary endpoint and continuation premises of the single-walk theorem. Suppose the same-order comparison has selected a pure rule at each order, initially possibly a different one. Count scores the number of histories. A character scores the squared modulus of the signed sum at $z_m^s=\exp(2\pi i s/m)$. Real masks fold the labels $s$ and $-s$ together. [Derivation from position programs](Preparing_signed_sheet_residues.md)

There is also a purity test with **one unfiltered setup** on each single carrier. After four ticks record $A=P(0,L)$ and $B=P(2,R)$ from the same histogram. The first reported port contains RRLL, RLRL and LRRL with signs $-,+,+$ and gains $1,0,-1$. The second contains RRLR, RLRR and LRRR with those gains and all signs positive. For the count-and-character mixture write $C_j=\sum_s w_s\cos(2\pi sj/m)$; count has weight $c$. Then

$$16A=3-2C_2,\qquad 16B=3+4C_1+2C_2,\qquad
V=\frac{1+C_2}{2}-C_1^2=\operatorname{Var}(X)+\frac c2.$$

Here $X=0$ for counting and $X=\cos(2\pi s/m)$ for each character. Both moments are recovered from $A,B$. Consequently $V=0$ identifies a pure folded character, its label given by $C_1$. Count has $V=1/2$, including at orders where a quarter-turn character shares its first moment. Thus the positive result can replace same-order two-copy purity in the character branch of the refinement theorem. The independent-copy experiments test the distinct physical premise of pooling two histories. If $V$ is small, $c\le2V$. When $c<1$, the total mass outside a most probable coherent label is bounded above by $c+2V/[(1-c)(1-\cos(2\pi/m))^2]$, from the pairwise variance sum and the minimum spacing of the folded cosines. Identification at high order can require substantial precision.

Even exact agreement at these two ports cannot establish the underlying model class. At order four a persistent memory selecting RRLL with weight $5/16$, RRLR with weight $1/16$, and RRRR otherwise gives $A=5/16$, $B=1/16$, exactly the unit-character panel. Storing the whole word violates sufficiency of the endpoint gain multiset for general preparations. The checks also retain a pair of apparently consistent coarse and fine first moments for which the fine variance is $3/4$. For $m\mid n$ let $q=n/m$. Identify one coarse gain with $q$ fine gains by $\iota(\epsilon,j)=(\epsilon,qj\bmod n)$. Let $U,V$ be the multisets at two selected ports. Assume a joint readout for which only the multiset of pairwise gain products $\iota U*V$ matters, and suppose separate preparations have product probabilities. Both assumptions have operational content. For example, a joint readout retaining both residues can support independent pure characters that disagree on the image of the coarse group. The map $\iota$ preserves the angle of a coarse stroke in the proposed common frame; projecting the fine residue modulo $m$ would implement a different identification. [Retaining both residues](Joint_residue_masks.md)

Here is a finite test of the restriction on the labels. For each $\ell=6,7,8,9$, the coarse walker has a fork with two equal-sign histories differing by $\ell$ in gain. The fine fork differs by $q\ell$. Each fork has a reference setting admitting only its upper history. Append a forced return ending on R to each pair to fix the common clock and ports. Swap which walker takes its fork and which takes its reference. The two settings have identical pooled histograms: one entry from the reference pair and a second entry with the same sign displaced by $-q\ell$. A coincident entry retains its multiplicity. Physical masks and both successors at each admitted well, including seam launches, are checked in the archive.

The integer score of a reference is one. A character's fork scores $2+2\cos(\ell\theta)$ and count scores two. Coarse and fine fork angles here use the same denominator $m$, with $\theta=2\pi s_m/m$ or $2\pi s_n/m$. Four swaps test equality of the four cosine values, counting represented by four zeros. Writing $c_j=\cos(j\theta)$ gives $c_{j-1}+c_{j+1}=2\cos(\theta)c_j$. The two middle terms cannot both vanish: consecutive zeros would require $\theta$ to be a multiple of $\pi$, where neither is zero. The four equalities therefore distinguish counting from a character and determine $\cos\theta$ in the character case. The two selected laws are both counting, or both characters with $s_n\equiv\pm s_m\bmod m$. Conversely these conditions give an explicit sufficient joint score on *all* Cartesian preparations. Use total count or evaluate the pooled multiset in the fine character. The pooled sum in that character is the product of the two character sums up to conjugation of the coarse factor when taking its modulus.

The fork programs at even lag $h\ge4$ use the words $\mathrm{RR}\,w\,\mathrm{LLR}$ and $\mathrm{LL}\,w\,\mathrm{RRR}$ with $w=\mathrm{R}^{(h-4)/2}$. At $h=7$ use RRLRLLR and LLRLRRR; at $h=9$ use RRRLLLR and LLLRRRR. At odd $h\ge13$ the equal-clock construction gives the required pair. After the initial split the special words have no intersecting arms before reunion, and no allowed cross-steps. For the general construction the separated arms stay within a fixed transverse width regardless of the lag. The cost grows with the refinement ratio. For a dyadic cover the archived schedule uses 22 ticks per copy irrespective of $m$. These are comparisons of exact per-launch probabilities, with a common factor $4^{-22}$ in that panel. A second test uses the strictly positive five-path preparations and has a retained false pass when marginal purity is dropped. ## Testing compatibility on single walkers with mixed responses

There is a separate way to compare a putatively common law at two resolutions. It uses only single-walker launches. Suppose counting has weight $c_m$ and the folded characters have weights $w_{m,s}$, summing to $1-c_m$. Prepare a cancelling pair at residue zero on R at the origin. Its score is $2c_m$. Then, for each $h=0,\ldots,\lfloor m/2\rfloor$, prepare two positive histories at residues $0,h$. Their score is $2+2C_m(h)$, where

$$C_m(h)=\sum_s w_{m,s}\cos(2\pi sh/m).$$

On the fine walker keep the sign and multiply each requested residue by $q$. The compact compiler programs any of these two-item inputs at a named well and rail from one sharp launch. An isolated LR loop from $(0,R)$ shifts every surviving residue by $-1$ and returns in two ticks; use it as needed to match the clocks. The shift preserves count and all squared moduli. Compare per-launch rates at the matched clock, equivalently multiplying each by the same $2^T$. One must be able to program the masks and transfer or calibrate the detector responses between the two carriers.

Equality across the two preparations for this finite set of probes is **equivalent** to $c_m=c_n$ and

$$w_{m,s}=\sum_{t:\operatorname{fold}_m(t)=s}w_{n,t}.$$

Indeed the positive pairs measure $C_m(h)=C_n(qh)$. Reflect $h$ to fill all residues. The real discrete Fourier inversion recovers $w_{m,s}$ by summing the moments against $\cos(2\pi sh/m)$ and multiplying by $1/m$ for a self-conjugate frequency or $2/m$ otherwise. This also proves equality of the response on every coarse multiset and its embedded counterpart. A different detector or preparation at finer resolution can violate these equalities. This comparison puts no purity or tensor-product requirement on the marginal laws. A numerical replay covers seven order pairs, including an even-to-even cover with distinct frequencies folding onto the same self-conjugate mode. ## A fixed-time record at increasing resolution

Write $d_m=\min(s_m\bmod m,\,(-s_m)\bmod m)$. On the character branch, compatibility reads $d_m=\operatorname{fold}_m(d_n)$ on every cover. A sharp unfiltered launch from $(0,R)$ has just two positive words at $(1,R)$ after three ticks, RLR and LRR. Their gains are $0,-1$. The probability per launch is

$$P_m=\frac{1+\cos(2\pi d_m/m)}4.$$

Consider the following condition on records. For **each** starting order $m\ge3$, all sufficiently late members $n=2^j m$ of its doubling sequence give $P_n\ge1/8$. The cutoff can depend on $m$. The inclusive threshold corresponds exactly to $3d_n\le n$. For example, convergence of this port to its zero-field value $1/2$ on every such sequence implies the condition. Comparing field-on and field-off outgoing **rail totals** also supplies a sufficient continuity test using a detector that does not resolve individual sites. The companion paper gives its short-time formula, a smooth-packet version, and the qualification about what continuum convergence would have to control. [Microscopic read and macroscopic flight](Refinement_and_free_fall.md)

**Integer characterization.** The record condition is equivalent, on the compatible character branch, to the existence of one nonnegative integer $K$ with $d_m=\operatorname{fold}_m(K)$ at every order.

To prove sufficiency, on a doubling step the two possible folds upstairs are $d_m$ and $m-d_m$. When both levels lie strictly below a third of their respective orders, a reflected step is impossible: it would require $d_m+d_{2m}=m$. Such a tower therefore has a constant integer representative on its tail. To handle equality, if all folds vanish take $K=0$. Otherwise fix an order $z$ with $d_z>0$, and consider orders divisible by $3z$. A label exactly one third of such an order is a multiple of $z$ and would reduce to zero at $z$. Thus on all doubling towers based at multiples of $3z$ the closed inequality eventually becomes strict.

The tower based at $a=3z$ stabilizes at some $K$. For any target $m$, the tower based at $am$ stabilizes at $L$. For arbitrarily large $j$, $2^j a$ divides $2^j am$ and both stabilized values are below half the smaller modulus. Hence $K=\operatorname{fold}_{2^j a}(L)=L$. Descending to $m$ proves the assertion. Conversely fixed $K$ eventually has $d_n=K$ on every tower and $P_n\to1/2$. Arbitrarily large orders also give uniqueness of the folded integer $K$. The proof needs neither a signed lift chosen in advance nor a convergence rate common to all towers. The exact arithmetic statement, including the closed boundary and the all-zero case, is formalized in Lean. For the signed description, compatible residues across all orders form a profinite integer. [Lean proof](lean/SheetRefinement.lean) · [Yaroshevskaya, torsion quotient and divisibility chains, pp. 7–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07052#page=7)

## Selecting one turn using physical ports

The integer theorem still permits $K=0,2,3,\ldots$. A character faithful at every order has $K=1$, since any prime divisor of $K>1$ makes the corresponding subgroup invisible. The following readings express the extra condition without measuring a sheet coordinate.

* At order 4 take the unfiltered endpoint $(0,L)$ after four ticks. RRLL, RLRL and LRRL have gain/sign pairs $(1,-),(0,+),(-1,+)$. Its probability is $(3-2\cos(\pi K))/16$. A reading $5/16$ says $K$ is odd. It also excludes pure counting, which gives $3/16$.
* At each odd prime order $p$, the three-tick port above gives $P_p<1/2$ exactly when $p$ does not divide $K$. Its partner $(-1,L)$, reached by oppositely signed words RLL and LRL, has probability $(1-\cos(2\pi K/p))/4$. A positive rate there is the same test. For $K=1$, the mean number of independent launches to the first ideal, background-free click grows as $2p^2/\pi^2$.

Taken together with the cover comparisons, the same-order purity tests and the tail bound, these records hold **if and only if** the family on the specified carriers is the ordinary folded character. Every order is connected to order 4 by way of a common multiple, so the order-four counting exclusion propagates. In particular the boundary test at $1/8$ alone would admit counting. These are mathematical conditions on a family of probabilities. No experiment has established all of them. For the ordinary character the margin $1/2-P_p=\tfrac12\sin^2(\pi/p)$ vanishes quadratically with prime order. Finite uncertainty can matter even when the exact predicted probabilities differ.

For comparison, take a classical walk with a time-homogeneous two-rail Markov coin, $r=P(R\mid R)$ and $u=P(R\mid L)$. At $(0,L)$ after four ticks its probability is $(1-u)r^2(1-r)+2u r(1-r)^2$. Maximizing the two end values over $r$ gives $4/27$ and $8/27$, respectively. Thus the order-four observation would exceed the entire convex class by at least $7/432$. A larger classical memory can reproduce this port: choose a permanent hidden mode at launch and cycle through RRLL with probability $5/16$ or RRRR otherwise. The full data, the faithfulness criterion and the tail comparison require more than this one port. [Exact paths, extremum and hidden-memory control](check_prime_port_records.py) · [Prime and finite-cutoff controls](results/prime_port_records.json)

An independent bound $K\le B$ makes a finite certificate possible once the integer characterization holds. One unfiltered three-tick reading consistent uniquely with fold 1 at an order $m>B+1$ gives $K=1$. Without such a bound, an integer $1+\operatorname{lcm}(m_1,\ldots,m_r)$ exactly reproduces the ordinary label at every order in a finite panel. Two experiments with indistinguishable finite results can therefore imply different forces on an untested refinement.

A fixed mixture with half its weight on $K=3$ and half on $K=5$ illustrates the role of purity. Both odd labels give $5/16$ at order four; at each odd prime at least one gives a positive dark-port rate. The qualitative tests would pass even though neither component is ordinary. A different test for mixed preparations uses the *size* of the dark-port rate at finer resolution. [Mixed probability laws under refinement](Mixed_refinement_records.md)

## Controls on the range of refinements

* On powers of two, $K=3$ is faithful, compatible and has $P_{2^j}\to1/2$. Its weak-field flight radius is three times the ordinary radius with the same calibrated clock, site pitch and force. It fails the full-order tests at $m=3$.
* Use the Chinese remainder theorem to choose a signed residue equal to $+1$ on the 2-primary part of each order and $-1$ on its odd part. This is a faithful full family with ordinary readings on **every prime power**. At order 12 its fold is 5. Prime-power tests miss the relative sign at composite orders. More generally, one can agree with the ordinary rule on any prescribed finite panel even while requiring global faithfulness. Choose an untested prime $p\ge5$ outside its least common multiple, use 2 on the $p$-primary component and 1 elsewhere.
* Even a divisibility-cofinal chain can be misleading. Start $a_3=1$ modulo $3!$. For each $n\ge3$, choose $a_{n+1}$ equal to $a_n$ or $a_n+n!$. Always add at a composite $n+1$; at a prime add precisely when $a_n$ would be zero there. This preserves all earlier residues and keeps the new one a unit, since $n!$ is nonzero modulo the new prime. The integers are unbounded and satisfy $a_{n+1}<2n!$. They define compatible faithful residues at **every** order by reduction from a sufficiently large factorial. On the factorial subsequence, $a_n/n!\to0$ and the bright port approaches $1/2$. Eventually $a_n$ is its own folded representative there and is unbounded, ruling out every fixed $K$. The result retains the individual factorial levels and the prime-power and mixed-prime controls. [Arithmetic checker](check_sheet_refinement.py) · [Exact results](results/sheet_refinement.json)

**Consequence for number embeddings.** If the response is represented by a *single field embedding* of the union of cyclotomic fields, ordinary folding at every order implies identity on its real subfield. Indeed $\zeta_m\mapsto\zeta_m^{\pm1}$ fixes $\zeta_m+\zeta_m^{-1}$ and every real element of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)$, including each signed-character squared probability. In particular the order-eight value $\zeta_8+\zeta_8^{-1}=\sqrt2$ has its ordinary sign. The complex sign is common on the entire tower: look at an order divisible by both 3 and $m$. Whichever sign it gives on the nonreal $\zeta_3$ fixes the sign on $\zeta_m$ for that embedding. Independent sources of real path data need not come equipped with a field embedding. The direct conclusion for them is equality of the folded score functions.

This identifies a response relative to the named physical generator, normalized port readings and cross-order identification. Global complex conjugation preserves these probabilities. Simultaneously relabeling the generator, apparatus and preparations is outside this comparison. The existence of those instruments, their uniformity under refinement, the origin of the carrier and the probability rule for other physical systems remain open.
