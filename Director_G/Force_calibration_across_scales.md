## Native calibration with the field left on

Start at base site zero with incoming rail $R$ on a line or sufficiently large ring, with departure gain $g(x)=x\bmod M$. Count carries weight $c$ and the square responses of characters $k$ carry weights $b_k$, with nonnegative weights summing to one including $c$. The sign of the real Hadamard is negative only at $L\to L$. Write

$$D_j=\sum_k b_k\cos(4\pi kj/M),\qquad D_0=1-c.$$

At $t=n+1$ read the port $(n-1,R)$. It contains the $n$ positive histories with one left move among the first $n$ ticks. Moving this left move one step later increases the departure area by two. Its gain set is $n$ equally spaced terms, up to translation. If $W_n$ is $2^{n+1}$ times the unconditioned port probability and $W_0=0$, then $W_1=1$ and $W_{j+1}-2W_j+W_{j-1}=2D_j$. At five ticks the port $(1,R)$ contains six paths. Their area/sign pairs are $(4,-),(2,+),(0,-),(0,+),(-2,+),(-4,-)$. Expanding its squared character response gives, for every $M$, with cyclic lag coincidences included,

$$32P_5(1,R)=4+2c-4D_1+2D_2-4D_3+2D_4.$$

Thus six ticks suffice to identify $c$. In particular, pure counting gives $3/16$ at the five-tick port while the coherent zero-field character gives zero. With $H=M/\gcd(M,2)$, measuring also the one-left rows through $t=\lfloor H/2\rfloor+2$ gives every folded Fourier weight of the doubled frequency by inversion on the order-$H$ cycle. The combined depth is the larger of this and six. The proof for the staircase carrier applies here after replacing the unit lag by two. The checker tests the actual uniform-force word sums. At $M=63$ the full set of ordinary ports already has sufficient rank at tick 14; the sparse explicit construction uses tick 33. ## Match the prepared ensemble before comparing it with the flight

Choose the diagnostic or flight arm independently *after* the common preparation, including any herald. In the diagnostic arm read both the site and incoming rail without reading the sheet. Keep every result. From the occupied base port take the same field ticks. Use the translated one-left or five-tick port when incoming $R$; for incoming $L$ reflect the initial rule in position and direction. A new origin contributes a common area $tx_0$. Reflection negates the differential area; for fixed entrance and final rails the signed products of reflected words differ by one common sign, since the parity of the total number of turns is fixed. Both changes preserve the squared weights and count law. Consequently the conditional terminal law reads that branch's weights and summing over *all* entrance outcomes measures the original marginal weights. No return to a special address, spatial postselection or new gain register is required. The old base wave may have been correlated with the sheet or coin. A diagnostic that chooses only one reset outcome generally changes the answer. [Explicit correlated inputs and reflected-port controls](check_force_reference_transfer.py)

The flight arm omits that read and evolves the original prepared packet. A measurement on another independently redrawn walker estimates ensemble weights; it cannot herald the hidden phase of the first walker. Nor do the marginal weights by themselves determine the incident coin-sheet correlations: two preparations with the same sheet marginal can transmit their next rail with probabilities zero and one. Across the two arms the physical response law must be held fixed. An apparatus that attaches an extra route record only to the diagnostic arm violates this premise. ## Conditioning can change which force is observed

Let $m$ be the number of successful two-tick direction switches in a run without a position read. A counting walk passes with probability $p_C=2^{-m}$. With a constant field $h_k=2\pi k/M$, the coherent sector passes with

$$p_m(h_k)=\int_0^{2\pi}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\sin^2(q+(2j+1)h_k)\,\frac{dq}{2\pi}.$$

The common zero-field yield is $p_0=\binom{2m}{m}/4^m$. Hölder bounds every coherent yield by $p_0$ and the counting yield is also at most $p_0$. For a weak target with $p_m(h_*)/p_0\to1$, any pre-filter residual weight $\eta=o(1)$ therefore remains $o(1)$ after the herald, since its posterior is at most $\eta p_0/[(1-\eta)p_m(h_*)+\eta p_0]$. This improves the earlier worst-case bound that allowed arbitrary nuisance pass probability up to one. If every coherent sector is subjected instead to the alternating field schedule, each has exactly the same yield $p_0$. Its relative weight within the coherent part is preserved. Only its odds against counting change by the known ratio. A pre-existing pure zero-field component survives this filter just as well as the desired weak sector. For the constant schedule $h=\pi/2$ also attains the maximum, despite being outside the weak-flight chart. [Earlier phase-preserving filter identity](Field_reversal_during_preparation.md)

With a nonzero overall acceptance $Z=c\,2^{-m}+\sum b_kp_m(h_k)$, the accepted weights are $c\,2^{-m}/Z$ and $b_kp_m(h_k)/Z$. For the constant field these numbers can be far from their asymptotic behavior. In a tested family with $m=6$ and an initially equal weak/count mixture the coherent yield was only $0.008658$ times $p_0$, and the posterior count rose to about $8/9$. With $m=1024$ in the same family the ratio was still only $0.037081$, yet the posterior count had fallen below $10^{-304}$. All yields, conditional bins and such adverse rows are retained. ## Which accelerations correspond to those weights?

Let the field increment in the fundamental character be $2\pi/M=F_0\varepsilon\delta^3$, $F_0>0$. Write $r_k$ for a centred signed lift of $2k\bmod M$, divided by two. Then $h_k=2\pi r_k/M$ modulo $\pi$. At the antipodal boundary either lift is allowed and a weak-field expansion cannot generally be applied. In a bounded weak sector the effective force slope is $r_k F_0$. For an unpolarized single-rail direction-filtered packet whose hypotheses match the two-ray theorem, the limiting position characteristic function at time $T$ is $\cos(\xi |r_k|F_0T^2/2)$. The zero-field sector stays centred in this shrinking-action limit. Any remaining counted component with concentrated starting position also stays there, by the displacement variance bound $T\varepsilon$. These equal macroscopic positions for count and coherent zero field explain why a microscopic five-tick discriminator is needed.

For a finite set of such bounded weak sectors add their position laws using the **post-preparation** weights. The extension to a uniformly tight family of effective multipliers uses truncation: require that the mass outside $|r_k|\le K$ tends uniformly to zero as $K\to\infty$, and that weights converge on finite sets. Apply the sectorwise bound on each finite set; for any bounded test function the omitted contribution is at most its sup norm times the discarded mass. A substantial unclassified strong-field component prevents this conclusion. The returning-loop spinor changes the curvature weights by a known sine of the relative path phase while preserving the base packet. This supplies a way to distinguish the accelerations of several candidate phase multipliers without claiming that either is the actual measured one. ## A consistency condition across clock refinements

Suppose an assignment of cyclotomic units $k_M$ is compatible under divisibility for odd orders, so $k_N\bmod M=k_M$ whenever $M\mid N$. If the absolute centred representatives of $2k_M$ have one common bound $B$ on a divisor-cofinal set of orders, a single integer $n$ represents $2k_M$ at every level. To see this, compare two cofinal levels larger than $2B$ through a larger common multiple in the cofinal set. Reduction cannot change the unique representative in $[-B,B]$. Smaller levels follow by compatibility. No odd prime divides $n$ because reduction at that prime was stipulated to be a unit. Also $n\ne0$. Thus $|n|=2^a$ for some $a\ge0$. Conversely signed half, signed one and all signed positive powers of two define compatible units on odd orders and give just these bounded doubled representatives. The minimum weak multiplier $1/2$ is genuine: the inverse of two is $(M+1)/2$ as an unsigned residue and its doubled residue is one.

Hence bounded mechanical response under independently fixed units still allows the scales $\tfrac12,1,2,4,\ldots$. If an independent calibration gives a limiting magnitude *equal* to the fundamental value at cofinally many large odd levels, discreteness makes $|2k_M|_M=2$ there and forces $k_M=+1$ everywhere or $-1$ everywhere. The common sign has no effect on squared moduli. Sparse levels do not suffice: $3$ remains a unit on every power of five. Changing the force scale or effective mass compensates a common multiplier in acceleration ratios, and recalibrating the generator along with the apparatus preserves native observations. The signed compatibility theorem concerns an actual family of unit assignments, not an arbitrary mixture prepared independently at each order. Its implication is for the odd cyclotomic tower; an independent $\sqrt2$ choice in a larger field still requires its own identification.
