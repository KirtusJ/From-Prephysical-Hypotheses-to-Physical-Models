# How much memory reproduces the three half-ring readings?

The [half-ring instrument](Reading_one_half_of_the_eight_site_ring.md) gives three repeatable binary measurements at walk frames 0, 8 and 16. A classical instrument with six persistent internal states reproduces their full sequence statistics, including adaptively chosen settings, when a read can update the stored state. Five states cannot reproduce even the six preparations immediately after a sharp read. The second result below quantifies a different resource: the dependence of the hidden state on which source procedure prepared the same observable mixture.

## Specify the instrument menu

Write $Q_i$ for the observable in frame $8i$, for $i=0,1,2$. All operators are on the 16-dimensional position-and-rail space of the ideal walk. To make each setting selectable on demand in a common laboratory frame, advance $8i$ ticks, perform the binary half-ring measurement, then advance the remaining $24-8i$ ticks. Since $U^{24}=I$, each full block has the selective projector $P_{i,a}=(I+aQ_i)/2$, $a=\pm1$. Coupling only to the half-ring label preserves coherence within each half. Every block lasts the same 24 ticks. This independently selectable menu extends the earlier experiment at three prescribed times. The memory bound assumes either setting can be chosen after a preparation, including repeating it.

The circuit identity $Q_0+Q_1+Q_2=0$ implies $\{Q_i,Q_j\}=-I$ when $i\ne j$. Thus $P_{i,a}Q_jP_{i,a}=-(a/2)P_{i,a}$ for a distinct next setting. After any nonzero branch, the same setting returns $a$ with certainty and either other setting returns $-a$ with probability $3/4$. This remains true after any history. Before the first reading a density operator $\rho$ has response vector $x_i=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho Q_i)$ in the central section $C=\{x\in[-1,1]^3:\sum_i x_i=0\}$. The three directions separated by 120 degrees are commonly called a *trine*. One can see the planar structure by putting $Z=Q_0$, $X=(Q_0+2Q_1)/\sqrt3$: these are anticommuting Hermitian involutions. $X$ pairs the two eight-dimensional eigenspaces of $Z$. In the paired basis the two operators act as the usual two-by-two Pauli matrices with an eightfold multiplicity. This statement identifies an algebra of the stipulated instrument.

## Six states suffice

Store one of the six sign triples with exactly one sign different from the other two. A reader of setting $i$ reports coordinate $i$. After returning $a$, resample among the three stored triples whose $i$th coordinate is $a$. Give probability $1/2$ to the triple whose minority sign is at $i$, and $1/4$ to each other triple. This reset reproduces all subsequent conditional probabilities. Before the first measurement, the six states also suffice for arbitrary quantum inputs. The vertices of $C$ are permutations of $(1,0,-1)$; each is the average of two available sign triples. Any initial response vector can therefore be represented by a mixture. This encoding can depend on how a preparation was made. The model permits independent fresh randomness. Every persistent random seed, shared record, or feedback channel that affects a future outcome must be included in the counted state. External settings may be supplied afresh. This count measures distinguishable internal states of this classical simulator, not a number of bits communicated in one trial.

**Why five fail.** Allow stochastic readers. Each possible stored state supplies a vector $b\in[-1,1]^3$ of expected signs for the three independently selectable next readings. Its conditional response after a preparation is a convex mixture of these vectors. The six post-read preparations require the permutations of $\pm(1,-1/2,-1/2)$, the midpoints of the six sides of $C$.

Intersect the convex hull of the response vectors with the central plane. The resulting polygon lies in $C$ and contains its six side midpoints. On each side, give one unit of credit to a polygon vertex in the relative interior of that side, and half a unit to each incident vertex at a corner of $C$. A side needs at least one unit: either it has a sole vertex at its midpoint or it has at least two vertices bracketing the midpoint. A vertex supplies at most one unit overall. Thus the polygon needs at least six vertices. At exactly six every side has its sole midpoint or both corners. A corner forces the latter choice for both adjoining sides, which propagates round the boundary. The polygon is then either the six-midpoint hexagon or $C$.

If $s$ of at most five candidate response points are on the plane and $p,q$ are on the two open sides, the section has at most $s+pq$ vertices. A new section vertex must be the crossing of an edge with one endpoint on each side. Reaching six requires $s=0$, a two-against-three split, and all six pair crossings distinct. Consider one of the two points on the smaller side. If the section is the midpoint hexagon, its three crossings lie on three different supporting cube faces. The point must itself lie in all three faces. The other point on the same side uses the remaining three faces, forcing the two points to be antipodal cube corners. Such corners are on opposite sides of the central plane. If the section is $C$, each crossing lies on two adjacent cube faces. The six such face pairs form a cycle of length six; any three distinct pairs use at least four faces. A point of a cube can meet at most three faces. Both possibilities are excluded. This lower bound uses only the next-read statistics of the six eigenpreparations.

## What the simulator retains about the source

Prepare $I/16$, measure source setting $i$, keep the fair result $a$ in a separate log, and allow a later independently chosen read $j$ to produce $b$. The conditional states are $P_{i,a}/8$. With the source answer hidden, every source setting presents exactly $I/16$ to the reader. This equivalence holds for every terminal quantum measurement in the ideal specification. Matching several experimental readouts is a weaker statement.

Define $E_{ij}=\mathbb E[ab\mid i,j]$. Its diagonal entries are 1 and its off-diagonal entries are $-1/2$. A four-cell score is $S=E_{00}-E_{02}-E_{10}-E_{12}=5/2$. Suppose the classical laws delivered by the two balanced sources are $\nu_0,\nu_1$. The reader may use the stored state and independent randomness. Under a common source law, the source and reader signed expectations $A_i,B_j\in[-1,1]$ give the ordinary CHSH bound $S\le2$, since $|B_0-B_2|+|B_0+B_2|\le2$. In general write $d=\operatorname{TV}(\nu_0,\nu_1)$. Their common positive measure has mass $1-d$. Its contribution to the score is at most $2(1-d)$; each of the two residual source measures contributes at most $2d$. Hence $S\le2+2d$. This is a preparation-and-readout argument in a single system. It assumes the stored state accounts for all information accessible to the later reader. [Preparation contextuality](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406166) · [Four-preparation operational inequalities](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.98.022112)

The ideal table forces $d\ge1/4$. Relabeling yields this bound for every pair of balanced sources. In the six-state model, the minority index has mass $1/2$ at $i$ and $1/4$ at each other index after averaging over the fair source sign. Each triple and its global negative have equal weight. Thus the distance is exactly $1/4$ for every source pair. This distance refers to hidden distributions; the ideal averaged observables agree exactly.

If all correlations are multiplied by the same visibility $v$, an identical-source model exists exactly for $v\le4/5$, with fair marginal signs. The score gives necessity. At $v=4/5$, choose common source and reader response triples $(A,B)$ as follows: with probability $9/10$ choose $A=B$ uniformly among the six nonconstant sign triples. Otherwise choose $B$ uniformly from the two constant sign triples and set $A=-B$. The resulting diagonal is $4/5$ and the off-diagonal is $-2/5$. Independent white noise handles lower visibilities. Convexly mixing this boundary example and the ideal six-state example with a stored branch flag attains $d=\max(0,(5v-4)/4)$, matching the lower bound for this noisy prepare-and-read table. This last construction does not claim a minimum state count for the noisy table.

## Comparison with photon counts

Mazurek and colleagues implemented three nominal trine pairs on photon polarization. Their public coincidence files permit a direct check of the four correlations above. Using one-based workbook names, take sources 1 and 2, readers 1 and 3, and give the two opposite preparations equal weight. Normalize each output pair by its own detected coincidences. The four raw correlations in the score’s order are 0.999759185, −0.494842494, −0.492316492 and −0.506790056. Thus **the nominal raw score is 2.493708227**. The two unconditioned nominal sources give outcome-zero differences of +0.000966156, +0.000085394, −0.001088623 and −0.002527831 on the four tested readers. These observed differences cannot upper-bound the distance between arbitrary hidden-state laws. The raw score alone therefore supplies no preparation-equivalence certificate. [Published optical experiment](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms11780)

Across the six matched source/reader preparations the two tables give 6,054 opposite outcomes among 46,247,837 coincidences. Summing the 100 runs reproduces all 48 target count cells in the pooled workbook. The two auxiliary preparation rows labeled $P_{4,0}$ and $P_{4,1}$ exchange places between the files; all 16 auxiliary counts agree after that exchange. Those rows do not enter the four-cell score. The authors' separate analysis used fitted and randomized secondary preparations and measurements, under stated tomography and sampling assumptions. They report a matched-measurement statistic $A=0.99709\pm0.00007$ against $5/6$. Their result belongs to their optical test of noncontextuality. No half-ring acquisition or optical sequential-memory measurement was performed in this analysis.
