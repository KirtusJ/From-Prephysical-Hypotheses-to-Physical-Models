# How one position record changes a classical flight

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A position meter can report the correct place to vanishing error and still change the later position by a finite amount. On the program's one-band constant-force walk, the controlling ratio for a Gaussian meter is its momentum spread in the walk's units, $\varepsilon/(2w)$, where $w$ is its position resolution. A site-conditioned translation of a single pointer gives the joint law below, without preparing a new state after the first read.

## One walker and two readings

Use the line, smooth positive-band section, localized normalized packets, site pitch $a=\varepsilon\delta$, and tick duration $\varepsilon\delta^2$ of the [one-band forced envelope](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md). Let $\varepsilon,\delta\to0$ with $\delta^2/\varepsilon\to0$. The rescaled initial packet has momentum concentrating at $p_0$ and position concentrating at $Y_0$ with the norm bounds in that construction. In particular it can have a common fixed compact momentum support. The unmonitored ray is $y(s)=Y_0+p_0s-Fs^2/2$. Use attained ticks tending to $0<t<T$. Both readings use the stipulated squared-coordinate rule.

Take a normalized pointer profile $g\in L^2(\mathbb R)$, independent of the walker, and $w=w_{\varepsilon,\delta}>0$ tending to zero in the same position units as $Y$. Couple position to a continuous pointer by the controlled translation $g_w(z)\mapsto g_w(z-Y)$, where $g_w(z)=w^{-1/2}g(z/w)$. Read the pointer position $Z$ immediately at $t$, keep that outcome, and read walker position $Y_T$ at $T$ with no outcome-dependent intervention. The first outcome has density $\sum_x \|\psi_t(x)\|^2|g_w(z-ax)|^2$. Every first read is kept. The walker conditional on $z$ is obtained by multiplying the actual spinor at each site by $g_w(z-ax)$ and normalizing if its norm is nonzero.

**Joint law.** If $\varepsilon/w\to\lambda\in[0,\infty)$, then

$$ (Z,Y_T)\ \Rightarrow\ \big(y(t),\ y(T)-(T-t)\lambda Q\big),\qquad Q\sim |\widehat g(q)|^2\,dq. $$

Here $\widehat g(q)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int e^{-iqu}g(u)\,du$ with the usual $L^2$ extension. The sign of the recoil is fixed by the pointer translation above. For a real Gaussian amplitude with $|g|^2=N(0,1)$, $Q\sim N(0,1/4)$. Thus $w/\varepsilon\to\infty$ gives the undisturbed pair of ray positions. If $w/\varepsilon\to c>0$, the first record still concentrates at $y(t)$ but the final position has standard deviation $(T-t)/(2c)$ in its limiting law. The weak limit does not assert convergence of lattice variances. A single smooth, compactly supported pointer in the scaled coordinate can give a different, non-Gaussian recoil law.

**Derivation.** Tracing the pointer multiplies a lattice density matrix entry $(x,x')$, including its internal-rail indices, by

$$\int g_w(z-ax)\overline{g_w(z-ax')}\,dz
=\int |\widehat g(q)|^2 e^{-iq a(x-x')/w}\,dq.$$

This exact translated-profile identity is Plancherel's theorem. Therefore the second-position marginal is exactly the average of flights in which the actual pre-read spinor receives the site phase $e^{i K Y/\varepsilon}$ at $t$, with $K=-(\varepsilon/w)q$. This describes the unread channel only. It makes no claim of an independently observed pointer outcome and kick on a given launch.

Fix $q$. Its kicks $K$ stay bounded and tend to $-\lambda q$. Multiplication by the site phase translates lattice momentum by $\delta K$. After replacing the evolved spinor at $t$ by its encoded envelope using the published norm estimate, its scalar envelope is translated exactly. Its old band section $u(k-\delta K)$ differs from the new $u(k)$ by at most $C\delta|K|$ on their common smooth chart, so the translated scalar envelope can be re-encoded with vanishing norm cost. For bounded $q$ the supports before and after the kick and subsequent force shift all lie in one fixed compact $p$ interval. The same full-spinor estimate on the remaining flight is uniform on that interval and in the scalar input. Over both segments the two error bounds and the frame mismatch tend to zero. The Schrödinger position centre at $T$ shifts by $(T-t)K$. Its deviation in norm is bounded by the initial position deviation plus $T$ times the initial momentum deviation: uniform acceleration and a constant kick change the centres but not these fluctuation bounds. Transfer of bounded position characteristic functions on the chart is exactly as in the linked envelope proof. A norm-small source cutoff error contributes at most twice its norm to bounded expectations. Dominated convergence over the fixed probability density $|\widehat g|^2$ now gives the stated second marginal, with no moment assumption on the pointer. This argument takes each fixed kick through the finite lattice before averaging and needs no global chart for unbounded $q$.

For the first marginal, the pointer output law is the convolution of the unmeasured site law with the independent *readout noise distribution* $wU$, $U\sim|g|^2$. Hence $Z\to y(t)$ in probability. A coordinate converging in probability to a constant can be joined with the weak limit of the other coordinate regardless of their correlations. This completes the two-read assertion without requiring conditional convergence uniformly in rare pointer outcomes.

## What the first record identifies

Put $g_\kappa(u)=e^{i\kappa u}g(u)$. Every first-output probability for every input state is identical for $g$ and $g_\kappa$. Their Fourier densities differ by translation by $\kappa$. The limiting late-position laws differ by $-(T-t)\lambda\kappa$ whenever $\lambda>0$ and $\kappa\ne0$. In fact the two outcome-conditioned outgoing walker states differ by a deterministic momentum shift already on the lattice, up to the outcome's scalar phase. Measuring only the first detector's point-spread function therefore cannot establish that the apparatus is nondisturbing. Calibrating a bound on position imprecision does not calibrate the undetected phase of its pointer.

The [earlier sequence of coherent coarse bins](Sequential_position_records.md) follows a Newtonian branch through a crossing with appropriate margins. The [passive rank trajectory](Rank_paths_and_classical_flight.md) and a sharp record at every tick give different path laws. Here the intermediate instrument is a single resolved, translated pointer. Its record can be spatially sharp in the limit while making a finite change to the original walk. The regime $\varepsilon/w\to\infty$ can leave the compact effective momentum chart and requires other estimates. The preparation of the oriented one-band state, the pointer's physical realization, its coupling duration and phase, the clock, and the probability law remain premises. The comparison concerns a line; on a ring a periodic pointer or a specified coordinate cut is additional data. For independent work on repeated detection and semiclassical particle tracks, see [Benoist, Fraas and Fröhlich](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09558).

The [finite checker](check_position_pointer_recoil.py) tests the translated-pointer identity on a cycle with a finite pointer, signed lattice evolution, two pointer phases, all pointer readings and all final wells. Adding two units of pointer momentum on 13 wells preserves the first marginal and changes the second by total variation 0.07918. A separate free positive-band calculation averages the Gaussian impulses by 24-node quadrature and retains the full periodic base. At $\varepsilon=\delta=1/16$ the three characteristic-function discrepancies from their ray-plus-recoil comparisons are still about 0.13–0.15.
