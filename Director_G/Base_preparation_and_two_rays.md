# Preparing the base walk and resolving two classical rays

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Mathematics and numerical checks by Director G (Codex).

The native sheet filter ends at a known lattice site and direction. Can the walk then prepare the extended base packet needed for the constant-force limit? With full site-and-direction readings and free waiting the answer is no. If a direction can instead be measured coherently across sites, the same coin supplies a momentum filter. It yields a family of narrow packets with two opposite effective masses. On a specified scaling the lattice position law converges to two Newtonian point trajectories. No externally prepared base band is needed for this two-ray result. It still assumes an operational instrument, a weak-field sheet plane and the previously specified probability rule.

## What full readings can prepare

Work on the line with one walker. At the last full read the state is \(|x,c\rangle\otimes s\) for some sheet vector \(s\). Its base momentum density within every occupied sheet character is uniform. Free steps translate momentum by a constant within that character and multiply each momentum spinor by a unitary matrix. Thus the density stays uniform for every waiting time. Conditioning on earlier reads, randomizing the wait from a preceding record, stopping adaptively or forgetting recorded outcomes preserves the conclusion. A fixed site's input before any reading gives the same proof. In a chart \(|k-k_*|\le w\le\pi\) the mass is exactly \(w/\pi\); the squared overlap with any normalized packet supported there is at most \(w/\pi\). The two disjoint parity-related charts have total mass \(2w/\pi\) for \(w<\pi/2\). This rules out using the sheet filter followed by unobserved waiting as the base-packet preparation in the [constant-force estimate](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md).

## A direction measurement that preserves the sites

Start at \(x=0\) in direction \(R\). Take two ordinary Hadamard steps, then read only the outgoing direction and retain \(L\). Repeat in two-step blocks, retaining the *opposite* of the previous direction each time. The projector for a direction is \(I_{\rm site}\otimes|c\rangle\langle c|\), with sheets also unresolved. This is a selective coherent projection across sites. A full site measurement followed by erasure of its label gives an incoherent sum and obeys the flat-density result above. Both instruments use coordinate records, but they have different backaction. The existence of the coherent projector in the walk algebra does not establish that the apparatus can operate it.

First put the field to zero to see what the protocol does. Write \(U(k)=\mathrm{diag}(e^{-ik},e^{ik})H\), with \(H\) the real signed Hadamard coin. The off-diagonal entries of \(U(k)^2\) are \(-i e^{\mp ik}\sin k\), with the sign in the exponential set by the **output** direction. After \(m\) successful blocks the amplitude therefore has modulus \(|\sin k|^m\). The site amplitudes are alternating binomial coefficients divided by \(2^m\), on spacing two, centred at \(0\) for even \(m\) and \(-1\) for odd \(m\). Thus the joint success probability is \(\binom{2m}{m}/4^m\sim1/\sqrt{\pi m}\) and the conditional site variance is \(m^2/(2m-1)\). For the variance, the squared binomial weights give the hypergeometric distribution obtained by drawing \(m\) objects from \(2m\) with \(m\) marked; the site coordinate changes by two per marked object. The two momentum peaks are at \(k=\pm\pi/2\). Localization on one position parity forces their weights to agree. Extended base preparations and separate coin-band selection have experimental and mathematical precedents. [de Valcárcel, Roldán and Romanelli](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0976) · [Cardano et al., wave-packet experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4643825/)

The field may stay on **throughout** preparation. Let \(h\) be the effective departure phase slope and \(k\) the momentum at the output of \(m\) blocks. Along a momentum characteristic the first and second momenta of a block are \(k_1,k_2=k_1-h\). The switch amplitude is \(-i\sin k_1\,e^{-id k_2}\), where \(d=1\) for \(R\) and \(-1\) for \(L\). Applying this identity successively gives the scalar profile, up to constant phase and a site translation of at most one,
\[
             A_{m,h}(k)=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\sin(k+(2j+1)h).
\]
For even \(m\) the translation is zero. The peaks are centred, in the narrow-field regime below, around \(k=\pm\pi/2-mh\). The shift in preparation is essential when the field acts for many blocks.

**Uniform comparison.** If \(m\to\infty\), \(m^3 h^2\to0\), the normalized output spinor approaches the one with profile \(\sin^m(k+mh)\) and the same outgoing coin, phase and site translation, in \(L^2\). The success probability divided by its zero-field value tends to one. To see this let \(q\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\) be measured from the nearest translated peak and \(t_j=(2j+1-m)h\). Their sum is zero and \(\sum t_j^2=m(m^2-1)h^2/3\). On \(|q|\le1/2\), the second derivative of \(\log\cos(q+t_j)\) is uniformly bounded for large \(m\). Taylor's formula gives a log ratio against \(\cos^m q\) of order \(m^3h^2\), uniformly. On the rest of the fundamental interval each factor is bounded in absolute value by a common \(\rho<1\), since \(\max|t_j|\to0\). The exponentially small tail is negligible compared with the reference squared norm of order \(m^{-1/2}\). Signs in the second peak agree with the reference in its central interval. This proves the state comparison, including phase, rather than only its momentum-intensity version.

## A prepared two-ray limit

Take the [scaled walk](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md) with \(\varepsilon=\delta\to0,\ h=F\delta^4,\ Y=\varepsilon\delta x,\ t=n\varepsilon\delta^2\), for fixed \(F\). Choose even \(m\) nearest to \(\delta^{-5/2}\). Odd sheet orders can realize \(|h|=\pi/M\) in the selected conjugate plane with the parity modulation already derived. The signed high-field dynamics agrees with its weak-field version for these coordinate projections: they commute with parity, so the known modulation pulls through each measured block. Opposite characters have opposite effective \(F\). A translated starting site adds an initial centre and a phase; use the recorded origin for the following positions.

With this choice \(m^3 h^2=O(\delta^{1/2})\), the scaled preparation drift \(mh/\delta=O(\delta^{1/2})\), the momentum width in \(p=(k-k_*)/\delta\) is \(O(\delta^{1/4})\), and the scaled position width is \(O(\delta^{3/4})\). The retained word succeeds with probability asymptotic to \(\delta^{5/4}/\sqrt\pi\), conditional on an ideal weak sector. Suppose a preceding sheet preparation has residual mass \(\eta\) outside the two target characters. Success of the base filter can reweight the sectors. Its new residual mass is at most \(\eta/[(1-\eta)p_{\rm target}+\eta]\), where both target components have the same success probability \(p_{\rm target}\). Thus \(\eta=o(\delta^{5/4})\) suffices. The earlier [three-step sheet instrument](Records_and_complex_structure.md) can reach any such tolerance at each finite \(M\) under its specified projection and postselection. Its cost must be counted separately. If its last direction is \(L\), interchange the starting direction and all subsequent direction labels. The magnitude profile and the limiting equal masses are unchanged. These statements condition on positive-probability preparations at each finite scale.

For each fixed \(t>0\) attained in the limit of integer step times, the sheet-unresolved position law relative to the prepared origin converges weakly to
\[
             \tfrac12\,\delta_{-Ft^2/2}+\tfrac12\,\delta_{+Ft^2/2}.
\]
Here \(\delta_y\) denotes a point mass. This is an outcome about a position measurement at a specified time. It does not supply a joint law of noninvasive observations along a trajectory.

**Derivation of the two weights and the limit.**

1. The two translated peaks each have probability \(1/2\) by parity. At either edge, \(P_+(k)\) has diagonal entries \(1/2,1/2\), since the \(z\) component of its Bloch vector vanishes. The outgoing basis coin thus has squared projection \(1/2\) on each band at that edge. The two band-edge curvatures in either band are opposite; shifting momentum by \(\pi\) exchanges the band labels since \(U(k+\pi)=-U(k)\). There are four charts with limiting norm squares \(1/4\), two with curvature \(+1\) and two with curvature \(-1\).
2. The zero-field comparison profile translated in momentum has vanishing position and momentum uncertainties in the scaled variables about the origin of each chart. More precisely, its position-operator norm about the site centre is \(\varepsilon\delta m/\sqrt{2m-1}\). Its conditional second moment of \(\sin q\) is \(1/(2m+2)\) by integration of \(\cos^{2m}q\); on the chart \(|q|\le(\pi/2)|\sin q|\). Include the drift \(mh/\delta\) in the momentum bound. One can apply a smooth cutoff in each chart, equal to one on \(|p|\le1\) in that chart and zero on \(|p|\ge2\). The omitted norm tends to zero by the scaled momentum bound. In \(Y=i\varepsilon\partial_p\), differentiating the cutoff costs \(O(\varepsilon)\), and differentiating the band projector or local eigenvector costs \(O(\varepsilon\delta)\). Thus each of the four initially encoded scalar components has vanishing position and momentum deviations. This proof uses a derivative estimate only for the explicit comparison profile. The normalized actual preparation is close in norm, which suffices for bounded observations.
3. The forced-envelope proof applies on each cutoff chart, with dispersion sign \(b=\pm1\), after removing its carrier eigenphase. The projector estimates follow by complementing \(P_+\) and shifting \(k\) by \(\pi\); the fourth-order bound for the phase is unchanged. The limiting envelope has Hamiltonian \(bp^2/2+FY\). Differentiating its explicit phase along characteristics bounds the final position deviation from \(-bFt^2/2\) by the initial position deviation plus \(t\) times the initial momentum deviation. These errors and the spinor comparison error tend to zero. For \(e^{i\xi Y}\) with fixed \(\xi\), matrix elements between the two separated momentum edges vanish once \(\delta\) is small. Within an edge, the two eigenvectors at momenta separated by \(\xi\varepsilon\delta\) have overlap \(O(|\xi|\varepsilon\delta)\) between different bands, by smoothness and pointwise orthogonality. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the cross terms even when the carrier phases oscillate. Summing the four limiting characteristic functions gives \(\cos(\xi Ft^2/2)\). Tracing the two sheet characters leaves the same mixture after the force reverses. The continuity theorem gives the asserted weak convergence.

The coherent channel projector has prepared enough base structure for a *pair* of classical motions. It does not separately herald a single curvature or an externally oriented sheet. Position-resolved observations at a later time can distinguish the two spatial peaks for \(Ft^2\ne0\); a full site reading there localizes the base again. A primitive warrant for the direction-only instrument, and a way to prepare one curvature while preserving the packet, remain distinct obligations.

## Checks and adverse record

The [checker](check_band_preparation.py) enumerates the signed binomial amplitudes for 40 lengths, compares the finite-field product against independent full-sheet integer propagation at three sheet orders, and measures both-band populations and position characteristic functions directly from evolved spinors. Across 1,300 flight rows, the largest probability-sum error was below \(5\times10^{-14}\), and the scaled position mean agreed with the independently accumulated current to \(6\times10^{-16}\). The finite rows have nonzero residuals against the two central rays, including large residuals in the fixed-action controls. The asymptotic claim requires shrinking action. The original predicted position centre was off by two sites for odd block counts; reading the literal paths fixes it at \(-1\). All outcomes of the first check remain available.
