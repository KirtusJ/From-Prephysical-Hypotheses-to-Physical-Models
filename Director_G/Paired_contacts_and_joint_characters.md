# Can pair contacts recover a joint cyclic-sheet law?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Four independently calibrated walkers can each have the same local response and can agree in every triple, while giving opposite answers to one four-walker measurement. The readout needs only two local comparison pointers. The two pointers must act on the **same launch**, and the inference depends on how they jointly register missed returns. In the specified count-plus-character model the construction extends to any finite number of walkers. This gives a test of the weights in that probability model. Deriving its score law and observing the required preparations remain open.

## Sources, records and composition

Use $n\ge1$ distinguishable copies of the signed one-dimensional staircase walk, each with its own residue in $\mathbb Z_M$, a source on rail R, and the same physical names for site, rail and residue shift on every run. For a fixed depth, give fair path counting weight $c$. Give coherent character sectors $[s]=\{s,-s\}$ total weights $b_{[s]}\ge0$ summing to $1-c$. In a sector a joint history with sign $e$ and residue vector $j$ contributes $e\exp(2\pi i s\cdot j/M)$ to its recorded-output amplitude. Square the absolute amplitude and use the fair-path denominator. The single count component counts all joint histories with that denominator. Sectors are fixed within a launch and sampled from the same mixture across settings. Real path multiplicities imply simultaneous conjugation symmetry. No independence assumption is made on the frequency coordinates.

Independently calibrated one-walker laws determine only folded local weights. Even an independent product law on every Cartesian record can leave a relative conjugation free. Here each selected pair can meet at a compatible well and undergo its own incoming-rail comparison. The construction assumes product instruments across disjoint groups conditional on a pure sector, including position masks, individually addressable walkers, clocks, comparison pointers, final exits and misses. Within a block the pointer retains the stipulated alternatives coherently. Mixtures need not factor over blocks. These premises extend the two-walker instrument. They differ also from assigning four fixed directed-arc gains **within one walk**, as in Director Z's four-gain theorem. ## Constructing the joint Fourier moments

At each requested lag $l_i$, prepare the two equal-sign arms of the one-walker position-mask construction at a common depth $T$. They meet at one well with opposite incoming rails. The upper arm ends on L, the lower on R, and their gain difference is $l_i$. Forced prefixes synchronize the arms. Translate and synchronize sources only where the lattice parity permits the specified meetings. A common origin suffices if each disjoint pair can be addressed separately. Apply one final native Hadamard tick. A single walker's survival mass is $2^{1-T}$ in every sector and under counting because each accepted incoming rail has only one history. With the stated product operations, joint survival per launch is $Y=(2^{1-T})^n$. [Physical word construction, flags and denominators](Local_contact_and_joint_sheet_records.md)

Write $\theta_i=2\pi s_i l_i/M$. On a surviving singleton read $h_i=+1$ for outgoing R and $-1$ for L. Its conditional mean is $\cos\theta_i$. On a pair $i,j$ use the comparison pointer before the Hadamards, preserving the two histories within each mark. Write $\chi=+1$ when the outgoing rails agree and $-1$ when they differ. The two incoming marks are equiprobable conditional on survival. The conditional means of $\chi$ within the equal and different marks are $\cos(\theta_i+\theta_j)$ and $\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)$ respectively. Score $H_{ij}$ as $-\chi$ on equal and $+\chi$ on different. Its conditional mean is $\sin\theta_i\sin\theta_j$.

For each even subset $S$ of the coordinates choose any perfect matching of $S$. On a launch multiply all the matched-pair scores by the singleton scores on its complement. Record zero if any required walk misses. Let $D_S(l)$ denote the **per-launch** mean. In a coherent sector $D_S/Y$ is the product of the sines on $S$ and the cosines elsewhere. Fair counting gives zero since the final exits are independent fair directions. Taking the real part of $\prod_i(\cos\theta_i+i\sin\theta_i)$ now gives

$$
 C(l)=Y^{-1}\sum_{|S|\ {\rm even}} (-1)^{|S|/2} D_S(l)
     =\sum_{[s]}b_{[s]}\cos(2\pi s\cdot l/M).
$$

If the common depth is changed between settings, divide each $D_S$ by its own calibrated $Y_S$. Finite Fourier inversion gives $w(s)=M^{-n}\sum_l C(l)\cos(2\pi s\cdot l/M)$. A singleton orbit has mass $w(s)$ and a two-element orbit has mass $2w(s)$. The count weight is $1-C(0)$. No sine inversion divides by a vanishing sine, so zero and half-order frequencies are included. The procedure uses up to $2^{n-1}$ matching settings per lag and $M^n$ lags; it makes no efficiency claim. Hardy and Wootters distinguish measurements on single pairs from simultaneous operations on disjoint pairs in their account of bilocal tomography. Their operational framework and state space differ from the finite cyclic-sheet mixture used here. ## Four walkers with equal marginal laws

Take $M=4$, $c=0$ and $s_i=\sigma_i\in\{-1,1\}$. Represent each orbit by dividing its weight equally between its two signs. For four walkers write $m_{ij}=E[\sigma_i\sigma_j]$, $t=E[\prod_{i=1}^4\sigma_i]$ and $a_\sigma=1+\sum_{i<j}m_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j$. Walsh inversion gives $16w(\sigma)=a_\sigma+t\prod_i\sigma_i$. Thus the six pair means allow **exactly**

$$
 \max_{\prod\sigma_i=+1}(-a_\sigma)\ \le t\le\
 \min_{\prod\sigma_i=-1}a_\sigma.
$$

An empty interval rejects the pair calibrations within this model. Every point in a nonempty interval gives a normalized nonnegative law with those moments. If all pair means vanish, the interval is $[-1,1]$. At its endpoints the sign vectors are uniform on the two opposite parity classes. Every proper subset has exactly the same marginal under both laws, since fixing fewer than four signs leaves an equal number of completions of either parity. For even $n\ge4$ the two parity ensembles have the same property. Even $n$ ensures the parity is invariant under simultaneous reversal. [Walsh expansion on the Boolean cube](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10386)

At tick two keep the return to the origin on each walker. RL and LR have positive sign and residues $0,-1$. At tick three each two-walker comparator has success probability $1/4$ and, on success, returns $H_{ij}=\sigma_i\sigma_j$ with certainty. Pair walkers $(1,2),(3,4)$ and retain the two decoded values, using zero for each failed pair. The product on a complete return is the fourfold parity. Its per-launch success is $1/16$. More generally a simultaneous perfect matching on even $n$ walkers gives $2^{-n}$ success. For the two uniform parity ensembles, the distribution of every proper subset of the pair signs is identical. Misses are independent of sector in the specified product instrument. Thus with equal priors and $N$ independent launches, the minimum error **for these decoded observations** is $(1-2^{-n})^N/2$. A complete return decides the class, while the likelihoods are equal without one. This is a probability statement about the model. It supplies neither experimental counts nor a selection of the ordinary unit character from the walk.

## A joint loss that passes the local calibrations

Suppose that the four orientation signs are actually independent and uniform, so $t=0$. Write $u=\sigma_1\sigma_2$ and $v=\sigma_3\sigma_4$. Let the pair readers each succeed with probability $1/4$ for **every** $u,v$. Give simultaneous success probability $j=1/8$ if $u=v$ and zero if $u\ne v$. Give each single-success pattern probability $1/4-j$ and the double miss $1/2+j$. On success each pair reports its true sign. These are nonnegative probabilities. Averaged over $u,v$, double success has exactly its predicted rate $1/16$.

The compatible signs always yield matching values on double success. Each of $(+,+)$ and $(-,-)$ has probability $1/32$. Each of $(+,0),(-,0),(0,+),(0,-)$ has probability $3/32$, and $(0,0)$ has probability $9/16$. These are **all** the same decoded frequencies as for the ideal $t=1$ ensemble. Even the two one-sided miss streams and the measured coincidence rate pass. The hidden joint acceptance violates conditional product composition. Inferring $t$ therefore requires independent control of that premise or a different experiment that rules out this response.
