# Selecting a reusable unit winding from the flight record

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A failed preparation can be retried at the well where it fails. The same walker and sheet then suffice to select the unit winding at an original-launch yield approaching the entire incident unit weight. The final position outcome can also leave a walker available for another experiment. The law assumed here is the squared-coordinate law on a localized sheet, with a coherent passing rail and a calibrated failure-site read. A persistent counting alternative is treated below. ## One deadline, one sheet

Use the $4M$-well staircase with permanent field and ordinary Hadamard coin. A sharp sheet address has Fourier weights $1/M$. One block consists of two streamed ticks and an observation of whether the rail has changed. A success preserves coherence across its wells. At the first failure measure the current site and rail, retain the same sheet, and retry from that sharp port. Release on completing $m$ consecutive changes, with $m$ even and $m/M^{2/3}\to\lambda>0$. Stop and record a timeout if this has not happened in $H=\lfloor Bm\rfloor$ blocks. The clock counts blocks across every attempt. The recorded start of the successful attempt defines its local origin.

Put $\delta=(\pi/(FM))^{1/4}$, site pitch $\delta^2$, tick duration $\delta^3$, and $n\delta^3\to T>0$. After release take $n$ uninterrupted ticks. Read membership in the union of two intervals of fixed radius $0<R<FT^2/4$ about displacements $\pm FT^2/2$ from the recorded origin. Let $E$ mean both release before the deadline and membership. Every preparation timeout and every miss contributes to the original denominator. The apparatus can implement the last test by completing a position read and pooling outcomes, or by a coherent binary aperture. They have the same rate but different subsequent states.

For every fixed $B>1$, the true-unit yield is $\Theta(1/M)$ and the conditional unit fraction given $E$ tends to one. For any prescribed loss $\epsilon>0$, some **fixed** $B$ gives asymptotic unit completeness at least $1-\epsilon$. A sufficiently slowly increasing deadline multiplier gives

$$
 \Pr(E)=\frac{2}{M}(1+o(1)),\qquad
 \Pr(s=\pm1\mid E)\longrightarrow1,\qquad 2H\delta^3\longrightarrow0 .
$$

The subsequent flight still takes time $T$. These are rates for a single walker with internal retries. A second fresh localized walker carries a new unit prior $2/M$. Nothing in the protocol creates that weight.

## Conditional retry calculation

Condition first on the conserved physical charge $s$. Write $a_j=P_j(\pi s/M)$ for the probability of $j$ consecutive switches from a sharp port, $a_0=1$, $p=a_m$, and $\mu=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_j$. An attempt has length $L$ equal to the first failing block or $m$ at the latest. Thus $\mathbb E_s L=\mu$. Translation, reflection, and conjugation give the same length/success law on both sharp rails at every starting well. They also give the same terminal *union-of-tubes* probability $q_s$ after a successful word relative to its own home. The two rails can interchange individual tube probabilities at finite order. Failed words retain a sheet-dependent amplitude on each character at their sharp endpoint. The next attempt is independent of its history **conditional on the character** for these scalar records. Only at the end do we average over $s$. [Failure-site covariance and renewal mean](Field_on_source_and_release.md)

Let $C_s(H)$ be the probability of release within $H$ blocks, and $T_s$ the number of blocks to first success with no deadline. Renewal gives $\mathbb E_sT_s=\mu/p$ when $p>0$. The incidence contribution to $E$ is exactly $C_s(H)q_s/M$. To bound small $p$, continue hypothetical attempts even after success. Count the attempts which start before block time $H$, completing the last one even if it overshoots. The completed lengths sum to at most $H+m$. Whether an attempt starts is decided before its length and success are drawn. Summing expectations gives $\mu\,\mathbb E N\le H+m$ and expected successes $p\,\mathbb E N$. Thus

$$ C_s(H)\le \min(1,(H+m)p/\mu). \tag{1} $$

For the exact deadline law, put $C_s(h)=0$ for $h<m$. Its first-attempt recursion at $h\ge m$ is $C_s(h)=p+\sum_{j=1}^m(a_{j-1}-a_j)C_s(h-j)$. Failure on block $m$ allows a fresh attempt if time remains. In the count component $a_j=2^{-j}$.

## Controlling the sum over sheet order

Write $r=\operatorname{dist}(s,(M/2)\mathbb Z)$. The one-pass estimate gives $p\le C m^{-1/2}\exp[-c\min(m,r^2)]$. We need a lower bound on $\mu$ as well. Up to translation of the momentum integration variable the absolute sine product for $a_j$ has the same form within distance $e=\pi r/M$ of the zero field. Center its factors on the maximum of the sine, with offsets $(2i-1-j)e$, $1\le i\le j$. For $j\le c_0m/(1+r)^{2/3}$ one has $j^3e^2\le C_\lambda$. In an arc of width $c/\sqrt j$ around this center, the sum of the squared arguments of the cosines is bounded by $C(1+j^3e^2)$. Choose $c_0,c$ small enough to keep all those arguments within a fixed subinterval of $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. The inequality $\cos^2 u\ge e^{-C u^2}$ there gives $a_j\ge c_\lambda/\sqrt j$. Integrating the polynomial on the circle agrees with its physical grid average for $j\le m=o(M)$. For a cutoff less than a fixed integer, the term $a_0=1$ supplies the same bound. Consequently

$$
 \mu\ \ge\ c_\lambda\frac{\sqrt m}{(1+r)^{1/3}},\qquad
 C_s(H)\ \le\ C_\lambda(B+1)(1+r)^{1/3}e^{-c_\lambda\min(m,r^2)} . \tag{2}
$$

At fixed $B$, intermediate distances have a summable Gaussian bound. The sum over $r^2>m$ is at most $C(B+1)M(1+M)^{1/3}e^{-cm}$. At fixed distance from zero the successful flight goes to the two positions for the corresponding absolute winding. Therefore $q_{\pm1}\to1$ and $q_s\to0$ for the other bounded weak windings. Near a half turn the successful packet travels at microscopic speed of magnitude $1/2$ and $q_s\to0$, including half-integer offsets on odd orders. Applying (2) first outside a fixed number of these offsets, then the flight estimates inside them, proves $\sum_{s\ne\pm1} C_s(H)q_s=o(1)$. This order of limits matters when $B$ will subsequently increase.

For the unit, $\sqrt m\,p\to a=e^{-\pi^2\lambda^3/3}/\sqrt\pi>0$, while $\mu\le\sum_{j<m}\binom{2j}j4^{-j}\le C\sqrt m$. Thus $\Pr(T_1>H)\le C_\lambda/B+o(1)$. For any fixed $B>1$ there is already a strictly positive limiting lower bound on the unit release probability. Generate in advance independent failure lengths with their failure-conditioned law, and an independent geometric count of failures before first success. The mean of one such length is at most $\mu/(1-p)\le C'\sqrt m$. Choose $K=\lfloor (B-1)\sqrt m/(4C')\rfloor$. With probability at least $1/2$, the first $K$ hypothetical failure lengths fit in $H-m$. Independently, the first success occurs within $K+1$ attempts with probability $1-(1-p)^{K+1}$, which has positive limit. Together these events ensure completion. At $B=1$ only a perfect first word fits, so its genuine original-launch rate is of order $M^{-4/3}$.

For a single sequence approaching full unit completeness, pick successively larger fixed integers $B_j$ with $C_\lambda/B_j\to0$. At each stage wait until the fixed-$B_j$ wrong sum is below $1/j$, the unit loss is below $C_\lambda/B_j+1/j$, and $2B_jm\delta^3<1/j$ for all larger orders. Increase these order cutoffs strictly and use $B_j$ up to the next cutoff. This proves existence; the argument supplies no finite error certificate for a freely chosen growing $B(M)$. For a fixed persistent count fraction $c<1$ with the remaining input a localized square sheet, the genuine joint rate becomes $2(1-c)/M$ asymptotically. At a fixed budget the count false passage is at most $(H+m)2^{-m}$ by (1), and the diagonal cutoffs can enforce $M(H+m)2^{-m}\to0$. A zero unit prior gives zero genuine unit output.

## Using the retained walker

A coherent last aperture can herald the unit pair **and leave the selected flight available**. Within a unit sector the ideal unmeasured post-flight state assigns $1-o(1)$ to the union of the tubes. If its pass probability is $q$, a pure state and its normalized projection have trace distance $\sqrt{1-q}$. This follows from their overlap $\sqrt q$. The bound is uniform over success histories after expressing displacements relative to their recorded homes and allowing the two launch rails. A later bounded-time position read consequently gives the same two accelerating unit branches as if the union had not been measured. A subsequent side read at a separated positive time can keep a single branch, and any fixed finite number of coherent position tubes following it give the corresponding same-branch transcript by contraction and the branch concentration at each read. These statements concern measured histories. [Sequential coarse records](Sequential_position_records.md)

For other downstream experiments one can instead record the exact site and rail after the terminal herald, keep that sharp result, and use it as the next launch. The charge projection commutes with all these reads. Summing over the sharp results preserves the selected charge fraction; a further individually rare result can have a different posterior. The nonunit conditional weight bounds the change of any later **sheet-blind** event probability relative to the charge-projected herald ensemble, since such operations are diagonal in character. Two consecutive site records can encode the sharp rail if that is the calibrated instrument. New dynamics coupling different characters require control of off-diagonal coherences as well. The identification of the physical force unit $F$, the clock, the incident sheet, and the coherent pass instrument remain premises. [Sharp two-well read and probability transfer](Position_transcripts_and_selected_laws.md)

At $M=129$, with $m=12$ and $H=96$, the preparation passes on $0.0705851$ of original localized launches. It retains $97.51\%$ of the incident unit weight. Replacing the sheet after each failed attempt would give $0.268571$ instead. The terminal position bins can still contain no occupied well at small orders, so these release rates alone give no finite guarantee for the final herald.
