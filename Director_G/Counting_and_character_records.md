# Measuring counting and character weights with the walk's own ports

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist  
Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Director G (OpenAI Codex), research supplement to G10 edition Z17.

The walk can determine the counting coefficient and all conjugate-pair weights in a fixed, normalized multiset law by measuring only its final base position and direction. It needs neither a prepared Fourier state nor a detector on the gain sheet. The result is an identifiability theorem: different parameter vectors must give different records. Which vector a physical walk realizes requires a measurement. An ordinary quantum realization with inaccessible path records can also mimic counting. ## One fixed carrier and one launch point

For $M\ge3$, use a $4M$-site base ring and a departure sheet increment $g(x)=\lfloor x/2\rfloor\bmod M$. Adding $4M$ to a representative leaves this increment unchanged modulo $M$. Start at $(0,R)$ and apply the same balanced signed Hadamard and left/right stream at each tick, with a minus only for $L\to L$. The site schedule is a specified model component. The uniform-force walk used in the mechanical limit has $g(x)=x$; adjacent exchanges there change the gain by two, which loses information at even orders. A fixed staircase avoids this degeneracy.

Let $c$ be the counting fraction and $b_s$ nonnegative character weights with $c+\sum_{s=0}^{M-1}b_s=1$. A gain $(\epsilon,j)$ is evaluated as $\epsilon\exp(2\pi i sj/M)$. At odd $M$ the underlying gain group is cyclic of order $2M$. At even $M$ the sign is an independent factor; the signed two-input theorem covers this gain alphabet when its full block and switching premises hold. Only the sums of the weights of a character and its conjugate are identifiable using these real records. The zero mode and, at even order, the Nyquist mode are each a single character. For the event ending at one base port, let $N$ be its number of histories and $D_j$ its signed count at residue $j$. The model predicts the unconditional probability

$$2^tP=cN+\sum_s b_s\left|\sum_j D_j e^{2\pi i sj/M}\right|^2,\qquad
C(h)=\sum_s b_s\cos(2\pi sh/M). \tag{1}$$

All such laws are normalized here: the counting walk has $2^t$ paths and each character walk has normalized unitary steps. The source, record convention and weights must remain the same across launch depths. A coordinate-resolved signed walk with an initially localized sheet and the squared rule, viewed only at its base, realizes uniform character weights with $c=0$ by Parseval. In another physical preparation the apparent character weights may describe its spectral populations; preparation and law must be controlled separately. [Earlier prepared-sector experiment](Equal_time_gains_and_history_records.md)

Read $(t-2,R)$ at $t=n+1$. Exactly one of the first $n$ directions is left and the final direction is right. These are $n$ positive paths. Moving the left step past the next right step changes one departure, increasing the gain by $\lfloor(x+1)/2\rfloor-\lfloor(x-1)/2\rfloor=1$. The gains are a length-$n$ consecutive interval up to a common shift. The ring is large enough to exclude a second base endpoint lifting to the same port at the prescribed horizons. Let $W_n=2^{n+1}P_{n+1}(n-1,R)$ and put $W_0=0$. Counting ordered pairs in the interval gives

$$W_n=n+2\sum_{h=1}^{n-1}(n-h)C(h),\qquad
W_{n+1}-2W_n+W_{n-1}=2C(n). \tag{2}$$

At lag $M$, $C(M)=1-c$. The other lags give the folded character weights by the inverse real Fourier transform. Equivalently the symmetric individual weight is $M^{-1}\sum_{h=0}^{M-1} C(h)e^{-2\pi i sh/M}$; double it for each nonsingleton conjugate pair. Thus fresh terminal reads through $t=M+2$ suffice for all coefficients on this single unmodified carrier. If $n\le M$, each positive interval path occupies its own residue and pure counting agrees exactly with uniform character squares at these specified ports. At $n=M+1$ their probability difference is only $2/2^{M+2}$. Long-horizon inversion of exact probabilities by itself supplies no favorable experimental sample cost. The [Lean proof](lean/NativeWeightRead.lean) verifies path classification, signs, the staircase gains, the ordered-pair identity for integer-scaled kernels and an additional five-tick cancellation. Equation (2) for real coefficients uses the same finite linear rearrangement. [Independent finite-ring enumeration and Fourier inversion](results/character_tomography.json)

## A short measurement of the counting component

At tick five, the six paths ending at $(1,R)$ have gain/sign pairs $(1,-),(0,+),(-1,-),(-1,+),(-2,+),(-3,-)$. Before cyclic folding their signed row is $(-1,1,0,1,-1)$ in ascending gain order. At $M\ge5$,

$$32P_5(1,R)=4+2c-4C(1)+2C(2)-4C(3)+2C(4). \tag{3}$$

For example, the two alternatives pure counting and uniform squares give $3/16$ and $1/8$. Measure $C(1),\ldots,C(4)$ from (2) through six ticks and insert the observed base probability into (3). This identifies $c$ at a depth bounded independently of $M$. A null at a separately instrumented dark port is unnecessary. At $M=3$ the six residues fold to a zero signed row, and $32P=6c$. At $M=4$ the same port has signed energy six, equal to the history count. The sixth-tick one-left wrap recovers $c$ at that order. Estimation error in (3) depends on ordinary probability errors with finite coefficients; it does not grow with the sheet order. [Small orders, exact degeneracies and reconstruction](check_character_tomography.py)

Using *all* ordinary outputs can shorten the horizon further. At order 64 the complete position histograms through tick 14 attain affine rank 33, even after both directions are combined. At order 4, tick 5 still cannot distinguish count from uniform squares using the complete output. Rank is computed from integer autocorrelations with the normalization already imposed. A rank deficiency gives distinct positive weights near an interior point with the same finite observations. Exact ranks do not control conditioning or determine the first sufficient depth beyond the examined orders. [Per-order ranks and retained row witnesses](results/character_tomography.json)

A different fixed graph can trade launch control for probability resolution. Choose launch sites whose three-tick diamonds realize respective relative gains $h$. The two interfering ports have probabilities $(1+C(h))/4$ and $(1-C(h))/4$; the four other singleton ports each have probability $1/8$. With a zero relative gain the destructive port reads $c/4$. Assign $+1,-1,0$ to these respective outcome types. With $K=\lfloor M/2\rfloor+1$ settings, $\lceil 8\log(2K/\alpha)/\epsilon^2\rceil$ fresh independent launches per setting suffice for every lag estimate to lie within $\epsilon$ with probability at least $1-\alpha$. Individual folded weight errors are then at most $2\epsilon$. This design needs reproducible launch selection and the same law on all the programmed diamonds. A local fair walk choosing its direction independently gives the counting predictions, respects the arrow of time and clicks at the purportedly dark zero-gain port. Signed arithmetic or locality alone cannot impose a measured zero. [Physical menu and route checks](results/character_tomography.json)

## One local detector bit

At $M=5$, use the previous two-force-tick preparation with $k=\ell=1$, the original base modes retained, and a fresh right-incoming probe on the same physical sheet. After five balanced ruler ticks, flip a blank bit only at the marked cell on the *right-moving* rail. The two force lobes have four and six paths arriving there. Their respective signed sums are both zero. The click probability is zero under the signed rule and $5/32$ under counting. In the resolved signed apparatus, each terminal port contains only positive and negative histories on the same physical sheet. Every allowed odd-character square agrees on that port, so the mixture's click probability is $5c/32$. A bounded upper limit on observed clicks constrains $c$ after accounting for the detector's false positive and false negative rates. An exact zero here is a model prediction. The coupling is an on-site controlled permutation at the marked sheet cell. All retained base modes must have access to that coupling, or be routed to it while preserving their orthogonality. A scheduled final contact uses a timing signal. A permanently active bit instead records the parity of visits to a marked rail. At the left rail and the same five-tick horizon, its probabilities are $7/32$ signed and $3/8$ counting. An absorbing detector coupled to a new orthogonal outgoing bin on every arrival yields fifth-bin first-capture probabilities zero and $1/16$. Replacing those bins by a single reversible bit cannot implement an absorbing first-arrival record. These three instruments answer different questions. ## What the measurements identify

There is a second physical interpretation of every nonnegative family in (1). An ordinary Born model can put probability $c$ in a sector with a fresh orthogonal path record at every step and the weights $b_s$ in unmarked sectors carrying the corresponding phase characters. In the first sector each direction word has its own memory state and contributes $2^{-t}$ irrespective of sign. In each unmarked sector amplitudes add before squaring. A terminal base read sums the orthogonal sectors and gives (1) exactly. Pure superposition over orthogonal sectors and a classical mixture have the same effect for measurements that preserve the sectors and ignore the hidden records. For a finite adaptive sequence of base/rail projectors and common choices from earlier outcomes, applying the same argument to each retained transcript gives the shared-sector joint law. One may choose a model that reselects its sector per fresh launch; this changes some joint laws. Extending a scalar phase apparatus by a physically accessible sheet tracker also changes the admitted measurement operations. [Exact finite dilation and records](results/record_dilation.json) · [Quantum walks with path information in multiple coins](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.67.052317)

A balanced split, copy of the rail into a blank bit, undo of the copy while its source is still present, and a second balanced pulse give a direct test of the accessible memory. With all route records coherently erased, the initial rail returns with unit probability in the Born model. Path counting still gives half at either rail. An additional surviving orthogonal route record also gives half in the Born realization. Consequently the experiment probes the completeness of the proposed physical record algebra. Base statistics alone identify an effective counting fraction even when microscopic explanations of that fraction differ. [Echo controls](check_record_dilation.py)

There is a conditional consequence for the mechanical limit. Suppose a family of independently prepared force flights carries counting weight $c_\delta$, the counting and coherent sectors start with the same position law concentrated near $Y_0$, and in the signed sector the prepared band and reference give convergence to a single force trajectory point $Y(t)$. Under counting with the balanced coin the subsequent path increments are independent fair signs regardless of the force phases. With lattice spacing $\varepsilon\delta$ and $n=\lfloor t/(\varepsilon\delta^2)\rfloor$, the variance of their displacement is $n(\varepsilon\delta)^2\le t\varepsilon$. As $\varepsilon\to0$ the counting component therefore stays at $Y_0$ in probability. If $c_\delta\to c$, the combined limiting position law is $c\,\delta_{Y_0}+(1-c)\,\delta_{Y(t)}$. At a time with $Y(t)\ne Y_0$, convergence of the measured ensemble to the single moving trajectory requires $c_\delta\to0$; for example, test with a bounded continuous bump around $Y_0$ disjoint from $Y(t)$. This uses compatible initial localization and sector weights in the actual mechanical preparation. A counting component after one preparation cannot be inferred from a separately calibrated optical or staircase device without a common physical law.
