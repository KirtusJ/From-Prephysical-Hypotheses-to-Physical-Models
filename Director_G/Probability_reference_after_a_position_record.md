# A probability reference carried through an unknown walk state

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex), with independent signed-path, circuit and adversarial checks from Codex workers.

Can the walk supply a known test mass *after* an earlier position reading, when nobody knows the remaining wave packet? Two steps of the signed walk split an arbitrary incident state into two equally weighted transformations. A reversible position mark tells the transformations apart. Undoing each transformation returns the original state, including its correlations with earlier records. Repeating the operation supplies any dyadic test mass at one fixed circuit depth. A second reading on the walker distinguishes this circuit from preparing the same reference in an independent apparatus. These are predictions for specified controls. ## What one coarse position mark preserves

Let a Hadamard coin $H$ act on right and left rails, followed by a streaming operation $S$ that shifts them by $+1$ and $-1$. Write $U=SH$. Work on the infinite integer line unless a compatible ring size is stated. Use the binary mark $c(x)=\lfloor x/2\rfloor\bmod2$. In two forward steps the walker can move by $0,+2,-2$. The mark is unchanged exactly for the zero displacement. For any state supported in a known initial mark sector, including a coherent superposition of many sites and both rails, the squared mass in that same sector after two steps is $1/2$.

The analogous three-step experiment uses $r(x)=x\bmod3$. Equality of the initial and final mark then selects displacements $\pm3$. The two accepted histories continue straight after the first coin. Each ends at a site and rail identifying its origin, even when two rays meet at the same well. Their squared amplitudes sum to $1/4$ of the incident norm. Equivalently, with $P$ the initial color sector, $P U^{-t}P U^t P=sP$ with $(t,s)=(2,1/2)$ or $(3,1/4)$ under the respective coloring. A sign flip on that same sector followed by reverse flight returns to it with squared mass $(1-2s)^2$. This extends the sharp-origin calculation to every state in a whole sector, tensored with any unacted-on spectator. On the line $x\bmod3$ also serves the two-step test.

The minimal number of classical *launch labels* for these literal masks is two at two steps and three at three steps. A deterministic whole-site final mask may depend on the label and the chosen clock. At two steps origins separated by 2 need opposite answers at a common reachable site. At three steps origins separated by 2 or 4 conflict, so $0,2,4$ require three distinct labels. The colorings give the upper bounds. A single fixed two-step mask can have half mass for every sharp source while selecting the complementary displacements for one launch color. A prescription for a mass and a prescription for the marked event therefore have different costs. Rings need separate checks. On a six-site ring the two-step literal mask needs three labels, and on rings of eight and ten sites the three-step mask needs four and five respectively. On each such ring all the even origins conflict pairwise and an even/odd pair can share a launch label because its reachable sets have opposite parity. ## Splitting and returning an arbitrary incident state

The two-step binary scheme also works across *both* sectors at once, without measuring the initial mark. Take a clean, coherent bit initially in $|0\rangle$. XOR $c(x)$ into it, run $U^2$, and XOR the final $c(x)$ into the same bit. It now records whether the walker moved by two sites. Maintaining coherence between the two writes is part of the instrument.

Let $X$ exchange rails, $Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ and $S_2=S^2$. Direct path enumeration gives
\[
 U^2=(V_0+V_1)/\sqrt2,\qquad V_0=XH,\qquad V_1=S_2 ZH .
\]
The two zero-displacement paths end on opposite rails. In the ordered output basis their signed matrix is $\tfrac12\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)=V_0/\sqrt2$. The two straight paths give $V_1/\sqrt2$, each on its own translated rail. Thus the bit holds 0 beside $V_0\psi$ and 1 beside $V_1\psi$, each divided by $\sqrt2$, for **every** input $\psi$. The statement includes coherent superpositions across the original color boundary. It holds on rings with circumference divisible by four.

Correct the two branches coherently with $V_b^\dagger$. An explicit implementation runs $S_2^{-1}$ on bit 1, applies $Z$ on bit 1 and $X$ on bit 0, then applies a final $H$ on both rails. Matching the hold time in the stationary branch matters. The resulting unitary $W$ obeys
\[
 W(|0\rangle\otimes\psi)=|+\rangle\otimes\psi .
\]
Linearity and an untouched spectator give the same restoration for an unknown mixed input, for entanglement with a stored record, and for any post-measurement state conditional on a prefix already obtained. No replacement walker, sharp origin, direct Hadamard on a label, or assumed distribution of the incident rails is needed in this construction. The controllable translation, rail gates, untouched register and initialized bit are supplied operations. In particular, a force or sheet-dependent coin running during these two steps would require a new calculation.

A check on the other input column matters experimentally. Put $A=V_0^\dagger V_1$. Unitarity of $U^2$ implies $A^\dagger=-A$. Applying the gates to input bit 1 interchanges the two corrections, giving $W(|1\rangle\otimes\psi)=|-\rangle\otimes A\psi$. A bit initialized to 1 still gives equal address masses while potentially changing the walker. The coherent contaminated input $(|0\rangle\psi+|1\rangle A^\dagger\psi)/\sqrt2$ yields a certain zero output. A perfect reversal of *all* gates tests reversibility even for such inputs. It cannot alone certify the required blanks.

## A dyadic menu at one clock, and a check on the carrier

Repeat $W$ with $m$ clean bits, using each in turn. Let $F$ be the resulting unitary, $P_0$ the projector on the all-zero address with unrestricted carrier, and $Q_k$ the predicate that the final unsigned address is less than $k$, for $0\le k\le 2^m$. Unread, coherently grouped addresses have mass $s=k/2^m$ for every unknown carrier and spectator. In particular
\[
 P_0 F^\dagger Q_k F P_0=sP_0 .
\]
For a sign $I-2Q_k$ between $F$ and $F^\dagger$, the amplitude returning to the blank address is $(1-2s)$ times the original carrier. The mass escaping that address is $T(s)=4s(1-s)$. No observation of the individual addresses occurs before the sign. Every $k$ at fixed $m$ has the same preparation depth. The threshold, binary routing, reverse and direct-arm delay or equivalent endpoint matching must be implemented and checked. Addresses require $m$ protected bits; the circuit uses $2m$ ordinary walk steps in the forward splits plus coherent corrections. Context must be matched again if depth, hardware or clock changes.

An independent Hadamard reference acting only on fresh bits reproduces the direct and binary escape masses on this menu. There is a simple second read that can reject this alternative. Set $m=k=1$ and launch the walker at a known $x$ with any rails. The sign on address zero maps $|+\rangle$ to $-|-\rangle$. Undoing $W$ gives $-|1\rangle A^\dagger\psi$. Since $A^\dagger=-H X S_2 ZH$, its support lies entirely at $x\pm2$. The qubit-only preparation and inverse put the bit in the same escape output while leaving the walker exactly at $x$. A faithful final position read separates these predictions on the line and on the specified rings. A device expressly programmed to move the carrier on escape is a different rival. The address and carrier results can be observed on the same launch in this endpoint control. ## What conditional records can identify

Fix a previously observed history $h$. Assign the binary direct and return experiments so their incident populations, timing and terminal read are comparable at that history. Suppose both use one nondecreasing, complementary observed response $G_h(s)$ to the pooled kinematic mass. Its dependence on $h$ is allowed. Sharp endpoints also need support. The direct and escape rates are then $G_h(s)$ and $G_h(T(s))$. The observed discrepancy against $T(G_h(s))$ tests the ordinary read at that prefix. The normalized-square response $G(s)=s^2/[s^2+(1-s)^2]$ already fails by $27/50$ at $s=1/4$. All these rates are predictions until such an experiment is run.

By the angular bound in the [sign-return proof](Checking_probability_by_reversing_a_shell_sign.md), a simultaneous upper bound $\delta$ on these discrepancies at all dyadic nodes gives
\[
 \sup_s |G_h(s)-s| \ \le\
 \sin\!\left(\min\{\pi/2,\ \arcsin\sqrt{\delta}+2\arcsin(2^{-m/2})\}\right).
\]
Here the angular grid gap is at most $\arcsin(2^{-m/2})$, attained at an endpoint. With $m=8$ the ideal zero-residual bound is $\sqrt{255}/128<0.125$. This is a design value at one depth, not an observed error bar. A larger register changes the apparatus and needs its own data. The address reference and a spatial trajectory use different predicates. Transferring this calibration to an actual position read needs evidence that both enter the same meter with comparable timing, populations and disturbance.

Repeated two-step *measured* color reads give a simpler conditional control. After a faithful binary sector projection both branches prepare a known sector again. If the law of the actual next read is fair on **every full recorded history**, the word of length $n$ has probability $2^{-n}$ under ordinary conditioning, irrespective of coherent position and rail structure inside a sector. A response complementary at half on that conditional population suffices. The claim does not follow from matching every pair of successive readings. For independent fair $a,b$, the period-three sequence $a,b,a\mathbin{\rm XOR}b,a,b,\ldots$ has uniform adjacent pairs, while two readings determine its entire future. Likewise a distribution of detector types can yield the correct mean response at every fixed reference mass and still be biased when the position mass itself depends on type. Using a fresh port-swap coin to establish fairness consumes an independent source, and assigning another experiment with these bits requires independence from the science-side preparation. The diagnostic can follow a recorded prefix without requiring the post-read packet to collapse to a single site. This removes a localization premise from the calibration. It supplies neither a disturbance bound for inserting the diagnostic into a field-driven trajectory nor conditional probability data on that trajectory.
