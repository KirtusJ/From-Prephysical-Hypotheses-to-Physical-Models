The two-walker model uses an integer gain that reads both positions at departure. A signal traveling around the ring can implement that gain using only local reversible updates. The walkers must wait for the signal. At the times when they resume motion, the complete signed state agrees with the earlier model, including its quantum and classical position records. Counting the waiting time changes the physical interpretation. In fact, two localized initial preparations give an obstruction to realizing the stated Newton law with uniformly finite propagation speed in its own continuum clock. ## Local implementation of a departure gain

Park distinguishable walkers $A,B$ at sites $x,y$ of a ring of size $N$. Walker $A$ carries the cyclic sheet $s\in\mathbb Z/M$. At each site there is a signal register, initially empty, with other possible states labeled by $t\in\mathbb Z/N$. Supply one table $g(t)\in\mathbb Z/M$ at every site. This includes the table previously computed from integer path counts. Apply the following actions coherently, including on superpositions of the two walker positions.

1. At the site occupied by $B$, exchange the empty signal state and signal $0$. This local exchange is its own inverse.
2. Repeat $N$ rounds. At the site occupied by $A$, a signal $t$ adds $g(t)$ to its sheet. Increment each occupied signal’s counter modulo $N$ and move the signal register one lattice site to the right. The walkers stay parked during these rounds.
3. Apply the same exchange at $B$ to absorb the returned signal. Release both walkers with their original signed coins and conditional shifts. Walker $A$ takes the sheet along on its outgoing channel.

Immediately before round $t$, for $0\le t<N$, the single signal is at $y+t\bmod N$ with counter $t$. There is exactly one encounter, at $t=(x-y)\bmod N$. At the end the signal has returned to its own emission site with counter zero. The exchange restores the entire signal track to the same empty state on every branch. Hence the map on the input subspace is precisely

$$
 |x,y,s\rangle\,|\mathrm{empty}\rangle
 \longmapsto |x,y,s+g(x-y)\rangle\,|\mathrm{empty}\rangle .
$$

This is basis equality with identical coefficients. It extends linearly to entanglement with any spectator. Measuring the signal en route could reveal which departure occurred and destroy that equality. All constituent operations are permutations on the full carrier before the coin release. An occupied initial track can alter the kick. Clearing an unknown track by overwriting it would lose reversibility. One implementation of the rightward stream uses a second, equal-type track at each site. First swap the tracks on site. Then swap the auxiliary at site $i$ with the main register at $i+1$ on every bond. The main output at $i$ is the original main register at $i-1$, and the auxiliary output at $i$ is the original auxiliary at $i+1$. A uniformly empty auxiliary track is restored. Each cross-bond swap uses distinct subregisters. If gates sharing a site must be scheduled separately, the bonds admit two colors on an even ring and three on an odd ring of length at least three. Thus the depth per shift stays bounded in this site-register gate model. A fixed elementary bit-gate cost for arbitrary tables and growing counters would require a separate resource analysis. Strict locality of discrete lattice dynamics and its relation to quantum walks have prior formulations. [Schumacher and Werner](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0405174)

Composition gives the original joint state at every release, for every finite sequence of releases. If records and feedback are applied at those boundaries, their joint probabilities agree branch by branch. The proof also applies to a sheet vector and a reference gain $g(t)h_*$ frozen after calibration by copying the same classical program to the interaction sites beforehand. Distribution time of that program belongs to preparation. The circuit supplies stable parking, synchronous release, a signal alphabet of size $N+1$ and an on-site lookup. It takes the spatial ring and the shared internal degree of freedom as given. [Calibrated gain and flight conditions](Internal_phase_reference.md)

## How much delay is necessary?

For a circuit whose output at a site depends on sites at most $r$ edges away per layer, an observable on a set $S$ after depth $D$ has its backward support in the $rD$-neighborhood of $S$. This follows by iterating support inclusion. Two initial states with identical reductions on that neighborhood give the same outcome probabilities. Pre-existing auxiliary correlations are allowed under this precise equality condition. Input-dependent auxiliary data already available to the receiver change the test. Continuous-time local Hamiltonians can have tails beyond an effective cone and require a quantitative tail bound instead of strict support. [Beckman et al. on signalling](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0102043) · [Bravyi, Hastings and Verstraete on Hamiltonian light cones](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0603121)

**Full sheet gate.** With a clean localized sheet readable at $A$, two distant input positions for $B$ giving different offsets modulo $M$ yield orthogonal sheet records at $A$. For example, on $N=4k$, take $A=0$, $B=k$ or $2k$, any $M\ge2$, and a table with $g(3k)=0$ and $g(2k)=1$. The states differ only at distances at least $k$ from the receiver, so an exact implementation needs $rD\ge N/4$. A uniform trace-distance approximation with error strictly below $1/2$ on both inputs has the same lower bound. Constant or modularly collapsed gain tables are adverse controls. Sensitivity of a sheet-coordinate read alone cannot establish a lower bound when only a single character is prepared and the sheet stays unread.

**A three-tick position and direction record with a localized sheet.** Use the actual integer count table of the two-body construction on $N=32$, with $m=5120$, sheet order $M=17$, and integer amplitude $L=3$. Set $A=0$, take $B=8$ or $B=16$, and initialize both directions as right and the sheet at zero. Use three ordinary joint walk steps. Read only whether $A$ is at site $1$ moving right. Its probabilities are exactly **$16/64=1/4$ and $32/64=1/2$**, respectively. The nearest changed input site is seven edges from this single-site read. A range-one circuit in three layers cannot realize both probabilities from these localized inputs and identical local auxiliary states. At least one binary probability must have error at least $1/8$. The discrepancy is already present before packet calibration, choice of a Fourier sector or a continuum limit.

The only $A$ histories at this port are right-left-right and left-right-right. Their coin signs agree, their positions at the first and third departures coincide, and they differ by two sites at the second departure. In the exact count table the five accessible even separation values around $B=8$ lie on a linear part of the table, with $g(20),g(22),g(24),g(26),g(28)=5,4,3,2,1$. Adjacent entries relevant to the second departure around $B=16$ are all six. Thus for each fixed $B$ path the two $A$ histories differ in accumulated translation by $-1$ or $0$, respectively. Summing over *coherent* $B$ histories at the same endpoint gives, in the sheet translation variable $z$, the port-norm Laurent polynomials $(16+8z+8z^{-1})/64$ and $32/64$. A localized sheet extracts the constant coefficient. A prepared character evaluates $z=e^{2\pi i k/17}$, giving $(1+\cos(2\pi k/17))/4$ or $1/2$. The trivial character is an exact null control. The other interference port $(31,L)$ gives $1/4$ or zero on the localized sheet. The remaining four ports have equal probabilities in the two trials. These differences use one fixed physical measurement and are invariant under a simultaneous change of algebraic labels. They distinguish the two remote *preparations*. **Prepared phase sector.** A sheet-unread test uses the ideal frozen cosine phase at integrated action $\pi$. Let $N=8q$, let $A$ start in $(|0\rangle+|q\rangle)/\sqrt2$, and let $B$ occupy $2q$ or $4q$. The entire $A$ observable is supported in the patch $[0,q]$. For $\phi(x,y)=\pi[1-\cos(2\pi(x-y)/N)]$, measure the real swap $X=|0\rangle\langle q|+|q\rangle\langle 0|$ on its two-mode subspace. Then $\langle X\rangle_y=\cos[\phi(q,y)-\phi(0,y)]$. The relative phases are $-\pi/\sqrt2$ and $\pi(1/\sqrt2-1)$. The two binary output laws differ in total variation by $|\cos(\pi/\sqrt2)|=0.605699867\ldots$. Complex conjugation and an unread mixture of both phase orientations preserve this real test. The closest changed site is $q$ edges away, so any implementation with total-variation error below $|\cos(\pi/\sqrt2)|/2$ on both inputs needs $rD\ge N/8$.

This test uses a coherent detector on a whole patch and a fixed accumulated action with parked positions. It bounds the depth of the complete frozen phase operation. It does not give a fixed numerical error floor per weak departure kick in a moving flight. Finite-order sheet realizations have rounding error. In operator norm a phase error at most $e$ changes each tested state by at most $e$, so the two-input distinguishing gap decreases by at most $2e$. ## A position-only test during the moving flight

There is also a test in the actual interacting classical limit, using localized packets and one ordinary position bin. Take the positive-curvature band in both coordinates, coupling $\kappa=1$, and zero initial momenta. Walker $A$ starts at angle zero in both trials. Walker $B$ starts at $\pi/2$ in trial I and at $\pi$ in trial II. The earlier convergence estimate gives concentration at the corresponding torus Newton trajectories under its packet, clock, sheet and scaling hypotheses. With $r=q_A-q_B$ the equations are $q_A''=-\sin r$, $q_B''=\sin r$ and $r''=-2\sin r$. [Packet theorem and encoded initial data](Periodic_interaction_and_two_limits.md)

In II both centres are stationary. In I, $|r(s)+\pi/2|\le s^2$ by two integrations of $|r''|\le2$. Consequently $q_A''(s)=\cos(r(s)+\pi/2)\ge1-s^4/2$. For $0<t\le1$,

$$
 t^2/2-t^6/60 \ \le\ q_A(t)\ \le\ t^2/2.
$$

Fix any finite proposed continuum speed $c\ge0$ and any positive $t\le\min(1,\pi/[8(1+c)])$. In the bin $S_t=\{|Y_A|<t^2/4\}$, the limiting probability is zero in I and one in II. Both predicted centres are strictly separated from the bin boundaries at this fixed time. The bin enlarged by distance $ct$ stays strictly away from both initial $B$ centres. The bin event means detecting an $A$ occupancy in that patch; it can be treated as an observable supported on that patch before any time needed to collect its component readings.

**Finite-speed obstruction.** Suppose a candidate lattice family encodes both preparations physically, with the full initial reduced states on the enlarged bin agreeing in trace distance $o(1)$. Suppose its readout is local there and its causal radius after the *same elapsed physical time* $t$ is at most $ct+o(1)$. Assume initial agreement also on a fixed open enlargement covering that $o(1)$ margin. Locality and contractivity then bound the difference of the two candidate bin probabilities by $o(1)$. Their target difference tends to one. Hence the larger absolute probability error over the two inputs is at least $1/2-o(1)$. This test needs neither intermediate observations nor access to sheet characters at readout.

The initial-agreement premise has operational content. Take local occupation registers for each species, put the shared sheet at $A$, and use the same $A$ packet and background in both trials. Prepare the two $B$ packets in distant disjoint neighborhoods. Compact support gives the same initial state in the tested region. For tails of total probability tending to zero there, projection onto its $B$ vacuum changes the state by trace distance $o(1)$ for the product preparations. In the mathematical band encoding the single-particle positions concentrate at their respective initial angles, which suffices for this tail condition for product starts. A proposed physical encoding must also check its auxiliary registers. A field at $A$ prepared differently according to the distant source, an initial nonlocal logical register, or a control law announcing the distant choice can invalidate initial agreement. A comparison that first propagates the changed field must count that preparation time. The obstruction concerns the instantaneous smooth force on this specific pair of localized starts.

## Two clocks for the same sequence of records

The original spacing is $a=2\pi/N=\varepsilon\delta$ and the original departure time is $\tau=\varepsilon\delta^2$. If the whole local tour counts as one release separated from the next by $\tau$, its signal covers circumference $2\pi$ in at most $\tau$. Its speed in these scaled units must grow at least as $2\pi/\tau$. Alternatively, assign duration $\tau$ to each signal round. A circuit now takes $N\tau=2\pi\delta$ plus local overhead per release and approximately $N$ times the intended duration for a fixed simulated flight. Finally, if physical signal speed is fixed at $v>0$ on this ring of fixed circumference, each tour takes at least $2\pi/v$. The number of releases in a nonzero simulated time grows on both the fixed-action and shrinking-action scalings. Exact equality at the releases cannot identify their physical timestamps.

For this construction the instantaneous two-position lookup has been replaced by local communication with an explicit cost. The two-preparation obstruction applies to *any* strict bounded-speed candidate satisfying the initial agreement, local readout and common elapsed-time premises. A physical map still needs a justified space-time scaling or a dynamical account of how the sources prepare the intervening field. The spectral limit and the classical record comparison by themselves supply neither.
