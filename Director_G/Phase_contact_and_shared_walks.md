# Preparing a common complex structure by contact

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Two walks can prepare their phase references independently and later establish a common orientation. At contact, a local meter compares two real coordinate planes. Either outcome can be used to prepare the same aligned space before logical data are loaded. When a walk arrives with unknown complex data, correcting its orientation can conjugate those data relative to an external reference. The construction assumes coherent signed controls, a projective meter with the squared-norm law, and causal access to its record. The earlier walk derived an algebra containing a quarter turn on a selected plane. Operating that turn conditionally at an interface is an additional physical capability. ## A contact with two usable outcomes

On a real plane put \(J|0\rangle=|1\rangle\), \(J|1\rangle=-|0\rangle\). At a shared site prepare a control in \(|0\rangle\), apply \(H\), implement \(Q=-J_AJ_B\) on the control-one route, then apply \(H\) and read the control. The minus sign is relative to the bypass and belongs to the controlled signed permutation. The two data operators are \(P_\pm=(I\pm Q)/2\). Since \(Q=Q^\mathsf{T}\) and \(Q^2=I\), another read of the same contact returns the recorded sign with certainty. Independent real preparations, including mixed local resources, give probability \(1/2\) for each sign because every real local state has zero expectation of its skew operator \(J\). A previously aligned pair instead gives the positive outcome with certainty. [Signed-coordinate proof](lean/PhaseContact.lean) · [Complete contact records](results/phase_contact.json)

For input \(|00\rangle\), the normalized outputs are \((|00\rangle\mp|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\). Reflecting the second plane with \(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\) after the negative sign prepares the positive state in both cases. Its individual plane marginals are \(I/2\). If plane \(B\) has already been matched to a cyclic sheet, its calibration also matters. In complexified coordinates let \(J_B\) have eigenvalue \(s_B i\), \(s_B=\pm1\), and let the sheet translation have eigenvalue \(\exp(2\pi i k_B/q)\). The held reference uses the relation \(k_B=s_B\bmod q\). Reflect the sheet about its physical fiducial, \(F|y\rangle=|-y\bmod q\rangle\), as well as applying \(Z\). This flips both labels and preserves their relation. In the ideal matched pair a joint stroke then measures \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)\) on either grade. Plane-only correction on the negative grade gives \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)\). Finite preparation tails enter the measured contrasts. With two order-eight sheets, rational rotation \((3,4,5)\), and four bright reads per sheet, the negative-grade joint contrasts were \(16/25\) with only the plane corrected and \(-112/625\) with both corrections. An actual reflection has a transport cost if the sheet supports only unit hops. ## Preparing an aligned network

Compare the edges of a rooted tree. On a joint chirality sector the edge \(ij\) reports \(s_i s_j\). The contact projectors commute, so every edge can be read before applying the corrections. The product of the edge signs on the root path determines whether to reflect each vertex. This aligns all vertices and leaves two globally conjugate assignments. Every tree transcript has probability \(2^{1-n}\) for \(n\) initially independent localized planes. A fresh child can also be attached one edge at a time. A reflection of an already attached internal vertex must extend through the affected subtree if its internal relations are to stay aligned. A controller can keep the signs as classical records and compensate later operations once the information arrives. [Tree and cycle proof](lean/PhaseContact.lean) · [Exact branch data for chains of two to five sites](results/distributed_complex_walk.json)

A deterministic circuit can attach a blank child without a measurement. Apply \(H\) to the child and apply \(-J_i\) to the parent on the child-one route. If the current real code basis is \(e_0,e_1=J_i e_0\), its first vector grows to \((e_0|0\rangle-e_1|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\). In an \(n\)-site chain with disjoint nearest-neighbour gates per layer, grow from the centre on both ends. This takes \(\lceil n/2\rceil\) contact layers for \(n\ge2\), counting each two-site attachment as one gate. The depth is necessary from a product source: after \(d\ge1\) matching layers any single input can influence at most \(2d\) consecutive sites. For \(2d<n\) the end sites have disjoint input cones. Their reduced state factors, so an end-to-end \(Q\) read is positive with probability \(1/2\); the aligned code requires probability one. Local measurements on the two alternating edge matchings can acquire the signs in two layers. Knowing global success, or using the corrected frame at distant sites, still requires communicating the records. The deterministic circuit was checked through eleven sites. [Circuit and causal assumptions](check_distributed_complex_walk.py) · [Growth schedules](results/distributed_complex_walk.json)

For two moving tokens initially \(D\) edges apart on a line, unit speed and local storage permit their first common site at \(\lceil D/2\rceil\) ticks. The midpoint protocol can return both outputs home within \(2\lceil D/2\rceil\) ticks, apart from on-site gate time. If one token stays home the contact takes \(D\) ticks. Compulsory equal-time, one-edge motion on a bipartite base changes the answer at odd \(D\): both tokens flip parity together and remain on opposite sublattices. A hold, offset launch, or allowed contact across an edge changes this assumption. [Distance and parity controls](results/phase_contact.json)

## Local implementation and source order

On the aligned space each \(J_i\) acts as one common \(J\). Take orthonormal \(e_0,e_1\) with \(J_i e_0=e_1,\ J_i e_1=-e_0\). Encoding \(\psi\) as \(E\psi=\operatorname{Re}\psi\otimes e_0+\operatorname{Im}\psi\otimes e_1\) gives

\[
 (A\otimes I+B\otimes J_i) E\psi= E((A+iB)\psi)
\]

for any real matrices \(A,B\) on the addressed data. Linear extension applies to each Kraus operator, so the same encoded source and allowed instruments give the same complete record law. A laboratory operating on local data needs access only to its own synchronized plane. McKague, Mosca and Gisin established this distributed real simulation. The contact circuit supplies one preparation with explicit initial-state and timing requirements. An exact three-site circuit with the rational phase \((3+4i)/5\), an amplitude-damping read and feedback agreed with the independently evaluated complex circuit on every terminal branch. The damping records had weights \(17/25\) and \(8/25\). In a separate two-site example, local \(Y\) reads of a real Bell input had four equiprobable results with unaligned blank planes. After correction, the two opposite-sign outcomes each had probability \(1/2\) for either contact sign. [McKague, Mosca and Gisin](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.102.020505) · [Circuit and individual probabilities](results/distributed_complex_walk.json)

Source order is observable. If the two planes already encode \(\psi_A,\psi_B\), the positive projection represents \(\psi_A\otimes\psi_B\), while the corrected negative projection represents \(\psi_A\otimes\overline{\psi_B}\) in the fixed common convention. In the tested two-site phase read, alignment before loading gave one joint outcome with certainty. Alignment after loading put half the probability on the outcome with the second recorded sign reversed. A universal undo on unknown data with an independently held complex spectator would implement a transpose on one factor. The partial transpose of the Bell density has expectation \(-1/2\) in the antisymmetric state, excluding such a completely positive channel. Shared blank resources can therefore be prepared before separate subsequent data inputs; an entangled reference produced by contact also changes an experiment's source-independence assumptions. ## Transport around a loop

The tree signs above compare planes in fixed local frames. For a physical orthogonal edge transport \(U_{ij}\in O(2)\), the identity \(U_{ij} J U_{ij}^{\mathsf T}=\det(U_{ij})J\) shows how a held quarter turn is transported. A choice of local orientation at every vertex makes all edges orientation-preserving exactly when the product of edge determinants is positive around every closed path. Necessity follows by telescoping vertex signs. For sufficiency, choose a root orientation and transport it on a spanning tree; each remaining edge closes a loop and agrees precisely when its determinant product is positive. An orientation-reversing loop returns \(J\) as \(-J\); an orientation-preserving loop can still have nonzero rotation angle. The determinant criterion characterizes orientation transport, not the full holonomy. The signed-graph condition was already present in the plane-algebra analysis. The new contact supplies a physical relative comparison and, on suitable blank inputs, a correction. Simultaneous conjugation of planes, sheets, controls and readouts preserves their squared record probabilities. Alignment fixes which devices can share complex amplitudes; it leaves the absolute choice of imaginary unit and the origin of the record law open.
