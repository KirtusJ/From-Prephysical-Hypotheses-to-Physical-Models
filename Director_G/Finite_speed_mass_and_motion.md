**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

One nearest-neighbor walk on a signed cyclic sheet admits a massive one-dimensional Dirac limit with a prescribed force, and a semiclassical position record bounded by the same speed. A packet from rest gives a particularly stringent test of ordinary probability selection. When the sheet sets both mass and force, every fixed odd phase index gives the same limiting position. Other records on the very same sheet can distinguish them. The derivation uses a squared record law, a supplied clock and controls, and a locally applied field; their physical origin remains open. ## A microscopic mass operation

Use base spacing $\ell=c\tau$, tick duration $\tau$, positive action unit $\varepsilon$, and $\mu=mc^2>0$. The right/left stream has Fourier matrix $\exp(-iq\sigma_z)$; the onsite coin is $R_y(\theta)=\exp(-i\theta\sigma_y)$ with $\theta=\mu\tau/\varepsilon$. A phase at departure $\exp(-if\ell\tau x/\varepsilon)$ changes physical momentum by $-f\tau$. A step advances a basis walker by exactly one base site. Thus its base signal cone has speed $c$ at every resolution. In contrast, a single fixed coin with bounded quasienergy curvature cannot acquire a nonzero continuum inverse mass merely by changing its clock while $\ell/\tau\le c$ and $\ell/\varepsilon\to0$. Its curvature in physical units is $\omega''\ell^2/(\tau\varepsilon)\to0$. [Analytic and literal controls](results/causal_mass_flight.json)

A variable real rotation can itself arise from the signed sheet. Normalize two real onsite Hadamards $H$, take $4\mid M$, and write $T$ for the positive unit translation in the sheet coordinate. Select

$$D=\operatorname{diag}(1,T^{M/4}),\quad X_j=H\operatorname{diag}(T^j,T^{-j})H,\quad C=D X_j D^\dagger.$$

Apply a signed sheet translation proportional to the departure position, then $C$, then stream. Write $h=2\pi/M$. For the Fourier convention $T\mapsto e^{-ikh}$, both $k=\pm1$ see the same real coin $R_y(-jh)$. The mass angle can be rounded to this grid with one-step operator error at most $h/2$. If $h=|F|\ell\tau/\varepsilon$ for a nonzero fixed field magnitude, the accumulated error through time $T_0$ is at most $T_0|F|c\tau/(2\varepsilon)$. The integer shifts can grow very large. The cone refers to the **base**; a sheet with its own physical distance and bounded shift rate must pay for the $M/4$ and $j$ translations. This is a model with supplied on-site operations and an external background. [Signed integer sheet recurrence and all-character checks](results/sheet_mass_coin.json)

## From the localized packet source to a force flight

Begin at $(0,R)$, with zero applied base field. For $B=2^s$, split each occupied R by $H$ and coast along the outgoing rail for $d=B/2,B/4,\ldots,1$ ticks in successive stages. Swap L to R at the specified left endpoints. The two children of each centre are new distinct sites, so a reversible site-dependent rail exchange clears the direction label. The resulting normalized box has $B$ equal positive amplitudes on the parity grid $1-B,3-B,\ldots,B-1$. The maximum displacement and elapsed ticks are both $B-1$; reversal gathers it coherently.

For controlled momentum variance, follow the box by one $H$, $d$ streams and one $H$ at each of the same scales, retaining the plus (R) channel. A co-moving reversible first-failure flag allows a single terminal success query. On that branch the packet is a triangle with integer coefficients $1,2,\ldots,B,\ldots,2,1$ on one parity. The success probability is $(2B^2+1)/(3B^2)>2/3$ at finite $B$. Parseval applied to the nearest difference on that parity gives $\langle\sin^2 q\rangle=3/[2(2B^2+1)]$. Thus the mean square of the distance to $\{0,\pi\}$ modulo $2\pi$ is at most $3\pi^2/[8(2B^2+1)]$. Choose $B$ dyadic of order $\sqrt\varepsilon/(c\tau)$. Position RMS, folded physical momentum RMS, and the physical duration are all $O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$ for fixed $c$. The two charts give the same flight because $U(q+\pi)=-U(q)$. This source uses preprogrammed local rail exchanges and $O(\log B)$ on-site control stages. Its time counts streamed ticks and assumes a bounded number of on-site gates fit each cycle. For a fixed prepared sector take $q=\ell p/\varepsilon$ relative to its chart. At zero force the quasienergy satisfies $\cos\omega=\cos q\cos\theta$. The positive/negative continuum energies are $\lambda E(p)$ with $E(p)=\sqrt{\mu^2+c^2p^2}$ and $\lambda=\pm1$. Along the momentum characteristic $p(t)=p_0-ft$ the limiting two-component equation is

$$i\varepsilon\,\partial_t v=[c p(t)\sigma_z+\mu\sigma_y]v.$$

Over a bounded time $T_0$ on a fixed momentum interval $|p(t)|\le P$, replacing each interval with its endpoint Hamiltonian costs at most $T_0\tau c|f|/(2\varepsilon)$ in norm. Splitting that constant Hamiltonian into stream and coin costs at most $T_0\tau cP\mu/\varepsilon^2$ by the unitary commutator bound. Thus at fixed $\varepsilon$ the full spinor has a controlled quantum limit, and $\tau=o(\varepsilon^2)$ suffices when also sending $\varepsilon\to0$. For the triangular source, first discard arbitrarily small norm outside a fixed neighborhood using its folded variance. The chart at $\pi$ has a common parity carrier and the same spin matrix. Distinct charts have vanishing cross terms in bounded fixed spatial Fourier reads once the truncated supports are disjoint under the $O(\varepsilon)$ physical momentum shift. One can check the semiclassical step without presuming a scalar band equation. Write
$u_\lambda(p)=\exp[-i\arctan(cp/\mu)\sigma_x/2](1,i\lambda)/\sqrt2$. This is a normalized parallel eigenframe. With phase $\exp[-i\lambda\int_0^t E(p(s))\,ds/\varepsilon]$, add $\varepsilon v$ to $u_\lambda$, where $v=-i\dot u_\lambda/(2\lambda E)$. The residual after substitution has norm $\varepsilon^2\|\dot v\|$. Since $|\dot\beta|\le c|f|/\mu$ and $|\ddot\beta|\le2c^2f^2/\mu^2$ for $\beta=\arctan(cp/\mu)$, both endpoint corrections and the integrated residual together cost at most $\varepsilon[c|f|/(2\mu^2)+7T_0 c^2f^2/(8\mu^3)]$. Replacing a rest spin by the smooth eigenframe initially costs $o(1)$ for this packet. These estimates extend to either band and to a fixed nonzero signed mass by a real rail change.

For clarity, $Y=i\varepsilon\partial_p$ on the smooth chart. Differentiating the scalar phase gives $\lambda\int_0^{T_0} E'(p_0-ft)\,dt$. Its variation across the packet is bounded by $T_0 c^2/\mu$ times the initial momentum width. Differentiating the eigenframe costs $O(\varepsilon c/\mu)$ and the source position width vanishes. A smooth momentum cutoff has commutator $i\varepsilon\chi'$ with $Y$, so truncation preserves this localization at fixed cutoff. Norm comparison transfers the conclusion to bounded spatial records. For a rest launch the limiting center and Newtonian error obey

$$Y_\lambda(T_0)=\frac{-\lambda f T_0^2}{m(1+\sqrt{1+(fT_0/mc)^2})},\qquad
\left|Y_\lambda(T_0)+\frac{\lambda fT_0^2}{2m}\right|
\le \frac{|f|^3T_0^4}{8m^3c^2}.$$

The speed is bounded by $c$. The Newtonian comparison requires low velocity. At $m=c=1,T_0=1/2,f=3,\lambda=+1$ the limiting position is $-0.267592$, while the Newtonian value is $-0.375$. ## What the acceleration measures

The sheet-built coin is especially informative outside the nominal sector. For fixed odd signed $k$, put $s_k=\sin(\pi k/2)$. Then $D_k=\operatorname{diag}(1,-i s_k)$ and $C_k=R_y(-k s_k jh)$. The physical mass term is $\mu_k=k s_k\mu$ and the force is $f_k=kF$ when $-jh$ approximates $\mu\tau/\varepsilon$. The same $D^\dagger H$ from R prepares $(1,i s_k)/\sqrt2$. At rest its energy band is $\lambda_k=\operatorname{sgn}(k)$. The positive energy satisfies $E_k(kp)=|k|E_1(p)$. Substitution in the velocity $\lambda_k c^2p/E_k(p)$ at $p=-kFt$ cancels $k$ entirely. Thus **every fixed odd signed index gives the same limiting trajectory from rest**. This also holds at scaled initial momenta $p_{0,k}=k p_*$. A common nonzero unscaled launch or independently fixed mass generally breaks the equality. Convergence has been taken at each fixed $k$; indices growing with $M$ need a separate argument. [Independent signed-coordinate, flight and fixed-mass controls](results/shared_mass_force.json)

At finite resolution mass and force need not cancel in the whole position distribution. At $M=32$ and ten ticks the position total variation distances from $k=1$ were approximately $0.790,0.599,0.669$ for $k=3,5,7$. The missed prediction for $k=15$ is retained: its distribution agrees exactly. In fact $k'=k+M/2$ induces $G=\operatorname{diag}(1,(-1)^{M/4})$ on the prepared rail, $C_{k'}=(-1)^jG C_k G$, and an extra force sign $(-1)^{f_t x}$ for integer programmed field multiplier $f_t$. The parity of a departure coordinate is fixed by the endpoint and the tick. At any recorded endpoint these factors form a scalar sign and a final $G$. The corresponding input preparation and any intermediate diagonal site/rail projectors respect the relation. A bare rail interferometer or an odd unit sheet translation can give different records. ## A pair of records on the same prepared sheet

After the common preparation, route fresh trials to two independent settings. Supply a clean R comparison rail; one can retain the original rail and base as memory. Apply $H$, translate the L arm by $d$ sheet units in an equal-time comparison, apply $H$, and read both rail ports. Under the squared sheet-coordinate read, the measured contrast $m_d=2P_+(d)-1$ equals $\mathbb E[\cos(khd)]$ for the actual marginal sheet distribution. The effect is diagonal in the character basis, so coherences and correlations with retained memory do not enter. Use matched preparations, including all conditioning, and count both output ports. The two settings on separately prepared copies estimate that ensemble; they do not individually tag a hidden index of a different walker.

Set $x_*=\cos(2\pi/M)$ and $x_k=\cos(2\pi k/M)$. The first two contrasts give

$$V_*=\frac{1+m_2}{2}-2x_* m_1+x_*^2=\mathbb E[(x_k-x_*)^2].$$

For $M\ge4$, $V_*=0$ exactly when the whole distribution occupies the ordinary conjugate pair relative to the calibrated unit. With $g=(1-x_*)^2$ the off-pair weight is at most $V_*/g$, since zero frequency is a nearest competitor. At $M=4$ the two competing endpoints tie. A separate counting component splits fairly in these two two-H measurements and contributes $1/2+x_*^2$ per unit mass to the residual. For count treated as a distinct nuisance, divide by $\min(g,1/2+x_*^2)$ instead. At $M=32$ a zero/third-mode mixture matches the first contrast exactly but has $V_*\simeq0.00286906$ on the combined test. Imperfect empirical contrasts need upper errors $\Delta_2/2+2|x_*|\Delta_1$, and $g\sim4\pi^4/M^4$ makes the fixed-unit two-setting test poorly conditioned at large order. [Two-setting positive certificates and all-count controls](results/shared_mass_force.json)

There is an internal version that avoids entering any numerical target or preparing comparison sectors. Repeat one fixed primitive physical translation $d$ times in each setting, on identically prepared trials. Require the following three equalities or comparisons of measured contrasts:

$$V=(1+m_2)/2-m_1^2=0,\qquad m_1<1,\qquad m_1=\max_{1\le d<M}m_d.$$

The variance condition gives just one folded cosine class, since $V=\mathrm{Var}(x_k)$ for a coherent-sector mixture. For positive counting fraction its contribution to the centered square about $m_1$ is $1/2+m_1^2>0$, ruling that out as well. For a nonzero nonunit $k$ the power $d=M/\gcd(k,M)<M$ returns contrast one, while $m_1<1$. For a unit, the nonzero powers permute the nonzero residues, and the maximizing powers solve $kd=\pm1\bmod M$. Thus the one-stroke setting maximizes exactly for the fundamental conjugate pair. The exclusion of contrast one handles $k=0$. This test uses the rank of observed probabilities, the order of the physical stroke, and the assumed contrast law. Statistical uncertainty must enter before declaring a comparison: with $|\delta m_d|\le u$ and contrasts in $[-1,1]$, the variance error is at most $5u/2$ and the error of a pairwise gap is at most $2u$. All 137 pure-sector tests at seven orders, including ties and lower-order returns, give the predicted answer. [Rank tests and mixture controls](results/shared_mass_force.json)

If calibrated powers are affordable, a randomized read avoids dividing by the small one-stroke gap. On each independently prepared trial choose $d$ uniformly from $0,\ldots,M-1$, perform the same binary comparison, and score its sign $S=\pm1$ as $Z=2S\cos(2\pi d/M)$. Orthogonality of the cyclic characters gives $\mathbb E Z=w_{\{\pm1\}}$, including under fair count contamination, and $\mathbb E Z^2=2$ for $M\ge3$. Hoeffding gives absolute error $\le e$ with failure probability $\le\alpha$ for $n\ge8\log(2/\alpha)/e^2$ trials. The bound is independent of $M$ in shots, while the mean number of positive physical strokes per trial is $(M-1)/2$. Assume branch synchrony, calibrated power controls, stationary contrast law, and settings independent of the prepared register. A persistent latent register reused between trials can invalidate the concentration statement. With uniform per-setting binary total-variation error at most $\Omega$, systematic bias is at most $2\sqrt2\,\Omega$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. The very same sign and setting pairs estimate every folded weight by replacing the cosine with its $j$th harmonic and the prefactor with the multiplicity of the conjugate class. A union bound supplies simultaneous error intervals. The zero and even antipodal classes have multiplicity one. This score uses a named harmonic. A separately certified pure reference can supply the cosine physically through an independent sign at the same $d$; two untagged independent copies of one mixture instead return a sum of squared weights divided by their multiplicities.
