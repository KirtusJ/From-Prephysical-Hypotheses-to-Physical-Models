# What the line hides and the ring reads with four arc gains

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Four constant arc gains give one observable relative gain at a sharp space-time port on the line. On a ring a second gain enters when different lifted paths meet. Position masks cannot prescribe an arbitrary multiset of these second labels once their quotient has order at least three. With the squared terminal-port law and both characters sending $H$ to $-1$, an eight-tick mask on seven wells distinguishes two characters that agree on every sharp-launch position product event on the line. All rates below are model predictions per original sharp launch. ## Exact word gains

Start at $(0,R)$ with gain 1. A right step sends a path to $(x+1,R)$, a left step to $(x-1,L)$. The two letters in an arc label are its incoming and outgoing rails. Give RR, RL, LR and LL respectively the spatially homogeneous gains $a,b,c,d$ in an abelian group. A position product event retains a prescribed set of whole wells at each tick; all paths reaching a retained well can continue through either rail. For a word of length $t$ write $l$ for the number of left steps, $m$ for the number of LL arcs, and $e=1$ if its final rail is L and 0 otherwise. Then

$$n_{LL}=m,\quad n_{RL}=l-m,\quad n_{LR}=l-m-e,\quad n_{RR}=t-2l+m+e.$$

Indeed every L arrival uses RL or LL, and the number of R-to-L transitions exceeds the L-to-R count by $e$. Multiplying the four arc factors gives the exact gain

$$G(w)=a^t(a/c)^e B^l H^m,\qquad B=bc/a^2,\quad H=ad/(bc). \tag{1}$$

On the line $l=(t-x)/2$ is fixed at the terminal site. Every relative gain at a given clock, site and rail therefore lies in $\langle H\rangle$. The same-end, same-clock words LRLR and LLRR differ by $H$, so this is the exact subgroup generated by such comparisons over all clocks. Words at one particular port need only attain a subset of it.

On a ring of $N$ wells let $D=N/\gcd(N,2)$. The terminal condition is $x=t-2l\bmod N$, hence the possible $l$ form one residue class modulo $D$. Choose any representative $l_0$. Label a lifted endpoint by its *winding index* $k=(l-l_0)/D$. A unit change in this index changes the lifted physical displacement by $-2D$; on an odd ring this is two laps. The relative gains lie in $\langle H,B^D\rangle$. Modulo $\langle H\rangle$ they lie in a single coset of the subgroup generated by $\Omega=[B^D]$, with normalized labels $\Omega^k$. A different reference $l_0$ translates every label by the same group element. The fork below attains relative gain $B^D H^{D-1}$, and the preceding four-tick comparison attains $H$. Thus both generators are realized among relative gains for $D\ge2$. This statement about a generated subgroup does not prescribe independent multiplicities in its elements.

## Winding support under whole-position masks

**Splicing theorem.** Fix a complete terminal port $(t,x,\mathrm{rail})$ on a ring and any position product event from the sharp source. The set of its attained integer winding indices is empty or a consecutive interval. If the extreme indices differ by at least two there are at least four distinct admitted histories at the port.

For a path $p$, its deficit $l_p(j)$ is its number of left steps through tick $j$. It starts at zero and increases by either zero or one. Suppose two admitted words $p,r$ at the chosen port end with deficits $l$ and $l+kD$, where $k\ge2$. Compare $l_p(j)+D$ with $l_r(j)$. Their difference starts at $D>0$, ends at $(1-k)D<0$ and decreases by at most one per tick. They meet at some $0<j<t$. The paths are then at the same physical well. Either prefix can be followed by either suffix because masks depend only on position and both outgoing directions exist on each incoming rail. Splicing may change the LL count at the join, which affects $H$ but preserves the winding argument. The unchanged terminal step of either suffix gives the specified rail.

The two new words have final deficits $l+D$ and $l+(k-1)D$. Both differ from the original extreme words. Even when $k=2$ makes the new deficits equal, the words are distinct. Their prefixes have left counts differing by $D$; the two suffixes also have counts differing by $(k-1)D$. Thus the two prefixes and two suffixes give four distinct combinations. Induction on $k$, using the two retained paths at deficits $l+D$ and $l+kD$, fills every intermediate index. [Lean check of the arc identity and the integer crossing lemma](lean/FourGainWinding.lean)

Two consequences apply even if the clock can be chosen anew for the request and all gains can be translated by a common factor.

* If the order of $\Omega$ is at least four (or infinite), two singleton labels whose quotient is $\Omega^2$ cannot be the entire port histogram. Any two lifts realizing this quotient are at least two indices apart. The splicing theorem forces further histories. Adjacent labels can occur alone, as the fork shows.
* If the order is at least three, three singleton labels in three distinct winding classes cannot be the entire histogram. Any three distinct classes require a lifted span of at least two, forcing a fourth history. This covers the order-three case in which every unequal pair of labels is adjacent on the quotient cycle. For orders one and two this argument leaves unrestricted preparation undecided. The previously proved single-gain case $a=b=c=1$ has $B=1$.

## A physically named positive comparison

In a $D+1$ tick block from $(0,R)$, retain the wells $+j,-j\bmod N$ at each $1\le j<D$, then retain $D$ and $D+1\bmod N$ on the last two ticks. In deficits the successive masks are $\{0,j\}\bmod D$ before tick $D$, followed twice by $\{0\}$. At the first tick the two directions are distinct for $D\ge2$. Before reunion a path at deficit 0 can continue only at 0 and a path at $j$ can continue only at $j+1$. Specifically, for $1\le j\le D-2$, the unintended deficits $1$ and $j$ miss $\{0,j+1\}$ modulo $D$. At reunion only the R step from 0 and the L step from $D-1$ reach 0 modulo $D$. The last mask keeps just their R daughters. This includes $D=2$, where there is no intermediate tick between splitting and reunion, and $D=3$, where there is one. Exactly $R^{D+1}$ and $L^D R$ end at the same oriented well. Repeating this block from its translated terminal well yields winding counts $\binom{j}{k}$ for $k=0,\ldots,j$ after $j$ blocks, since every block returns to R. On $N=1,2$ a position mask cannot separate directions and this two-path enumeration fails.

A unitary character $\chi$ assigns the phase $\chi(G(w))$ to each word. For the balanced local step the two columns, with rows indexed by outgoing R and L, are $(\chi(a),\chi(b))/\sqrt2$ and $(\chi(c),\chi(d))/\sqrt2$. They are orthogonal exactly when $\chi(H)=-1$. The usual squared terminal-port law after coherent position filtering then gives $2^{-t}|\sum_w\chi(G(w))|^2$ at each complete port. On the line, (1) makes every such squared law equal to $2^{-t}|\sum_w(-1)^m|^2$. The common phase may depend on the port. Summing the equalities over any selected terminal wells preserves them.

Take $N=D=7$ and let $z,g$ generate $C_2\times C_3$, of orders two and three respectively. Put $a=c=1$, $b=g$, $d=zg$. Thus $H=z$, $B=g$. Choose $\chi_0(z)=\chi_1(z)=-1$, $\chi_0(g)=1$, $\chi_1(g)=\exp(2\pi i/3)$. At $(1,R)$ after eight ticks the two fork words have gains $1$ and $g^7 z^6=g$. In a reference panel retaining only $j\bmod7$ at each tick, $R^8$ alone survives at the same detector. The squared-law and fair path-count probabilities per launch are

| Eight-tick records | $\chi_0$ | $\chi_1$ | Fair count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Two-arm fork, $(1,R)$ | $1/64$ | $1/256$ | $1/128$ |
| Singleton reference, $(1,R)$ | $1/256$ | $1/256$ | $1/256$ |
| Fork / reference | $4$ | $1$ | $2$ |

The comparison assumes a stable sharp launch, known relative exposures, coherent passage through each retained set, and terminal detection at the named rail. The original-launch denominator includes paths rejected by an earlier mask. Recording which arm passed at every tick would remove interference. Complex conjugation of all phases leaves these real probabilities unchanged, so this comparison distinguishes the trivial winding phase from the conjugate nontrivial pair. Exactly preparing *every* winding histogram is unnecessary for this measurement. A physical identification of the gain group or of either character remains open.

For every $D=N/\gcd(N,2)\ge2$ the general fork rate per original launch is $2^{-D}[1+(-1)^{D-1}\operatorname{Re}\chi(B^D)]$. The singleton rate is $2^{-(D+1)}$ and fair count gives $2^{-D}$ on the fork. The two words have relative gain $B^DH^{D-1}$, proving the formula. Thus two squared laws differ exactly when their real parts on $B^D$ differ. A squared law differs from fair count exactly when this real part is nonzero. The two cubic characters both give $1/256$ on the nine-well fork ($D=9$, ten ticks, two in-phase words giving squared numerator four), and give $0$ and $3/32$ respectively on eight wells ($D=4$). At $D=7$ an order-four character with $\chi(B)=i$ ties counting at $1/128$. An unfiltered sequence of read times on a separately chosen even ring with coprime deficit period resolves this count tie and identifies all folded characters within the specified mixture. The Lean arc theorem holds for every integer assignment to the four arcs. Choosing each coordinate vector in turn yields equality of the four integer exponents. Evaluation in any abelian gain group gives (1). The strict crossing lemma uses the opposite strict endpoint signs to exclude both endpoints. The splicing of words, interval argument, gain-group evaluation, fork enumeration, unitarity and interpretation as probability remain mathematical arguments in the text. ## What an unread winding register preserves

Give the walk an initially zero register in $\mathbb Z/m$, $m\ge1$. Crossing the chosen seam shifts the register by $+1$ or $-1$ according to the crossing direction. Take the register states to be orthonormal and use the squared norm for detection. At one complete terminal port after arbitrary whole-well masks, let $A_w$ be the sum of path amplitudes with integer signed seam count $w$, including all coin factors. The joint terminal vector has register component $\sum_{w\equiv r\bmod m} A_w$ at $r$. Its detection probability is exactly

$$\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\left|\sum_{w\equiv r\bmod m} A_w\right|^2
=\frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left|\sum_w A_w e^{2\pi i jw/m}\right|^2.$$

Expanding the square gives the identity because the average character of $w-v$ equals one for $w\equiv v\bmod m$ and zero otherwise. Thus every such walk-only terminal record also has a model in which a uniformly random seam phase is fixed for the entire launch. Testing more position masks or more clocks does not distinguish these two mechanisms. The phase must persist through all fork repetitions within a launch for this equivalence. An integer register gives the same identity with a uniform phase integral, since each finite clock has finitely many windings.

The register's resolution nevertheless matters. On twelve wells repeat the seven-tick fork above $n\ge1$ times, with the balanced coin giving a minus sign at LL and unit signs elsewhere. A path taking the left arm in $k$ blocks has relative winding $-k$, sign $(-1)^k$, and multiplicity $\binom nk$. With denominator $2^{7n}$ the numerator is $2^n$ for an orthogonal record of the entire arm sequence, $\binom{2n}n$ for the integer winding register (sum of squared binomial coefficients), and $4^n/2$ for the parity register (the even and odd binomial sums each equal $2^{n-1}$). All three give $2/128$ on the first fork. After two forks their numerators are respectively **4, 6 and 8** over 16,384. For instance, left then right and right then left have the same net winding and interfere in the last two models. An unread cumulative record can therefore preserve interference that full history recording erases. These rates compare specified register dynamics under an assumed readout. They supply no derivation of that readout or of classical mechanics. For a related but different walk using memory carried by multiple coins, see [Brun, Carteret and Ambainis](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0210161).

### Earliest position measurement of register wraparound

Use the same balanced real coin, sharp R launch and unread winding register. Let $N\ge3$, $D=N/\gcd(N,2)$ and $s=2D/N$. For modulus $m$ put $h=m/\gcd(m,s)$, and set $h=\infty$ for the integer register. For terminal well measurements at a single clock, including arbitrary earlier whole-position filters, the **effective order $h$ completely classifies these registers**. If effective orders agree, every such rate agrees. If $h_1<h_2$, the first clock at which any rate can differ is $T=h_1D+1$. Free evolution followed by reading well $T\bmod N$ attains this bound. The rate for the smaller order minus that for the larger order is $2(-1)^{T-2}/2^T$ per launch.

Indeed, within one terminal port two paths have left counts differing by $kD$ and seam counts differing by $-ks$. Their register states coincide exactly when $h$ divides $k$ (for the integer register, when $k=0$). This fixes every surviving cross term, irrespective of masks. At tick $t>0$ the left counts at fixed final rail span at most $t-1$. Hence no new cross term occurs before $T$. At $T$ the only possible new pair on R has left counts $0,T-1$, realized uniquely by $R^T$ and $L^{T-1}R$. Their signs are $1,(-1)^{T-2}$. The only possible new pair on L has counts $1,T$, hence lands at $T-2\bmod N$, a different well. Summing rails at the stated detector therefore keeps the entire R difference. The launch itself has no path pair.

For example, on a triangle a parity register and a register of one state agree at **every** clock in this measurement family. Each has effective order one, since winding differences at a port are even. On a square their effective orders are two and one respectively, and the first difference occurs at tick three. This equivalence concerns observations on the walk; directly reading the register would be a different experiment. The comparison uses the coined walk on a cycle as in [Aharonov, Ambainis, Kempe and Vazirani](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0012090); the seam register and its onset bound are specified here. The replay updates register states on physical seam crossings and checks the complete unfiltered well distribution through $5D+1$ ticks on $N=3,\ldots,8$ for moduli one through five and the integer register.

### Can an arbitrarily large seam memory produce diffusion?

With an integer register the joint well and register are exactly a coordinate on the line. Write $y=x+Nw$ for $0\le x<N$. The identification $|x,w,r\rangle\mapsto |y,r\rangle$ (rail $r$) carries each physical step with its register update to a unit step at $y$. Whole-well masks lift to their periodic preimages. The same identification for a modulus $m$ gives an ordinary coherent walk on the $mN$-cycle; its base-well probability is the sum over the $m$ lifts. For the integer register this is the sum over the line lifts. Orthogonality of the memory states justifies this last summation under the assumed readout.

This gives a position-only test on a scale where a detector can see the walk go around the ring. Let the free walk run for $t$ ticks on rings with $N_t/t\to c$ for any fixed $0<c<\infty$, always from $(0,R)$. Read the physical well as a point $Z_t=\exp(2\pi i x/N_t)$ on the unit circle. For the integer memory,

$$Z_t\ \Longrightarrow\ \exp(2\pi i V/c),$$

where $V$ is the nondegenerate absolutely continuous velocity limit of the Hadamard walk on the line. Indeed the lifted law gives exactly $\exp(2\pi i X_t/N_t)$ with $X_t$ the line position, and the result follows from the line weak-limit theorem and continuity. The limit on the circle has no atoms: each point has at most countably many real preimages. This conclusion also holds for **any** sequence of register sizes $m_t\to\infty$. Eventually $m_tN_t>2t$, so projection to the covering cycle is injective on the entire reachable interval $[-t,t]$ before the final projection to physical wells. [Grimmett, Janson and Scudo, weak limits for quantum random walks](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0309135)

In contrast, retaining each step in a separate orthogonal record gives the fair line walk projected to the same physical ring: every word has squared weight $2^{-t}$. Its lifted second moment is $t$, so its displacement divided by $N_t$ converges in probability to zero and $Z_t$ converges to $1$. For example, the bounded position statistic $1-\operatorname{Re} Z_t$ has a strictly positive limiting mean for the winding memory, while the full-history mean is at most $2\pi^2 t/N_t^2$. The difference is observable without reading either register or reconstructing a lifted trajectory. Thus increasing the capacity of this particular memory cannot explain the transition to fair diffusion in this scaling. The argument concerns free motion, a sharp launch, a specified record coupling and the stipulated squared readout.
