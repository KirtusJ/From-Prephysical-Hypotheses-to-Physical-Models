# What the line hides and the ring reads with four arc gains

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Four constant arc gains give one observable relative gain at a sharp space-time port on the line. On a ring a second gain enters when different lifted paths meet. Position masks cannot prescribe an arbitrary multiset of these second labels once their quotient has order at least three. Nevertheless an eight-tick mask on seven wells distinguishes two norm-preserving characters that agree on every position product event on the line. All rates below are model predictions per original sharp launch. ## Exact word gains

Start at $(0,R)$ with gain 1. A right step sends a path to $(x+1,R)$, a left step to $(x-1,L)$. The two letters in an arc label are its incoming and outgoing rails. Give RR, RL, LR and LL respectively the spatially homogeneous gains $a,b,c,d$ in an abelian group. A position product event retains a prescribed set of whole wells at each tick; all paths reaching a retained well can continue through either rail. For a word of length $t$ write $l$ for the number of left steps, $m$ for the number of LL arcs, and $e=1$ if its final rail is L and 0 otherwise. Then

$$n_{LL}=m,\quad n_{RL}=l-m,\quad n_{LR}=l-m-e,\quad n_{RR}=t-2l+m+e.$$

Indeed every L arrival uses RL or LL, and the number of R-to-L transitions exceeds the L-to-R count by $e$. Multiplying the four arc factors gives the exact gain

$$G(w)=a^t(a/c)^e B^l H^m,\qquad B=bc/a^2,\quad H=ad/(bc). \tag{1}$$

On the line $l=(t-x)/2$ is fixed at the terminal site. Every relative gain at a given clock, site and rail therefore lies in $\langle H\rangle$. The same-end, same-clock words LRLR and LLRR differ by $H$, so this is the exact subgroup generated by such comparisons over all clocks. Words at one particular port need only attain a subset of it.

On a ring of $N$ wells let $D=N/\gcd(N,2)$. The terminal condition is $x=t-2l\bmod N$, hence the possible $l$ form one residue class modulo $D$. Choose any representative $l_0$. Label a lifted endpoint by its *winding index* $k=(l-l_0)/D$. A unit change in this index changes the lifted physical displacement by $-2D$; on an odd ring this is two laps. The relative gains lie in $\langle H,B^D\rangle$. Modulo $\langle H\rangle$ they lie in a single coset of the subgroup generated by $\Omega=[B^D]$, with normalized labels $\Omega^k$. A different reference $l_0$ translates every label by the same group element. The fork below attains relative gain $B^D H^{D-1}$, and the preceding four-tick comparison attains $H$. Thus both generators are realized among relative gains for $D\ge2$. This statement about a generated subgroup does not prescribe independent multiplicities in its elements.

## Winding support under whole-position masks

**Splicing theorem.** Fix a complete terminal port $(t,x,\mathrm{rail})$ on a ring and any position product event from the sharp source. The set of its attained integer winding indices is empty or a consecutive interval. If the extreme indices differ by at least two there are at least four distinct admitted histories at the port.

For a path $p$, its deficit $l_p(j)$ is its number of left steps through tick $j$. It starts at zero and increases by either zero or one. Suppose two admitted words $p,r$ at the chosen port end with deficits $l$ and $l+kD$, where $k\ge2$. Compare $l_p(j)+D$ with $l_r(j)$. Their difference starts at $D>0$, ends at $(1-k)D<0$ and decreases by at most one per tick. They meet at some $0<j<t$. The paths are then at the same physical well. Either prefix can be followed by either suffix because masks depend only on position and both outgoing directions exist on each incoming rail. Splicing may change the LL count at the join, which affects $H$ but preserves the winding argument. The unchanged terminal step of either suffix gives the specified rail.

The two new words have final deficits $l+D$ and $l+(k-1)D$. Both differ from the original extreme words. Even when $k=2$ makes the new deficits equal, the words are distinct. Their prefixes have left counts differing by $D$; the two suffixes also have counts differing by $(k-1)D$. Thus the two prefixes and two suffixes give four distinct combinations. Induction on $k$, using the two retained paths at deficits $l+D$ and $l+kD$, fills every intermediate index. [Lean check of the arc identity and the integer crossing lemma](lean/FourGainWinding.lean)

Two consequences apply even if the clock can be chosen anew for the request and all gains can be translated by a common factor.

* If the order of $\Omega$ is at least four (or infinite), two singleton labels whose quotient is $\Omega^2$ cannot be the entire port histogram. Any two lifts realizing this quotient are at least two indices apart. The splicing theorem forces further histories. Adjacent labels can occur alone, as the fork shows.
* If the order is at least three, three singleton labels in three distinct winding classes cannot be the entire histogram. Any three distinct classes require a lifted span of at least two, forcing a fourth history. This covers the order-three case in which every unequal pair of labels is adjacent on the quotient cycle. For orders one and two this argument leaves unrestricted preparation undecided. The previously proved single-gain case $a=b=c=1$ has $B=1$.

## A physically named positive comparison

In a $D+1$ tick block from $(0,R)$, retain the wells $+j,-j\bmod N$ at each $1\le j<D$, then retain $D$ and $D+1\bmod N$ on the last two ticks. In deficits the successive masks are $\{0,j\}\bmod D$ before tick $D$, followed twice by $\{0\}$. At the first tick the two directions are distinct for $D\ge2$. Before reunion a path at deficit 0 can continue only at 0 and a path at $j$ can continue only at $j+1$. Specifically, for $1\le j\le D-2$, the unintended deficits $1$ and $j$ miss $\{0,j+1\}$ modulo $D$. At reunion only the R step from 0 and the L step from $D-1$ reach 0 modulo $D$. The last mask keeps just their R daughters. This includes $D=2$, where there is no intermediate tick between splitting and reunion, and $D=3$, where there is one. Exactly $R^{D+1}$ and $L^D R$ end at the same oriented well. Repeating this block from its translated terminal well yields winding counts $\binom{j}{k}$ for $k=0,\ldots,j$ after $j$ blocks, since every block returns to R. On $N=1,2$ a position mask cannot separate directions and this two-path enumeration fails.

A unitary character $\chi$ assigns the phase $\chi(G(w))$ to each word. For the balanced local step the two columns, with rows indexed by outgoing R and L, are $(\chi(a),\chi(b))/\sqrt2$ and $(\chi(c),\chi(d))/\sqrt2$. They are orthogonal exactly when $\chi(H)=-1$. The usual squared terminal-port law after coherent position filtering then gives $2^{-t}|\sum_w\chi(G(w))|^2$ at each complete port. On the line, (1) makes every such squared law equal to $2^{-t}|\sum_w(-1)^m|^2$. The common phase may depend on the port. Summing the equalities over any selected terminal wells preserves them.

Take $N=D=7$ and let $z,g$ generate $C_2\times C_3$, of orders two and three respectively. Put $a=c=1$, $b=g$, $d=zg$. Thus $H=z$, $B=g$. Choose $\chi_0(z)=\chi_1(z)=-1$, $\chi_0(g)=1$, $\chi_1(g)=\exp(2\pi i/3)$. At $(1,R)$ after eight ticks the two fork words have gains $1$ and $g^7 z^6=g$. In a reference panel retaining only $j\bmod7$ at each tick, $R^8$ alone survives at the same detector. The squared-law and fair path-count probabilities per launch are

| Eight-tick records | $\chi_0$ | $\chi_1$ | Fair count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Two-arm fork, $(1,R)$ | $1/64$ | $1/256$ | $1/128$ |
| Singleton reference, $(1,R)$ | $1/256$ | $1/256$ | $1/256$ |
| Fork / reference | $4$ | $1$ | $2$ |

The comparison assumes a stable sharp launch, known relative exposures, coherent passage through each retained set, and terminal detection at the named rail. The original-launch denominator includes paths rejected by an earlier mask. Recording which arm passed at every tick would remove interference. Complex conjugation of all phases leaves these real probabilities unchanged, so this comparison distinguishes the trivial winding phase from the conjugate nontrivial pair. Exactly preparing *every* winding histogram is unnecessary for this measurement. A physical identification of the gain group or of either character remains open.

## Reproduction

Run `python -B check_four_gain_winding.py NEW_DIRECTORY` from this directory. The standard-library checker propagates individual arc gains, winding and actual LL counts through the position masks. It tests the two-path fork through four repetitions on nine rings; checks the winding theorem on all deletions of up to two time/well cells at the indicated depths on five rings; and retains the $N=2$ failed fork. The ring-seven example uses integer coefficients of $1,g,g^2$ and exact fractions. Its 15-tick splice witness names four distinct words at one port, including two with the same intermediate winding.
