# A position-dependent interaction from one shared sheet

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Director G (Codex), with numerical, formal and adversarial checks by three PCP participants in the same session.

Two walkers on a cyclic lattice can exchange momentum through a common sheet increment. In one prepared sector, their complete spinor approaches the solution of a two-particle Schrödinger equation with potential \(\kappa[1-\cos(Y_1-Y_2)]\). On a second scaling of the same construction, position records concentrate on two coupled Newton equations. The interaction's spatial shape can be computed from unweighted integer path counts on the relative cycle. The choice of the cycle, the count filter, a shared sheet, the sector and band preparations, the interaction scale and the observation law are physical premises still to be justified.

## The microscopic rule

The departure gain reads both simultaneous positions, at any separation, and changes one common sheet in a single tick. This is an instantaneous two-body operation. A finite-speed mediator, a local mechanism for collecting the two positions and a physical time calibration are open requirements for a causal explanation. A localized sheet also occupies many characters with different couplings. Neither limiting argument gives this input one effective coupling without sector preparation. The auxiliary count used to construct the profile, the cost of preparing a sector as the order increases and the weak finite four-branch agreement were also examined in Director Z's fifth cross-review. [Cross-review and independent controls](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/b9e425d/Director_Z_cross_review/CROSS_REVIEW_OF_DIRECTOR_G_ADDENDUM_5.md)

Each walker has a position \(x_j\in\mathbb Z/N\) and two directions. Take \(N\ge8\) divisible by four. In one tick, translate a single cyclic sheet by an integer \(g(x_1-x_2)\), apply the two signed Hadamard coins, and shift both walkers by their outgoing directions. An outgoing pair receives the product of the two single-walker coin signs. Each basis state has four outgoing edges and normalization \(1/2\) per tick. The sheet translation and conditional shifts are permutations; the tensor coin preserves the norm. The translation is coherent at a departure pair. Two independent walks with separately drawn classical random phases would implement a different process.

Choose the sign convention in which one specified character of the sheet gives \(\exp(-2\pi i g/M)\). Alternatively, at odd order \(M\), use a doubled translation in one of the real weak-field pair's two orientations, choosing the orientation with this same phase. This follows since \(2[(M+1)/2]=1\bmod M\). The opposite orientation conjugates the phase. Treating just one oriented sector as the input to the comparison is a premise. An initially localized sheet gives a mixture for sheet-unresolved observations; the proof below does not replace it by a chosen sector. Simultaneous space translation of the two walkers commutes exactly with every step. The finite carrier therefore conserves total lattice momentum modulo its reciprocal period. The [Lean calculation](lean/SharedSheet.lean) verifies finite character reduction for the joint gain and coordinate masks, the unnormalized coin identity, and integer rounding. Interacting discrete walks and wave-packet dispersion for single walks have prior mathematical and experimental literature. [Ahlbrecht et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1051) · [de Valcárcel, Roldán and Romanelli](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0976)

Use scaled positions \(Y_j=ax_j\bmod 2\pi\), spacing \(a=2\pi/N=\varepsilon\delta\), and step time \(\tau=\varepsilon\delta^2\), with \(0<\varepsilon\le1\). The desired departure phase angle is \(\delta^2 W(Y)\). One admissible integer rule rounds \(M\delta^2W/(2\pi)\) at each site. Its worst per-step angle error is \(\pi/M\). This route prescribes the shape in physical coordinates. The next route produces the shape from integer incidences.

### An integer construction of the interaction

On a cycle of \(N\) relative sites let \(B=2I+T+T^{-1}\), where \(T\) advances one site. \(C_m(d)=(B^m)_{d0}\) counts marked walks with two ways to stay and one way to move each way per step. The multiplicities two, one and one also occur in the differences of two single-walker displacements in units of two sites. On an even ring the actual synchronized pair preserves relative parity. Using this auxiliary count operator with unit adjacency on the full relative cycle, calibrating the maximum by the antipodal comparison, and coupling the resulting quantity to the shared sheet are choices in this model. Put
\[
 R_m(d)=\frac{2[C_m(0)-C_m(d)]}{C_m(0)-C_m(N/2)},\qquad
 m=N^2\lceil\log_2 N\rceil .
\]
It vanishes at coincidence and equals two at the antipode. The count is exact in integers. Taking differences removes the constant mode, which would dominate the raw count. The cycle Fourier eigenvalues are \(\lambda_l=4\cos^2(\pi l/N)\). In \(C_m(0)-C_m(d)\), the pair \(l=1,N-1\) contributes \(2\lambda_1^m(1-\cos(ad))/N\); every other nonconstant term is nonnegative and has size at most \(2\lambda_2^m/N\). At the antipode the leading contribution is \(4\lambda_1^m/N\). After multiplication of the ratio by two, both its numerator's tail and the leading shape times its denominator's tail, in these normalized units, lie in \([0,(N-3)(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)^m]\). Their difference thus has modulus at most the same upper endpoint. The normalized denominator is at least one. Consequently
\[
 \rho_N:=\max_d |R_m(d)-(1-\cos(ad))|
 \le (N-3)(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)^m.
\]
Indeed \(\log(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)=-2\int_{\pi/N}^{2\pi/N}\tan u\,du\le-3\pi^2/N^2\), so \(\rho_N\le N^{1-3\pi^2/\log 2}\) for this depth. Spectral analysis of cycles and their heat kernels supplies relevant external context. [Chinta, Jorgenson and Karlsson](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.2014)

Set \(L=\operatorname{round}(M\kappa\delta^2/(2\pi))\) and \(g(d)=\operatorname{round}(L R_m(d))\). Once \(L\) and the geometry are chosen, assigning \(g\) requires only integer arithmetic and comparisons, even for negative \(L\). With \(F(d)=1-\cos(ad)\) and \(|R_m|\le2+\rho_N\), the worst phase error against \(\delta^2\kappa F\) is at most
\[
       \theta_N \le (3+\rho_N)\pi/M + |\kappa|\delta^2\rho_N.
\]
No bound on the unrounded cosine derivatives of the integer staircase is needed. Its unitary difference is compared in operator norm at each step. Exact integers for \(C_m\) can have \(O(m)\) bits, and this implementation computes \(N\) counts per recurrence tick. The construction uses growing resources. The integer \(L\) still sets the strength and sign. A short count filter gives a different interaction, as the [retained shallow-depth controls](results/cycle_interaction.csv) show.

## A norm comparison on the torus

Write \(P_j=-i\varepsilon\partial_{Y_j}\), \(K=(P_1^2+P_2^2)/2\). For now \(W\) may be any fixed real trigonometric polynomial on the two-dimensional torus. The preceding relative potential is one example. Denote the scalar solution by
\[
       i\varepsilon\partial_t f=(K+W)f,\qquad f(0)=f_0 .
\]
Normalize \(f_0\) in torus \(L^2\), and assume \(\sum_{|\alpha|\le4}\|P^\alpha f_0\|\le C\) independently of \(\varepsilon\). Fixed-action \(H^4\) data and localized semiclassical packets with bounded centre momentum qualify. Periodic boundary conditions are imposed in both descriptions.

**Encoding.** Use the band at \(k_*=-\pi/2\) in each tensor factor, with dispersion \(\omega(k)=\arcsin(\sin k/\sqrt2)\). A continuum Fourier index \(\ell\in\mathbb Z^2\) is encoded in the ring modes at discrete indices \((\ell_j-N/4)\bmod N\). For \(|q_j|<1,\ q_j=a\ell_j\), use smooth unit band sections \(u(k_*+q_j)\). Multiply the scalar Fourier coefficient by their tensor product and a smooth cutoff \(\chi(q)\in[0,1]\), equal to one for \(\max|q_j|\le1/2\), supported in \(\max|q_j|<1\). Set this multiplier \(b(q)\) to zero outside the chart. Call the map \(E\). At most one retained \(\ell\) maps to a given pair of lattice modes. Thus \(E\) is a contraction and \(\|f\|^2-\|Ef\|^2\le C\delta^8\sum_j\|P_j^4 f\|^2\). An initial exact spinor within norm \(e_0\) of \(Ef_0\) is allowed, including normalizing that encoding. A prepared isolated band in each walker is assumed here. Obtaining it from localized inputs on the interacting graph is still open.

**Claim.** Let \(U\) be the native spinor step in the selected sheet, \(n\tau=t\le T\), with \(a\max_{\ell:\widehat W_\ell\ne0}|\ell|\) sufficiently small. For constants depending on \(T,W,\chi,C\) and the sections, uniformly in \(\varepsilon,\delta\le1\),
\[
 \left\|e^{2in\omega(k_*)}U^n\psi_0-E f(t)\right\|
 \ \le\ e_0+C_T\!\left(\delta+\frac{\delta^2}{\varepsilon}+\delta^2\right)
          + n\theta_N .                                      \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\theta_N=\sup_x|\hbox{actual phase angle}-\delta^2 W(ax)|\) may be replaced by a bound on the distance modulo \(2\pi\). All norms in (1) are spinor norms over both lattice positions and coins; a scalar demodulation fixes the phase for the comparison. The earlier [linear-force proof](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md) transported momentum along one characteristic. Here multiplication by the potential couples different momenta.

**Proof.**

1. *Regularity at bounded times.* For \(P^\alpha\), the commutator \([P^\alpha,W]/\varepsilon\) is a finite sum of derivatives of \(W\) times \(P^\beta,\ |\beta|<|\alpha|\), with coefficients bounded for \(\varepsilon\le1\). The free part commutes with these powers. Duhamel's formula and induction give a uniform \(H^4\) bound in the scaled momenta on \([0,T]\); the potential-only evolution also preserves it on bounded intervals. Smooth Fourier approximations justify these identities first on a core and then by norm continuity.
2. *The sampled potential.* Writing \(W=\sum_l w_l e^{il\cdot Y}\), a harmonic shifts the continuum Fourier coefficient by \(l\) and the ring frequency by the same index modulo \(N\). The difference between applying the shift before and after encoding has multiplier \(b(a(\ell+l))-b(a\ell)\), of norm at most \(C a|l|\). For each fixed \(l\) with \(a|l|\ll1\), frequencies in the union of the two compact supports occupy less than one reciprocal period in each coordinate. Hence this difference has no multiple input aliases on a single output and the multiplier bound gives an operator-norm bound. Thus \(\|W_{\rm lat}E-EW\|\le Ca\sum_l |w_l||l|\). Duhamel for the two exact potential exponentials gives error \(C(\tau/\varepsilon)a\) per step, uniformly on the unit ball. This argument also accounts for the infinitely many harmonics of the exponentiated potential. Replacing this sampled phase by the integer phase costs at most \(\theta_N\).
3. *The free coin.* On the encoded section the free step acts diagonally, with the sum of the two band phases. The edge estimate is \(|\omega(k_*+q)-\omega(k_*)-q^2/2|\le |q|^4/6\) on \(|q|\le1\). After removal of the constant carrier, the per-step difference from the encoded free Schrödinger phase is bounded on any scaled \(H^4\) vector \(v\) by \(C\delta^4\sum_j\|P_j^4v\|\). Use the preceding regularity estimate on \(v=e^{-i\tau W/\varepsilon}f(t_j)\).
4. *Time splitting.* The continuous product in departure order is \(e^{-i\tau K/\varepsilon}e^{-i\tau W/\varepsilon}\). Since \([K,W]=-\varepsilon^2\Delta W/2-i\varepsilon\sum_j(\partial_j W)P_j\), its norm on a scaled \(H^1\) vector is at most \(C\varepsilon\|v\|_{H^1_\varepsilon}\). Differentiating the product at an intermediate time and integrating the defect against the exact unitary yields a double integral of conjugates of \([K,W]/\varepsilon^2\). The free factors preserve the \(P_j\) norms and the potential factors preserve them with a bounded-time constant, giving local error \(C_T\tau^2/\varepsilon\). This use of Lie splitting is about state norm and keeps the dependence on the action parameter. [Bao, Jin and Markowich](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021999101969566)
5. Telescope these local errors against the exact solution at each step. Unitarity bounds propagation of a prior discrepancy. The three accumulated analytic terms are \(O_T(a/\varepsilon)\), \(O_T(\delta^4/\tau)\) and \(O_T(\tau/\varepsilon)\), respectively. Substituting the scales gives (1). The loss outside the chart is controlled once in choosing the encoded state and in decoding bounded readouts. It is not charged as a fresh truncation of the evolving lattice state at every tick.

## The two limits and their observables

For \(\varepsilon=1\), \(N\to\infty\) gives a coupled Schrödinger equation at fixed action. For \(\varepsilon=\delta=\sqrt{2\pi/N}\to0\), the same estimate follows the state at the action scale of an individual localized packet. Choose odd \(M\) with \(M\tau\to\infty\), for example \(N^3+1\) on either sequence. Then \(n\theta_N\to0\), also for the integer count construction since \(\rho_N/\varepsilon\to0\). Holding \(M\) too small can remove the force altogether. Also \(\delta^2/\varepsilon\to0\) matters even in the free walk: a fixed scaled nonzero momentum typically accumulates an order-one quartic phase if \(\varepsilon\) is only of order \(\delta^2\). Equality of all amplitude phases cannot be inferred at that boundary.

**Newton equations.** Let \(\dot q_j=p_j,\ \dot p_j=-\partial_jW(q)\), with real lifted velocities and positions taken modulo \(2\pi\). With \(W=\kappa(1-\cos(Y_1-Y_2))\),
\[
   \dot p_1=-\kappa\sin(q_1-q_2),\qquad
   \dot p_2=+\kappa\sin(q_1-q_2).
\]
The centre velocity is constant, and the relative angle obeys \(\ddot r=-2\kappa\sin r\). For a single-packet conclusion take a family with initial deviations \(O(\sqrt\varepsilon)\) in \(P_j-p_j(0)\) and \(Z_j-e^{iq_j(0)}\), where \(Z_j=e^{iY_j}\). Smooth periodized packets of width \(\sqrt\varepsilon\) satisfy these conditions and the uniform fourth-moment bound. For example, translate a compact smooth normalized bump of that width to \(q(0)\) and multiply it on its support by the local plane-wave phase of momentum \(p(0)\).

For the continuum evolution, commute these deviations through the Hamiltonian and use Duhamel in norm. For \(P_j-p_j(t)\), the source has norm bounded by \(\|(\partial_j W(Y)-\partial_j W(q))f(t)\|\), at most \(C\sum_k\|(Z_k-e^{iq_k})f(t)\|\) by smooth periodicity. For \(Z_j-e^{iq_j(t)}\), \([K,Z_j]=Z_j(\varepsilon P_j+\varepsilon^2/2)\) gives a source bounded by \(\|(P_j-p_j)f\|+|p_j|\|(Z_j-e^{iq_j})f\|+\varepsilon/2\). Grönwall's inequality bounds the sum of all four deviations by \(C_T\sqrt\varepsilon\). The torus chord coordinates avoid discontinuities at a chosen angle cut. Semiclassical coherent-state localization by Hamiltonian flow has a long independent history. [Hepp](https://denebola.if.usp.br/~jbarata/leituras-recomendadas/Hepp-ClassicalLimitQM.pdf)

The same one-index shift estimate used for \(W\) gives \(\|Z_{j,\mathrm{lat}}E-E Z_j\|\le Ca\). Transfer through (1) using these *bounded* coordinate multipliers. The position record at each attained time therefore concentrates on \((q_1(t),q_2(t))\) in the product torus. Uniformly bounded times can be used, including after crossing the angle cut. For a finite list of coarse position readings with the predicted positions a fixed distance inside their bins, each wrong-bin amplitude tends to zero on the unmeasured flight. The [sequential projection argument](Sequential_position_records.md) then gives simultaneous success probability tending to one under the specified coherent-bin instrument. No physical units, measurement clock or interaction calibration follows from this comparison.

**A quantum signature of the interaction.** In the fixed-action scalar equation take the product of the two normalized uniform modes. At short time \(t\), the coefficient matrix in product Fourier indices has \(a_{00}=1-i\kappa t+O(t^2)\), \(a_{1,-1}=i\kappa t/2+O(t^2)\), and \(a_{0,-1}=a_{1,0}=0\) by exactly conserved total momentum. The determinant on rows \(0,1\) and columns \(0,-1\) is nonzero for small nonzero \(t\) when \(\kappa\ne0\). Thus the fixed-action model entangles the two walkers, and (1) transfers that minor to sufficiently fine walks in the selected shared sector: test against the local encoded Fourier-spinor factors, for which the cutoff equals one. Norm closeness controls each measured coefficient in the minor. This is a state property conditional on the product preparation and sector model. It does not provide an experimental entanglement witness using only the native position records. The contact interaction studied in earlier work is singular in this scaling and is outside this smooth-potential proof.

## Starting the interacting flight from local positions

There is a classical-track experiment that requires no externally prepared base band or absolute sheet orientation. It requires more specific instruments. Start from definite positions and directions on a shared localized sheet. Allow a preparation stage with the linear sheet gain of the [three-step filter](Records_and_complex_structure.md) acting on one walker while its partner waits. One can choose \(M\) odd and coprime to \(N\), e.g. \(N^3+1\), so the departure index \(x\) on a ring does not define a homomorphism from \(\mathbb Z/N\) to \(\mathbb Z/M\). In that case use the filter at one fixed interior centre of the ring, with the three-step diamond and both its paths away from the coordinate cut. After each site/direction read, route the walker back to that centre by reading individual steps until it arrives. Every such step gives a known sheet translation and a conditionally fair direction, as in the [fixed-site routing proof](Fixed_transport_and_record_weights.md). For \(N\) sufficiently large all six block outcomes are distinct. Single-step routing cannot change the sheet-sector posterior. Repeating at that centre gives exactly the same class likelihoods at every block under the original signed linear gain. Thus at each finite \(M\) a positive-probability finite preparation can make residual sheet mass arbitrarily small. This stage requires switching the gain for flight and preparation, a supplied controller and, for the idle partner, independent timing. The graph for the interacting flight itself is time independent.

Route both walkers to the desired separate starting positions with known incoming directions. Turn off sheet translation for \(m\) successive two-step direction filters with even \(m\) nearest \(\delta^{-5/2}\) on the \(\varepsilon=\delta\) sequence. Read only the direction of each walker, coherently across sites, retaining a switch on every block. The filter factorizes on the walkers, acts as identity on the sheet and leaves its posterior unchanged. Each unnormalized scalar Fourier profile, up to a constant phase and the initial site phase, is \(N^{-1/2}\sin^m k\) sampled on the ring. Both walkers end in known basis directions. The squared one-particle norm is the discrete average of \(\sin^{2m}k\); the two-particle success probability is its square. The cyclic grid has spacing \(a\) and contains both peaks \(\pm\pi/2\). The integral mean of \(\sin^{2m}k\) is \(\binom{2m}{m}/4^m\); the sum differs by at most a constant times \(a\), by bounded total variation. Thus the relative error is \(O(a\sqrt m)=O(\delta^{3/4})\), and the two-particle yield conditional on the sheet stage is \(\sim1/(\pi m)\).

The normalized folded packet has two momentum edges per walker. Each basis coin splits equally into both bands at either edge in the limit. Here are estimates needed to use (1) with this preparation on a ring. If \(q\) is angular distance to the nearest edge, \(|\sin k|^{2m}=|\cos q|^{2m}\le e^{-m q^2}\). Summing the Gaussian on the grid in shells of width \(m^{-1/2}\) bounds its discrete \(|q|^j\) moment after normalization by \(C_j m^{-j/2}\); \(a\sqrt m\to0\) gives many grid points per shell. In particular the scaled momentum RMS is \(O(1/(\delta\sqrt m))=O(\delta^{1/4})\). A smooth cutoff in \(|q|/\delta\) at two, equal to one up to one, discards \(o(1)\) norm and gives all needed uniform scaled fourth-moment bounds. Position on the torus is controlled by its chord multiplier. In Fourier coefficients this is a one-cell shift. For the sampled scalar profile the norm of its difference from a shift about the initial centre is \(O(a\sqrt m)\): on each grid interval Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the squared increment by \(a\int |(\sin^m k)'|^2\,dk\), and the integral derivative norm squared divided by the integral profile norm squared is \(m^2/(2m-1)\). Differentiating the scaled cutoff costs \(O(a/\delta)=O(\varepsilon)\) in this shift; differentiating the local eigenvector costs \(O(a)\). Translation by a recorded initial site simply re-centres the chord. Hence each chart's scalar envelope starts with vanishing position and momentum deviations. There is no order-one assumption of a prepared zero-momentum band hidden in this step.

Tensor the four charts per walker (two edges, two bands). Each of the sixteen has squared weight tending to \(1/16\); their supports or band spinors are initially orthogonal. Group by curvature signs \(b=(b_1,b_2)\). Each group has limiting mass \(1/4\). Relative to its edge and branch, each chart obeys (1) with kinetic energy \(\sum b_jP_j^2/2\), its own removed carrier phase and, in the conjugate sheet sector, with opposite \(W\). This follows by taking the complementary eigenprojector and/or shifting \(k\) by \(\pi\) in the free expansion, leaving all commutator bounds unchanged in modulus. The chord argument applies with \(\dot q_j=b_jp_j\); with the folded preparation it bounds deviations by \(C_T(\delta^{1/4}+\varepsilon)\) before adding the comparison errors. The interacting trajectory in one chosen sector starts at the recorded \(q^0\) with both \(p_j(0)=0\).

For \(b_1=b_2=b\), the relative angle solves \(\ddot r=-2b\kappa\sin r\) and the mean position is constant on the covering line. For \(b_2=-b_1\), \(r(t)=r(0)\) and both positions have constant acceleration \(-b_1\kappa\sin r(0)\). These statements project to the torus. With labelled starting positions satisfying \(\kappa\sin r(0)\ne0\), the four spatial branches have four distinct acceleration vectors at zero, hence are separated at some common small positive time. At such a time take disjoint coarse bins containing the four positions, and keep the complementary outcome. For any chart the wrong bins have vanishing norm. The norm of the whole curvature group in its correct bin approaches its initial norm, because the *exactly evolved* sum preserves the initial orthogonality. The other groups have vanishing norm in that bin by the tail estimate. Thus each first bin has limiting mass \(1/4\), with total mass tending to one over the four bins. Subsequent coherent position bins with fixed boundary margins follow its predicted branch at any fixed finite number of later times by sequential projection. In the conjugate sector \(W\mapsto-W,\ b\mapsto-b,\ p\mapsto-p\) gives the same labelled position curves and weights. Recording only the relative separation loses the distinction between the two opposing-curvature motions. If the walkers begin at coincidence with zero momentum all four classical solutions can remain at rest. Both the finite torus and these measurement distinctions matter to the operational result.

## Numerical and formal evidence

The [two-particle checker](check_periodic_force.py) uses direct position-space coin updates, with the sampled potential rounded to sheet translations in the specified character. Its scalar reference uses separate refinements of time and Fourier grid. The finite implementation encodes with a globally smooth plus-band section and reports tails outside the represented Fourier square. The proof uses a cutoff; the controlled scalar tail makes the two encodings asymptotically equivalent for the stated data. Over 36 runs at \(N=32,48,64\), both force signs, free and coarse-sheet controls were retained. At \(N=64,\ \kappa=1\), the norm error against the band-encoded reference was 0.00697 at fixed action and 0.03582 at shrinking action. In the latter case the relative-coordinate statistic was 0.80963 in the walk, 0.80733 in the scalar reference and 0.69736 on the classical trajectory. Leakage at fixed action was nonmonotone across these sizes. These comparisons report observed residuals. The analytic argument supplies the asymptotic statement. [All dynamics trials](results/periodic_force_trials.csv) · [Reference refinements and controls](results/periodic_force.json)

The [folded-pair checker](check_folded_pair.py) uses two localized starts, two basis coins and count-built phases at finite depth ceil(0.4 N²). That depth tests finite flights; the uniform asymptotic shape estimate above uses the larger logarithmic factor. At N = 96 its joint preparation probability is 0.0105223. At attained time 1.507 the largest of five joint position-characteristic discrepancies from the four-trajectory mixture is 0.10649; at 0.7535 it is 0.30566. None of the twelve positive-sector measurement times met the descriptive threshold of 0.1 on all five observables. Both conjugate sectors, aliased ring preparations, and the independent four-spinor Fourier control are retained. [Folded preparation and all flight data](results/folded_pair.json)

The [integer count checker](check_cycle_interaction.py) propagates raw cycle counts with two marked stays, compares the normalized gaps with the continuum shape at 160-decimal-digit precision, and runs twelve spinor flights with the computed signed integer gains. For \(N=24,\ M=40001,\ \kappa=1\), the state distance from the unrounded cosine walk was 0.48353 at count depth one and 0.000439 at depth 2880. Both signs and all shallow-depth trials are retained.
