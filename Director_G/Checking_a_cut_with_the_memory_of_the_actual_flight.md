# Testing a position detector with the memory left by the actual flight

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A position detector can behave ideally every time it starts fresh and give the wrong classical trajectory when used twice on the same walker. The order of the calibration resolves this problem for a fixed schedule of cuts. Run the actual flight through the preceding reads. At the next read, privately exchange the walker for an independently prepared probe, leaving the detector and its history intact. Three forward ticks of probe preparation and readout suffice to test the action of that detector even on the actual walker, which could have been entangled with its memory. Exact success at each clock certifies the visible history law for the specified flight. No upper bound on the finite memory dimension or lower bound on the probabilities of rare memory states is needed. The measurements below assume ordinary quantum probabilities. [Forward-cut tests and reflecting step](Certifying_a_position_read_with_three_forward_ticks.md) · [Reflected classical history](Reflected_position_histories.md)

## At which clock to change the input

Fix the source, \(L\ge4\) wells, positive reflecting wall signs, all cut locations and the finite sequence of read clocks. The controls are fixed independently of the displayed words. Between reads only the stipulated free walk acts on the walker; separate private evolution of the apparatus can be included in its next operation. Let \(S\) be the incoming \(d=2L\) directed modes and \(M\) all private resources that can affect this and later reads. Copies of already displayed words are kept for the final comparison. Include any input of those words to the apparatus in \(M\). Reproduce the *ensemble* of initial science preparations in all arms. An unknown initial memory and correlations accumulated during earlier reads are allowed.

For each intended clock \(j\), start a separate ensemble of flights, running the actual science procedure unchanged up to the instant before that read. Independently choose between continuing science and exchanging the incoming walker with a probe already prepared off the interaction wires. Hide the displaced walker from the detector. Trace preservation on that subsystem leaves the actual memory marginal \(\sigma_j\) intact. Bring in a probe independent of the entire prefix. Its joint state with the memory is its prepared state times \(\sigma_j\). A physical implementation needs the switch, identical timing and control signals at the detector, and an independent terminal read. If the prefix has a miss or failure, keep it in the incident ledger. The theorem assumes normalized live walk inputs at the tested clocks. An absorbing loss must be represented explicitly as a terminal history or included in an enlarged tested model.

Prepare each sharp mode \(i\), and each \(U^k|x,R\rangle\), \(k=1,2\), for the ordinary reflecting step \(U\). Insert the exchanged probe at the original clock. For a sharp input, observe the correct bin and output mode immediately. For the other inputs complete \(3-k\) forward ticks after the read and count the joint event consisting of the appropriate bin and the directed dark port of the three-tick test. Only pairs of interfering modes on the same side give a required zero. The preparation ticks take place on the probe before exchange. The memory sees the same absolute read time. After the read the detector must have no further interaction with the probe during its checked free propagation. There are at most \(4L\) settings per clock. The analysis uses the entire prefix ensemble at each \(j\) and does not divide by an individual history probability. Intervention by replacing the measured system to study environmental memory is known as a causal break. Here it is used to test a specified instrument on a particular trajectory schedule. [Pollock et al., operational interventions on processes with memory](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.040405)

## Exact transfer to the correlated walker

Write the detector at clock \(j\) as an isometry \(W:S\otimes M\to S_{\rm out}\otimes E\), with orthogonal sectors of \(E\) for the reported classical labels. Extra private outputs may be retained in \(E\); the independent external record makes distinct labels classical. Drop the clock index. Given a purification \(\Omega\) of \(\sigma\) on \(M\otimes R\), the probe sees a fixed isometry \(i\mapsto (W\otimes I_R)(i\otimes\Omega)\). Exact sharp checks force this vector to lie in the single correct joint output cell. The two opposite paths to the three-tick dark output then force equality of the tags for each same-bin comparison. These comparisons connect both rails at every site, sites two apart in each bin, and the two parity chains through a reflecting endpoint in each bin. Thus there is one unit tag \(\eta_b\in E_b\otimes R\) for each side \(b\).

Take a Schmidt expansion \(\Omega=\sum_\alpha\sqrt{\lambda_\alpha}\,|\alpha\rangle|\alpha\rangle_R\) on the support of \(\sigma\). In every sharp column the same tag equality holds coefficient by coefficient in \(|\alpha\rangle_R\). Consequently, for each \(b\) there is a linear map \(B_b\) on that support such that
\[
 W(|i\rangle|v\rangle)=|i\rangle B_{b(i)}|v\rangle
 \quad\text{for all }v\in\operatorname{supp}\sigma.
\]
Each \(B_b\) is an isometry because \(W\) is one and any one mode in the bin can be held fixed. The private output may be entirely different in the two bins and may retain the old memory. The formula applies to the actual correlated input because its memory marginal is \(\sigma\), and positivity gives it zero support outside \(S\otimes\operatorname{supp}\sigma\). After recording \(b\) and tracing the private output, the action on the walker and any untouched reference is exactly projection onto the bin, with the input memory traced out. This argument uses no inversion of an estimated density matrix.

Apply this at every clock on its actual prefix. The marginal state of the visible walker and old displayed words follows the ideal update at that step. The ideal free walk carries this to the next clock. Thus all final visible joint word and walker probabilities are ideal. There need be no single memory state common to different clocks. Testing an ensemble selected on a different prefix, or assuming an untested preparation of the same memory, would not justify this argument.

## An error bound without resolving the private memory

Let \(a_i\) be the joint correct-label and correct-mode probability on the sharp replacement probe. Put \(A_i=\langle i|\Pi_{b(i)} W|i\rangle\), the surviving map from \(M\) to the correct environment sector. It is a contraction and \(a_i=\operatorname{tr}\sigma A_i^\dagger A_i\). Enlarge each outcome sector by a common private copy of \(M\), extend the original \(W\) by zero on those extra output coordinates and set
\[
 B_i v=A_i v\ \oplus\ (I-A_i^\dagger A_i)^{1/2}v,\qquad
 r_i=\sqrt{2(1-a_i)}.
\]
The \(B_i\) are isometries, including when \(a_i=0\). For a map \(X\) define \(\|X\|_\sigma^2=\operatorname{tr}\sigma X^\dagger X\). Let \(W_i v=W(|i\rangle v)\) and \(W_0(|i\rangle v)=|i\rangle B_i v\). Orthogonal projection onto the correct cell and the orthogonality of the added private output give \(\|W_i-|i\rangle B_i\|_\sigma=r_i\).

For a short probe \(u=U^k|x,R\rangle\), the norm of the difference between its actual and \(W_0\) outputs on the purified memory is bounded by \(\eta_u=\sum_i|u_i|r_i\). Projecting onto the joint dark event after the remaining walk ticks is a contraction. For the comparison isometry its returning vector on a tested edge \(i,l\) is \(\pm ((B_i-B_l)\otimes I_R)\Omega/\sqrt8\). If the observed joint dark rate is \(h\), the edge has
\[
 \|B_i-B_l\|_\sigma\le\min\{2,\sqrt8(\sqrt h+\eta_u)\}.
\]
Use the smallest available length per edge. For each bin pick a root and let \(D_i\) be the shortest-path distance to its root. Choosing the root that minimizes the sum of squared resulting column bounds in that bin gives the best bound within this prescription. Define \(W_* (|i\rangle v)=|i\rangle B_{\mathrm{root}(b(i))}v\). This is a bin-preserving isometry with a branch-dependent action on private memory. Write \(T=W-W_*\). Its \(i\)th column satisfies \(\|T_i\|_\sigma\le r_i+D_i\).

For the actual state \(\rho_{SM}\) at this clock, pinching in the \(d\) input modes gives
\[
 \rho_{SM}\ \le\ d\sum_i |i\rangle\langle i|\otimes \rho_{ii}
 \ \le\ d\,I_S\otimes\sigma .
\]
The first inequality follows by applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the sum of \(d\) vectors obtained by acting with \(\sqrt\rho\) on the individual components of a test vector. The second uses \(\sigma=\sum_i\rho_{ii}\). Thus \(\operatorname{tr}T\rho_{SM}T^\dagger\le d\sum_i(r_i+D_i)^2\). Purifying the actual input and taking the pure-state vector distance, then reading the classical label and discarding the memory, bounds the trace distance of the joint actual visible state and old records from the ideal update of their actual input marginal by
\[
 \epsilon_j=\min\left(1,\sqrt{d\sum_i(r_i+D_i)^2}\right).
\]
The argument holds when the untouched reference includes earlier words or an entangled spectator. The two terms compared use the *actual* input at this step. Separately, applying the ideal update contracts its input distance to the ideal history. Induction therefore gives final error at most \(\min(1,\sum_j\epsilon_j)\) in total variation for the displayed word, or in trace distance for the visible word and walker together. Use simultaneous lower bounds on sharp pass rates and upper bounds on dark rates to propagate sampling uncertainty. Calibration and source errors also enter any application. To transfer the reflected classical path limit, the sum must tend to zero at the compared system sizes. No physical probe data enter this result.

## What fails on a fresh detector

Use four wells, launch at \((1,R)\), cut after well 1, and read at ticks 2, 4 and 8. Set a private bit to the first reported label, with label 1 for wells 2 and 3. At tick 4 apply a minus sign on \((2,L)\) if the private bit is one and otherwise apply the ideal cut. The sign commutes with the cut itself. With an initially blank bit the detector read once in isolation passes all the stationary sharp and dark tests. On the actual prefix it is armed with probability \(1/4\). The word counts for \(110,111\) change from \(28,20\) to \(12,36\) in 256 launches' worth of probability mass; the other six masses stay the same. Total variation is \(1/16\).

Send the tick-two rightward probe from well 2 through the read at the actual tick 4 and finish its remaining forward tick. The two paths at the dark port test the two rails at well 2. When armed, the sign makes the joint dark probability \(1/2\); the unconditional actual-prefix rate is \(1/8\). The sharp checks still pass. A physical switch that secretly resets the detector bit precisely on the probe trials would give a dark rate of zero and leave the science discrepancy intact. Isolation of the late choice from the apparatus matters. If an experiment only calibrates the memory after measuring a prefix and selecting the left bin, this example also passes in that selected ensemble.

Correlations can amplify small product-probe errors. Inside a single bin take \(n\) orthogonal input modes and \(n\) private memory modes, with marginal \(I/n\). Trigger a wrong output label on the projector onto \(\Phi=n^{-1/2}\sum_i|i,i\rangle\). On any independent pure input and the same memory marginal the wrong-label rate is \(1/n^2\). For the actual joint input \(\Phi\) it is one. This is a general device example; its joint input is stipulated. It shows why transferring a small event rate from an independent probe to correlated science needs a dimension-sensitive estimate. In the exact zero case neither this amplification nor a rare memory sector evades the support argument.
