# A linear potential from real rail rotations every four ticks

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Consider a specific extension of the signed two-rail Hadamard walk. Before each group of four ordinary ticks, rotate the real pair of rail amplitudes at each site through a programmed angle. There is no motion during the plate. On the decoded complex field this rotation is exact multiplication by a phase. A linear spatial variation produces a constant force in the fixed-action Schrödinger limit. The four-tick timing makes the free approximation accurate enough to survive repetition with an intervening potential. The result is conditional on the plate, the source and the readout.

**Carrier and convention.** Write \(U(k)=\mathrm{diag}(e^{-ik},e^{ik})H\), where \(H(r,l)=(r+l,r-l)/\sqrt2\). In position space this is the real normalized signed walk. On a single occupied site parity decode \(f_t(x)=i^x e^{-i\pi t/4}(R_t(x)+iL_t(x))\). Squared site mass is preserved exactly. Time \(t\) in this formula counts Hadamard ticks. Every four ticks the occupied parity returns, and the demodulation factor acquires a real minus sign. The Fourier coordinate \(q\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2)\) uses the physical sites of that parity and measure \(dq/\pi\). The replica at \(q+\pi\) differs by the fixed parity sign at block boundaries. [Free encoding, tail identity and conventions](Folded_field_and_native_gaussian.md)

**Free four-tick bound.** Let \(k=-\pi/2+q\), \(\omega=\arcsin(\sin k/\sqrt2)\), \(d(q)=\omega+\pi/4\), \(\lambda_+=e^{-i\omega}\), \(\lambda_-=-e^{i\omega}\), with spectral projections \(P_\pm\). The row \(\ell=(1,i)\) annihilates \(P_-(k_*)\) at \(k_*=-\pi/2\). Here \(P_+=(I+\boldsymbol n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/2\) and \(\boldsymbol n=(\cos k,\sin k,\cos k)/\sqrt{1+\cos^2 k}\), so \(\|P_-'\|\le1\) and \(\|\ell P_-\|\le\sqrt2|q|\). Since \(d'(q)=\sin q/\sqrt{1+\sin^2 q}\), \(|d(q)|\le q^2/2\) and \(|d(q)-q^2/2|\le |q|^4/6\) for \(|q|\le1\). The demodulated fourth eigenpowers are \(e^{-4id(q)}\) and \(e^{4id(q)}\). Their difference is bounded by \(8|d(q)|\le4q^2\). Multiplying this by the \(O(|q|)\) row bound yields the extra power of bandwidth.

Denote the exact decoded four-tick map on the parity sublattice by \(B\) and the Fourier multiplier \(e^{-2iq^2}\) by \(S\). The decoded map is a real-linear isometry independent of the block index, since demodulation at consecutive block boundaries differs by a real sign. For any scalar source \(z\) supported on \(|q|\le Q\le1\),

\[
 \|(B-S)z\|_2\le D(Q)\|z\|_2,\qquad
 D(Q)=4\sqrt2 Q^3+\tfrac23 Q^4 .
\]

Indeed, row projection gives the cubic term on the input spinor, and the positive-branch phase difference gives the quartic term on the scalar. Reality and parity identify the spinor and scalar norms on every **symmetric** folded region by pairing \(q\) with \(-q\). Thus bounded scalar support suffices even for a directional complex source. A high-frequency scalar tail can instead be removed from the initial datum in norm and restored at a cost of at most twice its norm in comparing two isometries.

**Force and accuracy.** Let \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), \(Y=\varepsilon\delta x\), \(p=q/\delta\). One block has duration \(\Delta=4\varepsilon\delta^2\). Rotate the rails before that block by

\[
 O(-hx)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(hx)&\sin(hx)\\-\sin(hx)&\cos(hx)\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad h=F\delta\Delta=4F\varepsilon\delta^3.
\]

The decoded plate \(K\) multiplies by \(e^{-ihx}\), so momentum moves from \(q\) to \(q-h\) on the chosen Fourier convention. This corresponds to \(V(Y)=FY\) and force \(-F\). Work on the line first. Take a normalized initial scalar supported on \(|p|\le P\), an integer \(M\) of blocks, and an attained time \(T=M\Delta\). Set \(L=P+|F|T\) and require \(Q=\delta L\le1\), which keeps ideal translated support away from the folded seam. On that support, after the \(j\)-th plate and before its free stage the momentum is \(p_j=p-jF\Delta\).

The exact discrete evolution is \((BK)^M\). Compare it to \((SK)^M\) by replacing each free step on the corresponding *ideal* intermediate input. Telescoping uses real-linearity and norm preservation, so any frequency leakage in the exact evolution costs at most \(M D(Q)\) in total. No compact-support assumption is made on the actual evolved spinor. The split scalar phase is the right-endpoint sum \(-\varepsilon^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^M\Delta p_j^2/2\). The continuum solution at momentum \(p-FT\) has phase

\[
 -\varepsilon^{-1}\int_0^T (p-Fs)^2/2\,ds
 =-\varepsilon^{-1}(Tp^2/2-FT^2p/2+F^2T^3/6).
\]

The integrand has derivative bounded by \(|F|L\). Consequently the norm error to the exact free-particle Schrödinger evolution **with the linear potential** \(i\varepsilon\partial_Tg=-\varepsilon^2\partial_Y^2g/2+FYg\), in the rescaled momentum chart, satisfies

\[
 E\le MD(Q)+\frac{T\Delta}{2\varepsilon}|F|L .
\]

At fixed \(\varepsilon,F,P\) and bounded time this tends to zero with \(\delta\). For shrinking \(\varepsilon\) the same estimate suffices if \(\delta/\varepsilon\to0\). With the localized semiclassical packets specified in the earlier position-limit proof, exact continuum terminal positions concentrate at \(Y_0+p_0T-FT^2/2\). The following preparation realizes the same terminal law directly from a sharp launch. [Continuum trajectory and bounded-observable argument](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md)

**Integer implementation and its price.** A real rotation primitive can normalize the integer columns of \(G=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&-4\\4&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) by 5, or use \(G^T\) for the reverse setting. A matched idle column is \(5I\). Each gives raw norm squared 25 per input. One explicit gain-graph description uses 25 labelled outgoing paths per rail: use the required signed multiplicities to each terminal port, then add opposite-sign pairs to reach count 25. All 25 must contribute coherently to their respective oriented output. Columns are orthogonal, hence the terminal transformation is exactly norm preserving after division by 5. Recording each individual path changes the interference. For the padding chosen in the checker the first-input return frequency would be \(21/25\) with distinguishable paths and \(9/25\) with the coherent column.

Put \(w=(3+4i)/5\). If it had finite order then \(w+\bar w=6/5\) would be a rational algebraic integer, hence an integer. Thus its powers are dense on the circle. For a finite cone \(|x|\le B_0\), \(B_0\ge1\), choose \(k\ge0\) with \(|w^k-e^{-ih}|\le\eta/B_0\). At each site apply \(k|x|\) copies of the appropriate direction of rotation and pad with the idle to exactly \(kB_0\) cells at every site. The normalized coefficient is \(w^{kx}\), its raw denominator is the same \(5^{kB_0}\) at every port, and the phase error is at most \(|x|\eta/B_0\le\eta\), including negative sites. After \(M\) plates this adds at most \(M\eta\) in norm. Taking \(\eta=o(1/M)\) is a sufficient construction. This does not give a uniform depth, power, or energy budget.

For the finite-cone implementation, truncate the real parity source in position with arbitrarily small norm error, normalize, and approximate by normalized finite integer vectors. Use this source for the *actual* device and compare with the original band-limited line source by unitarity. A nonzero compactly supported source cannot itself have strictly bounded Fourier support. If its initial support lies in \([-B_1,B_1]\), \(B_0=B_1+4M\) covers every propagated amplitude. A ring of circumference divisible by 4 with the cone away from the seam gives exactly these finite raw updates. An ideal ramp is globally periodic only when \(Nh\) is a multiple of \(2\pi\); cone matching eliminates that restriction locally. An aperture, source sign, parity gauge, supplied coordinate origin, the orientation of the force and read context must be calibrated as appropriate. Momentum shifts differing by \(\pi\) on a single parity give identical site intensities up to a common sign at the plate. The small-chart choice and force reference specify the branch in the comparison.

The larger fan and local program are new assumptions. A common-depth deterministic two-output binary gadget with no surviving auxiliary state and with the original path normalization would have first column \((a,b)/2^{d/2}\) with integers \(a,b\) satisfying \(a^2+b^2=2^d\). For \(d\ge2\), squares modulo four force both even. Descending to \(d=0\) or \(1\) leaves only axial or equal-magnitude columns. It excludes an exact \((3,4)/5\) column in this class.

**Heralded binary implementation.** The earlier rational selector uses binary Walsh splitters, reversible signed routing and a common success test. Adapt it to the finitely many site rotations \(R_x=(a_x I+b_x J)/D\) desired in one plate, with integers \(a_x^2+b_x^2=D^2\) and \(J(r,l)=(-l,r)\). A common denominator exists for all finite powers above, including the idle. Choose the **least** power of two \(K\ge2\sqrt2 D\). Prepare a uniform register on \(K\) addresses by a Walsh transform. At site \(x\) let \(2|a_x|\) addresses perform signed \(I\), and \(2|b_x|\) addresses perform signed \(J\), using their respective coefficient signs. Fill the remaining even number with cancelling pairs \(+I,-I\). Invert the Walsh transform. On testing only whether the whole address equals zero, the yes map on all positions and rails is \((2D/K)\bigoplus_x R_x\). It therefore preserves relative amplitudes and phases of an unknown coherent source on success, including any spectator. Since \(2\sqrt2D\le K<4\sqrt2D\), the per-call success probability \(p=(2D/K)^2\) obeys \(1/8<p\le1/2\). A larger dyadic register can have a lower success probability. With fresh flags and \(L\) required calls the unconditional probability that all succeed on their first try is \(\prod_{j=1}^L p_j\), exponential in \(L\). It multiplies the window acceptance in the sharp-source experiment. Keeping the coarse failures permits recovery by the previous inverse construction: from a failed call apply the full adjoint, test return to the initial address, reapply the call, and test success. Either failure leaves the same normalized bad subspace vector up to phase; each additional pair succeeds conditionally with probability \(2p(1-p)\). Reading specific failure addresses or losing a site-dependent ancillary record changes the instrument. The address length is \(\log_2 K\) and the two matched Walsh layers plus routing, inverse and read clocks must be costed. One can run a selector per primitive matched rotation, or encode an entire rational plate with a potentially very large common denominator. Any physical implementation needs a clock that fits these operations into the assigned block time or a new limit for the actual schedule. The detector law must transfer to the retained flag records.
