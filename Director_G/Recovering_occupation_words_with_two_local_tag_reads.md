# Recovering an occupation record with two local tag reads

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Give the two occupied sites different, readable internal tags. After a flight through an unknown coherent contact, read each tag in a common basis and keep the complete occupation record whenever the two tag results agree. Three calibration returns suffice to make this retained record follow the scalar determinant walk exactly. Even perfectly distinguishable input tags succeed on half the launches. The result concerns the statistics of occupied positions through a crossing. The tag read used for erasure gives up which input tag arrived at which output. The previous unread-tag result provided a one-sided transfer bound for a specified SWAP contact. ## Calibrating the contact on the walk

Work on the open line with two occupied scalar sites. List the two qubit tags in left-to-right spatial order. When a particle hops into a vacant neighbor, its tag moves with it. A gate addressing two occupied neighbors applies an unknown, fixed unitary $V$ to their ordered pair of tags. Empty and singly occupied bond gates have their calibrated scalar amplitudes, carry no hidden history record and otherwise preserve the tags. These assumptions apply to both the calibration and the flight. All probabilities below use the ordinary squared-norm readout. Internal qubit preparation and analysis add apparatus to the binary occupation carrier.

Launch on $\{0,2\}$. Address bonds $01,12,01$ successively, with diagonal and off-diagonal entries $(3,-4i)/5,(4,3i)/5,(3,-4i)/5$ on the respective one-occupant scattering blocks. The return to $\{0,2\}$ has tag amplitude $(36I-80V)v/125$ for normalized input $v$. The history staying at the outer sites contributes $3\cdot4\cdot3$. The other history first hops into site 1, encounters the occupied central bond, and hops back, contributing $(-4i)5(-4i)V$. With both histories unresolved and $R(v)$ their combined return rate,
$$ R(v)-\frac{1936}{15625}
=\frac{2880}{15625}\|(V-I)v\|^2. $$

Test the three inputs $|00\rangle$, $|11\rangle$, and $|++\rangle$ in a common tag frame, where $|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$. Each attains the minimum precisely when its vector is fixed, including its phase relative to the bypass history. The three vectors span the symmetric triplet of two qubits. Unitarity leaves its one-dimensional orthogonal complement invariant. If all three attain the minimum, the contact therefore has the form
$$ V=\Pi_+ + z\Pi_-,\qquad |z|=1,\qquad \Pi_\pm=(I\pm S)/2, $$
where $S$ exchanges the two internal slots. The antisymmetric phase can differ between contacts. A longer flight requires the test for each distinct gate used, or a separately justified spatial and temporal repeatability assumption. In particular the addressed gate under test must be available as the middle contact of the calibration circuit with the specified bypass and return amplitudes.

Two computational-basis tests are insufficient. The diagonal unitary $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,1)$ passes the first two inputs and yields $7696/15625$ on the third. It can make the nominal equal-read return for orthogonal tags $13456/15625$ in place of $1936/15625$. Monitoring whether isolated output tags occupy the same input rays would also miss a common gate phase observable against the bypass.

## A local eraser and its complementary port

Fix a normalized pure input pair $\alpha,\beta$, independent of the initial spatial state and expressed in the same rank order on every occupied pair. Choose the irrelevant phase of $\beta$ so $\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=r\geq0$. For $r<1$ the orthonormal erasing basis consists of $u=(\alpha+\beta)/\sqrt{2+2r}$ and $v=(\alpha-\beta)/\sqrt{2-2r}$. Put $c=\sqrt{(1+r)/2}$ and $d=\sqrt{(1-r)/2}$. For $r=1$ use $u=\alpha$, any orthogonal $v$, and $d=0$. In this basis the input is
$$ \alpha\otimes\beta=c^2 uu-d^2 vv-cd\,uv+cd\,vu. $$
The first two terms are symmetric. The last two together are antisymmetric. Each calibrated contact acts on these components by the scalar phases $1$ and $z$ respectively. Every other gate and every occupation pointer acts identically on both internal states. This factorization persists through all forward gates and any common occupation-record-dependent control policy. A terminal tag measurement in the $u,v$ basis at each occupied site picks the first component exactly when the results agree. Comparing the two recorded bits can be done after both local measurements. For orthogonal computational-basis inputs a local Hadamard at each occupied site supplies this basis.

Let $E$ be any event of the whole occupation record, including intermediate reads with the same instrument in the two comparison flights. Write $P_1$ for the scalar walk and $P_{\boldsymbol z}$ for the scalar walk using the measured device's antisymmetric phases in their gate order. Squaring the sector amplitudes gives
$$ P(E,\mathrm{equal})=pP_1(E),\qquad
P(E,\mathrm{unequal})=(1-p)P_{\boldsymbol z}(E),\qquad
p=c^4+d^4=(1+r^2)/2. $$
Thus $P(E\mid\mathrm{equal})=P_1(E)$, and the unconditional equal-read yield is $p$. Given a further preparation event $A$ with $P_1(A)>0$, the success probability per original launch is $pP_1(A)$, and the conditional occupation law is $P_1(\,\cdot\mid A)$. The fraction of equal reads *within* an occupation event can depend on the event. The local equality projector omits the symmetric vector $(uv+vu)/\sqrt{2}$. Its use here relies on the fixed input product and the calibrated triplet action.

For orthogonal inputs in the three-gate return, the two conditional rates are $1936/15625$ and $(7696-5760\,\mathrm{Re}\,z)/15625$. The unequal-read port gives $1936/15625$ for $z=1$, $13456/15625$ for $z=-1$, and $7696/15625$ for either imaginary unit phase. Multiplying by $1/2$ gives the corresponding joint rates per launch. The real interference control leaves the sign of the imaginary part unresolved. Internal-state overlaps in passive bosonic multiports also enter distinguishability calculations. Here the phase is a property of an addressed binary-occupation contact and the two output bits are explicit. [Tichy, *Sampling of partially distinguishable bosons*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.7687)

## Finite calibration and crossing records

Suppose each of the three true calibration excesses is at most $e$ for one fixed unitary. Then $\|(V-I)v_j\|\leq\eta=\sqrt{15625e/2880}$ on the probe vectors. Their Gram matrix has diagonal 1, off-diagonal entries $0,1/2,1/2$, and least eigenvalue $\lambda=1-1/\sqrt{2}$. The smallest singular value of the synthesis matrix on the symmetric subspace is $\sqrt{\lambda}$. Its image under $V-I$ has Frobenius norm at most $\sqrt{3}\eta$. Hence $\|(V-I)\Pi_+\|\leq h=\sqrt{3/\lambda}\,\eta$ in operator norm.

In the triplet/singlet block form $V=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, unitarity gives $\|B\|=\|C\|=\sqrt{1-|D|^2}\leq h$, using the row and column containing the scalar $D$. Choose $z$ of modulus one with the phase of $D$, or any $z$ when $D=0$. Then $|D-z|=1-|D|\leq h$. Combining the symmetric input bound and the two singlet-column errors gives $\|V-(\Pi_++z\Pi_-)\|\leq3h$. An experiment estimating $e$ would need simultaneous upper confidence bounds for the three true rates and control of source, bypass, and analyzer errors.

For a finite circuit, sum these bounds over contact opportunities, using a worst-case bound over any record-conditioned choice of gate at each opportunity. Replacing one gate at a time and retaining the occupation pointers gives total variation at most $\epsilon=\min(1,\sum_j3h_j)$ on the complete classical output. This follows by telescoping a purified circuit and contracting trace distance under its output reads. Other gates, preparations and analyzers are assumed exact in this bound. For $H=\{A,\mathrm{equal}\}$ the ideal probability is $q=pP_1(A)$. When $\epsilon<q$ the measured-device conditional law is well-defined. Its distance from $P_1(\,\cdot\mid A)$ is at most $\min(1,2\epsilon/q)$, by bounding the error in the joint numerator and the success probability separately.

A joint occupation-word test can now use equal reads to compare its own sorted-position and escape statistics with the scalar prediction at the same read times. Along a sequence of increasingly long or rare-preparation flights, this bound transfers a conditional concentration statement when $\epsilon=o(pP_1(A))$. The earlier hard-point limit supplied fixed-time concentration of sorted positions. A measured two-body crossing would additionally require a joint word, its disturbance control and an observed yield. The equal-read outcome erases the tag information needed to assign a persistent particle identity through contact. No such measured crossing or empirical calibration is reported here.
