# What probabilities do an electric string’s preparations and detectors select?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

On a finite open chain with two occupied sites, a fixed real hopping angle and a uniform electric phase suffice to approximate every pure preparation and every complete analyzer in the vacuum Gauss sector. Even perfect agreement with both one-hop calibrations leaves a nonquadratic probability rule on the exact finite circuits. A two-hop read separates that rule from the ordinary law. A positive result follows if a routed detector retains its marginal under programs protecting its port and its response is stable as the analyzer approaches a limit. These conclusions concern an apparatus with addressed gates. The same matter and link registers, with both staggered tilts and a phase retuned to the clock, were used to derive [quantum binding and classical rebound](Electric_string_quantum_and_classical_motion.md).

## Control with one hopping angle and one field setting

Let the scalar sites be \(0,\ldots,n-1\). In the defect-free sector, each occupied pair \(a<b\) determines the whole electric string: precisely the links from \(a\) to \(b-1\) carry flux. Write \(d=\binom n2\), \(P_{ab}=|ab\rangle\langle ab|\) and \(N_a=\sum_{b\ne a}P_{ab}\), using unordered subscripts. The computational coordinates refer to the **joint** matter-and-link state. An addressed adjacent dressed hop has one-particle rotation \(R=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}3&-4\\4&3\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)/5\). Its exterior square acts on the pair coordinates. A separately selectable field pulse is \(D=w^V\), where \(w=(3-4i)/5\) and \(V|ab\rangle=(b-a)|ab\rangle\). A localized adjacent source suffices for the density result. Individually addressable bonds and timing of the uniform pulse are control resources. Repetition of a single fixed flight cycle has abelian closure. The field pulse uses the aligned phase planes and the Gauss-preserving hop described in the [local circuit](Gauss_string_records.md).

**Proposition.** At fixed \(n\ge3\) the closure of the positive words in these controls is \(U(d)\). This is a statement about amplitude matrices before assigning probabilities.

The two angles have infinite order. Otherwise \(w+\bar w=6/5\) would be a rational algebraic integer, hence an integer. Positive powers of each generator therefore give its entire circle in the closure. Adjacent real rotations generate signed permutations of the modes there. Conjugating \(iV\) by them yields \(iV\) in every ordering of the sites. To see what the ordering changes, place \(u,v\) at positions 0 and 1. Subtract the diagonal after exchanging the two from the diagonal before exchange. The result is \(N_u-N_v\). Instead put \(h,u,v\) at positions 0, 1, 2. The same difference is now \(N_u-N_v-2(P_{hu}-P_{hv})\). Only the spectator at position 0 reverses the sign of the distance difference. Configurations containing both or neither of \(u,v\) contribute zero. [Lean proof for arbitrary injective layouts](lean/StringControl.lean)

The generator algebra thus contains \(i(P_{hu}-P_{hv})\) for each triple of distinct modes. Rotate \(u,v\) together. Its skew generator has one edge \(hu\leftrightarrow hv\) for each spectator \(h\), with signs fixed by the exterior-square ordering. Commutation with the displayed diagonal selects exactly the named edge, since the two diagonal entries differ by 2 there and by zero on every other edge of that rotation. Commuting again gives the second skew-Hermitian quadrature. The graph of pair configurations joined by a common occupied site is connected. Brackets along paths give both quadratures between arbitrary distinct configurations, and the edge diagonals span all traceless diagonals. This gives \(\mathfrak{su}(d)\). The nonzero trace of \(iV\) gives the scalar direction as well. The closed group contains \(U(d)\) by its Lie algebra. For \(n=2\) there is just a phase on a single ray. For \(n<2\) there is no two-particle sector. At \(n=3\) every two-form is decomposable even though the group is full. The proof includes the half-filled case \(n=4\), where the distinction matters for extensions of fermionic linear optics. [Oszmaniec and Zimborás](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.11188)

An exact commutator calculation gives dimensions \(1,9,36,100,225\) on two through six sites. Reduction modulo a split prime supplies lower bounds for the characteristic-zero ranks, each equal to the ambient dimension. On four sites start from the dressed pair 02, rotate bonds 01, 23 and 12 in that order, then pulse the field once. The Plücker expression \(z_{01}z_{23}-z_{02}z_{13}+z_{03}z_{12}\) becomes \((-811008+2156544i)/9765625\); a single Slater preparation has zero there. Position populations immediately before and after the pulse coincide. A further 12 rotation gives probability \(589824/1953125\) at port 23 with the kick and \(147456/390625\) with it omitted under squared-coordinate readout. These are model predictions. The literal link-bit and reduced circuits agree at each step. Among all six localized launches and the four positive buttons on this array, none of 510 words with launch counted separately through length three give a nondecomposable output; eight of 1,536 at length four do. No claim of a short synthesis for arbitrary large arrays follows. [Exact witness, rank controls and complete counts](results/string_control.json) · [Checker](check_string_control.py)

## From a protected click to a frame probability

First suppose each prepared branch has a nonnegative probability for each accessible rank-one effect, depending only on its projector, normalized over every complete native analyzer. Suppose it extends continuously to limit projectors. A sufficient precise condition is uniform continuity on the accessible rays in operator norm for each fixed preparation. Whole **frames** are dense by the control proposition. Taking limits therefore gives a normalized frame function on all complex rays. For \(d\ge3\), Gleason’s theorem gives \(p(P)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho P)\) for one positive trace-one matrix. Sharp source calibration at its own ray \(P_\psi\) gives \(\rho=P_\psi\): all other diagonal entries in a basis completing that ray vanish, and positivity then kills the off-diagonals. A calibrated coherent undo or its stable limit supplies this certainty for a prepared word. No hypothesis about probabilities of subsets of unmeasured paths enters this inference. [Gleason’s original paper](https://doi.org/10.1512/iumj.1957.6.56050)

There is a more specific detector premise on the routed instrument. In addition to the fixed controls, permit **exact dressed signed adjacent swaps**, the quarter turns already used to route the pair source. Negative rotations for the fixed hop can then be realized by flipping the signs of one addressed mode and a third mode, using the square of a routed quarter turn. Reversing a uniform pulse also has a finite projective implementation. Multiply its conjugates by all mode permutations except the identity. Including the missing factor would have given a scalar, since the sum of all permuted distances is constant on pairs. This existence construction can be very long. It requires no new continuously adjustable angle. The finite controls give an exact obstruction to routing. In the one-tilt library every matrix entry lies in \(B=\mathbb Z[i,1/5]\). Modulo \(1+i\) the hop and the uniform pulse both reduce to the identity. If a finite word takes a localized pair to a coordinate ray, its reduced column has its unit entry at the original pair, so the target pair must be the original one. Arbitrarily accurate approximation is compatible with this obstruction.

The obstruction also holds if the two tilts are individually addressed on alternating bonds of the **open** chain, one tilt per bond, with the same rational field. Reduce modulo \(1+i\) and modulo \(3\), as in the two-prime readout below. In the first field only the complementary-tilt bonds swap site labels. In the second field only the original-tilt bonds swap them. Diagonal phases and signs preserve supports in both reductions. An exact route between coordinate rays must therefore preserve the number of occupants in every component of each of the two matchings. For the difference \(\delta_k\) of the target and source site occupations, this gives \(\delta_k+\delta_{k+1}=0\) on every bond. Site 0 is isolated in one matching, so \(\delta_0=0\), and induction fixes every occupation. This argument also covers a cycle of staggered layers on the same open chain.

Gate placement changes the conclusion. If the positive rotations with \((c,s)=(3,4)/5\) and \((4,3)/5\) are both selectable on a given bond, their product has cosine zero and sine one. It is the exact signed quarter turn. With that choice on every bond, successive adjacent turns can route any localized pair. These facts concern exact finite programs for this pulse library; the control proposition above remains a density statement. [Exact checks on dressed port states](check_string_readouts.py)

The two layout differences now give an exact word, up to a common phase, for \(A=\exp[2i\theta(P_{hu}-P_{hv})]\), with \(w=e^{-i\theta}\), taking its inverse if required. For a dressed routed rotation \(R_{uv}\), both \(A\) and \(R_{uv} A R_{uv}^{-1}\) act only on the two-dimensional configuration subspace \(hu,hv\), projectively. Their powers approach rotations about two nonparallel axes there, because \(\theta/\pi\) is irrational and both entries of the real tilt are nonzero. The commutator of the two infinitesimal axes gives a third. These exact words generate a dense \(SU(2)\) on that edge while fixing every other terminal projector. Deleting any named port \(j\) from the pair graph leaves it connected: two disjoint surviving pairs have four common neighboring pairs, at most one deleted, and overlapping pairs are already adjacent. Thus the closure of words protecting \(j\) contains \(SU(d-1)\) on its complement. A diagonal circle \(\exp[it(P_j-P_k)]\) for a neighbor \(k\) supplies its relative phase. Together they give the full projective stabilizer of \(P_j\). [Compiled selective word on the dressed chain](results/string_readouts.json)

For each fixed preparation take the following as operational premises.

* A complete terminal read yields one named position pair, with nonnegative normalized probabilities. For every port, two sequences of analyzer programs converging to the same projective matrix have the same limiting probability at that port. This requires stability at the limits and equivalence of exact implementations, including any environment retained by the apparatus.
* Appending the compiled selective words just constructed, supported on edges missing \(j\), or diagonal words fixing \(j\), preserves that port’s marginal. The condition concerns the output of a coherent program. An intermediate hop in one compilation can touch the protected pair. A pointer inserted there changes the experiment.
* Undoing a known preparation and routing its localized source to any named port gives that port with certainty. Empirical use requires calibration of the routes and readout. A common physically unobservable phase is part of the ray description.

For an analyzer \(U\), write its effect for port \(j\) as \(U^*P_jU\). The first two premises extend invariance to the full stabilizer. If two analyzers give the same effect for this port, their quotient on the output side belongs to that stabilizer. Hence port \(j\) has a continuous response \(f_j(P)\) depending only on the effect. To identify different physical ports, exchange two orthogonal projectors \(P,Q\) in a complete ordered frame. Subtract the two normalization equations. They give \(f_j(P)-f_k(P)=f_j(Q)-f_k(Q)\). Any two rays have a common orthogonal ray when \(d\ge3\), so \(f_j-f_k\) is constant. A single source can be routed with certainty to both labels after undoing its preparation. Its own projector therefore sets this constant to zero. All ports give one frame function and the preceding argument selects

\[
             \Pr(j\mid S,U)=|\langle j|US|s\rangle|^2
\]

for localized source \(s\), preparation \(S\) and analyzer \(U\). This proves a conditional uniqueness result in physical coordinates. It replaces equivalence across arbitrary detector contexts by concrete families of protected-program comparisons. Their empirical validity, especially for a matter-and-link circuit with a persistent environment, remains to be established.

## Two exact ways the ordinary law can fail

**A stable response that changes when other ports are mixed.** Start from 01 on three or more sites. Rotate 12, then compare direct detection with detection after one further rotation on 01. This second gate fixes the doubly occupied configuration 01. With \(x_j=|z_j|^2\), the continuous rule \(x_j^2/\sum_k x_k^2\) changes its probability there from \(81/337\) to \(50625/136897\), while the ordinary value is \(9/25\) in both settings. The three ordinary masses in the second setting are \((225,144,256)/625\) at 01, 02, 12. Inserting the uniform diagonal kick immediately before either analyzer leaves these masses unchanged. These are different completions of exactly the same protected port.

**A calibrated response that respects identical effects on every finite word.** All finite fixed-gate matrices lie in \(B=\mathbb Z[i,1/5]\). Reduce them first modulo \(1+i\), giving \(\mathbb F_2\), and separately modulo 3, giving \(\mathbb F_9\). Complex conjugation descends in both fields. Modulo \(1+i\) the real tilt acts as identity. Modulo 3 it is a signed swap. The optional second tilt \((4,\pm3)/5\) reverses those two roles. The field pulse is diagonal with nonzero reduced entries, and signed routes are monomial in both reductions. Thus every prepared sharp projector and every finite ray effect reduce to **one coordinate projector** in each field. In field \(r\), assign a click probability of one if the reduced source and effect projectors agree and zero otherwise. This rule depends only on the projectors. Exactly one outcome matches on each complete analyzer, and reducing a common unitary conjugation preserves agreement.

Mix the first rule with weight \(9/25\) and the second with \(16/25\). This gives precisely the ordinary single-hop probabilities from every localized input, for either tilt, and exact certainty on sharp routed reads. Two coherent 12 rotations from 01 return the coordinate label in both residue fields. This rule predicts return **1**, whereas squaring the ordinary return amplitude \((9-16)/25\) gives **49/625**. Odd powers can also approach the identity in the ordinary operator norm while the competing return stays at \(9/25\). The resulting failure of stability holds for this actual control library. It can be detected by finite comparisons; density alone cannot exclude it. Real physical detection frequencies have yet to be supplied. The exact arithmetic concerns this rational library and the signed routes. New gates with other coefficients may change the reductions. ## Two independent preparations

Write $q=9/25$ for a single localized return after one tilt. If two truly independent copies each use this calibration, the probability of both returns is $q^2=81/625$. Applying the two-prime prescription to the tensor matrix with one common choice of residue gives $q$ instead. Independently drawing a residue for each copy reproduces the product calibration for separate local analyzers. This distinction parallels Director Z’s same-formula product premise on history events. [Z22, Corollary 5.28](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/f37613b/Director_Z/addenda/ADDENDUM_EDITION_Z22_SESSION_4.md)

One additional exact joint operation would also test the independent-label escape. On two labeled logical paths let $g(a,b)=(a,b\mathbin{\oplus}a)$. Assume this controlled routing is available and preserves the sharp 00 preparation and named output marginal. Compare the 00 row of $g(R\otimes I)g^{-1}$, equal to $(3,0,0,-4)/5$, with that of $R\otimes R$, equal to $(9,-12,-12,16)/25$. Their projectors reduce to the **same pair** of coordinate projectors, 00 at the first prime and 11 at the second. Yet the required click probabilities are $q$ by routing protection and $q^2$ by independence. No common joint effect law that depends only on those two reductions can pass. Exact controlled routing between distinguishable copies is an additional resource; dense single-chain preparation control supplies no such finite two-copy word. More general discontinuous laws remain possible.

## Sequential records and the mechanical limit

For a resolved rank-one occupation click, impose the same response premises on each conditional branch and require a repeated immediate read of that port to be certain. Positivity forces the branch’s reconstructed density to be that sharp projector. Given the calibrated action of the next word on the branch, ordinary conditional multiplication then gives the full sequence of squared transition amplitudes. Distinct persistent records can still guide the next chosen word. A coarse click gives only support in its subspace. For example a click in the span of 01 and 02 can be followed by identity or by a 12 rotation. Starting at 01, both instruments repeat the coarse click with certainty, but subsequent resolution at 01 has probabilities 1 and \(9/25\). Coherent coarse position records in a classical trajectory experiment require specifying the instrument. [Sequential position records](Sequential_position_records.md)

The carrier of the preparation theorem also supports the two-particle Schrödinger limit in the linear string potential, followed by a transverse classical collision. That flight uses the complementary real tilt on odd bonds. In the routed menu its positive-angle version is a quarter turn followed by the inverse fixed tilt. The flight also uses a lattice phase that **varies with spacing and clock**; holding this paper’s rational \(w\) fixed under refinement would change that Hamiltonian. At each fixed finite size the control proof works for any common per-link phase of infinite order. The earlier limit allows rational approximants with phase error \(o(\tau b)\); one may even choose positive powers of the fixed phase with exponent congruent to 1 modulo 4. Those powers are dense on the circle, remain of infinite order and preserve the two reductions since the mod-3 phase has order four. Replacing one prescribed pulse by many elementary pulses can change the time budget; the continuum theorem assumes the assigned phase fits in each tick. The dense control result supplies neither a uniform time bound on sources and analyzers nor a measurement model on the infinite line. Finite arrays and their dressed records precede any limiting claim. [Scaling, sources, clock and collision hypotheses](Electric_string_quantum_and_classical_motion.md)

For the stated finite gate library all squared-coordinate cylinder probabilities from pure localized launches and sharp coordinate reads lie in \(\mathbb Z[1/5]\), conditional ratios in \(\mathbb Q\). Simultaneous complex conjugation of the sources, field and analyzers preserves these records. Other rational phase choices may introduce additional prime denominators. The residue alternative tests the probability law on actual ports by reducing coefficients to finite fields. A complex field embedding preserves the characteristic and obeys different algebraic constraints. Ordinary frequencies conditional on stable protected detection still leave a simultaneous renaming of an unanchored phase unobservable. The physical origin of the controls, Gauss constraint, detector stability, time and units remains open.
