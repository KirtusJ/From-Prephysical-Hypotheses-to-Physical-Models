# What degree-two continuation forces on whole multisets of gains

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist  
Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada  
Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex), in response to Director Z's edition Z16.

Paper G10 classifies degree-two rules that first add the complex gains of histories ending at a vertex. Director Z asked what follows if the input to the rule is the *entire multiset* of gains there. For an abundant signed Hadamard block, a real function of that multiset has only two kinds of freedom: a linear count and a quadratic form on the differences between opposite-gain counts. Requiring invariance under a common change of gain origin reduces the quadratic terms to the squared characters that send the sign gain to $-1$, including any nonfaithful ones. This derives the degree-two form without first assuming a pair kernel or summing the gains in one complex representation. ## The block identity

Let a finite gain alphabet have a fixed-point-free sign involution $J$, with $m$ pairs and one arbitrary representative per pair. Addition of multisets adds their multiplicities. For a multiset $U$ write $u_i^+,u_i^-$ for its counts and $D_i(U)=u_i^+-u_i^-$. Let $F$ be any real function on all multisets. Assume the unnormalized signed Hadamard block has the same factor-two continuation identity for every two inputs:

$$F(U+V)+F(U+JV)=2F(U)+2F(V). \tag{1}$$

**Classification.** Equation (1) holds exactly for

$$F(U)=\sum_{i=1}^m c_i(u_i^++u_i^-)+D(U)^\mathsf{T}A D(U),\qquad A=A^\mathsf{T}\in\mathbb R^{m\times m}.$$

The coefficients are unique. No continuity, growth bound or positivity assumption is needed.

**Proof.** The empty-input equation gives $F(0)=0$. With $U=0$ the identity gives $F(JV)=F(V)$. For a single cancelling pair $C_i=e_i+Je_i$, inserting $V=C_i$ gives $F(U+C_i)=F(U)+F(C_i)$. Set $c_i=F(C_i)/2$. The linear count $L(U)=\sum_i c_i(u_i^++u_i^-)$ satisfies (1) and $F-L$ is unchanged by adjoining any $C_i$. Deleting cancelling pairs leaves the unique reduced multiset representing the given difference vector. Thus $F-L=q\circ D$ on the whole integer lattice, where every $x\in\mathbb Z^m$ is realized by splitting its coordinates into positive and negative parts. Equation (1) becomes

$$q(x+y)+q(x-y)=2q(x)+2q(y). \tag{2}$$

For completeness, $q(0)=0$, $q(-x)=q(x)$ and $q(2x)=4q(x)$. Put $b(x,y)=[q(x+y)-q(x-y)]/4$. Evenness makes $b$ symmetric. Equation (2) applied with common displacement $z$ at $x+y$ and $x-y$, then subtracted, gives $b(x+z,y)+b(x-z,y)=2b(x,y)$. Interchanging $x,z$ and using oddness of $b$ in the first argument gives the difference of the same two terms equal to $2b(z,y)$. Addition gives $b(x+z,y)=b(x,y)+b(z,y)$. Hence $b$ is biadditive and $q(x)=b(x,x)$. Its values on basis vectors give a unique real symmetric matrix. Conversely $D(JV)=-D(V)$, so the parallelogram identity and additivity of $L$ prove (1). This is the elementary polarization step; the classical norm characterization has stronger norm and positivity hypotheses. [Jordan and von Neumann](https://www.mathematik.uni-muenchen.de/~michel/jordan-von_neumann_-_parallelogram_identity.pdf)

The [standalone Lean proof](lean/MultisetBlock.lean) checks the equivalence, cancellation on signed-difference fibres and separate additivity of the polarization for integer-valued functions at arbitrary finite rank. Factors of two and four are cleared. The proof above covers real values; the switching spectrum, positivity and availability of physical events are outside the Lean statement.

## Switching, positivity and the five-sheet example

Suppose the gains are $\mu_{10}=\{\pm\zeta_5^j:0\le j<5\}$, with switching invariance $F(gU)=F(U)$ for every common unit gain $g$. The five count coefficients coincide. The generator $\zeta_5$ cyclically permutes the five signed coordinates and the gain $-1$ negates all of them. A real symmetric matrix invariant under the cycle is circulant. The discrete Fourier transform pairs frequencies $s$ and $-s$ on real inputs. In this convention the precise family is

$$F(U)=c\,|U|+
  \sum_{s=0}^2 b_s\left|\sum_{j=0}^4D_j(U)\exp(2\pi i sj/5)\right|^2.$$

There are four real coefficients. With no switching the dimension is $5+15=20$. The term $s=0$ is the order-two gain character $\chi(s'\zeta_5^j)=s'$ with $s'=\pm1$. It is constant on the five sheet rotations and detects the sign. Full switching on a general finite abelian gain group containing a sign element of order two yields the same reasoning on the odd part of the group algebra. The signed differences furnish the regular representation restricted to characters $\chi$ with $\chi(-1)=-1$, each once. An invariant symmetric form is diagonal on these complex characters with equal real coefficients on conjugate pairs. The count contributes one further degree of freedom. Thus G10's known character family reappears when no pair rule is assumed at the outset. [G10 kernel rules and degree-two corollary](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/8af3e24d3327f8b9ee129ebc82b320430ffbff00/edition/PAPERS/G10_Histories_as_Events_the_Order_of_the_Pair_Rule_First_Order_Weights_an/source/body.tex#L182)

If $F$ is nonnegative on all multisets, a cancelling pair forces $c\ge0$. For each integer vector $x$, choose a reduced multiset with $D=x$ and repeat it $N$ times. The count grows linearly in $N$, while the quadratic grows as $N^2$. Nonnegativity therefore implies $x^\mathsf{T}A x\ge0$. Homogeneity and density of rational vectors give a positive semidefinite form on all real vectors. In the Fourier expression this is equivalent to $b_s\ge0$ for each frequency. Conversely those coefficient inequalities suffice. Normalizing the singleton to one would impose $c+\sum_s b_s=1$ in the displayed convention.

At the seven-tick port $(3,R)$ in the five-sheet force walk, two ten-history subsets with the same zero primitive sum have difference rows $(2,2,2,2,2)$ and $(0,0,0,0,0)$. Only $s=0$ distinguishes them. With $F=\sum_jD_j^2$ the respective unnormalized values are 20 and 0; with $F=(\sum_jD_j)^2$ they are 100 and 0. A programmable selector using sixteen coherent labels, a path mask per label and a common final label read gives unconditional accepted sheet probabilities $5/8192$ and zero. The other label outputs and mask failures are retained. The original walk with no selector gives $5/128$ at its one full base port. ## When the carrier supplies the identity

Every normalized two-by-two scalar block whose entries are unit gains and whose columns are orthogonal has a row- and column-switched signed Hadamard form. Indeed, for entries $a,b,c,d$, cancellation gives $d=-ca^{-1}b$. Under full switching, row gains leave the output rules unchanged and column gains bijectively transform the incoming multisets. If arbitrary multiset pairs can be realized by distinct equal-depth path subsets to the two inputs, continuation gives (1). Full abundance in Z15 Proposition 5.14(b,c) supplies this multiplicity for the finite strongly connected derived graph under its hypotheses. General coset-restricted or disconnected carriers require a separate account of the attainable multisets. The proof concerns the assumed additive sum of local end-vertex rules and the formal history-event algebra. Implementing physical selectors for those events is a further requirement. The factor two is the signed-H normalization; a normalized nonzero singleton and switching give this factor by continuing a single history to its two successors. [Z15 abundance and Z16's use of a full block](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/8af3e24d3327f8b9ee129ebc82b320430ffbff00/edition/PAPERS/G10_Histories_as_Events_the_Order_of_the_Pair_Rule_First_Order_Weights_an/source/body.tex#L305)

For a sparse signed walk whose gains are only $\pm1$ inside $\mu_{10}$, every attainable endpoint uses one opposite pair. Let $t_i=u_i^++u_i^-$ and add $\sum_i t_i^2t_{i+1}$ to the ordinary count, with cyclic indices. The extra term vanishes on every such input and its switched copies. With $U,V$ single positive histories in adjacent, different classes, the extra term is one on each of $U+V,U+JV$ and zero on the inputs, violating (1). Agreement on a sparse carrier cannot justify the full classification away from its attainable events. A degree-five Fourier block also preserves the primitive scalar norm while failing the requisite signed-sheet block identity. The signed-pair proof uses degree two. Z17 classifies higher-degree cyclic blocks by testing which characters preserve their block norms. A classification in arbitrary degree for general noncyclic gains goes beyond both arguments.
