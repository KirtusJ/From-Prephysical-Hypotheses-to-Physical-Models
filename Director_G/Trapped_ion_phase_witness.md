# Comparing trapped-ion qubit records with the two-mode phase witness

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Blume-Kohout and colleagues repeatedly rotated a single trapped ytterbium ion, then read its two internal states by fluorescence. Six of their public gate-set tomography tables contain the same balanced operation repeated twice and three times, together with a preparation read and a single-operation calibration. Under the authors’ nominal pulse map, these measured qubit frequencies can be compared with the two-well witness at a quarter-turn phase. In the April 2014 run the reported bright counts were 37 of 2,850 after three repetitions, versus 9 of 1,425 for a preparation read. Under a stable detector and the specified mixture family, those data bound the fair-count fraction and the spread in phase cosine. The ion remained in one trap; the measurement gives no evidence of propagation around two spatial wells. [Original paper, §§II and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms14485) · [Authors’ experimental tables](https://github.com/pyGSTio/supplemental-info-arXiv-1605.07674/tree/fc939f90f7a2b32a20aec34f36465259f38837a3/ExperimentalData) · [Two-well witness](Two_well_phase_witness.md)

## Mapping a pulse word to a walk step

Identify R with the prepared computational state $|0\rangle$, L with $|1\rangle$. In the authors' target model the recorded plus label reads $|1\rangle$, so the return probability uses the complementary count. Their target rotations are $G_x=\exp(-i\pi X/4)$ and $G_y=\exp(-i\pi Y/4)$. The literal gate word **GyGx** applies $G_y$ first and $G_x$ second. Its unitary is
$$
U=G_xG_y=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1-i&-1-i\\1-i&1+i\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Every entry has modulus $1/\sqrt2$. Let $H$ be the usual real Hadamard matrix, $Z_\beta=\operatorname{diag}(1,\beta)$, and $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$. Then $U=e^{-i\pi/4}D Z_{-i}H D^{-1}$. The similarity changes the two basis kets by phases, leaving the prepared rail and the rail measurement invariant under any number of repetitions. Hence this pulse word realizes the ideal two-mode step with $x=\operatorname{Re}\beta=0$. Its return probabilities at depths 0 through 3 are $(1,1/2,1/2,1)$. The sign-conjugate phase gives the same data. The single pulse $G_y=D'Z_{-1}H(D')^{-1}$ with $D'=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ supplies a second balanced control with $x=-1$ and ideal probabilities $(1,1/2,0,1/2)$. The transformations here describe the nominal design of the gates, not a measured equality of experimental matrices. [Authors’ target gate, preparation and effect definitions](https://github.com/pyGSTio/supplemental-info-arXiv-1605.07674/blob/fc939f90f7a2b32a20aec34f36465259f38837a3/AnalysisNotebooks/ExperimentalDataToResultsObjs.ipynb)

These targets and all their products are one-qubit Clifford operators. A one-qubit Clifford acts on the Bloch sphere by a rotation of the cube, with possible angles $0,\pi/2,\pi,2\pi/3$ up to reversal. Consequently $|\operatorname{tr}U|^2\in\{0,1,2,4\}$. For a balanced step $Z_\beta H$ the same number is $|1-\beta|^2/2=1-\operatorname{Re}\beta$. Any balanced Clifford word therefore has real phase part $-1,0$ or $1$. The cubic-unit target $-1/2$ in the two-well paper cannot be obtained with these nominal gates. The published optical line-walk data use spatially resolved, separately propagated paths; summing those positions omits the two-well interference. [Authors’ Clifford gate scope, §II C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.07674) · [Existing optical records and preparations](Optical_walk_records_and_phase_labels.md)

## What the tables measure

Let $q_j$ be the bright fraction among the **reported repetitions** of the $j$-block circuit. The tables report both the count and its denominator. Suppose the detector's bright probabilities given true R and L are $a,b$ at every chosen clock, for every phase component, on one experimental date. Suppose the null sequence prepares R and one balanced block gives equal rail populations. Then $q_0=a$, $q_1=(a+b)/2$ and $q_j=a p_j+b(1-p_j)$. Provided $q_1>q_0$, the operational estimate is
$$p_j=1-\frac{q_j-q_0}{2(q_1-q_0)},\qquad W_0=2(1-p_3)=\frac{q_3-q_0}{q_1-q_0}.$$
For an arbitrary common coherent subprobability $\nu$ on $x\in[-1,1]$ and a fair-count mass $c$, the model gives $W_0=c+\int x^2d\nu$. Thus $c\le W_0$ and $\nu\{|x|\ge\delta\}\le W_0/\delta^2$. The second-clock comparison tests the target prediction $p_2=1/2$, equivalent to $q_2=q_1$ when the same detector applies. Two-sided Fisher exact tests (conditional on the combined number of bright outcomes, summing tables no more likely than the observed one) give raw $p$-values 0.897, 0.866, 0.807, 0.150, 0.741 and 0.317 by date. None rejects equality at 5%. These data do not establish exact equality. For the zero-cosine target, $W_0$ itself uses only the third clock and the two calibration rows. There is no independent herald count for trials outside the reported file denominators and no per-shot time ordering in this analysis. ## Checks on the measurement assumptions

The inferred $b=2q_1-q_0$ equals $151/150$, $71/70$ and $7/5$ on 9 February, 2 March and 19 March, respectively. These point estimates lie outside the probability interval. December has an inferred $p_3=261/256$, giving the negative witness in the table. They remain in the record. Finite counts contribute sampling variation, and the reported data cannot on their own allocate the differences to sampling, state preparation, drift, or readout.

The nominal swapped-rail controls $G_x^2$ and $G_y^2$ test the fitted bright response for L on the same dates, conditional on accurate swaps. For example, on 30 March both gave 50/50 bright, versus the YX calibration estimate $b=23/25$. On 19 March, $G_y^2$ gave 20/20 and the one-block estimate was $7/5$. The four-$G_y$ nominal return checks the dark response. April’s three archived four-$G_y$ rows average 16/1425 bright versus 9/1425 for the null sequence. The single-$G_y$ witness for target $-1$ has estimated values $20/699,-15/41,1/73,4/67,-10/7,3/7$ in date order. For this control $W_{-1}=3+(q_3+q_0-2q_2)/(q_1-q_0)$. Intersecting the model’s nonnegative range with conservative binomial bounds, its six-date simultaneous upper limits are 0.420, 0.685, 0.863, 1.189, unbounded by the calibration, and 1.893. These use eight one-sided Clopper–Pearson limits per date with tail $0.05/48$, an interval for the numerator and a positive interval for the denominator; the archive includes the raw outer intervals. The March 19 calibration interval includes zero. The opposite ordering $G_xG_y$ has a recorded complete second/third-clock comparison in April only. The parser preserves that comparison and all five missing third-clock rows. [Selected rows and alternate calibrations](results/ion_phase_records.json)

## A classical two-state comparison with the same observations

Allow an arbitrary distribution of time-homogeneous classical two-state transition matrices for the repeated YX block. For one matrix let $u=P(R\to L)$ and $v=P(L\to R)$, each between zero and one. Starting in R, its left probability $l_t$ obeys $l_{t+1}=u+(1-u-v)l_t$. Subtracting adjacent recurrences gives
$$l_3-l_2=u(1-u-v)^2\ge0.$$
If $b\ge a$, the bright probability changes by $(b-a)(l_3-l_2)\ge0$. This sign survives arbitrary stable mixtures of transitions, including different detector contrasts for different hidden labels provided each contrast has that orientation. No numerical gain calibration or balanced-pulse assumption enters this sign test. The hypotheses are the same pure R start, a fixed transition per label for the repeated block, shared mixture weights, and a response conditional on the final state and label that stays fixed across clock settings.

Every date has $q_3<q_2$. Under independent binomial repeats of the two circuits, the one-sided exact conditional test of $q_3\ge q_2$ conditions on their total bright count. At equality it has the hypergeometric lower tail for the three-block bright count; increasing the three-block odds moves probability to larger counts. The table’s six base-ten log tail probabilities in date order are $-450.302,-46.246,-55.271,-20.723,-4.647,-11.266$. Even the 20-repeat date has six-comparison-adjusted $p<0.000136$. The April calculation treats the two duplicate rows per circuit as independent repeats with a common probability, as for the interval above. The archive gives the exact fractions as well, including April’s tail below binary64 range. The manuscript’s original GST analysis detected non-Markovian effects, particularly in the March 2 data. Unrecorded order effects or correlated repeats affect these count-level tests. [Original observations and non-Markovianity analysis, §II D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.07674) · [Exact test numerators and denominators](results/ion_phase_records.json)

Director Z’s reviewer identified an order test that also allows **arbitrary orientation of the detector**. For each classical hidden label, $q_2-q_3=(a-b)u(1-u-v)^2\le a$, since $0\le u\le1$, $|1-u-v|\le1$ and $a,b\in[0,1]$. The same pure R launch yields $q_0=\mathbb E a$ for the mixture. Hence $q_2-q_3-q_0\le0$ must hold with the same label distribution and detector across all three settings. The result allows a different sign and magnitude of $b-a$ for each label. It still requires a homogeneous two-state update for each label and the specified preparation. Taking a lower one-sided binomial bound for $q_2$ and upper bounds for $q_0,q_3$, each at tail $0.05/18$, gives six-date simultaneous 95% lower limits on this difference of $0.4265,0.2981,0.3728,0.2182,-0.2295,0.1545$ (rounded down). Five remain positive; the 20-repeat control does not resolve this broader alternative at the reported confidence. [Director Z’s cross-review and independent checks](../Director_Z/cross_review/subagent_xr72_ion_records_and_flight/REPORT.md)

A deterministic three-state classical cycle with a binary emission illustrates the remaining ambiguity. Starting from state 0, traverse $0,1,2,0$ and use return probabilities $1,1/2,1/2$ at the three respective states. This reproduces the nominal YX repetition series at every clock since the unitary block cubed is a scalar. Other pulse words can test the classical model after its updates for those words are specified. The two-state result constrains a memoryless classical account with rail as its only dynamical state. It establishes neither a universal exclusion of classical models nor a microscopic explanation of the probability law.

## A phase sign between two repetitions

Four dates contain a further terminal experiment. Write $U$ for the same chronological `GyGx` block, with the ion prepared in R. Between two repetitions insert the ten-pulse word `GxGyGxGyGyGxGxGyGxGy`. Its action on the three Pauli axes in the authors' target gate set is $(-X,-Y,Z)$, the rail phase sign up to a global phase. The complete 14-pulse word appears as `Gy(GxGxGyGxGyGy)^2Gx`. The checker searches both outer block orders and all pulse lengths, preserving the literal outer blocks and excluding idle gates. It selects the shortest available nonidentity sign circuit for YX by pulse geometry alone. Other sign circuits of 32, 1,022 and 2,048 pulses remain separate in the archive. No qualifying circuit occurs in the April or December tables. [Authors’ target gates and acquisition procedure](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.07674) · [Pulse-word audit and every matching row](results/ion_phase_echo.json)

Let a coherent component use a fixed $U$ with flip probability $q$, and let $r=|\operatorname{tr}U|^2$. Write $L_2,L_3$ for the left probabilities after two and three free blocks, and $E$ for the left probability with the midpoint sign. The two amplitudes for ending in L after two ticks have equal modulus $\sqrt{q(1-q)}$. Changing the midpoint sign subtracts them where the bare circuit adds them. The sum of squared moduli is $4q(1-q)$. The fair independent count alternative of mass $c$ gives $L_2=E=1/2$ on that component. For the shared coherent subprobability $\nu$ and the quarter-turn target $r=1$,

$$D=\int(q-1/2)^2\,d\nu=\frac{1-L_2-E}{4},\qquad L_3=\int q(r-1)^2\,d\nu+c/2.$$

These two quantities vanish together precisely for balanced unitary components with $r=1$ and zero fair-count mass. The earlier three-clock fit used a balanced one-block calibration. This comparison admits arbitrary $q$ and uses an independently observed bright control. It assumes sharp R preparation, calibrated rail-diagonal readout, a common latent population, repeated application of its chosen coin, and a midpoint that applies the prescribed sign without changing that coin. [Free-clock characteristic recurrence](Testing_an_unknown_two_mode_coin.md)

Let $A,B$ be the bright probabilities for R and L on a single date. For a true flip probability $l$ the observed rate is $y=A+(B-A)l$. The null read gives $A$. The `GxGx` control produces some mixture of the two rails; its bright rate $h>A$ implies $B\ge h$, even if the control is an imperfect swap. Since $B\le1$,

$$D\le\frac{1+A-y_2-y_E}{4(1-A)},\qquad L_3\le\frac{y_3-A}{h-A},\qquad c\le2L_3.$$

Here $y_2,y_E,y_3$ correspond to the bare two-block, midpoint-sign and bare three-block settings. Limits below combine one upper and one lower binomial limit for $A$, lower limits for $y_2,y_E,h$, and an upper limit for $y_3$. Each one-sided Clopper–Pearson tail is $0.05/36$ for simultaneous coverage of this comparison across all six dates. The ratio is capped at one if its numerator can exceed the control contrast. The first expression is capped at the physical $D\le1/4$. Within each date the inference assumes binomial repeats and the same two detector responses across hidden labels and all five circuits, including the bright control. The displayed upper bounds are rounded outward.

On March 30 the two point frequencies sum to 1.16 in bright units; the checker retains both. Among nine additional settings consisting of the identical ten-pulse middle with zero or one X/Y quarter turn on each end, only the X-prepared/Y-analyzed setting occurs on the four dates. That setting is compatible with multiple midpoint maps, including the ideal identity and phase sign, which both predict a half-bright result for orthogonal axes with ideal preparation and readout. There is no standalone read of the middle. The nominal pulse composition identifies the candidate instrument; the counts alone cannot verify its action on each hidden component. If an independent calibration bounds the difference in terminal flip probability from the prescribed sign by $\eta$, the $D$ cap increases by at most $\eta/4$ (still capped at $1/4$). GST characterizes gates within a two-dimensional stationary model; its fitted gate errors do not by themselves give this hidden-component guarantee. The observations remain conditional on the squared-amplitude rule for the coherent family.
