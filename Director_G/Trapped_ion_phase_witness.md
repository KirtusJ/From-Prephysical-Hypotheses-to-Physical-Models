# A trapped-ion measurement of the two-mode phase witness

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Blume-Kohout and colleagues repeatedly rotated a single trapped ytterbium ion, then read its two internal states by fluorescence. Six of their public gate-set tomography tables contain the same balanced operation repeated twice and three times, together with a preparation read and a single-operation calibration. They supply measured frequencies for the two-mode dynamics of the two-well witness at a quarter-turn winding phase. In the April 2014 run the reported bright counts were 37 of 2,850 after three repetitions, versus 9 of 1,425 for a preparation read. Under a stable detector and the specified mixture family, those data bound the fair-count fraction and the spread in phase cosine. The ion remained in one trap; the measurement gives no evidence of propagation around two spatial wells. [Original paper, §§II and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms14485) · [Authors’ experimental tables](https://github.com/pyGSTio/supplemental-info-arXiv-1605.07674/tree/fc939f90f7a2b32a20aec34f36465259f38837a3/ExperimentalData) · [Two-well witness](Two_well_phase_witness.md)

## Mapping a pulse word to a walk step

Identify R with the prepared computational state $|0\rangle$, L with $|1\rangle$. In the authors' target model the recorded plus label reads $|1\rangle$, so the return probability uses the complementary count. Their target rotations are $G_x=\exp(-i\pi X/4)$ and $G_y=\exp(-i\pi Y/4)$. The literal gate word **GyGx** applies $G_y$ first and $G_x$ second. Its unitary is
$$
U=G_xG_y=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1-i&-1-i\\1-i&1+i\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Every entry has modulus $1/\sqrt2$. Let $H$ be the usual real Hadamard matrix, $Z_\beta=\operatorname{diag}(1,\beta)$, and $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$. Then $U=e^{-i\pi/4}D Z_{-i}H D^{-1}$. The similarity changes the two basis kets by phases, leaving the prepared rail and the rail measurement invariant under any number of repetitions. Hence this pulse word realizes the ideal two-mode step with $x=\operatorname{Re}\beta=0$. Its return probabilities at depths 0 through 3 are $(1,1/2,1/2,1)$. The sign-conjugate phase gives the same data. The single pulse $G_y=D'Z_{-1}H(D')^{-1}$ with $D'=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ supplies a second balanced control with $x=-1$ and ideal probabilities $(1,1/2,0,1/2)$. The transformations here describe the nominal design of the gates, not a measured equality of experimental matrices. [Authors’ target gate, preparation and effect definitions](https://github.com/pyGSTio/supplemental-info-arXiv-1605.07674/blob/fc939f90f7a2b32a20aec34f36465259f38837a3/AnalysisNotebooks/ExperimentalDataToResultsObjs.ipynb)

These targets and all their products are one-qubit Clifford operators. A one-qubit Clifford acts on the Bloch sphere by a rotation of the cube, with possible angles $0,\pi/2,\pi,2\pi/3$ up to reversal. Consequently $|\operatorname{tr}U|^2\in\{0,1,2,4\}$. For a balanced step $Z_\beta H$ the same number is $|1-\beta|^2/2=1-\operatorname{Re}\beta$. Any balanced Clifford word therefore has real phase part $-1,0$ or $1$. The cubic-unit target $-1/2$ in the two-well paper cannot be obtained with these nominal gates. The published optical line-walk data use spatially resolved, separately propagated paths; summing those positions omits the two-well interference. [Authors’ Clifford gate scope, §II C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.07674) · [Existing optical records and preparations](Optical_walk_records_and_phase_labels.md)

## What the tables measure

Let $q_j$ be the bright fraction among the **reported repetitions** of the $j$-block circuit. The tables report both the count and its denominator. Suppose the detector's bright probabilities given true R and L are $a,b$ at every chosen clock, for every phase component, on one experimental date. Suppose the null sequence prepares R and one balanced block gives equal rail populations. Then $q_0=a$, $q_1=(a+b)/2$ and $q_j=a p_j+b(1-p_j)$. Provided $q_1>q_0$, the operational estimate is
$$p_j=1-\frac{q_j-q_0}{2(q_1-q_0)},\qquad W_0=2(1-p_3)=\frac{q_3-q_0}{q_1-q_0}.$$
For an arbitrary common coherent subprobability $\nu$ on $x\in[-1,1]$ and a fair-count mass $c$, the model gives $W_0=c+\int x^2d\nu$. Thus $c\le W_0$ and $\nu\{|x|\ge\delta\}\le W_0/\delta^2$. The second-clock data independently check the target prediction $p_2=1/2$. For the zero-cosine target, $W_0$ itself uses only the third clock and the two calibration rows. There is no independent herald count for trials outside the reported file denominators and no per-shot time ordering in this analysis. For the last column assume binomial repeats for each selected circuit within each date. Construct four exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson limits per date for $q_0$ (lower and upper), $q_1$ (lower), and $q_3$ (upper), each at tail probability $0.05/24$. A union bound gives simultaneous coverage of at least 95% without requiring independence between different circuit rows. Where $\ell_1>u_0$, a conservative upper limit is $\max(0,u_3-\ell_0)/(\ell_1-u_0)$. If the calibration interval includes zero denominator no finite bound follows by this method. The table rounds bounds upwards. April consequently bounds $c$ by 4.257% and coherent mass at $|x|\ge1/2$ by 17.028% under the stated conditions. These are model-dependent population bounds. An error in the preparation, pulse balance or setting-independent readout is outside those intervals. ## Checks on the measurement assumptions

The inferred $b=2q_1-q_0$ equals $151/150$, $71/70$ and $7/5$ on 9 February, 2 March and 19 March, respectively. These point estimates lie outside the probability interval. December has an inferred $p_3=261/256$, giving the negative witness in the table. They remain in the record. Finite counts contribute sampling variation, and the reported data cannot on their own allocate the differences to sampling, state preparation, drift, or readout.

The nominal swapped-rail controls $G_x^2$ and $G_y^2$ test the fitted bright response for L on the same dates, conditional on accurate swaps. For example, on 30 March both gave 50/50 bright, versus the YX calibration estimate $b=23/25$. On 19 March, $G_y^2$ gave 20/20 and the one-block estimate was $7/5$. The four-$G_y$ nominal return checks the dark response. April’s three archived four-$G_y$ rows average 16/1425 bright versus 9/1425 for the null sequence. The single-$G_y$ witness for target $-1$ has estimated values $20/699,-15/41,1/73,4/67,-10/7,3/7$ in date order. The opposite ordering $G_xG_y$ has a recorded complete second/third-clock comparison in April only. The parser preserves that comparison and all five missing third-clock rows. ## A classical two-state comparison with the same observations

Allow an arbitrary distribution of time-homogeneous classical two-state transition matrices for the repeated YX block. For one matrix let $u=P(R\to L)$ and $v=P(L\to R)$, each between zero and one. Starting in R, its left probability $l_t$ obeys $l_{t+1}=u+(1-u-v)l_t$. Subtracting adjacent recurrences gives
$$l_3-l_2=u(1-u-v)^2\ge0.$$
If $b\ge a$, the bright probability changes by $(b-a)(l_3-l_2)\ge0$. This sign survives arbitrary stable mixtures of transitions, including different detector contrasts for different hidden labels provided each contrast has that orientation. No numerical gain calibration or balanced-pulse assumption enters this sign test. The hypotheses are the same pure R start, a fixed transition per label for the repeated block, shared mixture weights, and a response conditional on the final state and label that stays fixed across clock settings.

Every date has $q_3<q_2$. Under independent binomial repeats of the two circuits, the one-sided exact conditional test of $q_3\ge q_2$ conditions on their total bright count. At equality it has the hypergeometric lower tail for the three-block bright count; increasing the three-block odds moves probability to larger counts. The table’s six base-ten log tail probabilities in date order are $-450.302,-46.246,-55.271,-20.723,-4.647,-11.266$. Even the 20-repeat date has six-comparison-adjusted $p<0.000136$. The April calculation treats the two duplicate rows per circuit as independent repeats with a common probability, as for the interval above. The archive gives the exact fractions as well, including April’s tail below binary64 range. The manuscript’s original GST analysis detected non-Markovian effects, particularly in the March 2 data. Unrecorded order effects or correlated repeats affect these count-level tests. [Original observations and non-Markovianity analysis, §II D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.07674) · [Exact test numerators and denominators](results/ion_phase_records.json)

A deterministic three-state classical cycle with a binary emission illustrates the remaining ambiguity. Starting from state 0, traverse $0,1,2,0$ and use return probabilities $1,1/2,1/2$ at the three respective states. This reproduces the nominal YX repetition series at every clock since the unitary block cubed is a scalar. Other pulse words can test the classical model after its updates for those words are specified. The two-state result constrains a memoryless classical account with rail as its only dynamical state. It establishes neither a universal exclusion of classical models nor a microscopic explanation of the probability law.
