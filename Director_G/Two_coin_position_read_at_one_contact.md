# Two three-tick position records read a relative coin phase

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex) and the Codex workers.

Place two distinguishable walkers two wells apart. At every tick each receives the real Hadamard coin and its ordinary conditional displacement. When they occupy the same well, their joint amplitude changes sign. Three ticks later, reading just their positions distinguishes two circular input coins prepared with the same handedness from two prepared with opposite handedness. One binary event has probabilities \(3/8\) and \(5/8\), respectively, in the stipulated model. It achieves the full distinction available in the terminal positions. The result concerns relative orientation. Simultaneously conjugating both inputs leaves every complete record invariant.

## The contact and its two position masks

Use the infinite line, or a device that realizes the same path table without wraparound. Launch the labeled walkers at \(0,d\) in an arbitrary joint two-coin density matrix \(\rho\) on the ordered inputs \(RR,RL,LR,LL\). No independent-source assumption enters until interpreting the joint correlation as a product. Each tick transforms \(R,L\) by \(H=(X+Z)/\sqrt2\), displaces \(R\) by \(+1\) and \(L\) by \(-1\), then applies \(-1\) on coincident positions and \(+1\) elsewhere. Use the same contact on every well and at every tick. Whole-site detectors resolve each walker's own terminal displacement and ignore the terminal directions. A phase on the coincident sector is a known model of two interacting quantum walkers. The scientific premise here is that this phase and the position detector are available to the program's walk. A mere encounter, a persistent copy of the encounter, or particle exchange is a different operation. [Ahlbrecht et al. on point interactions in a quantum walk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1051) · [Earlier local two-sheet comparison with a pointer and return masks](Local_contact_and_joint_sheet_records.md)

For each walker retain *every* terminal displacement. Assign a positive sign to \(-3,+1\), and a negative sign to \(-1,+3\). The event is agreement of the two reported signs. To see what this event reads, first freeze the logical basis just after the first split. Its two modes are the outgoing \(R,L\), each at its own site. For two further real ticks the effects of the four final offsets on this basis are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
u &-3&-1&+1&+3\\ \hline
4(E_u)_{RR}&0&1&2&1\\
4(E_u)_{RL}&0&-1&1&0\\
4(E_u)_{LL}&1&2&1&0 .
\end{array}
\]
For example, at \(+1\) the two outgoing directions receive the rows \((1/2,1/2)\) and \((1/2,0)\) from the first-split modes. The two orthogonal outgoing directions contribute separate Gram matrices. The signed sum of the four \(E_u\) is \(X/2\).

With \(d=2\) only the \(RL\) first-split mode meets. Thus this first-plane contact is \(D=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,1,1)\). A contact immediately before the last tick gives a common sign to all paths ending at a specified pair of positions **and** directions: the preceding pair of positions can be recovered uniquely from these terminal data. That sign drops from each terminal Gram, including after summing terminal directions. A contact at the final plane also drops. A permanent contact therefore gives the full three-tick position table obtained by applying only the first contact. With \(Y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), conjugation by \(D\) sends \(X\otimes X\) to \(-Y\otimes Y\). Both first-tick \(H\)'s reverse their respective \(Y\), leaving the joint sign intact. For equal-sign positions the ordinary kinematic probability is
\[
 p_{\rm near}=\tfrac12-\tfrac18\operatorname{tr}(\rho\,Y\otimes Y).
\]
For independent sources with individual \(Y\)-means \(y_A,y_B\) this term is \(y_Ay_B\). Real one-coin effects see only \(I,X,Z\): for any \(\rho\), real Kraus branches, real blanks and coordinate projectors, all full transcript effects are real symmetric. Adaptive selection on those transcripts preserves this property. A bare independent complex coin and its conjugate cannot be distinguished this way, including in the presence of a localized sheet accessed only with real operations. Independent local protocols on two coins, with classical communication, give sums of product effects from these same local real spans and miss \(Y\otimes Y\). In contrast \(Y\otimes Y\) is real symmetric, and an interacting walk reads it. This algebra and bilocal tomography in real quantum theory predate this construction. The three-tick collision, position signs and launch-spacing control implement the comparison on the specified carrier. [Caves, Fuchs and Rungta](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0009063) · [Hardy and Wootters](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.4870) · [Other sheet preparations and the conjugate-sector restriction](Coin_qubit_review.md)

## Moving one launch site completes the real two-coin table

With \(d=4\) the first paths cannot meet. Later contacts in these three ticks are invisible as above. The same detector then has \(p_{\rm far}=1/2+\operatorname{tr}(\rho\, Z\otimes Z)/8\). The individual far-site effects pulled through \(H\) span \(\{I,X,Z\}\): their sum gives \(I\), the signed score gives \(Z/2\), and the two extreme wells distinguish the input \(X\) projectors with an overall factor \(1/4\). Thus the sixteen far joint effects span the nine products of \(I,X,Z\). Adding the near agreement read supplies the tenth direction in the space of real symmetric four-by-four effects. The full far histogram and the single near event determine \(\operatorname{Re}\rho\) under the stated readout. For any complex density matrix this real symmetric part is itself positive with unit trace and gives the same outputs on all real effects. A real density matrix therefore has full tomography in these two settings. Complex two-coin tomography still requires the components odd under simultaneous conjugation. The source and detector population must be comparable after moving the launch.

Near alone has rank nine: its position effects are a fixed orthogonal conjugation of the far product table. They miss \(Z\otimes Z\), just as the far table misses \(Y\otimes Y\). For launch separation zero the first collision amounts to a product of rail signs (\(-Z\otimes Z\)); the terminal signed correlation equals the far one. Reversing the two launch sites marks \(LR\) and gives the same near \(Y\otimes Y\) read. On an odd separation there are no simultaneous equal wells on the line. At any spacing a one- or two-tick whole-site detector in this fixed protocol is blind to the interaction: its first possible sign is already at the last plane or just before the final tick. These statements depend on the walk order and terminal algebra.

## What the observed difference could establish

On like versus opposite pure circular launches the probability **difference** at offsets \((u,v)\) equals \(-uv/8\) if both offsets are \(\pm1\), and zero at the other twelve pairs. Thus the total variation is \(1/4\) and the single near agreement event attains it. Separating the launches by four makes both circular panels \(1/2\). Switching off the contact also produces that far prediction. The four extreme displacements and the zero-difference pairs remain in the archive. For an observed event interval \([p_-,p_+]\) that already includes sampling, detection and any unclassified attempts, inversion gives the conditional \(Y\otimes Y\) interval \([4-8p_+,4-8p_-]\) intersected with \([-1,1]\). Count the original paired launches and retain terminal misses or bound their contribution to the event. A [Lean 4.19 proof](lean/RelativeCoinContact.lean) checks the contact sign, the signed agreement formula in rational rate units and the four-cell upper bound.

Repeated coin preparations need an additional independence assumption to infer \(\lvert y\rvert=\sqrt{4-8p_{\rm near}}\). Equal mixtures of \(++\) and \(--\) circular pairs and of \(+-\) and \(-+\) pairs have the same maximally mixed individual coins. They yield \(3/8\) and \(5/8\), respectively; independent maximally mixed coins yield \(1/2\). These are ordinary complex-separable mixtures and also valid real joint density matrices once the equal conjugate alternatives are mixed. A known reference with \(y_B\ne0\) would instead determine \(y_A\) *relative to that reference* for a product preparation. Its availability and orientation are additional physical resources. The contact yields a conditional test of the joint composition and readout, with no inference of a physical probability law from kinematics alone.
