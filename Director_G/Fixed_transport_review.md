## Relative transport and the spatial menu

I enumerated all eight three-step direction words at each start, retained the relevant outgoing position and coin, and accumulated signed coefficients at each sheet endpoint. For the linear, quadratic and static carriers at primes 3, 5, 7, 11 and 13, I evaluated the Fourier amplitudes for every character, both incoming directions and every starting position. All 48,276 destructive-port probabilities and 48,276 constructive-port probabilities agreed with the stated sine-squared and corresponding cosine-squared formulas to absolute tolerance \(10^{-12}\). The reviewed checker's CSV records the *null/non-null classification at the destructive port*. Its rows do not by themselves verify both output probabilities.

The relative shift is the difference of the two path translations. The projective-direction counts obtained by reducing the shifts were 1, \(p\) and \(p+1\) for the linear, quadratic and static carriers. Even and odd sites give the two claimed static differences by subtracting adjacent values on the opposite parity cycle, including wraparound in the field. One wording needs care. Reversing a sheet translation means applying its inverse. On the given departure-dependent walk, physically retracing a spatial edge applies the increment of its new departure site. For example, at \(p=5,x=0,c=+1\), paths \((+,-,-)\) and \((-,+,-)\) have gains \((0,0)\) and \((4,0)\). Composing the first with the inverse of the second gives \((1,0)\). Following the spatial reversal of the second path using further forward steps gives a loop gain of \((3,0)\). The interference calculation uses the former comparison.

I composed the sheet operators extracted directly from the enumerated physical paths, allowing their common translations. All 39 static words had squared mass \(2p\), supported on two adjacent cosets of \(uy+z\) with opposite unit signs. For nonzero \(u\), permuting rows by \(y\mapsto uy\) reduces the sheet-amplitude matrix to a difference of two cyclic permutation matrices whose relative permutation is a single cycle. Its kernel is one-dimensional and its rank is \(p-1\). For \(u=0\) the rows coincide and the rank is one, as required by the unit qualification. These word tests omit intervening route translations, which commute with every difference. They do not independently simulate a random first-passage schedule.

For the incomplete menu I extracted physical quadratic blocks at \(x=-j/2\pmod p\), giving \(h=(2,-2j)\), and composed every setting except \(j=u\). For each of 39 words I computed total integer mass directly and the squared projection onto the desired character line by grouping counts by \(uy+z\), squaring each bin sum, summing and dividing by \(p\). The common trivial character has zero amplitude. Desired mass was always \(p-1\). The total masses for the five primes in ascending order were 4, 18, 138, 16,806 and 208,024. The desired fractions were \(1/2,2/9,1/23,5/8403,3/52006\). Analytically a nonzero mode with \(s=0\) receives \((\zeta^{2r}-1)^{p-1}\). The \(p\) binomial coefficients in the corresponding polynomial occupy distinct exponents modulo \(p\). One-dimensional Parseval, with the two-dimensional point input, contributes \(\binom{2p-2}{p-1}/p\) to the total. This tests the actual quadratic increments as well as the denominator. The conclusion concerns the given word.

## Persistent flag and reversible reads

For the five-step example I enumerated the 32 direction sequences from \((0,R,0,0)\) without branch propagation code from the checker. I assigned the bit at two steps, kept it on each history and regrouped by its final physical endpoint. At the reading there were one and three unit magnitudes. At step five the yes branch had eight unit magnitudes. The other branch had twenty unit magnitudes and one double. Thus the odds for exponents 1, 2 and 4 move from \(1/3\) to \(4/11,1/3,2/9\), respectively. Equality of the recorded odds at both times gives \(\phi(2)=4\phi(1)\) under a common response and global normalization, with \(\phi(0)=0\) and \(\phi(1)>0\). These assumptions also account for unoccupied endpoints. The conclusion identifies the power in the stipulated power-law family. For a general response, changing \(\phi(3)\) alone leaves the entire comparison unchanged. Endpoints in which no flag was retained would not provide these same two disjoint outcome sets.

For a separate exact pointer test I built all 1,728 columns on the static \(p=3\) carrier with a four-tick clock and two binary slots in *all* initial states. At tick 1, XOR the negative-direction predicate into the first slot. At tick 2, conditional on that slot, XOR site parity into the second. All columns had squared length 2, and all distinct columns had dot product zero. Changing the first XOR to assignment produced 216 nonzero off-diagonal Gram entries. For the zero-gain three-step eraser I separately enumerated the eight histories. The two pointer counts at the destructive output were \((0,0)\) with a blank pointer, \((-1,1)\) with the first direction retained, and \((0,0)\) after reversible erasure. The first direction used for erasure can be recovered from the step-two output site and new direction.

At a fixed tick and physical output, a controlled addition is invertible when its decision and increment use data preserved by the addition. One sufficient construction uses a distinct possible write slot for each tick and earlier slots as controls. The inverse subtracts the same label. With a fixed initial pointer and a horizon before reuse, distinct observed transcripts then have disjoint terminal records. This establishes the stated coordinate-mask product by induction and permits summation over persistent transcripts. The argument assumes this apparatus and response model. I did not re-prove the cited all-history theorem, test physical implementability, or distinguish any absolute amplitude embedding. All checks here are on the mathematical model.

## Subsequent review of the coherent corridor

I read the corridor section and its proposed recurrence before writing a second implementation. The manuscript and checker at this reading had SHA-256 digests `0d7f2e49274f5daf29261510f248c1cba458952b99546f62c428e115533eb4f1` and `7dfe9f64bd71bf6cebcbcef2508ed33988417bc69e86d4f16d4c0f6a45a09689`. The supplied checker passed all eleven checks, including its 1,440 corridor stops. I independently checked the following points.

**Path counting on a ring.** Before the first turn, the offset from the right-moving front is zero. After one allowed left step the offset is minus two, and every further right step preserves it. Attempting a second left would produce minus four. For \(N\ge6\), this residue is distinct from both admitted offsets, so wraparound cannot admit extra turns. The first left step has positive sign, as do all right steps. Moving the turn by a full period changes the sheet sum by the difference of two complete period sums, which vanishes in an abelian group. At \(t=1+nN\) the \(n\) earlier turns are at the same trailing site, coin and sheet, and the turn on the final step has the other coin at the same site and sheet. A free coin step combines their counts as \(n+1\) and \(n-1\). The leading branch is one before, and two unit endpoints after. The claimed recurrence then follows for every \(n\ge1\) by comparing a single experiment at two times. No equality of the odds *between different choices of* \(n\) is needed.

**Which probabilities must be stable.** Write \(C\) for the retained all-yes transcript and \(F\) for the copied trailing-site bit. In a globally normalized endpoint table, \(P_t(C,F)/P_t(C,\neg F)=f(n)+1\). At the later horizon the ratio for those *same old records*, summed over every unrestricted last-step outcome, is \([f(n+1)+f(n-1)]/2\). Each horizon's normalizer cancels separately. The leading event has positive weight at both times because \(f(1)=1\). The equality of ratios is warranted if the joint distribution of the previously stored records is stable under the unobserved continuation. Requiring only an unconditional marginal for a trailing bit also assigned on rejected transcripts would be insufficient. Stable past probabilities are a causal consistency premise about the readout law. The physical fact that the pointer bits remain unchanged on each history alone does not imply stability after a nonlinear endpoint weighting. Independent trials can compare the common prefix with or without the last free step. Inspecting the particular admitted cell or conditioning on any succeeding outcome changes the experiment. Across increasing \(n\), the apparatus must retain the successful record through the chosen finite horizon and trigger the flag at that horizon. A ring clock of \(N\) phases specifies the mask schedule by itself; it does not specify an unbounded record store or a horizon selector.

**Independent and adverse checks.** I enumerated every signed direction history at horizons \((N,n)=(6,1),(6,2),(8,1),(10,1)\) with seeded heterogeneous transport in \(\mathbb Z_5\times\mathbb Z_7\). I retained every accepted and rejected transcript and both flag values, accumulated counts by full endpoint, and globally normalized at each horizon. At \(N=6,n=2\), trailing-to-leading odds on \(C\) changed from 3 to 2 under the linear response, stayed 5 under squaring, changed from 9 to 14 under cubing, and from 17 to 41 under the fourth power. Under squaring, all four aggregate joint probabilities for \(C\) or its complement and either old flag agreed across horizons in all four cases. The prior probability of \(C\) matched \((n^2+2)/2^{1+nN}\). A separate integer propagation with five seeded transport tables checked the common-sheet counts at all 800 stops through \(n=160\) on rings of lengths 6, 8, 10, 14 and 22. The proof does not require primality for this corridor. Replacing the coarse membership bit by a fine site-and-coin record at every tick separated the earlier turns. For \(N=6,n=2\) and zero transport the trailing counts were three unit endpoints before the free step and six after. That modified apparatus gives odds 3 at both times for every normalized endpoint response. As a wraparound control, setting \(N=2\) made the leading and trailing sites coincide, eliminating the leading-flag reference event.

With \(f(0)=0,f(1)=1\), the exact recurrence fixes its first differences to \(2n-1\) at index \(n\ge1\). With residual \(e_j\) the deviation at \(n\) is the sum of \((n-j)e_j\) over \(1\le j<n\), yielding the published finite-range bound. This is a uniqueness result within the common integer endpoint-response model under joint old-record consistency. Finite experiments constrain a finite range, and the squared-model success probability declines exponentially in the horizon. No physical measurements or tests of absolute character embeddings were part of this reading.
