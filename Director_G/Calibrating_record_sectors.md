# Transferring a measured probability law to the walk's record words

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A pointer that writes a bit creates another terminal coordinate. For a writer that copies the **outgoing rail** during a native Hadamard tick, conservation of the incoming event flux forces every recorded word to inherit the blank law's character coefficients. If counting is present, its weight can bias the two daughters; one additional sharp-rail comparison measures that bias. For a pointer that can copy a **position predicate** before the next tick, matched isolated-port comparisons transfer the entire blank law directly. Both results use a single sharp source and whole-position preparations on the signed cyclic staircase. They give calibrations through any specified finite record depth. ## What a pointer transition must preserve

Use a $4M$-well ring at order $M\ge3$, with the usual native Hadamard tick, signed rail transitions and departure sheet gain $\lfloor x/2\rfloor$. At a clock between two ticks, a clean pointer bit may receive
$(x,r,g,w,b)\mapsto(x,r,g,w,b\mathbin{\mathsf{xor}}1_S(x))$.
Here $w$ is the prefix and $b$ is the new bit. The physical operation keeps the old prefix, signed rail history and gain, including both rails when they occupy the same well. Slot order and write timing are specified. The write can be implemented as the controlled permutation on the full two-valued slot, even though only blank inputs are used. A no-write interval leaves the existing word fixed while the base evolves. Appending a recorded zero creates a new named word. If the apparatus identifies unused slots with zero-filled slots, impose that physical identification in both sides of the comparison. A write after a streamed tick is treated at its arrival clock, and a write before the next tick at its departure clock.

At a complete port $(v,w)$, where $v$ names well and rail, assign a finite nonnegative score $F_{w,v}(U)$ to its signed-residue multiset $U$, with $F_{w,v}(0)=0$. Add scores over the actual distinct terminal coordinates. The function for a given port and word is the same for all programs reaching it, including masks, pointers and stopping horizons. The pointer has no unreported state affecting these scores; an extra retained copy is a different port space. Assume the existing unrestricted-tick continuation law in each frozen word sector for every surviving position product event there. Thus extension by a free tick multiplies the total raw score by a common factor for the frozen sector. The root has a known profile and positive singleton flux.

The comparison across a write uses an **absolute per-original-launch event rate**. On a prepared isolated parent port, compare its rate just before the write with the rate of the corresponding joint port just after, keeping the same incident denominator. Use the matched blank interval if the read must be made at a common time. An unchanged base motion included on both sides requires its known continuation factor; a pointer-only permutation consumes no native splitting tick in this model. No per-survivor renormalization is performed. A matched odds comparison with a simultaneously retained, nonzero reference also suffices if the reference carries the same raw weight in the two settings: equal odds imply equality of the two test scores. This latter provision requires an accessible reference and its own stability. Reversibility of the pointer map, or equality of its output after composing it with its inverse, does not imply this probability comparison.

## Which shapes can be calibrated inside one word?

For any prefix needed in the test tree, isolate a single sharp R history with whole-position singleton masks. Straight R motion visits both any chosen $h_1\in S$ and any $h_0\notin S$. Schedule each write at the appropriate well and continue the sharp route to home, recording any known sign and gain. The resulting prefix contains a singleton. From that preparation, leave the word fixed and run the position-mask multiset compiler. Its initial singleton gain acts by an invertible common signed translation, which can be compensated in the requested bins. In this way one can put any desired $U,V$ on the two rails of any chosen well within the word. This uses idle intervals between writes and the ability to transmit both rails at an admitted site. It asserts no such reachability for an uninterruptible write-every-tick instrument.

Apply the frozen-sector theorem separately to each nonzero sector. For a single-rail preparation of $U$ the one-input continuation equation says that the sum of two successor values of $F_{w,v}(gU)$ equals the continuation multiple times the parent value. Its finite, irreducible signed-gain lift is balanced. Summing over the lift fixes the multiple at two whenever a score is nonzero; the maximum principle makes every vertex and simultaneous gain translation equivalent. The two-rail input then gives the signed block identity. The existing classification yields the **unnormalized** form

$$
 F_w(U)=a_w |U|+\sum_{d=0}^D b_{w,d}
 \left|\sum_{(\sigma,g)\in U}\sigma e^{2\pi i dg/M}\right|^2,\qquad
 D=\lfloor M/2\rfloor,\quad a_w,b_{w,d}\ge0 .
$$

The identically zero sector has all coefficients zero. A singleton comparison with a positive parent rules it out. No division by the singleton is made when comparing sectors; that division would erase an observable word-dependent change in incident flux. ## A finite calibration per transition

For any parent $w$, perform each preparation at $h_b$ and one chosen rail, so every surviving history takes the same pointer outcome $b$. Acquire the matched parent and child incident rates for these multisets:

| Preparation | Score in a frozen word |
|---|---|
| One positive zero-gain word | $A=a+\sum_d b_d$ |
| A positive and negative word at zero gain | $2a$ |
| Two positive words at gains $0,r$, for $r=1,\ldots,D$ | $2A+2\sum_d b_d\cos(2\pi dr/M)$ |

The last rows measure cosine moments; the missing zeroth moment is $A-a$. The folded cosine transform on $0,\ldots,D$ is invertible. To see this, split each nonsingleton folded coefficient equally between $d$ and $-d$ on the full cyclic group. Its finite Fourier transform is real and even. Knowing the listed cosine moments and moment zero then determines all Fourier coefficients, including zero and Nyquist when present. The measured equality on these $D+2$ preparations is therefore equivalent to $F_{wb}=F_w$ on **every** multiset. A common normalization established independently in every word would supply the singleton row and leave $D+1$ additional comparisons. The required physical events have at most two surviving histories; their aperture cost and incident denominators still depend on the compiler clock. [Compact physical preparation and finite masks](Preparing_signed_sheet_residues.md)

For a horizon of $k$ recorded bits, repeat the edge comparisons out to depth $k$. Every reachable word then inherits the root coefficients, so $F_{w,v}=F_{\varnothing}$ at all complete ports in the calibrated tree. The same one nonempty proper subset $S$ can write **all** the comparisons, because the program chooses on which side to place the entire test multiset; a singleton subset and its unmarked complement suffice. There is no need to compare every spatial subset before using the same score at a terminal port. Applying the conclusion to writes with other predicates requires the same stationary score law and faithful pointer transport on those operations. One constant predicate cannot realize both bit values without another pointer operation. Omitting idle intervals can also remove words: writing the singleton-well predicate on every tick cannot produce adjacent ones from a walker that must move.

More generally, the compared, reliably reversible relabels define a graph on the named frozen sectors. Equality on a spanning tree propagates their common profile. If only fresh-bit writes connect a word to its two children, that tree has $2^{k+1}-2$ edges through depth $k$. Alternative genuine controls may connect the sectors differently. This graph count concerns arbitrary independent word-indexed scores and edge-equality tests. It does not give a lower bound for joint apparatus tests that constrain many sectors simultaneously. Likewise rank $D+2$ is necessary in general to identify an unnormalized interior profile by linear single-score comparisons: any smaller-rank collection leaves a nonzero coefficient perturbation, and a sufficiently small multiple keeps every coefficient positive. For a specified boundary profile, extremal or zero readings can remove several coefficients at once.

There is a stronger sufficient premise. If conserving incident flux on every pointer-only extension is already guaranteed for *every* surviving position event, an isolated $U$ at $h_b$ has precisely one child port with the identical multiset. Flux conservation forces $F_{wb}(U)=F_w(U)$ for every $U$ immediately. Likewise if the write is followed obligatorily by a free tick, its total two-daughter frozen score is $2F_{wb}(U)$; the common continuation comparison with $2F_w(U)$ gives the same equality. The finite calibration above is a way to check the unrestricted conclusion using substantially fewer preparations after frozen-sector classification. Conservation over the entire unfiltered launch alone supplies no such grant for every masked event.

## Writing the outgoing direction during the native tick

A weaker controller can write just the outgoing direction of the streamed Hadamard, with bit $0$ for R and $1$ for L. The two alternatives from a single rail input generally populate **both** child sectors. The following conclusion uses this literal direction writer and frozen no-write intervals. To prepare a given prefix, transmit the R or L daughter selected by a singleton well at each writing tick. A known intervening singleton route brings that one history to a sharp R port. Its known sign and residue can be compensated in the subsequent multiset preparation. At the test well prepare arbitrary signed multisets $U,V$ on its two incoming rails in the one prefix. The R daughter has $U+V$; the L daughter has $U+JV$, where $J$ reverses every sign in $V$. Both receive the same departure residue. It drops out by frozen-sector gain-translation invariance. If one retains only an invariant of the outgoing rail different from this bit, the two daughters need to be worked out for that writer.

Let $F$ be the parent's frozen score, and let $C_0,C_1$ be the independently classified raw scores of the children. Assume the union of **both** child outputs in the streamed writing tick preserves the incident probability of each isolated input event being compared, with the same clock factor two as a nonwriting tick. This is conservation of the specified product-event flux across that transition. Its equation is

$$
 C_0(U+V)+C_1(U+JV)=2[F(U)+F(V)].
$$

Setting $V=0$ gives $(C_0+C_1)/2=F$. It suffices to check those single-rail equations on the $D+2$ calibration inputs of the preceding table. Put $\Delta=(C_0-C_1)/2$. The parent's signed block identity reduces the writing equation to $\Delta(U+V)=\Delta(U+JV)$. The count portions cancel because both multisets have the same cardinality. Choose $U$ to be a positive singleton of gain zero and $V$ a positive singleton of gain $r$. Then the difference of the two daughter values of $\Delta$ is

$$
 4\sum_{d=0}^D \Delta b_d\cos(2\pi dr/M).
$$

The $D+1$ jointly prepared pairs for $r=0,\ldots,D$ force every $\Delta b_d$ to vanish. Thus every child inherits *each* character coefficient of its parent, in the same raw units. The only possible change is $a_{w0}=a_w+\delta_w$, $a_{w1}=a_w-\delta_w$, with $|\delta_w|\le a_w$. Conversely every such choice respects the writing equation for **all** incoming multisets. The $D+2$ single-rail and $D+1$ two-rail scalar conservation tests therefore exhaust what undivided writer flux can determine. In the interior their number $2D+3$ equals the codimension of the one-parameter solution in the $2(D+2)$ child coefficients; fewer independent linear scalar equations cannot give the same conclusion uniformly there. Both-rail comparisons matter. At $M=4$, assign the parent equal weights on characters 0 and 1, and assign one character wholly to each child in that order. All one-rail tests pass. Two positive singleton inputs at gains 0 and 2 give total child score 8 when the conserved value is 4. Integer-coordinate tests verify the calibration rank, the surviving count direction and the fair-fork rank for thirteen odd and even orders. In particular a blank law with **zero count coefficient** transfers intact through every such direction write under the stated frozen-sector and writing-tick continuation premises and positivity. It can be a mixture of several characters. For a blank with positive counting weight, one additional rate comparison per tested prefix suffices: on a single positive history the two named daughters must fire equally often, without loss of incident flux to unreported outcomes. The shared character coefficients give identical square contributions on these two singletons, so equality fixes $\delta_w=0$. At a pure-count parent of unit score, $\delta_w=1/2$ instead assigns the two daughter scores $3/2$ and $1/2$, giving click fractions $3/4$ and $1/4$. Their total probability is unchanged for every incoming event. Keeping each child count coefficient constant in its later descendants gives a nonnegative countermodel even at unbounded horizon. These are raw complete-record scores; the incident probability divides each score by the accumulated clock normalization. A fairness comparison on the original blank alone cannot control a bias first introduced at an untested later prefix. Per-word renormalization would erase its signature in an isolated child; the informative measurements compare the two daughters of the same input.

## Consequences for actual records and failures

Once the scores agree, a terminal joint event sums the root score over its **separate** word ports. For sharp position-and-rail block records a given word has the convolution of its block multisets; evaluation at one character or count multiplies along that word. This gives the persistent-component transcript law for such sharp records. If a bit only reports a spatial subset, that word may leave several base ports coherent between writes, and a product of scalar block probabilities is generally incorrect. Histories stored in different words are summed after weighting, even when their base ports coincide. An interference experiment that erases or rotates the bit coherently before readout has a different terminal multiset. [Sharp two-well records and coarse graining](Position_transcripts_and_selected_laws.md)

Three failures distinguish the assumptions.

* Give the root the count score, and give a disconnected nonempty subtree the trivial-character square score. Every frozen sector obeys unrestricted free continuation, and every singleton has score one. Writing a singleton into that subtree and later undoing the write on it leaves the same visible singleton outcome. At an isolated cancelling pair, the parent count score is two and the child square score is zero. Thus singleton pointer checks and reversible route geometry do not secure conservation of that prepared event. This assignment violates a requirement of conservation across **all** pointer writes, if that stronger premise is made.
* Multiplying every score in word $w$ by $\lambda_w>0$ preserves its frozen continuation and all its conditional distributions when each word is separately renormalized. A complete trial with two differently weighted populated words changes their relative rates, and a locked incident-flux or stable-reference comparison detects the change. An arbitrary change in loss or denominator between the write and no-write settings can mimic it.
* If the copy leaks which rail into another surviving coordinate, two opposite-sign histories that should share a port need not cancel after the leak. A perfect read of the intended position bit and exact reversal of its visible slot can coexist with that additional tag. The stated equality applies to the specified full coordinates and to the pointer that leaves the signed multisets there as prescribed. It neither reconstructs omitted degrees of freedom nor selects an ordinary character in a blank sector that used a different law.
