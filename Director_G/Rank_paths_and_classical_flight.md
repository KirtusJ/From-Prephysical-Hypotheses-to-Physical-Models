# What terminal walk probabilities determine about an entire flight

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

For a one-dimensional nearest-neighbor walk on one site parity, the site distributions at consecutive ticks determine each oriented edge flow. They allow a marker to keep its cumulative position rank throughout a flight. This marker has a local realization on the sharp signed Hadamard walk and follows a single Newtonian ray in the program's one-packet limit. At a crossing of two rays its path law differs from the law obtained by taking successive coarse readings. The construction assumes normalized squared site probabilities.

## Consecutive positions fix the edge flows

Work on the integer line with a normalized field supported on one parity, or with a cone on a ring that can be unwrapped. At a motion tick the on-site coin preserves the sum of the squared rail amplitudes, and the shift sends its two outputs one link in opposite directions. On-site plates can change the two rails while leaving total site mass fixed. At the arrival side of each shift write \(\mu_t(x)\) for the two-rail site mass and \(F_t(x)=\sum_{z\le x}\mu_t(z)\). The same argument applies with extra internal coordinates summed when the shift still has these two directions.

For \(x\) on the source parity, all mass at sites \(\le x-2\) enters \((-\infty,x-1]\), and none at sites \(\ge x+2\) can enter. Hence the edge masses out of \(x\) are

\[
 L_t(x)=F_{t+1}(x-1)-F_t(x-2),\qquad R_t(x)=F_t(x)-F_{t+1}(x-1).
\]

They are nonnegative, sum to \(\mu_t(x)\), and equal the quantum left and right daughter masses by local conservation and the unique predecessor of each oriented port. For any nearest-neighbor trajectory model having these site marginals the same cut identity holds. Take one uniform rank \(0<q<1\), ignoring the null endpoints. Assign it to site \(x\) when \(F_t(x-2)\le q<F_t(x)\). The lower portion of that interval up to \(F_{t+1}(x-1)\) goes left, and the rest goes right. Thus the *same* rank at every tick defines a nearest-neighbor path realizing all the oriented arrival panels. At an on-site plate keep its position. Plate output rails measured before the next shift would define a separate instrument.

With both site parities present, every path preserves its initial parity class through alternating ticks. Restrict the site distributions to one such class, apply the cut formula, and repeat for the other. This still determines the edge flows and admits a separate rank within each class. A single global rank need not realize them. Starting at adjacent sites with raw rail pairs \((1,1)\) and \((1,-1)\) moves half the mass each way across their common bond in one tick. The site distributions stay fixed, so a stationary quantile would miss both directed flows. On a four-site ring, either clockwise or counterclockwise flow takes the uniform occupied-even-site distribution to the uniform odd-site one. A cut without control of circulation cannot identify the two directions. Continuous one-dimensional quantile motion and random walks reproducing discrete quantum position distributions both have precedents. [Brandt et al., quantile motion](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9809053) · [Yamagami et al., quantum-walk-replicating random walks](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9021583)

## A local implementation with fair bits

For the unmodified Hadamard walk from one sharp port, normalize raw integer rail counts \((u,v)\) at tick \(t\) by \(2^{-t/2}\). Represent each possible marker at the site by an integer slot \(0\le i<u^2+v^2\). Begin at slot zero. In each tick append a fresh independent fair bit \(B\) to give \(z=2i+B\). The first \((u-v)^2\) slots go left and the others go right. Their lengths sum to \(2(u^2+v^2)\). When moving right, subtract \((u-v)^2\) to obtain the new local slot. When moving left, add the squared incoming right-rail raw count at the daughter to \(z\). This orders arrivals from the western predecessor before arrivals from the eastern predecessor. Empty bins receive no marker.

To verify the construction, let \(K_t\) be the total number of slots strictly west of the marker plus its local index. Doubling the parent bins and bisecting each at the left count groups consecutive blocks exactly as in the cumulative cut above. The two translation rules give \(K_{t+1}=2K_t+B\), including at the ends of the support. The half-open intervals with these binary prefixes therefore realize the fixed-uniform-rank law for every finite path cylinder. The marker needs a persistent integer slot, a fresh bit and access to both arriving field counts at reassembly. It need not scan the cumulative distribution or know a stopping horizon. These are explicit extra primitives of this model. For a sharp launch the all-right edge at depth \(H\) has probability \(2^{-H}\). Any sampler starting deterministically with only independent fair bits, using at most \(b\) bits on every run, assigns probabilities of the form \(k/2^b\), by padding shorter branches. Thus \(b\ge H\). This worst-case bound concerns randomness supplied, not the minimum average memory. For general prepared fields the continuous-rank construction still applies; the integer slot and fair-bit implementation proved here is specific to sharp unplated flights. ## The first two-time difference

Launch the sharp right rail at zero. Compare the all-launch event \(X_2=2,\ X_4=0\). On the nearest-neighbor line it is precisely the word \(RRLL\). The three numbers below concern different procedures.

| Procedure | Event probability | Calculation |
|---|---:|---|
| Keep the same rank. | \(0\) | The rank intervals for \(X_2=2\) and \(X_4=0\) are \([3/4,1)\) and \([3/16,5/16)\). |
| Split afresh according to the local squared outgoing fractions, independently of earlier moves given site and field. | \(1/80\) | The mass at \(+2\) is \(1/4\). A left move then has probability \(1/2\), followed by \(1/10\) at \(+1\). |
| Measure position at tick two and measure the endpoint at tick four. | \(1/16\) | Projection onto \(+2\) has probability \(1/4\) and leaves a sharp right rail there. Two further left moves reach zero with conditional squared mass \(1/4\). |

Among silent nearest-neighbor markers with all the original single-time panels, the full attainable interval for \(RRLL\) is \([0,1/16]\). At tick three, \(x=1\) has incoming right and left masses \(1/2,1/8\) and a left daughter of mass \(1/16\). Assign any \(\theta\in[0,1/16]\) of this daughter to the incoming left rail, which has the unique history \(RRL\). Assign its remainder to the incoming right rail and fill the right daughter by conservation. All earlier histories are fixed: there is a unique history at each site through tick one, and at the sole site with two histories at tick two the next move is deterministic. Future ticks can use the same site-based splitter for every \(\theta\). An actual midpoint projection can give the upper endpoint for this particular word while changing other probabilities. Merely conserving one marker and calibrating its oriented single-time reads does not select its history. ## The one-ray limit over a whole flight

Adopt the line, source, scaling, loss conditions and four-tick force clock of the program's [renewal preparation](Renewal_launch_and_classical_records.md). Condition on release first and initialize uniform rank in the extended release packet. Carrying the same marker through failed preparations would require an additional model of the preparing instrument. The target is \(y(t)=Y_0+p_{\rm in}t-Ft^2/2\). At attained block times in a fixed compact interval the compact-momentum comparison error is uniformly \(O_T(\delta/\varepsilon+\delta^2)\). The cutoff source has vanishing position and momentum deviations, and under the continuum linear potential the position deviation about \(y(t)\) is bounded by the initial position deviation plus \(T\) times the momentum deviation. For each fixed Fourier test \(\exp(i\xi Y)\) away from the chart seam, the position characteristic function transfers to the lattice with a vanishing error. These estimates and the omitted source norm hold for any sequence of attained times converging to a time in the interval. [Four-tick comparison and its clock hypotheses](Four_tick_force.md)

Consequently for each \(r>0\), the supremum over attained block times of the one-time probability outside \([y(t)-r,y(t)+r]\) tends to zero. Otherwise select offending times, take a converging subsequence, and apply the characteristic-function convergence to its limiting point mass. For any fixed \(0<\eta<1/2\), both tails are eventually smaller than \(\eta\) at **every** block time. Every rank in \([\eta,1-\eta]\) then stays inside the tube simultaneously at all those times. Between block boundaries a rank path moves by at most four site pitches; the target changes by at most its modulus of continuity over four native ticks. Sending the pitch, tick duration, \(r\), and finally \(\eta\) to zero proves uniform-in-time convergence in probability on the flight interval for the interpolated rank path. There is no sum of error probabilities over the increasing number of ticks. Reinstating timeout as an isolated outcome costs its vanishing probability. This result covers the interval after release. It does not assert that every retry before release stays near a prescribed physical path.

## Which way a crossing continues

In the program's two-ray preparation with field reversal, the unprobed position marginals concentrate equally near \(-A(t)\) and \(A(t)\), with \(A(t)=\int_0^t(t-s)F(s)\,ds\). A fixed lower-half rank follows \(-|A(t)|\) and an upper-half rank follows \(+|A(t)|\). To justify this through a crossing, take any fixed tube radius \(d>0\). The two chart groups in the [sequential position proof, steps 1–3](Sequential_position_records.md) have squared norms tending to \(1/2\) and uniformly vanishing norm outside their own radius-\(d\) tubes, and the remainder has vanishing norm. When \(|A(t)|>d\) the tubes are disjoint. Projection onto either tube retains the corresponding group up to vanishing error and rejects the other up to vanishing norm, so interference cannot change the limiting half-mass there. When \(|A(t)|\le d\), the union lies within \(3d\) of either ordered target. Away from the exceptional ranks \(0,1/2,1\), these estimates give uniform tracking of the ordered targets, by first fixing a margin about those ranks and then shrinking it. This model has an ordering rule even when the two unprobed branches meet.

By contrast, the specified sequence of coherent coarse-bin measurements follows one of the signed branches \(\pm A(t)\) through the crossing. At fixed separated times on opposite sides of a sign reversal of \(A\), the probability of switching sides tends to zero for the silent rank path and to one for the measured coarse record (at nearby attained read times). For example, set the schedule in the definition of \(A\) to +1 up to time 1 and then −1. For \(t\ge1\), \(A(t)=t^2/2-(t-1)^2\), and the crossing occurs at \(2+\sqrt2\), after the force switch. This comparison is conditional on the two-ray source and a correctly implemented measurement. A model of the physical apparatus must explain which record it realizes. The comparison gives a full-flight criterion that one-time classical position limits alone cannot settle.

The [checker](check_rank_paths.py) follows every binary prefix through ten ticks for both launch rails, verifies each oriented occupancy against independently enumerated signed words, and keeps every two-time cell in the four-tick comparison. The numerical examples and Lean statements do not establish a probability law or a microscopic clock for the physical world.

## A complete position and rail record at every tick

Use the same four-tick potential, site pitch \(a=\varepsilon\delta\), native time \(\tau=\varepsilon\delta^2\), and block duration \(\Delta=4\tau\) as in the single-ray flight. Condition on successful release. Now measure both position and rail at release and after every native tick, retaining all readings. Allow no additional read duration on this clock. When a momentum plate prepares the launch, apply it before the release read. The force plates still precede the first native tick of each flight block. This experiment can be compared with taking a final read on the unmonitored packet or with the sparse coherent coarse-bin instrument. Periodic joint chirality and position measurements in discrete quantum walks have an established diffusive regime. [Romanelli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0403192)

At the first tick of each block, the reported previous rail and position give the conditional signed step mean \(m_n=-\sin(2hx)\) for \(R\) and \(+\sin(2hx)\) for \(L\), where \(h=F\delta\Delta\) and \(Y=ax\) uses the plate's coordinate origin. Acting on the first basis rail, \(HO(-hx)\) has raw outputs \(\cos(hx)-\sin(hx),\cos(hx)+\sin(hx)\); squaring and dividing by two gives the formula. For the remaining three ticks \(m_n=0\) because every previous read prepared one basis port. In particular \(|m_n|\le 2|F|\Delta |Y|/\varepsilon\) at the active tick. A tilted initial packet loses its momentum coherence at the release read.

Let \(S_n=\pm1\) be the measured step and \(M_j=\sum_{n<j}a(S_n-m_n)\). With \(N=\lfloor T/\tau\rfloor\), the martingale maximal inequality gives \(\mathbb E\max_{j\le N}|M_j|^2\le4Na^2\le4\varepsilon T\). Through time \(T\) there are at most \(T/\Delta+1\) active ticks, hence

\[
 \sum_{n<N}a|m_n|\le 2|F|\delta(T+\Delta)\max_{j\le N}|Y_j|.
\]

For sufficiently small \(\delta\) this implies \(\max_j|Y_j|\le2(|Y_{\rm release}|+\max_j|M_j|)\). If \(\varepsilon\to0,\ \delta/\varepsilon\to0\) with the renewal packet, the measured release position converges to \(Y_0\). The predictable drift and fluctuation above then both vanish uniformly in probability. The linearly interpolated **fully recorded flight stays at \(Y_0\)** in the limit, even for a force and launch momentum giving a nonconstant unmonitored Newtonian ray. Reinstating the source's vanishing timeout mass preserves this statement with timeout a distinct outcome. “Stays” describes the rescaled limit. The microscopic position moves at every tick.

For fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(\delta\to0\), assume the release positions converge in law, conditional on successful preparation. Use the same fresh uniform variable to generate a measured step with right probability \((1+m_n)/2\) and a fair reference step at each tick. The signs disagree with conditional probability \(|m_n|/2\). Stop when the absolute measured position first exceeds \(K\). Up to that stopping time the expected maximum physical discrepancy from the fair walk with the same release is bounded by \(2|F|\delta(T+\Delta)K\), by summing the mismatch cost \(2a\). The previous maximal bound gives compact containment for the measured positions, allowing \(K\) to increase after the mesh limit. The fair walk satisfies the functional central limit theorem with \(a^2/\tau=\varepsilon\). The limiting measured flight is therefore the release draw plus an independent Brownian motion of variance \(\varepsilon t\). A fixed-action release packet can have nonzero width. This statement requires the specified native clock, retained outcomes and joint measurements; changing the readout time changes the variance per unit of physical time.

The [exact projective checker](check_measured_flight.py) compares complete signed-amplitude histories with the measured transition kernel over eight ticks for both input rails and three initial sites, with and without a rational rotation plate. At \(x=1\) with cosine \(3/5\) and sine \(4/5\), a right-rail input has right-step probability \(1/50\), while a left-rail input gives \(49/50\). The rational plate stresses the sign convention; it is a finite test of the transitions, separate from the shrinking-angle proof. [All 96 panels](results/measured_flight.json)

## A two-clock test of the Brownian path claim

Consider the fair nearest-neighbor walk from zero on the line. Even observing its exact position law at every clock and its exact flow along every edge does not determine its diffusive path limit. Let \(Q_j\) be the left-continuous quantile of the position law of the fair walk at tick \(j\). Draw one uniform \(U\) per launch and set \(Y_j=Q_j(U)\). This is the common-rank construction above, also known as a [comonotonic coupling](https://jandhaene.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2002-kaas-dhaene-vyncke-goovaerts-denuit.pdf). Its steps are nearest-neighbor: the fair transition sandwiches the next quantile between \(Q_j(U)-1\) and \(Q_j(U)+1\), and parity excludes zero. The cut identity therefore fixes the same single-edge laws as for independent fair steps.

Yet the interpolated paths \(Y_{\lfloor ns\rfloor}/\sqrt n\) converge uniformly on each bounded time interval almost surely to \(\sqrt{s}\,Z\), where \(Z=\Phi^{-1}(U)\) is a single standard normal draw. Indeed the scalar central limit theorem at each fixed quantile \(0<u<1\) gives \(Q_j(u)/\sqrt j\to\Phi^{-1}(u)\). After any index \(J\) beyond which this error is below \(\epsilon\), the rescaled error at grid points \(j/n\le S\) is at most \(\epsilon\sqrt S\). The finitely many earlier errors vanish after division by \(\sqrt n\); interpolation preserves convergence by uniform continuity of the limiting curve. Independent fair steps instead give Brownian motion. Both have the same Gaussian marginals in the limit.

Read the strict event that positions at ticks \(n\) and \(2n\) have opposite signs, keeping ties as misses. Its common-rank probability is **zero for every \(n\)** by symmetry about the median. For independent fair steps it **tends to \(1/4\)**: the first and second blocks of \(n\) increments, divided by \(\sqrt n\), converge jointly to independent standard normals \(A,B\). In the isotropic \((A,B)\) plane, \(A(A+B)<0\) occupies two wedges of total angle \(\pi/2\); their boundary has zero limiting probability. This test requires a joint position record with a specified read disturbance. Two independently launched one-clock samples cannot perform it. One sufficient dynamical premise selecting independent fair steps is zero conditional drift given the **entire** observed past: for steps of size one this fixes each conditional right probability to one half, and multiplying the conditionals fixes the path law. Conditioning only on the current position already gives zero drift for both constructions.
### Limits of tests using a fixed number of clocks

For every fixed \(k\ge1\) there is a nearest-neighbor countermodel matching **all** joint position laws at up to \(k\) prescribed clocks while keeping a detectable discrepancy in the diffusive limit. Start with independent fair increments in \(b=k+1\) consecutive blocks of \(2n\) ticks each. In block \(i\) write \(D_i\) for the displacement in the first half minus that in the second. Reweight each path by \(1+\prod_i\operatorname{sgn}(D_i)\), using sign zero for a tie. The weights are nonnegative and average one by interchanging the two halves of one block. At most \(k\) fixed observation times leave the interior of at least one block unobserved. Swapping that block's halves preserves every observation and reverses the correction, proving exact equality of their joint laws. Extra independent fair increments can extend either walk beyond the test horizon.

Yet under the fair law the product of the signs is negative with probability \(\tfrac12[1-4^{-n}\binom{2n}{n}]^b\), since \(D_i\) has the law of a sum of \(2n\) fair signs. Under the alternative this event has probability zero. The discrepancy tends to one half. Reading each block's endpoints and midpoint suffices. In units of \(n\) ticks and \(\sqrt n\) sites the limiting alternative is Brownian path measure weighted by the same functional of the block half-increments. This follows from the functional central limit theorem since the density is bounded and the Gaussian half-increment differences have zero tie probability. Thus every fixed-order test passes even for a limiting process with a different path law. This is a finite-walk realization of the distinction studied by [Föllmer, Wu and Yor, *On weak Brownian motions of arbitrary order*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0246-0203(00)00133-3). The alternative depends on \(k\); equality of all finite-dimensional distributions for one continuous process would determine its law.
### How accurately must the observed steps be fair?

Write \(Q\) for the independent fair law on all \(T\)-step histories and \(P\) for another normalized law on the same paths from zero. The total entropy deficit in bits is \(\Delta=T-H_2(P)\). If \(m_j\) denotes the conditional mean of step \(j\) given its full preceding step history under \(P\), then

\[
\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^T \mathbb E_P[1-h_2((1+m_j)/2)],
\qquad
\sup_E |P(E)-Q(E)|\le\sqrt{(\ln 2)\Delta/2}.
\]

Here \(h_2\) is binary entropy and the supremum covers every event constructed from the recorded path. The entropy chain rule proves the identity; the inequality follows from Pinsker since \(D(P\Vert Q)=(\ln 2)\Delta\) in nats. In particular, an upper bound tending to zero on the **total** deficit transfers the fair-walk functional limit to the recorded process. A walk-level way to obtain such a bound is \(\Delta\le\sum_j\mathbb E_P m_j^2\): the binary deficit in nats has the expansion \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}m^{2\ell}/[2\ell(2\ell-1)]\), bounded by \(m^2\ln 2\), including \(|m|=1\) by continuity. This is a sufficient condition. Checking it from a particular walk instrument requires its history-conditional transition laws. [Duchi, *Lecture Notes on Statistics and Information Theory*, entropy and Pinsker inequality](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee377/lecture-notes.pdf)

For the block construction above put \(p=(1-4^{-n}\binom{2n}{n})^b\), the fair probability of no tied block. Its likelihood ratio \(L\) equals zero and two on sets each of fair mass \(p/2\), and one elsewhere. Therefore \(\Delta=\mathbb E_Q[L\log_2 L]=p\) bits and the total variation distance is \(p/2\). The deficit per tick tends to zero even though the path discrepancy tends to one half. As little as one bit of total entropy loss spread over a growing experiment can leave a macroscopic signal that escapes every preassigned set of at most \(k\) observation times. This comparison presupposes a probability law for records; it tests what its conditional transition data imply.
### Applying the deficit test to the force plate

For the fully measured four-tick force experiment above, compare the complete record through physical time \(T\) with an independent fair flight from the *same joint release law*. Stop both records when \(|Y|>K\), preserving the exit position. Given the past, an active tick before stopping has \(|m_j|\le 2|F|\Delta K/\varepsilon\). Every other tick is fair. The bit deficit bound, applied conditionally on release and with identical deterministic padding after exit, bounds relative entropy in bits by

\[
(T/\Delta+1)(2|F|\Delta K/\varepsilon)^2
=16F^2K^2(T+\Delta)\delta^2/\varepsilon .
\]

Here the reference for the stopped law is the stopped fair walk, so the left side being bounded is relative entropy against that reference, rather than a count of bits after stopping. Pinsker bounds the discrepancy of **every** event of the stopped record. The full-record distance is at most that stopped distance plus the two exit probabilities. Compact containment from the maximal estimate above, also valid at \(F=0\), lets \(K\) increase after taking the mesh limit for tight releases and bounded \(\varepsilon\). Thus when \(\delta\to0\) and \(\delta^2/\varepsilon\to0\), the complete microscopic position and rail record converges in total variation to its fair reference **on the same mesh**. After release each rail is determined by the observed step. This covers fixed positive \(\varepsilon\) and the stated single-ray scaling. It strengthens weak convergence of rescaled paths for this particular detector. Its conditional transition formula still uses the assumed squared projective readout.
### A rail correlation that survives at the boundary

For the same fully measured instrument consider instead \(\delta^2/\varepsilon\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\), \(\delta\to0\), and a common release position converging in probability to a fixed \(y_0\ne0\). Take \(T>0\) and \(F\ne0\). This comparison starts from the stated localized release and does not require the unmonitored renewal preparation to work at this scaling. The maximal estimate above gives \(\sup_{j\le T/\tau}|Y_j-y_0|\to0\) in probability since \(\varepsilon\to0\). The fair reference also stays there. Nevertheless the two full record laws have a limiting total variation distance

\[
2\Phi\!\left(2|F y_0|\sqrt{T\lambda}\right)-1 .
\]

To see it, freeze the position entering each force plate at \(y_0\), retaining the incoming rail. Stop at the first departure from \(|Y-y_0|\le\eta\). The probabilities of a right step under the actual and frozen plates differ there by at most \(4|F|\delta^2\eta\). The frozen Bernoulli probabilities stay in \([1/4,3/4]\) for small mesh, and their one-step relative entropy from an actual Bernoulli is bounded in the actual-to-frozen direction by \((p-q)^2/[q(1-q)]\) using \(\log x\le x-1\). Over \(M=\lfloor T/\Delta\rfloor\) complete blocks the stopped discrepancy is therefore \(O(\eta\sqrt{M\delta^4})\) by Pinsker. The actual exit probability tends to zero at fixed \(\eta\); the frozen exit probability differs by at most this stopped discrepancy. Taking \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves equivalence in total variation. A final partial block contributes at most one more active tick with vanishing bias.

At each active tick multiply the incoming rail sign by the outgoing sign (\(+1\) for R). Under the frozen model these products are independent signs of common mean \(b=-\sin(8F\delta^2 y_0)\), since their conditional laws are constant given the entire past. Under the fair model their mean is zero. The complete-path likelihood ratio is \(\prod_{i=1}^M(1+bJ_i)\) for these observed products \(J_i\). It is monotone in their sum and the other ticks have ratio one. In the direction of the bias the standardized sum \(M^{-1/2}\sum_i\operatorname{sgn}(b)J_i\) has limiting laws \(N(0,1)\) and \(N(v,1)\), with \(v=\lim\sqrt{M}|b|=4|Fy_0|\sqrt{T\lambda}\), by the bounded-increment central limit theorem for independent signs. Expanding the log likelihood puts its zero threshold at \(v/2+o(1)\) on this scale. The difference of the two upper-tail probabilities is the stated distance. The distinction is entirely in recorded microscopic rail changes, even while both macroscopic positions converge to the same point.
