# What terminal walk probabilities determine about an entire flight

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

For a one-dimensional nearest-neighbor walk on one site parity, the site distributions at consecutive ticks determine each oriented edge flow. They allow a marker to keep its cumulative position rank throughout a flight. This marker has a local realization on the sharp signed Hadamard walk and follows a single Newtonian ray in the program's one-packet limit. At a crossing of two rays its path law differs from the law obtained by taking successive coarse readings. The construction assumes normalized squared site probabilities.

## Consecutive positions fix the edge flows

Work on the integer line with a normalized field supported on one parity, or with a cone on a ring that can be unwrapped. At a motion tick the on-site coin preserves the sum of the squared rail amplitudes, and the shift sends its two outputs one link in opposite directions. On-site plates can change the two rails while leaving total site mass fixed. At the arrival side of each shift write \(\mu_t(x)\) for the two-rail site mass and \(F_t(x)=\sum_{z\le x}\mu_t(z)\). The same argument applies with extra internal coordinates summed when the shift still has these two directions.

For \(x\) on the source parity, all mass at sites \(\le x-2\) enters \((-\infty,x-1]\), and none at sites \(\ge x+2\) can enter. Hence the edge masses out of \(x\) are

\[
 L_t(x)=F_{t+1}(x-1)-F_t(x-2),\qquad R_t(x)=F_t(x)-F_{t+1}(x-1).
\]

They are nonnegative, sum to \(\mu_t(x)\), and equal the quantum left and right daughter masses by local conservation and the unique predecessor of each oriented port. For any nearest-neighbor trajectory model having these site marginals the same cut identity holds. Take one uniform rank \(q\in[0,1)\). Assign it to site \(x\) when \(F_t(x-2)\le q<F_t(x)\). The lower portion of that interval up to \(F_{t+1}(x-1)\) goes left, and the rest goes right. Thus the *same* rank at every tick defines a nearest-neighbor path realizing all the oriented arrival panels. At an on-site plate keep its position. Plate output rails measured before the next shift would define a separate instrument.

With both site parities present, every path preserves its initial parity class through alternating ticks. Restrict the site distributions to one such class, apply the cut formula, and repeat for the other. This still determines the edge flows and admits a separate rank within each class. A single global rank need not realize them. Starting at adjacent sites with raw rail pairs \((1,1)\) and \((1,-1)\) moves half the mass each way across their common bond in one tick. The site distributions stay fixed, so a stationary quantile would miss both directed flows. On a four-site ring, either clockwise or counterclockwise flow takes the uniform occupied-even-site distribution to the uniform odd-site one. A cut without control of circulation cannot identify the two directions. Continuous one-dimensional quantile motion and random walks reproducing discrete quantum position distributions both have precedents. [Brandt et al., quantile motion](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9809053) · [Yamagami et al., quantum-walk-replicating random walks](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9021583)

## A local implementation with fair bits

For the unmodified Hadamard walk from one sharp port, normalize raw integer rail counts \((u,v)\) at tick \(t\) by \(2^{-t/2}\). Represent each possible marker at the site by an integer slot \(0\le i<u^2+v^2\). Begin at slot zero. In each tick append a fresh independent fair bit \(B\) to give \(z=2i+B\). The first \((u-v)^2\) slots go left and the others go right. Their lengths sum to \(2(u^2+v^2)\). When moving right, subtract \((u-v)^2\) to obtain the new local slot. When moving left, add the squared incoming right-rail raw count at the daughter to \(z\). This orders arrivals from the western predecessor before arrivals from the eastern predecessor. Empty bins receive no marker.

To verify the construction, let \(K_t\) be the total number of slots strictly west of the marker plus its local index. Doubling the parent bins and bisecting each at the left count groups consecutive blocks exactly as in the cumulative cut above. The two translation rules give \(K_{t+1}=2K_t+B\), including at the ends of the support. The half-open intervals with these binary prefixes therefore realize the fixed-uniform-rank law for every finite path cylinder. The marker needs a persistent integer slot, a fresh bit and access to both arriving field counts at reassembly. It need not scan the cumulative distribution or know a stopping horizon. These are explicit extra primitives of this model. For a sharp launch the all-right edge at depth \(H\) has probability \(2^{-H}\). Any sampler starting deterministically with only independent fair bits, using at most \(b\) bits on every run, assigns probabilities of the form \(k/2^b\), by padding shorter branches. Thus \(b\ge H\). This worst-case bound concerns randomness supplied, not the minimum average memory. For general prepared fields the continuous-rank construction still applies; the integer slot and fair-bit implementation proved here is specific to sharp unplated flights. ## The first two-time difference

Launch the sharp right rail at zero. Compare the all-launch event \(X_2=2,\ X_4=0\). On the nearest-neighbor line it is precisely the word \(RRLL\). The three numbers below concern different procedures.

| Procedure | Event probability | Calculation |
|---|---:|---|
| Keep the same rank. | \(0\) | The rank intervals for \(X_2=2\) and \(X_4=0\) are \([3/4,1)\) and \([3/16,5/16)\). |
| Split afresh according to the local squared outgoing fractions, independently of earlier moves given site and field. | \(1/80\) | The mass at \(+2\) is \(1/4\). A left move then has probability \(1/2\), followed by \(1/10\) at \(+1\). |
| Measure position at tick two and measure the endpoint at tick four. | \(1/16\) | Projection onto \(+2\) has probability \(1/4\) and leaves a sharp right rail there. Two further left moves reach zero with conditional squared mass \(1/4\). |

Among silent nearest-neighbor markers with all the original single-time panels, the full attainable interval for \(RRLL\) is \([0,1/16]\). At tick three, \(x=1\) has incoming right and left masses \(1/2,1/8\) and a left daughter of mass \(1/16\). Assign any \(\theta\in[0,1/16]\) of this daughter to the incoming left rail, which has the unique history \(RRL\). Assign its remainder to the incoming right rail and fill the right daughter by conservation. All earlier histories are fixed: there is a unique history at each site through tick one, and at the sole site with two histories at tick two the next move is deterministic. Future ticks can use the same site-based splitter for every \(\theta\). An actual midpoint projection can give the upper endpoint for this particular word while changing other probabilities. Merely conserving one marker and calibrating its oriented single-time reads does not select its history. ## The one-ray limit over a whole flight

Adopt the line, source, scaling, loss conditions and four-tick force clock of the program's [renewal preparation](Renewal_launch_and_classical_records.md). Condition on release first. The target is \(y(t)=Y_0+p_{\rm in}t-Ft^2/2\). At attained block times in a fixed compact interval the compact-momentum comparison error is uniformly \(O_T(\delta/\varepsilon+\delta^2)\). The cutoff source has vanishing position and momentum deviations, and under the continuum linear potential the position deviation about \(y(t)\) is bounded by the initial position deviation plus \(T\) times the momentum deviation. For each fixed Fourier test \(\exp(i\xi Y)\) away from the chart seam, the position characteristic function transfers to the lattice with a vanishing error. These estimates and the omitted source norm hold for any sequence of attained times converging to a time in the interval. [Four-tick comparison and its clock hypotheses](Four_tick_force.md)

Consequently for each \(r>0\), the supremum over attained block times of the one-time probability outside \([y(t)-r,y(t)+r]\) tends to zero. Otherwise select offending times, take a converging subsequence, and apply the characteristic-function convergence to its limiting point mass. For any fixed \(0<\eta<1/2\), both tails are eventually smaller than \(\eta\) at **every** block time. Every rank in \([\eta,1-\eta]\) then stays inside the tube simultaneously at all those times. Between block boundaries a rank path moves by at most four site pitches; the target changes by at most its modulus of continuity over four native ticks. Sending the pitch, tick duration, \(r\), and finally \(\eta\) to zero proves uniform-in-time convergence in probability on the flight interval for the interpolated rank path. There is no sum of error probabilities over the increasing number of ticks. Reinstating timeout as an isolated outcome costs its vanishing probability. This result covers the interval after release. It does not assert that every retry before release stays near a prescribed physical path.

## Which way a crossing continues

In the program's two-ray preparation with field reversal, the unprobed position marginals concentrate equally near \(-A(t)\) and \(A(t)\), with \(A(t)=\int_0^t(t-s)F(s)\,ds\). A fixed lower-half rank follows \(-|A(t)|\) and an upper-half rank follows \(+|A(t)|\). To justify this through a crossing, take any fixed tube radius \(d>0\). The two chart groups in the [sequential position proof, steps 1–3](Sequential_position_records.md) have squared norms tending to \(1/2\) and uniformly vanishing norm outside their own radius-\(d\) tubes, and the remainder has vanishing norm. When \(|A(t)|>d\) the tubes are disjoint. Projection onto either tube retains the corresponding group up to vanishing error and rejects the other up to vanishing norm, so interference cannot change the limiting half-mass there. When \(|A(t)|\le d\), the union lies within \(3d\) of either ordered target. Away from the exceptional ranks \(0,1/2,1\), these estimates give uniform tracking of the ordered targets, by first fixing a margin about those ranks and then shrinking it. This model has an ordering rule even when the two unprobed branches meet.

By contrast, the specified sequence of coherent coarse-bin measurements follows one of the signed branches \(\pm A(t)\) through the crossing. At fixed separated times on opposite sides of a sign reversal of \(A\), the probability of switching sides tends to zero for the silent rank path and to one for the measured coarse record (at nearby attained read times). For example, set the schedule in the definition of \(A\) to +1 up to time 1 and then −1. For \(t\ge1\), \(A(t)=t^2/2-(t-1)^2\), and the crossing occurs at \(2+\sqrt2\), after the force switch. This comparison is conditional on the two-ray source and a correctly implemented measurement. A model of the physical apparatus must explain which record it realizes. The comparison gives a full-flight criterion that one-time classical position limits alone cannot settle.

The [checker](check_rank_paths.py) follows every binary prefix through ten ticks for both launch rails, verifies each oriented occupancy against independently enumerated signed words, and keeps every two-time cell in the four-tick comparison. The numerical examples and Lean statements do not establish a probability law or a microscopic clock for the physical world.
