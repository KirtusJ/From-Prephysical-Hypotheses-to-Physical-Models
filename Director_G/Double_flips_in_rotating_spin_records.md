# Double flips in four-read nuclear spin trajectories

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Huie and colleagues recorded four successive spin readings from each retained ytterbium-171 atom. Re-reading their Figure 5(b), we find that about a quarter of trajectories with a bright first read change rail twice, whether we retain all initially detected atoms or only those detected again at the end. The first read randomly heralds a rail, and a dark result can include atom loss. The ideal analyzer sequence has trace parameter $r=2$. The [walk's two-mode coin test](Testing_an_unknown_two_mode_coin.md) requires $r=3/2$ and a matched preparation and detector. These records illustrate the joint event and its sensitivity to selection. Independent fair bits already predict the same quarter rate. [Huie et al., published Figure 5 and §IV B](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.030337) · [Authors' version 4, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.02926v4#page=6)

## Preparation, records and denominator

The apparatus prepares a spin along $+X$. It rotates each analyzer axis onto the fluorescent readout axis, interrogates the spin and rotates the basis back. The four successive analyzer directions in panel (b) are $+Z,+X,-Z,-X$. A bright reading of that analyzer is labelled 0. Write the observed four-letter word as $b_0b_1b_2b_3$ in measurement order. Use $b_0$ to herald the incoming rail. The first three readings give two successive measured transitions. Sum over **both** possible fourth readings, including 1111. The fourth bit is another spin record. The final *atom-presence* read used for postselection is a separate operation.

For a bright first read the rate is $(p_{0100}+p_{0101})/\sum_{b_0=0}p_b$. For a dark first read the numerator is $p_{1010}+p_{1011}$ and the denominator sums over $b_0=1$. Each denominator includes every preparation in its rail and stated inclusion class, regardless of the second result. Pooling the rails weights them by their observed occurrence. For any binary data, the pooled event probability also equals $P(b_2=b_0)-P(b_0=b_1=b_2)$ exactly.

| Atom-presence condition | First flip given 0 | Double flip given 0 | First flip given 1 | Double flip given 1 | Pooled double flip |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Initially detected | 0.51 | 0.25 | 0.47 | 0.24 | 0.25 |
| Initially and finally detected | 0.49 | 0.26 | 0.50 | 0.25 | 0.26 |

The largest change in a single plotted frequency is for 1111, from 0.096 to 0.063 under final-survival selection. The experimenters report that atom loss favors strings ending in dark readings. A dark spin record can therefore have more than one physical cause. [Huie et al., §IV B](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.030337)

## What the drawings tell us about counts and uncertainty

Director Z's cross-review identified a common integer lattice in each series. We repeated the bounded integer fit on both the arXiv and journal drawings. For each total $N=500,\ldots,7999$, we minimized the largest of the sixteen distances from $N p_w$ to the nearest integer, where $p_w$ is the normalized height of bar $w$. For the arXiv initial-only series the best total is 3445, with maximum residual 0.024 count. For the initial-and-final series it is 3013, with residual 0.030. The next best totals are their doubles. Multiplying an integer table by any positive integer preserves its normalized frequencies exactly. Even the digitized rational frequencies admit a larger exact denominator. The authors give no trajectory total or error convention in the caption of Figure 5. We use the best-fitting counts to estimate sampling uncertainty **conditionally**. The number of independent apparatus cycles or sites contributing to these histograms is a separate question. Each marginal interval treats the fitted heralded trajectories as independent Bernoulli observations. The four plug-in standard errors are 0.010–0.011. Inverting a two-sided 5% score test for any one prespecified row would challenge the ideal quarter when that row's interval excludes it. All four include it. This nominal comparison uses neither a multiple-testing correction nor an apparatus-level model for drift or shared noise. [Wilson score method](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm)

All 32 plotted error bars are retained. If the half-height $e_w$ is a binomial standard error, the effective total is $p_w(1-p_w)/e_w^2$. The sixteen initial-and-final values range from 3004 to 3021, with median 3013. The sixteen initial-only values range from 3003 to 3020, with median 3016, whereas their bar heights favor 3445. The Fig. 3 caption specifies a binomial convention for that figure. The origin of this discrepancy in Fig. 5 remains unresolved. The plotted bars and fitted-table intervals answer different conditional questions. [Source captions](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.02926v4) · [Per-word heights, uncertainty marks and fits](results/rotating_spin_records.json)

The samples overlap. If the two integer tables give counts from the same launches, subtracting in every word leaves 432 trajectories rejected by final detection. Of those, 75 double-flipped, compared with 771 of 3013 survivors. The initial-only total is 846 of 3445. Writing $w=432/3445$, the survivor-minus-initial rate difference is $w(p_{surv}-p_{rej})=0.0103$. Given the two cohort sizes and independent trajectories within and across the two disjoint strata, its plug-in standard error is $w\sqrt{p_{surv}(1-p_{surv})/3013+p_{rej}(1-p_{rej})/432}=0.0025$. This calculation conditions on final detection status, estimates a selection contrast, and assigns no causal effect to an intervention on the spin.

## Which walk can be compared

The four ideal analyzers are separated by equal quarter turns about $y$. With $V_j=R_y(j\pi/2)$ taking $+Z$ to the $j$th axis, the coordinates immediately before the next projection change by $V_{j+1}^\dagger V_j=R_y(-\pi/2)$. In this ideal description the repeated two-mode rotation has $q=1/2$ and $r=|\operatorname{tr}R_y(-\pi/2)|^2=2$. Its projected double-flip probability is $1/4$ from either prepared rail. This coordinate relation assumes the specified rotations and projective records. The observed entries above can be extracted before making any assumption about those dynamics.

The cubic target in the coin test has $r=3/2$. Its all-free-clock imitation has a double-flip excess of $1/256$ under persistent coins and perfect readout. The measured change between the two inclusion rules here exceeds that difference in magnitude. It cannot supply a correction factor for a different apparatus. This experiment supplies a measured two-flip event and a concrete dependence on the treatment of loss. Using it as the missing cubic record would require matching the generator, preparation, intervening measurement, detector and denominator. Even within this spin apparatus a persistent hidden coin and one redrawn after each projection agree on all histories when every step has flip probability one half. These observations leave that persistence premise open.

The fourth spin read gives a check on the assumption of a stable, symmetric flip probability. Form the three successive flip indicators from each four-letter word. For any mixture of fixed per-launch flip probabilities $q$, with independent flips conditional on $q$, every specified three-flip-indicator sequence with $k$ ones has probability $\mathbb E[q^k(1-q)^{3-k}]$. Reordering the same number of ones preserves this probability. A model that redraws $q$ independently at each tick also has this symmetry. Among the three possible *two*-flip patterns, the pooled initial-detection frequencies for 011, 101 and 110 are 0.128, 0.116 and 0.129. Requiring final survival gives 0.134, 0.120 and 0.131. With the fitted counts and independent trajectories, each pairwise difference has multinomial standard error $\sqrt{(p_i+p_j-(p_i-p_j)^2)/N}$. The largest observed separations are 0.013 and 0.014, each about 1.5 of its pairwise standard errors. These pairs were selected after looking at the table. A reproducible order effect could challenge the symmetry of the transition or readout model. A classical model that draws a four-bit word from this histogram reproduces every frequency exactly.
