# Two recorded flips of a single nuclear spin

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Huie and colleagues interrogated individual ytterbium-171 nuclear spins four times per experimental trajectory. Their Figure 5(b) publishes all sixteen four-bit frequencies under two different rules for retaining shots. Together these give an empirical check on the joint event required by the [two-mode coin test](Testing_an_unknown_two_mode_coin.md). Conditioning on a bright first read, the rate of a dark second read followed by a bright third read is about **0.253** per such preparation. Requiring the atom also to survive through the final read increases the estimate to **0.261**. [Published experiment and correction record](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.030337) · [Authors’ version 4, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.02926v4#page=6)

## Preparation, records and denominator

The apparatus prepares a spin along $+X$. It rotates each analyzer axis onto the fluorescent readout axis, interrogates the spin and rotates the basis back. The four successive analyzer directions in panel (b) are $+Z,+X,-Z,-X$. A bright reading of that analyzer is labelled 0. Write the observed four-letter word as $b_0b_1b_2b_3$ in measurement order. Use $b_0$ to herald the incoming rail. The first three readings give two successive measured transitions. Sum over **both** possible fourth readings, including 1111. The fourth bit is another spin record. The final *atom-presence* read used for postselection is a separate operation.

For a bright first read the rate is $(p_{0100}+p_{0101})/\sum_{b_0=0}p_b$. For a dark first read the numerator is $p_{1010}+p_{1011}$ and the denominator sums over $b_0=1$. Each denominator includes every preparation in its rail and stated inclusion class, regardless of the second result. Pooling the rails weights them by their observed occurrence. For any binary data, the pooled event probability also equals $P(b_2=b_0)-P(b_0=b_1=b_2)$ exactly.

| Atom-presence condition | First flip given 0 | Double flip given 0 | First flip given 1 | Double flip given 1 | Pooled double flip |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Initially detected | 0.5080 | 0.2528 | 0.4716 | 0.2386 | 0.2456 |
| Initially and finally detected | 0.4946 | 0.2610 | 0.5017 | 0.2508 | 0.2559 |

The pooled change is about 0.0103. The largest bin change is 1111, which falls from 0.0958 to 0.0634 when final survival is required. The authors report that atom loss favors strings ending with dark readings. An initially detected atom can be lost before the last read, so its subsequently recorded dark bit has an ambiguous physical meaning. The two estimates use overlapping sets of shots. Their difference describes sensitivity to conditioning. The supplied histogram contains the plotted frequencies and uncertainty marks. The extracted rectangles give neither exact shot counts nor sampling covariance. We assign no confidence interval or significance to that difference. [Experiment, Sec. ## Which walk can be compared

The four ideal analyzers are separated by equal quarter turns about $y$. With $V_j=R_y(j\pi/2)$ taking $+Z$ to the $j$th axis, the coordinates immediately before the next projection change by $V_{j+1}^\dagger V_j=R_y(-\pi/2)$. In this ideal description the repeated two-mode rotation has $q=1/2$ and $r=|\operatorname{tr}R_y(-\pi/2)|^2=2$. Its projected double-flip probability is $1/4$ from either prepared rail. This coordinate relation assumes the specified rotations and projective records. The observed entries above can be extracted before making any assumption about those dynamics.

The cubic target in the coin test has $r=3/2$. Its all-free-clock imitation has a double-flip excess of $1/256$ under persistent coins and perfect readout. The measured change between the two inclusion rules here exceeds that difference in magnitude. It cannot supply a correction factor for a different apparatus. This experiment supplies a measured two-flip event and a concrete dependence on the treatment of loss. Using it as the missing cubic record would require matching the generator, preparation, intervening measurement, detector and denominator. Even within this spin apparatus a persistent hidden coin and one redrawn after each projection agree on all histories when every step has flip probability one half. These observations leave that persistence premise open.
