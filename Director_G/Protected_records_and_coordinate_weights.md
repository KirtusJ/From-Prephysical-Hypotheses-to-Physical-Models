## A coherent comparison

At first take a finite bank of even width with a basis of labeled coordinates. The apparatus can mix every pair of a chosen ordered perfect matching by the real Hadamard $H(a,b)=(a+b,a-b)/\sqrt2$, implement signed permutations and append clean modes. Keep the following assumptions explicit.

1. The final coordinate read has ordinary nonnegative, additive, normalized outcome probabilities. A basis source gives its named output with certainty. Adding clean coordinates preserves the original probabilities. A signed permutation carries them to the correspondingly permuted outputs. Equality of two complete signed vectors with the same final apparatus gives identical probabilities, including when prepared by different coherent programs. This comparison includes any memory or environment still coupled to the outputs.
2. A protected binary record keeps its marginal. More precisely, for a unitary program $V$ preserving a coordinate subspace and its complement, the probability of that subset at the end agrees with its initial probability. It suffices here to have this for the matched pairs and protected selector subsets used below. There is an apparatus formulation. Let $J$ copy the subset predicate into a clean pointer by a coordinate permutation. Then $(V\otimes I)J(\psi,0)=J(V\psi,0)$. If the marginal of the *unmeasured coherent copy* persists on the left, pure preparation equivalence, covariance of $J$ and clean padding give the direct subset marginal on the right. The circuit must allow this spectator operation. Mere survival of a bit read and stored earlier does not imply the equation for a coherent comparison.
3. Distinct trials can be compared at a common clock, with the same final read and the same explicitly supplied ancillary state. Interior route details that remain distinguishable in another system would invalidate the claimed equality of complete preparations. Probabilities for an unknown vector are otherwise unrestricted.

Fix $S=2^t$. For every signed integer vector $x$ with $\sum_i x_i^2=S$, interpret $x/\sqrt S$ as the normalized *amplitude* vector. Orthogonal evolution preserves this normalization independently of a probabilistic interpretation. Any such column is exactly synthesizable at this depth on the stated bank. When $t>0$ the number of odd entries is even by the norm identity, as is the number of even entries after padding to even width. Pair like parities. The new numerator pair at the one-smaller depth is $((a+b)/2,(a-b)/2)$, with half the original squared norm. Iteration reaches one entry $+1$ or $-1$. Reverse the matching sequence and signed initial permutation to synthesize the column. This is a special column form of exact synthesis for real orthogonal matrices with a common power of $\sqrt2$ in the denominator. [Amy, Glaudell, Li and Ross](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11305) · [Lean parity identity](lean/CoherentComparison.lean)

Denote a standalone probability by $p_x(i)$. Use a selector $A,B$ and an initially blank private bit. In **both** programs the supplied column enters $A$, the first input of every ordered selector pair $(A,B)$. Hence $H(x,0)=(x,x)/\sqrt2$ and $H(y,0)=(y,y)/\sqrt2$. If $y$ enters $B$ with that same ordering then $H(0,y)=(y,-y)/\sqrt2$, which leaves the final $B$ half with the wrong sign. For each data cell the split preserves its original total, and exchange of its two equal selector halves fixes the complete vector. Each half, summed over its two private slots, therefore has weight $p_x(i)/2$ or $p_y(i)/2$. [Ordered selector in both routes](check_coherent_comparison.py)

On the $x$ route apply $U_yU_x^{-1}$ to $B$, protecting the $A,i$ marginal. On the $y$ route apply $U_xU_y^{-1}$ to $A$, protecting the $B,j$ marginal. During the $2t$ matching layers the inactive half pairs each coordinate with its private partner. The sum over both private values is protected throughout and $H^{2t}=I$ restores the blank **amplitude** at the end. Conditional signed permutations in the active half can be padded by identities.

Both routes now give exactly the same vector, $(x,y)/\sqrt2$ with the private bit blank. When $x_i=y_j$, the two ordered pairs of private amplitudes at $(A,i)$ and $(B,j)$ are identical. Swapping the entire two-slot subsets fixes the full vector, so covariance equates their protected marginal sums. This step requires no assignment of probability to an individual private slot. It gives
$$
                    p_x(i)/2=p_y(j)/2.
$$
There is no conditioning in this equation. If an individual final private slot is read, a third preparation certifies its blank. Synthesize the joint column directly on the selector and data bank at depth $t+1$, take $t$ pairs of identity matching layers $H^2=I$, and append the clean private bit. Equivalently prepare with this bit as a protected spectator. This takes $3t+1$ balanced layers from a localized source, the same as preparing a supplied column in $t$ layers and running either comparison in $1+2t$. Match any separately timed permutation or padding operations as in assumption 3. Clean enlargement gives total mass zero on private value one, hence zero on each such slot by nonnegativity. Complete-preparation equivalence transfers this conclusion to the two routes.

Switching one sign preserves its coordinate probability by signed-permutation covariance. Equal depth and numerator magnitude therefore give a common probability $f_t(|n|)$. At any depth a normalized reference with an appended blank has zero probability there by clean padding and normalization. Compare a zero entry of the unknown table to this reference blank. This argument also covers odd depths, at which a single localized occupied cell cannot have an integer numerator over $\sqrt{2^t}$. [Exact two-route circuits and fourth-power controls](results/coherent_comparison.json)

An immediate normalization proof uses the uniform vector with $S$ unit numerators and a second vector with one signed $n$ and $S-n^2$ unit numerators, padding either with zeros. The uniform probabilities are $1/S$, so the distinguished port has probability $n^2/S$. This establishes the result at all accessible signed coordinates, including zero. No continuity, ensemble limit, or full set of real projectors is invoked. [Lean comparison and normalization theorem](lean/CoherentComparison.lean)

There is a finite sensitivity check for this larger calibrator. Suppose the uniform reference is exactly equiprobable and blank ports are sharp as specified and each comparison of equal signed entries used by this test agrees to within $\delta$ in probability. Each of the $S-n^2$ unit ports of the calibrator is within $\delta$ of $1/S$. Normalization bounds the distinguished port's error by $(S-n^2)\delta$. One more comparison with the target gives $|p_x(i)-n^2/S|\le (S-n^2+1)\delta$. This is a deterministic error bound conditional on a simultaneous bound for the compared probabilities. Repeated-run estimates of the constituent probabilities also have sampling error.

## Eight calibration coordinates at any depth

The uniform calibrator costs $2^t$ modes. The same implication follows using reference vectors of width at most eight, uniformly in $t$. The unknown input may itself be wider. Let $b=\lfloor\sqrt{S/2}\rfloor$. For $1\le n<b$, write $S-2n^2-2=p^2+q^2+r^2+s^2$. Such a representation exists by Lagrange's four-square theorem, with zeros permitted. Compare the two standalone normalized rows of numerators
$$
 (n+1,n-1,0,0,p,q,r,s),\qquad(n,n,1,1,p,q,r,s).
$$
Both have squared norm $S$. Subtract their normalization equations using cross-preparation equality for every common coordinate value. This gives $f_t(n+1)+f_t(n-1)=2f_t(n)+2f_t(1)$. Since $f_t(0)=0$, induction gives $f_t(n)=n^2 f_t(1)$ through $b$ for $t\ge1$. When $t$ is odd, two entries $2^{(t-1)/2}$ normalize the table. For even $t\ge2$, four entries $2^{t/2-1}$ do so. All are in the small range, hence $f_t(1)=1/S$. At depth zero the localized source is sharp. [Lagrange’s original proof](https://yuvalfilmus.cs.technion.ac.il/Manuscripts/Lagrange%20four%20square%20theorem.pdf)

For $b<n\le\sqrt S$, represent $S-n^2$ by four squares. Every filler has magnitude at most $b$, since the remainder is smaller than $S/2$. Normalization gives $f_t(n)=n^2/S$ as above. This supplies all signs, sharp zero ports and padding. For an input of even padded width $d$, the comparison requires at most $4\max(d,8)$ logical modes including the coherent label and private bit. A separate coherent indicator for the pointer formulation can double this bank. The numeral size and time still grow with depth.

At $1\le t\le14$ the proof uses 289 recurrence pairs, 127 large-amplitude rows and 14 normalizers, with the localized depth-zero case handled separately. The checker compiles a slightly larger grid. It includes the boundary $n=b$ whenever $2b^2+2\le S$, adding seven recurrence pairs, and tests a signed depth-zero large row. Its 424 compilations therefore comprise 296 recurrence comparisons and 128 comparisons of a large row to a sign-flipped copy. The 877 signed depth/value cases run from $t=0$ through $14$. ## A second test for a postselected input with arbitrary integer norm

A branch taken on its own generally has integer numerators $a_i$ with $Q=\sum_i a_i^2$ different from a power of two. A conditional probability can be obtained from the intact synchronized experiment as above. The following alternative works directly with the supplied branch, even without a known dyadic preparation route, on different operational assumptions.

Take a common positive multiple $L$ of all nonzero $|a_i|$. Choose $D=2^s>L$ and $m=L$, or any positive multiple $m<D$ of $L$. Conditional on coordinate $i$ build a normalized ancillary column with $a_i^2$ success entries of signed value $m/(a_iD)$ and $F=D^2-m^2$ failure entries of value $1/D$. For an empty input coordinate choose one success entry $m/D$ and the common failure entries. Orthogonal input labels make the different columns independent for compilation. Each column is dyadic with squared norm one. The parity synthesis above applies at depth $2s$. The implemented checker also reduces groups of four odd entries with the two-tick Walsh transform. On the joint input, every occupied successful cell has the *same signed amplitude* $m/(D\sqrt Q)$, and each individual failure tag leaves the original signed coordinate vector times a common scalar.

Assume the original coordinate’s complete marginal persists in this protected-label test, the successful fine coordinates have equal probabilities, empty cells are sharp, and each failure tag of positive probability restores the original standalone coordinate law upon conditioning. The last hypothesis is the equivalence of the reset preparation with the original input. It does not fix the chance of that failure. If $h$ is the total success probability, additivity gives $p_i=h a_i^2/Q+(1-h)p_i$. A nonzero $h$ implies $p_i=a_i^2/Q$. No formula for $h$ is needed; $h=0$ leaves the equation empty. For a non-dyadic reciprocal such as $1/3$, deterministic splitting into nine equal unit contributions under a protected input label cannot be implemented directly with synchronized Hadamards and signed permutations. The finite herald circuit avoids requiring such a matrix entry. [Gate recipes and retained counterrules](results/heralded_weights.json) · [Formal column and cancellation results](lean/HeraldedWeights.lean)

A finite repeatability check bounds this inference. If the observed old marginal and the mixture $h a_i^2/Q+(1-h)p_i$ agree within $\epsilon$, the resulting bound is $|p_i-a_i^2/Q|\le\epsilon/h$ for $h>0$. A small positive success probability can sharply limit experimental sensitivity. The reset assumption is separate from the algebraic equality of the failure vectors.

## Local implementation and an obstruction on the single ring

The matching construction permits routing between distant logical modes. Two finite local implementations specify what this access costs. All coordinates used for calibration occupy one parity bank of a flat even ring. A logical matching flips the parity on which that bank is read. Let $U=SH$, where $H$ mixes the two coins locally at each site and the stream $S$ moves right or left by one. The plus-rail basis has $2N$ modes, hence $N$ per site parity. Distinct labels in a larger input, including sheet and pointer labels, can be encoded into this bank once its size is large enough. For abstract finite words there is also a constructive encoding. Enumerate the complete physical endpoints, including clean pointer slots, in a parity bank on which both the preparation and the comparator run. Every original elementary coin is an ordered matching in that encoding, and each shift or reversible copy is a permutation. Permanent padded spectator pairs can undergo Hadamards on themselves while empty. Unused slots do not acquire amplitude. The initial occupied port maps to a single localized mode. For a copied predicate, use distinct wire copies for the pointer alternatives and apply the same routing word independently on each copy; an exchange controlled by the predicate can be implemented as a permutation when the copy is initialized. For a scheduled measurement defer the pointer read to the final common read. This explicit simulation realizes the finite preparation and its calibration on the same encoded carrier. Preservation of native clock durations, geometrical site distances, or the statistics of a separate physical walk under this encoding requires its own argument.

**Two propagation orientations.** On a second rail put $U^\mathsf{T}=H S^{-1}$. This rule is local; it streams first in the reverse direction and applies the real coin at the receiving site. Let $W=\operatorname{diag}(U,U^\mathsf{T})$ and exchange corresponding rails by the local pulse $J$. Then $J W J=W^{-1}$ exactly. An inverse costs one positive walk tick plus two rail-exchange pulses. There is no period hypothesis. A local direction exchange $X_x$ may be applied immediately before or after the coin at a selected site. On the plus rail the pre-coin tick is $U X_x$ and the post-coin tick is $S X_x S^{-1}U$. Composing the first on the left, or the second on the right, with the implemented inverse isolates $X_x$ or $S X_x S^{-1}$, respectively. Each takes two forward ticks. Shared switches on the other rail are allowed; with that rail blank at a logical boundary its companion action leaves it blank at the next one. It can be populated during the inverse.

The exchanges join $R_x$ to $L_x$ and $R_{x+1}$ to $L_{x-1}$. Their graph is connected on each site parity since alternating these edges advances a site by two. Edge transpositions on a connected graph generate every permutation of its vertices: to swap the ends of a path, swap forward along all edges, then reverse along all but the last. Intermediate wires return to their original values. Orient and route a desired matching into the local $(R,L)$ input pairs, apply one $U$, then route its streamed outputs to the desired labels of the other parity bank. This realizes an ordered matching with positive time and local controls. For any individual sign flip, conjugate a swap on one mixed pair by two such matching layers, since $HXH=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$; the other pairs take $HIH=I$. Inactive signs may be padded with the same two layers. Extra pairs of ticks implement exact identities. A pulse controller may be hardwired into a finite periodic clock by assigning the pulse and matching at each phase and advancing the clock cyclically. This supplies a signed degree-two graph: each homogeneous clock slab is a real orthogonal balanced step followed or preceded by permutations. The controller, switches, clean spectator registers, readout and preparation equivalence remain physical inputs.

On $N=6,10,18$ the tested non-neighbor matched layers took 37, 113 and 425 forward walk ticks respectively. These are serial upper bounds for those specific settings. Against the complete physical basis the test also checks both echo orders, all local generators, the other rail and constructive and destructive superpositions. Omitting one rail exchange fails on every tested size. Substituting the forward tick for an inverse happens to pass the echo control on $N=2$; it fails on every other tested size. There is a stronger obstruction for controlled forward ticks on many single rings. Model one step on the flat ring by its unnormalized signed numerator matrix, and allow any site-dependent sign changes or coin exchanges immediately before or after the fixed balanced coin. Modulo two each local coin has the same all-ones matrix $K$. Thus the tick is always $B=\operatorname{diag}(z,z^{-1})K$ over $\mathbb F_2[z]/(z^N-1)$. Its power is $B^t=(z+z^{-1})^{t-1} B$ for $t\ge1$. If $N$ has an odd divisor $m>1$, evaluate at a primitive $m$-th root in a finite extension of $\mathbb F_2$. Its sum with its inverse is nonzero, as equality to its inverse would make its order divide two. Either input coin then has a nonzero column for every $t$. At positive even depth a deterministic normalized basis output would require every numerator divisible by $2^{t/2}$. At odd depth its only nonzero numerator would have to be $\pm2^{t/2}$, which is not an integer. Therefore no such forward-only word sends even one localized input exactly to one basis port. This includes arbitrary scheduled swaps and signs in the allowed locations. It leaves approximate or heralded protocols and powers-of-two rings of larger size open.
