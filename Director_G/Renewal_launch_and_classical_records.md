# Recycling failed direction records prepares a classical launch

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Mathematical development and exact checks by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A sharp walker at a fixed home can supply a narrow packet with probability tending to one before a vanishing deadline. A rejected direction record triggers a site read and a local return of the same walker. Early stopping gives an exact mean of \(4m\) native Hadamard ticks to acquire an \(m\)-block packet. All finite deadlines have a separate timeout outcome. The result addresses source yield for free and constant-force terminal measurements. Its physical inputs include the switchable readout, a local return channel, a coherent hold and a clock.

## One attempt and its reset

Launch at site \(0\), rail \(R\). Apply two successive signed Hadamard ticks \(U=SH\), with \(H(r,l)=(r+l,r-l)/\sqrt2\) and conditional shifts right and left. Look for the opposite rail at the end of each pair. A pass flag leaves the positions of the passing rail coherent. A failure records the clicked site \(x\) and rail. After the first failure, route the localized walker along adjacent links to the same home, set \(R\), and restart. After \(m\) passes, with \(m\) even, hold the surviving spatial amplitude undisturbed. Keep every failure record in a separate transcript. A position-conditioned neighbour swap is reversible on its ports. Retaining the transcript makes return to a common input compatible with distinct failure histories. The apparatus must permit deterministic routing between ticks; freely waiting on the failed vector would perform a different operation.

Concretely, an array can send the unwanted rail at each site to a distinct persistent failure label, leaving every passing port with the same blank site label. Its projectors are the full desired-rail projector and the individual rejected ports. A failure puts that branch into a local return mode pointing home. A synchronized block counter can route a passing branch to its next block, or put it into the hold at the last block. It requires no site-index signal on a passing branch. Local clocks, blank registers, control of the hold, and supply of fresh transcript capacity until timeout are apparatus assumptions. They prevent a signal from the far edge being needed to reset a centralized controller on *every* passing block. An implementation that collects every blank detector at home before the next tick would need a new time bound. All read probabilities below assume the squared signed sum law on complete pointer records and its conditional update on a selected branch. A complete site read on the passing rail changes the prepared state. Let \(p_j=\binom{2j}{j}/4^j\), with \(p_0=1\) in this subsection. The off-diagonal entries of \(U(k)^2\) have magnitude \(|\sin k|\). Thus the joint probability of the first \(j\) switches is the Fourier average of \(\sin^{2j}k\), namely \(p_j\). First failure at block \(j\) has mass \(p_{j-1}-p_j\) and \(|x|\le 2j\). Reinitialization at the same home on every branch gives each new attempt this same distribution conditioned on its transcript. The expected active blocks in one attempt are \(A_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}p_j\). The ratio \(p_j/p_{j-1}=(2j-1)/(2j)\) gives by telescoping

\[
 A_m=2mp_m,\qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^m j(p_{j-1}-p_j)=A_m-mp_m=mp_m.
\]

The probability of \(k\) fully failed attempts is \((1-p_m)^k\). For any nonnegative cost accumulated in one attempt, the total mean through success is its one-attempt mean divided by \(p_m\), by summing over attempts reached. This holds when failure locations and costs are correlated. There are therefore exactly \(2m\) expected active blocks, \(4m\) native moving ticks, and at most \(2m\) expected nearest-neighbour return slots. The latter uses \(\mathbb E[|x|;\mathrm{failure}]\le2mp_m\) per attempt. The mean number of failures is \(p_m^{-1}-1=O(\sqrt m)\). The result assumes that direction read/control takes boundedly many clock slots per block, return takes at most a constant per traversed link and reset takes a bounded number of additional slots per failure. These conditions give \(O(m)\) expected total slots. The initial material preparation of the instrument and its growing spatial footprint are separate costs.

## A finite deadline for a packet at home

Use site pitch \(a=\varepsilon\delta\) and native tick duration \(\tau=\varepsilon\delta^2\). Take \(\varepsilon\to0\), \(\delta/\varepsilon\to0\) and an even \(m\sim\delta^{-2}\varepsilon^{-1/2}\). Each clock slot above must fit in \(O(\tau)\). A local return moves at most one link per route slot. The microscopic speed scale is \(a/\tau=1/\delta\) in these macroscopic units; the limit does not impose a fixed microscopic speed in those units. Choose the deadline \(d\) as \(\varepsilon^{1/4}\) rounded up to a common clock boundary with \(O(\tau)\) error. On an unfinished branch write TIMEOUT and stop. A branch already in the hold is released at the common deadline. The clock for decoding its two real rails starts at release. A hold is the normalized identity on the prepared rails, with no uncontrolled relative phase, free walk or force. Store the success time separately if rail phases are compared. The online protocol takes finitely many steps and records at every scale. From the infinite-horizon expectation, Markov's inequality gives

\[
 \Pr(\mathrm{TIMEOUT})\ \le\ O(m\tau)/d
 =O(\varepsilon^{1/4}).
\]

All completed branches hold the same normalized packet up to a real sign. Each interrupted branch is counted at timeout, including walkers partway home. On a line the launch has fixed physical location \(Y_0\) under a choice of coordinate origin. On a periodic device take a growing circumference divisible by four with the preparation and flight cones inside the seam. For maximum \(n\) free ticks after release, a radius bound \(2m+n+2\) about home suffices for all retries because every attempt starts there and the return takes the direct path. For a schedule with at most \(D\) slots the finite transcript can store the ordered failures, clicked sites in \([-2m,2m]\) and the final status, using \(O(D\log(m+2))\) bits. The ideal coherent memory and controller may occupy this growing array; no constant physical apparatus cost follows.

At release the signed tallies at \(x=2l-m,\ 0\le l\le m\), are \((-1)^{m-l}\binom ml\) in rail \(R\). On that parity write the decoded field \(i^x(R+iL)\), with the release clock set to zero. Its normalized folded Fourier amplitude is \(c_m\cos^m q\) up to an overall sign, \(-\pi/2\le q<\pi/2\). There are two elementary width estimates. The position law has weights \(\binom ml^2/\binom{2m}m\), hence site variance \(m^2/(2m-1)\) by the hypergeometric second moment. The folded momentum density satisfies \(\mathbb E\sin^2q=1/(2m+2)\) by the same cosine integrals that give \(p_m\). Since \(|q|\le(\pi/2)|\sin q|\), the centered deviations in physical position and scaled momentum \(p=q/\delta\) obey

\[
 \sigma_Y=\frac{a m}{\sqrt{2m-1}}=O(\varepsilon^{3/4}),
 \qquad
 \sigma_p\le\frac{\pi}{2\delta\sqrt{2m+2}}=O(\varepsilon^{1/4}).
\]

For a prescribed fixed launch momentum \(p_{\rm in}\), supply one position-dependent release plate. It acts by the real rotation of \(R,L\) through \(p_{\rm in}\delta x\), hence multiplies the decoded field by \(e^{ip_{\rm in}\delta x}\) and shifts folded momentum upward by \(p_{\rm in}\delta\). Its input ramp is measured from home. The uncertainty bound about \(p_{\rm in}\) uses translated chart distance; a fixed scaled-momentum cutoff near \(p_{\rm in}\) discards vanishing norm, well away from the seam. The position law is unchanged. For zero launch momentum the plate is unnecessary. On the finite release cone its angles can be approximated by powers of the matched integer rotation \((3+4i)/5\) with common padding, as for the force plate. A uniform one-time norm error \(o(1)\) suffices. No uniform time or power bound is known for this synthesis. A vanishing physical launch duration with that implementation requires its one-time gate clock to fit an additional \(o(1)\) time, and flight plates must fit their stated block clock. ## Position law with every original launch retained

For each fixed bounded flight time \(T\), use the four-tick force protocol for \(V(Y)=FY\) on a fresh ensemble, with block times tending to \(T\). The free case has \(F=0\). To justify using the Gaussian with a compact-chart theorem, multiply its translated Fourier profile by a smooth, real cutoff equal to one around \(p_{\rm in}\) and supported in \(|p|\le P\) for fixed \(P>|p_{\rm in}|\). The omitted norm is \(o(1)\) by the momentum estimate. In the continuum momentum chart \(Y-Y_0=i\varepsilon\partial_p\) in coordinates based at home. The unshifted profile has derivative norm times \(\varepsilon\) equal to \(\sigma_Y\) by parity Fourier isometry. Translation preserves this derivative norm on the folded circle. Multiplying by the cutoff adds \(O(\varepsilon)\). Thus the normalized cutoff vector has vanishing deviations in position about home and momentum about \(p_{\rm in}\).

For the continuum solution transported from \(p\) to \(p-FT\), differentiating its scalar phase \(-[Tp^2/2-FT^2p/2+F^2T^3/6]/\varepsilon\) bounds the position deviation about \(Y_0+p_{\rm in}T-FT^2/2\) by its initial deviation plus \(T\) times the initial momentum deviation. The four-tick norm comparison gives \(O_T(\delta/\varepsilon+\delta^2)\) on that fixed cutoff and finite force, including free motion. Restoring the omitted norm costs \(o(1)\). To transfer position concentration to lattice sampling, test the bounded exponential \(e^{i\xi aX}\) at fixed \(\xi\). Its momentum shift is \(-\xi a\) in \(q\), or \(-\xi\varepsilon\) in \(p\), so its overlap for the compact profile agrees with the continuum overlap once the supports stay away from the seam. Norm error controls this bounded observable. Convergence of the characteristic functions gives weak convergence of the lattice terminal position law. The time from sharp launch to the attained terminal read differs from the release-relative flight duration by \(o(1)\) under the clock hypotheses. Extended-state dispersion in coined walks was studied independently by de Valcárcel, Roldán and Romanelli. [Four-tick bound](Four_tick_force.md) · [Continuum phase and lattice position comparison](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md) · [External wave-packet study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0976)

The observable on the whole initial ensemble takes values in the position line augmented by an isolated TIMEOUT symbol. The timeout mass tends to zero. All successful records give the same terminal position law, so the unconditional observable converges weakly to the point mass at \(Y_0+p_{\rm in}T-FT^2/2\). This concerns one terminal read per ensemble. The argument leaves the microscopic origin of the instrument, square score, scales, force and clock open. In particular a full coordinate read on both rails produces a different yield \(2^{-m}\), and a bare direction-only apparatus with no position-dependent restart does not implement this preparation.

## Exact finite records

The checker propagates integer amplitudes and independently compares each survival mass with the binomial formula. Its concrete schedule charges three slots per two-tick block including its read, one slot per returned link, and two further controller slots at each failure. The deadline recursion sums all histories that fit, including a failure at the last filter. For \(m=2\) an explicit failure-prefix tree reproduces the recursion. The test gives a one-attempt yield \(3/8\), mean eight native ticks and two return slots, and mean total \(52/3\) slots. At deadline 24 the released mass is \(393/512\) with timeout \(119/512\). The earliest possible successful deadline is six slots. The recorded panel includes all failure coordinates for \(m=2,4,6,8,12\).
