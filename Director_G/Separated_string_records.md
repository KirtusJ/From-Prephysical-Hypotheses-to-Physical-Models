# Can separated occupation records select the ordinary probability law?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist  
Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Four sites suffice for a comparison that previously required a proposed two-copy gate. Two pairs of dressed hopping operations on the same prepared string give exactly the same two modular descriptions of a named detector. One circuit returns the source with certainty. In the other, independently calibrated conditional records predict a click at that detector. This gives a finite test of a whole family of arithmetic detector laws. A larger finite-memory family can reproduce every prescribed finite panel of ordinary sharp records. Its obstruction appears on arbitrarily long sequences. The results below describe calculations and possible measurements. No physical detection frequencies enter them. ## One source and the same two detectors

Use four sites \(0,1,2,3\) of the open two-occupant string. The label \(xy\), \(x<y\), includes the unique electric flux on the links between the occupants. Prepare \(s=02\) by routing the sharp adjacent pair \(01\) across the middle bond. On a singly occupied bond the dressed tilt acts by \(R=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&-4\\4&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)/5\). Empty and doubly occupied bonds are fixed. Let \(J\) denote the exact signed quarter turn on sites 1 and 2. With composition written rightmost first, compare \(U=R_{23}R_{01}\) and \(V=J^{-1}UJ\). The two tilts in \(U\) commute. After the first route in \(V\), the pair is \(01\) up to sign, so the two tilts act trivially and the reverse route recovers \(02\). An exact middle quarter turn is available in the routed library. It can also be synthesized as \(RT^{-1}\) on that bond for \(T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}4&3\\-3&4\end{smallmatrix}\right)/5\) if reciprocal addressed tilts are available.

For \(A=U,V\), reduce the **backward** effect \(A^*P_jA\) over \(\mathbb Z[i,1/5]\) modulo \(1+i\) and modulo 3. In the first field an \(R\) tilt fixes its labels. In the second it swaps its two sites. The two site permutations for the latter field are \((01)(23)\) for \(U\) and \((02)(13)\) for \(V\). Both exchange the unordered pairs \(03\) and \(12\). Thus the ordered pair of reduced projector labels at port \(03\) is \((03,12)\) for **both** circuits, and that at \(12\) is \((12,03)\) for both. Detector labels, initial preparation and physical Gauss boundary are held fixed. Any response determined by these two reduced projectors for this source and this port must give identical click probabilities across the two circuits. Sharp control \(V\) forces each mixed port in \(U\) to be dark. An observed difference at either port would already challenge that class, with detector error and uncertainty accounted for by the comparison. No identification between differently named detector ports is needed here. On the subspace with one occupant in each cell \(\{0,1\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\), the middle flux is fixed to one. The left and right flux bits are determined separately by their local occupants. Even the uniform electric phase restricts to \(w^{y-x}=w^{-x}w^y\) there. These statements identify the tensor coordinates for this sector. A source prepared by routing one sharp pair has a common preparation history. It can carry correlations into the two detector records. Boundary flux is relevant when dividing a gauge system into regions, and statistical independence of sources is itself a separate hypothesis in network experiments. [Donnelly](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.0036) · [Branciard, Rosset, Gisin and Pironio](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.4502)

Write \(q=9/25\) for the calibrated probability that an occupant stays on its starting site after \(R\). Both single-cell marginals can equal \(q\) while the probability \(u\) that both occupants stay ranges over \([\max(0,2q-1),q]\). In order \(02,03,12,13\) the joint table is \((u,q-u,q-u,1-2q+u)\). Drawing one common residue sets \(u=q\). Independent draws give \(u=q^2\). The sum of the two mixed bins differs by \(2q(1-q)=288/625\). For the common-residue distribution this is also its total variation distance from the product table. Changing the temporal order of the two spatially disjoint rotations does not distinguish them.

There is an operational way to test the product value. After the left tilt, copy its occupation into a blank pointer, read it, and retain the label. At this time the right occupant is still at 2. Apply the calibrated right tilt. Measure its stay frequency **in each heralded left branch**, comparing it with the corresponding sharp launch in the same apparatus. If both conditional frequencies are \(q\), multiplying the recorded conditional probabilities gives \(u=q^2\) and each mixed bin \(144/625\). A reversible pointer copy controlled on the left occupation commutes exactly with the dressed right tilt. The checker tests this on the complete four-site two-occupant basis for both pointer inputs. Moving an actual read in time also requires that its environment preserve the joint statistics. Transporting the result to the original terminal read requires agreement between the comparator with the pointer and the terminal apparatus without it, or an explicit implementation of the latter using that same pointer. In particular, equal populations of the right input before both heralded trials do not establish that equality. The weaker observation of a larger mixed frequency for \(U\) than \(V\) already tests the reduced-effect law directly. [Operational equivalence of procedures](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406166)

## Classification on any finite array

Fix a sharp pair \(s\) among \(n\ge3\) sites. Suppose a normalized complete read has a **common**, nonnegative response \(F(a,b)\) to the two reduced effect labels at this fixed source. Specify \(F(s,s)=1\). Calibrate the stay and transfer outcomes of each routed single \(R\) tilt from \(s\) to a pair \(v\) sharing exactly one site with \(s\). In reduced-effect notation these are \(F(s,v)=q,\ F(v,s)=1-q\). Exact signed routes transport the tested bond to any two physical sites. Using a single response across read labels is an additional premise of this classification. The preceding same-port test also works with separate response tables for different ports.

Let \(A\) be the pairs sharing exactly one site with \(s\), and \(B\) the pairs disjoint from \(s\). The complete family of such tables is

| Arguments | Response |
|---|---:|
| \(s,s\) | \(1\) |
| \(s,a\) and \(a,s\) for \(a\in A\) | \(q\) and \(1-q\), respectively |
| \(s,b\) and \(b,s\) for \(b\in B\) | \(t\) and \(1-t\), respectively |
| Both arguments different from \(s\) | \(0\) |

For \(n\ge4\) the single free parameter ranges over \(0\le t\le1\). For \(n=3\) there are no disjoint pairs; for \(n=2\) there is one normalized outcome. The classification allows arbitrary detector responses at the outset within the two-reduction hypothesis, including dependence on all named sites.

**Proof.** In either field a native word induces an actual permutation of the sites on pair labels. In the second field an addressed \(R\) gives the transposition of that bond while leaving the first permutation trivial. Exact signed swaps give the same site permutation in both fields. Thus every relative site permutation \(g\in S_n\) can occur, and every native frame has the form \(\{(x,gx)\}_x\) after reindexing. In particular \(\sum_x F(x,gx)=1\). The relative permutations here act on unordered pairs through their two endpoints.

If \(g\) fixes \(s\), the term \(F(s,s)\) exhausts the sum. The stabilizer of \(s\) acts transitively on \(A\) and on \(B\), since it can independently permute the two sites of \(s\) and all sites outside. Thus \(F\) vanishes on \(A\times A\) and \(B\times B\). If \(p\in A,\ b\in B\), choose an edge \(a\in A\) with one endpoint in \(b\). There is a site permutation carrying the ordered adjacent edges \((s,p)\) to \((a,b)\): send the shared vertex to the shared vertex and extend the bijection of the three endpoints. Then \(gs=a\in A\) and \(g^{-1}s\in A\). Their calibrated row and column exhaust the frame sum. Nonnegativity gives \(F(p,b)=0\). The inverse permutation gives \(F(b,p)=0\).

It remains to link the weights involving \(B\). For arbitrary \(b,c\in B\), map the two disjoint domain edges \(s,b\) to the two disjoint target edges \(c,s\), then extend to a site permutation. The surviving terms give \(F(s,c)+F(b,s)=1\). The independent choices of \(b,c\) force the displayed constants. Conversely, when \(gs=s\), a frame sums to one from the sharp term. When \(gs\in A\) or \(gs\in B\), \(g^{-1}s\) has the same overlap type, so the two surviving terms again sum to one. This proves sufficiency for every native frame. The Lean proof constructs physical four-site permutations, proves the zero entries for integral-scaled weights and checks the frame-sum reduction on arbitrary finite lists. The real-valued and arbitrary-site arguments are given above. [Lean proof](lean/ResidueFrames.lean) · [Finite enumeration](results/residue_frames.json)

In the \(U\) experiment the allowed outputs in this table are \(02\) and \(13\), with weights \(t\) and \(1-t\). Measuring just the left stay marginal under the simultaneous disjoint tilts to be \(q\) therefore fixes \(t=q\). At every analyzer for this sharp launch the table is now exactly \(q\,\mathbf1_{a=s}+(1-q)\mathbf1_{b=s}\). Either positive mixed bin would exclude the whole class even with \(t\) unrestricted. An arbitrary earlier coherent preparation requires an additional equivalence to the sharp launch followed by its preparation word to transport this classification.
