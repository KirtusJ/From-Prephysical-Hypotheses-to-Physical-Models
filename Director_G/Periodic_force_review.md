## Comparing the quantum states

Use angular coordinates of period \(2\pi\), \(a=2\pi/N=\varepsilon\delta\), \(\tau=\varepsilon\delta^2\), and physical Fourier momentum \(p=\varepsilon\ell\). Take \(4\mid N\) to put the edge \(-\pi/2\) exactly on the momentum grid. On the two-particle torus use the product of the two positive-curvature sections. \(J\) maps each scalar Fourier mode into the shifted lattice index with multiplier \(M(a\ell)=\chi(a\ell)u(k_*+a\ell)\), using the product version in two dimensions. A smooth cutoff compactly supported inside a chart makes this a contraction. Its inner product differs from the original one by the omitted Fourier tails; on bounded semiclassical \(H^4\) states the lost norm is \(O(\delta^4)\). The normalized initial walk can be taken as \(J\psi/\|J\psi\|\), incurring the same order. Treating arbitrary restrictions as exact isometries would lose this initial-state condition.

For a fixed Fourier shift \(l\), multiplication by \(e^{il\cdot Y}\) before or after encoding gives a difference bounded by the Lipschitz norm of \(M\) times \(a|l|\). On the continuum the shift acts on the entire Fourier sequence. On the lattice it wraps modulo \(N\). Both nonzero cutoff windows, including their translates by the finite set of potential harmonics, lie strictly inside the carrier chart once \(N\) is large. At most one representative per index contributes there. This proves \(\|W_NJ-JW\|\le C a\sum_l|l||W_l|\). A sampled *exponential* of a cosine has infinitely many harmonics. Integrating the generator difference between the two unitary potential exponentials bounds the one-kick error by \((\tau/\varepsilon)Ca\). This avoids assuming that the phase multiplier itself has finite Fourier support.

The free walk's demodulated selected-band phase has local error \(O(\delta^4|p|^4)\) against the kinetic step. On the compact chart this yields a state error \(O(\delta^4\|\psi\|_{H^4_\varepsilon})\); the same bound applies after a potential kick because the scaled Sobolev norms are stable. Here \(H^s_\varepsilon\) weights Fourier coefficients by \((1+|\varepsilon\ell|^2)^{s/2}\). For real, fixed smooth \(W\), commuting \(p^\alpha\) through \(W\) in the evolution equation contributes at least one factor \(\varepsilon\), which cancels division by \(\varepsilon\). Grönwall gives uniform \(H^4_\varepsilon\) bounds on fixed time intervals for initially bounded families. Lie splitting of the scalar flow has local error \(O_T(\tau^2/\varepsilon)\) on these states, since \([p^2/2,W]\) has \(H^1_\varepsilon\)-to-\(L^2\) norm \(O(\varepsilon)\). These arguments apply unchanged to \(W=\kappa(1-\cos(Y_1-Y_2))\) on the fixed configuration torus. The split order must match the departure-time kick.

Telescoping unitary steps gives \(O_T(\delta+\delta^2/\varepsilon+\tau/\varepsilon)\) on \(t=n\tau\le T\), plus initial error and any implementation error. This proves the fixed-action Schrödinger comparison as \(\delta\to0\), and a simultaneous comparison for \(\varepsilon\to0\) if \(\delta^2/\varepsilon\to0\). Uniform semiclassical fourth moments are sufficient for this explicit rate. For fixed \(\varepsilon\), density and unitary stability extend qualitative strong convergence to each fixed \(L^2\) input. This does not yield uniform convergence over the unit sphere. A bounded, smooth periodic \(W\) on the infinite line has a similar continuous-momentum shift proof, with separated cutoff windows before folding. A finite torus calculation does not prove convergence to arbitrary real-line data or an unbounded force field.

## Position readout and classical motion

Let \((q(t),v(t))\) solve the classical Hamilton equations for \(p^2/2+W\). For each torus coordinate set \(Z_j=e^{iY_j}-e^{iq_j(t)}\) and \(P_j=p_j-v_j(t)\). The Heisenberg derivation \(\partial_t+(i/\varepsilon)[H,\cdot]\) gives
\[
 D Z_j=i e^{iY_j}P_j+i v_j Z_j+(i\varepsilon/2)e^{iY_j},\qquad
 D P_j=-\partial_j W(Y)+\partial_j W(q).
\]
A periodic \(C^2\) potential has gradient difference bounded by a constant times the sum of coordinate chord distances. Duhamel in norm and Grönwall therefore control the sum of \(\|Z_j\psi_t\|\) and \(\|P_j\psi_t\|\) by \(C_T\) times their initial sum plus \(O_T(\varepsilon)\). Periodized Gaussian packets of width \(O(\sqrt\varepsilon)\) have vanishing initial deviations and bounded scaled \(H^4\) moments. On the circle a central plane-wave index may be chosen nearest to \(v_j(0)/\varepsilon\), with momentum error at most \(\varepsilon/2\). Transfer position concentration through bounded angular Fourier multipliers using the same shift-commutator estimate for \(J\), or use chord-distance squares directly. A momentum readout tested against a bounded continuous function of decoded physical momenta also follows from the state-norm comparison. Unbounded momentum moments on the walk require separate tail estimates. These are probabilities at specified times. Repeated observation of a trajectory would require an instrument and its backaction.

## Constructing the field from integer walks

On a second ring of \(N\) sites let \(B=2I+T+T^{-1}\) and \(D(d)=(B^m)_{00}-(B^m)_{d0}\). Its eigenvalues are \(\lambda_l=4\cos^2(\pi l/N)\). In the Fourier expansion of \(D\), the constant cancels and the paired first harmonics contribute \(2\lambda_1^m F(d)/N\), where \(F(d)=1-\cos(2\pi d/N)\). In the normalized profile \(R=2D(d)/D(N/2)\), write \(R=(F+A)/(1+b)\). With \(r=\lambda_2/\lambda_1\) one has \(A\ge0,\ b\ge0\) from \(1-\cos\ge0\); counting the \(N-3\) remaining modes gives \(A\le(N-3)r^m\) and \(b\le(N-3)r^m/2\). Hence both \(A\) and \(F b\) lie in \([0,(N-3)r^m]\), proving the claimed uniform bound on \(|R-F|\). The denominator is positive because the first harmonic contributes at the antipode. For \(N>4\), \(\log r=-2\int_{\pi/N}^{2\pi/N}\tan s\,ds\le-3\pi^2/N^2\). For \(N=4\) the tail vanishes. The stipulated \(m=N^2\lceil\log_2 N\rceil\) thus gives fast polynomial decay. Since \(1/N=\varepsilon\delta/(2\pi)\), the shape error divided by \(\varepsilon\) still tends to zero in the stated joint limits.

For odd sheet order \(M\), a doubled gain \(2h\) acts on character \((M-1)/2\) as \(e^{-2\pi i h/M}\) in the convention \(T\mapsto e^{2\pi ir/M}\). The conjugate character reverses its sign. If the scale \(L\) itself is rounded to match \(M\kappa\delta^2/(2\pi)\), and \(h\) then rounds \(L R\), both rounding errors are \(O(1/M)\) in the step phase because \(R\) is uniformly bounded. Evaluation from integers is exact when \(L\) is specified as an integer and the rounding tie convention is fixed. The accumulated error is \(O_T((N-3)r^m/\varepsilon+1/(M\tau))\). Thus the physical choice of sheet order, orientation, coupling strength and a common sheet interaction has content. Path counts construct a gain profile from the specified cycle; this does not derive that cycle, its relative-coordinate coupling, physical units or the preparation from observation.

## Independent numerical and adverse checks

- I implemented the two-coin walk in the invariant zero-total-momentum Fourier slice \((\ell,-\ell)\), encoded a coherent superposition of \(\ell=0,1,2\) and used \(\varepsilon=\kappa=1\). I applied the ideal relative-coordinate phase in position space with a radix-two FFT, the exact four-component coin in momentum, and both carrier corrections. The scalar comparison independently exponentiated the *unsplit* Fourier Hamiltonian with diagonal \(\ell^2+1\) and nearest off-diagonal entries \(-1/2\), using degree-13 local Taylor polynomials. At \(N=32,64,128\) the attained times were 0.23132, 0.25060, 0.25060, and the full spinor errors were 0.028013, 0.007348, 0.001865. Increasing the scalar cutoff from 10 to 16 and its time subdivisions from 100 to 200 together changed these references by at most \(4.4\times10^{-15}\). This compares the prescribed complex phase on a particular set of states; the small refinement differences are numerical diagnostics.
- For a free positive-curvature band the quartic term is \(-q^4/6\). With \(\varepsilon=\delta^2,\ \delta=(2\pi/N)^{1/3}\), momentum nearest 1 on the continuum grid, and \(t\) nearest 1 on the time grid, the demodulated residual phases were \(-0.166553,-0.163666,-0.165913,-0.166590\) at \(N=256,4096,65536,1048576\). A single scalar phase can be observed relative to a second free mode with zero momentum. This scaling cannot give general convergence of coherent states to the full scalar propagator.
- Replacing \(W\) by its tangent along the classical orbit gives a potential residual proportional to the square of the packet width, divided by \(\varepsilon\) in the Schrödinger generator. For a coherent width \(\sqrt\varepsilon\) this is generally order one. At a stationary point of \(W=1-\cos Y\), the tangent is zero, while the rescaled potential on \(Y=\sqrt\varepsilon z\) tends to \(z^2/2\) after division by \(\varepsilon\). Equal limiting position centres can coexist with different packet shape and phase. A tangent-force argument alone gives no general strong Schrödinger comparison.
- I computed exact integer count rows of \(B^m\) by repeated cyclic addition and compared their rational \(R(d)\) to the cosine with 150-digit arithmetic at \(N=8,12,16,20,24\). The analytic error bound held at every site for both \(m=1\) and the stated depth. At \(N=16\) the maximum error was 1.7071 at depth one and \(1.374\times10^{-53}\) at depth 1024. The doubled gain identity held for all integer gains from \(-100\) to 100 at odd orders 5, 7, 11, 15 and 21. The reviewed version of the project's periodic checker used a rounded cosine sample directly. The independent path counts verify the supplementary shape estimate; neither calculation is a physical measurement.

The analytic conclusions concern a supplied, normalized initial sector and band, an abelian character description, the selected update, the stated record law and the prescribed scaling at finite time. The shared finite sheet in the interaction is an additional model assumption. The quantum and classical limits do not identify an absolute complex embedding, a microscopic law from empirical evidence, or an arbitrary continuum potential from this single integer profile.

## Subsequent audit of folded native preparation and four classical branches

I reviewed the proposed extension after the preceding torus proof, including its specified zero-gain preparation stage and nonzero interaction in flight. The starting sites and directions of both walkers must be known after the sheet filter. For heralding, the scaled initial relative angle must tend to a point \(r_0\) with \(\kappa\sin r_0\ne0\). A coarse measurement must distinguish the two labelled positions in the product torus. If both walkers start at coincidence with zero velocity, all four limiting positions stay there. If the apparatus measures only relative separation, the two opposite-curvature branches always give the same relative readout.

**Preparation on the quotient ring.** With interaction disabled, the two-walker direction-switch preparation factors as a tensor product on the two bases, times the identity on the sheet. For known initial coins/sites the two required records have the same success probabilities in each sheet sector, so conditioning leaves the sheet-sector weights unchanged. Any preceding sheet-selection cost still applies. For an even number \(m\) of opposite-direction two-step reads on one walker the Fourier factor is \(\sin^m k\) up to a constant phase and the initial-site phase. For definite starting sites the two-walker success probability is \(S_N^2\), where \(S_N=N^{-1}\sum_j|\sin(2\pi j/N)|^{2m}\). The continuous mean equals \(\binom{2m}{m}/4^m\sim1/\sqrt{\pi m}\). The profile has variation four, so the absolute error of its grid mean is \(O(1/N)\), relative \(O(\sqrt m/N)\). Thus the joint success conditional on entering this stage is asymptotic to \(1/(\pi m)\). The scale \(m\asymp\delta^{-5/2}\) gives vanishing joint yield, as well as vanishing relative quadrature error with \(N=2\pi/\delta^2\). The labelled-time schedule switches off a nonzero interaction; if the walkers lack a zero-gain stage, the factorization requires another argument.

A finite-ring position estimate does not require \(m<N\); that inequality eventually fails in this scaling. For \(h(k)=\sin^m k\), multiplication by the angular position observable shifts Fourier modes by one grid cell. Cauchy–Schwarz on the \(N\) adjacent arcs bounds \(\sum_j |h(k_j+a)-h(k_j)|^2\le a\int|h'|^2\). Since \(\int |h'|^2/\int |h|^2=m^2/(2m-1)\), the normalized position chord deviation about the known initial site is \(O(a\sqrt m)\). Smooth band projection costs \(O(a)\) under this shift and a smooth fixed-\(p\) cutoff costs \(O(a/\delta)=O(\varepsilon)\). For the scaled momentum localization, write \(q\) for distance to the nearest edge. The weighted moment \(q^2\cos^{2m}q\) has integral \(O(m^{-3/2})\) and variation \(O(m^{-1})\) on one peak, by the Gaussian bound and monotonicity on either side of its maximum. Its normalized grid mean is therefore \(O(m^{-1})\). Dividing by \(\delta^2\) gives squared momentum deviation \(O(\delta^{1/2})\) and justifies a smooth cutoff equal to one on \(|p|\le1\) and zero on \(|p|\ge2\). Such a cutoff gives uniformly bounded fourth moments for the subsequent comparison.

**Band labels, conjugation and what the record resolves.** Translating a grid mode by \(N/2\) exchanges the two momentum peaks without changing \(|h|^2\). The two coin-band projections at each edge have equal diagonal entries on a definite incoming coin. Section smoothness and concentration then give mass \(1/4+o(1)\) per one-walker chart and \(1/16+o(1)\) per two-walker chart. A chart is specified by an edge and a band for each walker. The two-particle eigenphase can be degenerate, but the free coin diagonalizes on each product section; the generator commutator estimate measures coupling to the other sections and applies individually. It avoids an assumption of a joint-band spectral gap. Cross terms of distinct edge charts vanish for any fixed angular Fourier observable once the charts are separated from its finite index shift. At identical edges with different band choices, section orthogonality at the same momentum and smoothness at the shifted momentum bound the cross term by \(O(a|l|)\) for the harmonic \(l\). The finite sum of these terms vanishes. This gives weak convergence of position readouts; indicators of bins also follow when no limiting atom is on a boundary.

For fixed oriented potential write \(F_0=\kappa\sin r_0\). With curvature signs \(b_i\in\{-1,1\}\), the signed kinetic equation has time derivatives \(\dot q_i=b_i p_i\), \(\dot p_1=-\kappa\sin r\), \(\dot p_2=+\kappa\sin r\). The finite-time Sobolev, splitting and chord estimates used above continue to hold with signed kinetic coefficients because the free flow is unitary and signs change none of the norm bounds. Each sign pair is represented by four of the 16 tensor charts, hence has weight \(1/4\). At zero initial momenta the four acceleration vectors \((-b_1F_0,b_2 F_0)\) are different. At a sufficiently small *fixed* positive time the limiting positions are distinct in the product torus. For \(b_1=-b_2\) the relative angle stays equal to \(r_0\) exactly, and both positions move by \(-b_1 F_0 t^2/2\). A fixed coarse position bin containing one limiting point and excluding the other three has probability tending to \(1/4\). Under a coherent bin instrument it keeps that group with error tending to zero, since the norm of the wrong-bin part of each chart tends to zero. This resolves a group of spatial trajectories, not the four microscopic chart pairs within it.

For real initial data in the original sheet basis and real preparation maps, the complex-conjugate sheet amplitudes and their future evolutions remain conjugate under every real coordinate instrument. Corresponding real, sheet-unresolved records have exactly equal probabilities in the two orientations. Conjugation swaps the carrier edges and reverses the effective curvatures and force sign simultaneously, giving the same spatial accelerations. The observable branch is identified by its position bins, not by an absolute curvature or character sign. A position record in a single run is subject to the chosen instrument. Restoring an unresolved sheet sector outside the selected pair adds its residual probability to an error budget.

These finite checks address the preparation, exact reality symmetry and ODE consequences.
