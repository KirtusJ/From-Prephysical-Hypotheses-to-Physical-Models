# Which recorded walk bins can be split exactly into equal cells?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

After four signed Hadamard ticks from the right rail at the origin, site $+2$ has rail amplitudes $3/4$ and $1/4$. A reversible circuit can transform these two entries into ten entries of $1/4$ while leaving the recorded site-membership bit fixed. If the record protects the right rail separately, its single amplitude $3/4$ cannot be split into nine entries of $1/4$ by the same class of deterministic controls. The distinction can be decided for every finite integer table. The earlier [protected-coordinate experiment](Protected_records_and_coordinate_weights.md) gives a heralded construction for individually protected labels. Here the question is what can be done deterministically when amplitudes can interfere inside the recorded bin.

## Exact criterion for two known preparations

Use a common real amplitude scale and let $x,y$ be two finite signed integer rows in one bin. Permit blank coordinates, signs, permutations and dyadic orthogonal matrices on the padded register. A dyadic rational belongs to $D=\mathbb Z[1/2]$. A four-coordinate operation below is the normalized $H\otimes H$, with every other coordinate fixed. In the ordinary qubit circuit model the complete padded power-of-two-dimensional dyadic orthogonal matrix has an exact Toffoli–Hadamard implementation with clean computational ancillas. This requires coherent controls on the padded modes. The time cost and physical reach of the gates are separate from the algebraic criterion. [Li, Ross and Selinger](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01175) · [Amy, Glaudell, Li and Ross](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11305)

For a nonzero row define $g(x)$ as the positive odd part of the gcd of its entries. Set $g(0)=0$. **A padded dyadic orthogonal operation maps $x$ to $y$ exactly if and only if**

$$
  \sum_i x_i^2=\sum_i y_i^2,\qquad g(x)=g(y).
$$

For several protected bins both conditions hold separately in each bin. The circuits can act independently there, including coherently on a superposition of the specified bin vectors. Both rows may be dyadic after a common amplitude scale. Multiply both by the same power of two and apply the integer criterion. Multiplying by an additional power of two leaves the gcd's odd part unchanged.

Orthogonality preserves the first quantity. The ideal over $D$ generated by all input coordinates is $g(x)D$. Each output is a $D$-linear combination of inputs. Applying the inverse gives the reverse inclusion of ideals, so the two odd generators agree. Zeros added to a bin leave the ideal unchanged. This obstruction also survives standard full-register Hadamards, whose entries may have a common odd power of $\sqrt{2}$ in the denominator. Each ideal equality then holds over $D[\sqrt{2}]$. A rational member of this larger ring already belongs to $D$, by linear independence of $1,\sqrt{2}$ over $\mathbb Q$. Equality of the two integer-generated ideals therefore forces their generators' ratio and reciprocal to be dyadic. Thus allowing these additional gates cannot improve the criterion on two integer rows at the same scale. Different input/output global scales must first be accounted for.

## Constructing the circuit

Flip signs to make the entries nonnegative. Apply the following four-coordinate transformations within the bin.

1. For a positive even $a$, use $(a,0,0,0)\longmapsto(a/2,a/2,a/2,a/2)$.
2. If all positive entries are odd and two differ, choose $a>b>0$, and use
   $(a,b,0,0)\longmapsto(u,v,u,v)$, where $u=(a+b)/2, v=(a-b)/2$.

Each displayed transformation is one normalized $H\otimes H$ with the specified input ordering. It is its own inverse on the four slots. Pad with zeros when needed. Every transformed entry is a positive integer. With $E=\sum_i x_i^4$, the first move decreases $E$ by $3a^4/4$. In the second move, writing $a=u+v, b=u-v$, the decrease is $12u^2v^2>0$. The nonnegative integer $E$ cannot decrease indefinitely. The remaining positive entries must all equal a common odd number. Invariance of the ideal identifies this number as $g(x)$, so the final table has exactly $N=\sum_i x_i^2/g(x)^2$ occupied cells. The zero row stays zero. Sort these cells into the same chosen order for both tables. Run the first table's circuit and then undo the second table's circuit.

Each step also increases the occupied count, by three or two. It never requires more than $\max(W,N)$ coordinate slots when the initial table has width $W$, because a step needs exactly as many zero slots as the subsequent increase in support. For conversion between two tables, take $W$ as the larger of their two original widths. When the record fixes the slot partition, apply this bound to each sector individually. The sum of the per-sector capacities suffices. This bounds data-coordinate width before rounding up to a qubit register or adding synthesis workspace. The circuit depends on the known table. It need not implement specified conversions of two independent source vectors simultaneously. Such a request would at least require preservation of cross inner products within the bin.

The fourth-power descent was supplied by Director G's independent classical-records reviewer. [Lean 4.19 arithmetic](lean/StableBin.lean) verifies the integral pair splitting, its square-sum identity and the positive symbolic fourth-power decrease. The termination, gcd classification and interpretation of a gate as a physical control are the arguments above.

## Comparing protected records

In the four-tick example, $H\otimes H$ sends $(3,1,0,0)$ to $(2,1,2,1)$. Splitting both 2s gives ten ones. The complement already has six unit-magnitude numerators before sign correction. This yields sixteen equal amplitudes across both recorded alternatives. Two separately protected nonempty bins can each be flattened to one common amplitude exactly if their odd contents agree, allowing sufficient padding. At the fixed input numerator scale, the specific target of one unit per cell requires odd content one in every nonempty bin. The one-rail example has content three. In contrast, the pair $(3,5)$ can be converted to 34 ones. Both entries carry odd factors individually, but their gcd is one.

The proposed physical comparison can first copy the bin value into a blank bit. The circuit acts inside the two sectors of that bit, and a success herald is unnecessary. To infer counts from the construction, the experiment still needs evidence that the complete binary marginal is unchanged by its within-bin circuit, the equal final cells are operationally interchangeable, empty ports stay dark, and adding the disjoint cell counts recovers the same binary record. Under those premises all sixteen cells in this example have probability $1/16$ and the $+2$ result has probability $10/16$. Without these premises the circuit establishes a relation between signed preparations. A detector with access to labels moved by the circuit, or an unobserved site or rail record that remains entangled with the source, may respond differently. Coherent interference inside an arbitrarily chosen, spatially distributed bin and control of any external record are substantive capabilities. An untouched spectator factors through the result for any source whose two bin vectors are the stipulated ones tensored with their respective spectator vectors. Arbitrary unknown environment amplitudes would require a common operator on every corresponding source column.

Its [results](results/stable_flattening.json) give the completed sweep counts for every signed integer row of lengths one to four with entries from $-3$ through $3$. Individual words cover an isolated factor three, two equivalent rows with common odd content three, a zero table, an unequal-norm pair and a dyadic example. These records describe computations.
