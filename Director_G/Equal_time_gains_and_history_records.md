## Same-length abundance on a finite carrier

Use a finite strongly connected directed graph. Every vertex has $d\ge2$ outgoing edges to distinct vertices. An edge carries a gain in the cyclic group $\mu_q$ of $q$-th roots of unity. Fix a start $s$. Let $H$ consist of the ratios of gains of two closed directed walks at $s$ of the **same** length. Concatenating pairs multiplies their ratios; interchanging the pair takes inverses. Hence $H$ is already a subgroup. Write $h=|H|$. Appending a common return to $s$ shows that every ratio of two paths from $s$ to the same endpoint at the same length lies in $H$. A common approach and return lets one transport a closed pair based at another vertex to $s$. In particular $H$ is independent of the start. A change of phase convention at every vertex or a common phase per tick cancels from every ratio. Ordinary closed-path holonomy omits this clock comparison. [Voltage lifts and character decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04463)

**Constructive abundance.** Let $y_1,y_2$ be distinct vertices with a common successor. They belong to the same cyclic class of the underlying digraph. Thus paths $P_1,P_2$ from $s$ to these vertices can be chosen at one common length $D$. One elementary proof takes the gcd $p$ of return lengths at $s$. Paths to a fixed vertex have one length residue modulo $p$, by appending a return. Appending the edges to the common successor makes the two residues coincide. Finitely many return lengths already have gcd $p$; their nonnegative sums contain all sufficiently large multiples of $p$. For this last assertion divide by $p$, fix one generator $a$, choose a nonnegative representative sum in each residue modulo $a$, and add copies of $a$. Prepending such returns synchronizes the two lengths. [Periods of directed graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05762)

For $h>1$ choose same-length closed words $U,V$ whose ratio generates $H$, which exist by its definition. Let $L$ be their common length. In $B$ successive blocks with either word in each slot, the walks with exactly $j$ copies of $V$ have the same gain and there are $\binom Bj$ distinct walks. For $B\ge\max(K,h+1)$, the choices $1\le j\le h$ cover a common coset of $H$, each with at least $K$ walks. Append the two exits. At the single length $BL+D$, each of the two endpoints contains a full coset in abundance. Other paths to a given endpoint at that length stay in the very same coset. As an alternative multiplicity bound, encoding one bit by $UV$ or $VU$ gives $2^k$ choices at a common gain, after which $h$ blocks suffice to choose the residue. The Lean result proves this encoding for two actual closed edge lists with primitive gain difference, and for two equal-length exits. [Formal statement and common horizon](lean/GainAbundance.lean)

An exact finite test also works before finding the two words. In additive gain exponents choose a path to each vertex $v$, with length and integer gain $(l_v,b_v)$, taking the empty path at $s$. For each edge $u\to v$ of exponent $w$ form $(l_u+1-l_v,b_u+w-b_v)$, and include $(0,q)$. Let $\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z^2$ be their integer span. Residuals telescope around every closed walk. Conversely, appending the same return from $v$ to the two candidate prefixes expresses each edge residual as a difference of closed-walk pairs of length and gain. A zero-length combination therefore splits into two concatenations of closed words of equal actual length, modulo $q$ in gain. The vertical slice of $\Lambda$ is precisely the lift of $H$.

For computation, let $\Delta$ be the gcd of the absolute two-by-two determinants of the generators and $p$ the gcd of their first coordinates. A Bézout combination supplies $(p,b)\in\Lambda$. Subtract suitable multiples from each generator to leave vertical vectors $(0,v_i)$. They generate the vertical slice with step $u=\gcd_i |v_i|$; the gcd of all determinants is then $pu$. Here $u\mid q$. Thus **$h=q p/\Delta$**. Unrestricted abundance as stated in G10 Corollary 5.13 at the declared order holds at a merging pair precisely when $H=\mu_q$ (in this nontrivial branching setting). Variable-length reachability alone can give a different answer. Exact lifted supports, integer path counts and determinant arithmetic were compared separately. [Checker](check_gain_abundance.py) · [Finite graph trials and controls](results/gain_abundance.json)

**Agreement with Z15.** Let $Q$ be the group of gains of closed walks of unrestricted length at $s$. Their gains form a group because the semigroup is finite. Write a vector in $\Lambda$ of length $p$ as $(p,b)$. Then $Q/H$ is cyclic, generated by the class of $b$: every residual has length a multiple of $p$, and removing that multiple of $(p,b)$ leaves a vertical vector. The horizontal subgroup of lengths at gain zero in $\Lambda$ is therefore $p[Q:H]\mathbb Z$. Its positive generator is the period of the reachable component of the voltage lift. Indeed a difference of root walks at equal *gain* can be turned into a difference of zero-gain return walks by appending the same root loop of inverse gain. Conversely every such return contributes its length. Thus the lift period divided by the base period is exactly $[Q:H]$, including when the declared lift has several components. If physical edge gains are first projected by a nonfaithful character, this calculation uses their image. Z15 distinguishes actual group-element coverage from coverage of scalar character values. [G10 Proposition 5.14 and Corollary 5.15](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/3b61b19/edition/PAPERS/G10_Histories_as_Events_the_Order_of_the_Pair_Rule_First_Order_Weights_an/source/body.tex)

The returned period formula agreed with an independent reachable-component period calculation in all 22 cyclic trials. On Z15’s four-vertex example the base period is one, the lift period is two, and both selected endpoints have exponents $\{0,2\}$ at time four and $\{1,3\}$ at time five. On the Z15 challenger’s separate $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ control the full lift has period two, while its projection to the sign character has period one. ## The Z15 probability theorem on the attainable domain

Assume the G10 carrier hypotheses: its unnormalized gain matrix has one nonzero unit entry per edge and $A^*A=dI$. Suppose there is a merge. The rule for a history set $S$ at a fixed length is

$$
 \mu_\phi(S)=\sum_y\phi(|v_S(y)|),\qquad
 v_S(y)=\sum_{\gamma\in S:\,\gamma\to y}g(\gamma),
 \qquad \phi(0)=0,\quad\phi(1)\ne0 .
$$

Continuation appends every allowed next edge to every history in $S$. **On every attainable endpoint radius**, continuation consistency with a constant positive factor holds exactly for factor $d$ and

$$
 \phi(|\beta|)=\sum_{j} c_j\,|\sigma_j(\beta)|^2,\qquad
 \beta\in R_H=\mathbb Z[\mu_h] . \tag{1}
$$

Here $j$ indexes the embeddings of $\mathbb Q(\mu_h)$ into $\mathbb C$ up to complex conjugation. For $h=2$ there is one real embedding. A finite norm-preserving carrier with connected underlying undirected graph satisfies the strong-connectivity assumption here: the square matrix identity also gives $AA^*=dI$, hence equal in- and out-degree, so a source component of the condensation cannot have a net outflow.

**Necessity.** One history and its $d$ distinct continuations give factor $d$. For the equal-length exits above, write $\gamma_i=g(P_i)$. At a common successor $z$, the exits followed by the last edge are equal-length paths to the same vertex. Consequently

$$
 u_z=(\gamma_1 A_{z y_1})^{-1}\gamma_2 A_{z y_2}\in H .
$$

Column orthogonality gives $\sum_z u_z=0$. The sum has at least two terms and proves $h>1$. At fixed length each endpoint sum is a unit frame times an element of $R_H$. Conversely every element of $R_H$ is a sum of roots in $H$ with nonnegative integer multiplicities: negative terms can use $-1=\sum_{u\in H,\ u\ne1}u$. Choose $K$ at least the needed multiplicities and prepend the block construction to the exits. Independent subsets of the two resulting families realize any two prescribed elements $a,b$ at $y_1,y_2$, with endpoint frames $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$ times the *same* prefactor. In particular all and only the radii $|\beta|$, $\beta\in R_H$, occur.

Let $m$ be the number of common successors. The $d-m$ exclusive successors on each side contribute their original single-entry radii. After cancelling these, continuation gives

$$
 \sum_z f(a+u_z b)=m[f(a)+f(b)],\qquad f(a)=\phi(|a|).
$$

This is precisely the equation of Z14 Theorem 5.12 **on the effective ring**. Its vanishing-sums theorem gives (1). The finite-carrier reduction here relies on that theorem; its polynomial and Nullstellensatz steps are outside the present Lean file. [Theorem and proof in G10](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/cf1a47a/edition/PAPERS/G10_Histories_as_Events_the_Order_of_the_Pair_Rule_First_Order_Weights_an/source/body.tex)

**Sufficiency.** Each effective embedding extends to a cyclotomic embedding of the ambient field since $h\mid q$. In exponents this amounts to lifting a unit modulo $h$ to a unit modulo $q$ by avoiding the other prime divisors of $q$. The ambient embedding commutes with complex conjugation and takes endpoint frames to unit roots. Applied to the entries of $A^*A=dI$, it therefore gives $\sigma(A)^*\sigma(A)=dI$. The sum of the corresponding squared endpoint amplitudes continues by the required factor. Any real linear combination does likewise. The assignment (1) is single valued on ordinary radii, since $|a|=|b|$ implies the algebraic equality $a\bar a=b\bar b$. Values of $\phi$ outside the attainable radii never enter this experiment.

**Positivity and copies.** With $\phi(1)=1$, nonnegative weights on single-endpoint history sets force $c_j\ge0$ and $\sum_jc_j=1$. Indeed (1) extends by rational scaling from $R_H$ to its fraction field. Under all archimedean embeddings this field is dense in the real vector space $\mathbb Q(\mu_h)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$ (expand in a rational basis). Continuity of the *displayed quadratic form* and vectors approaching individual coordinate axes give the inequalities. There is no continuity assumption on an unknown function off the attainable radii. If independent copies realize the product amplitudes for independently varied single-endpoint sets, and their weights factor with the **same** normalized law, applying factorization to two identical inputs gives zero variance of the numbers $|\sigma_j(a)|^2$ under weights $c_j$. Density separates distinct coordinates, so exactly one coefficient survives. These are additional record and composition premises. They still leave the choice of real embedding undetermined. ## Which subgroup does the signed walk supply?

On the ring with $N\ge6$ and incoming coin $R$ or $L$, append the outgoing direction, advance one site, and use a minus sign only for $L\to L$. Let the extra departure translation at $x$ be $g(x)\in\mathbb Z/M$ with odd $M$. Combine the sign and the faithful sheet character into $\mu_{2M}$ using exponent $M\,1_{L\to L}+2g(x)$. The endpoint in this statement is the **base site and coin**. If the sheet coordinate is also an observed endpoint, a same-endpoint pair already has equal net sheet increment.

With initial coin $R$, the two words $RLR,LRR$ arrive at $(x+1,R)$ with like signs. The words $RLL,LRL$ arrive at $(x-1,L)$ with opposite signs. Both pairs have the same difference $h_x=g(x+1)-g(x-1)$ of departure translations. Fixed connectors from and to the start turn both into equal-length closed-path comparisons. Thus $H$ contains the independent sign and every $h_x$. Conversely, modulo the subgroup generated by the $h_x$, the departure gain is constant along the step-two spatial orbit. On an odd ring there is one orbit; on an even ring departures alternate between the two fixed parity classes. Once start and length are fixed, every history has the same gain in that quotient. This proves

$$
 H=\{+1,-1\}\times\langle h_x:x\in\mathbb Z/N\rangle,\qquad
 |H|=\frac{2M}{\gcd(M,h_0,\ldots,h_{N-1})}. \tag{2}
$$

For a purely signed degree-two norm-preserving carrier, one merging block contains two common successors. Their two normalized column ratios are opposite signs. Both lie in $H$ after the equal-depth exits, so $H=\{+1,-1\}$ even without the ring geometry. This recovers the signed case of Z15. In the spatial model a single site with $g=1$ and all others zero supplies full order $2M$. On the eight-site ring the first times with at least two histories per gain at both coins of site zero were 8, 10, 18 and 30 at $M=3,5,9,15$. A constant gain retains only the sign at a fixed clock. [Finite profiles and exact counts](results/gain_abundance.json)

**Counterexample at the declared order.** On four vertices $(i,c)$ with $i,c\in\{0,1\}$ put an edge to $(1-i,d')$ of exponent $i+2cd'\bmod4$. The two successive blocks are signed Hadamards with a common phase $i_{\mathbb C}$ on the second block. The whole matrix satisfies $A^*A=2I$; all four fourth roots occur on edges, and a two-step closed path has gain $i_{\mathbb C}$. At a fixed start and length the clock layer contributes the same phase to every path. Only signs differ: $h=2$. The attainable radii are integers. For example $\phi(\sqrt2)$ can be assigned freely in this carrier, although $\sqrt2=|1+i_{\mathbb C}|$ belongs to the ambient radius set. The eight-phase variant of exponent $i+2c+4cd'\bmod8$ gives $h=4$, showing that a proper effective group can itself contain nonreal phases. ## When does a set of histories give a detector outcome?

For the synchronized walk, let $C_S$ sum the **normalized** path contributions of a specified set of direction words, so the unrestricted sum is $U^t$. On the zero-gain ring from $(0,R)$ at three ticks, take every word except $RLL$. Removing one of the cancelling histories leaves an extra squared unit in the destructive port. Thus $\|C_S\psi\|^2=9/8$; the discarded single history has weight $1/8$. This is an exact obstruction to interpreting every raw history-set weight as an unconditional yes/no probability. More generally a localized start has $d^t$ histories in at most $|V|q$ endpoint-and-exact-gain classes. Selecting the largest class gives a single-end normalized squared weight at least $d^t/(|V|q)^2$, which can grow arbitrarily large. Measuring every path first and adding its classical probability changes these sums. Quantum measures on histories and their distinct sum rules were studied by Sorkin. [Quantum mechanics as quantum measure theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9401003)

There is an operational test with a known scale. Suppose the walk can coherently copy a direction bit, compute a reversible Boolean function, perform balanced mixing on an ancillary bit, and measure its coordinate. From a known localized start a length-$t$ word specifies a path. For $t\ge1$ copy the first $k=t-1$ directions to clean tape bits, leaving the final direction on the walker. Compute membership in $S$ using the tape and final direction, retain a binary flag, and uncompute temporary work. Apply a Hadamard once to each tape bit and read it in the coordinate basis. The joint amplitude at tape result zero and flag yes, restricted to an endpoint set $Y$, is $2^{-k/2}P_Y C_S\psi$. Every tape word has the same overlap with the zero outcome. A complete tape with $t$ bits also works at an extra factor of two in probability. The operation consumes fresh memory and a coherent membership computation. A record of each route leaked to an uncontrolled system can suppress interference.

Count the joint event **per launched trial**. With the specified squared-coordinate instrument

$$
 p_t(S,Y,0)=2^{-k}\|P_Y C_S\psi\|^2.
$$

Using all endpoints, the full-history setting has probability $2^{-k}$ and a specified singleton on the balanced walk has probability $2^{-k-t}$. Dividing the test probability by the full-history reference recovers the normalized class weight; dividing it by the singleton reference recovers the unnormalized endpoint weight. In the three-tick example the compressed tape gives $9/32$ for yes and zero tape, $1/32$ for no and zero tape, and $9/10$ for yes conditional on seeing zero tape. The conditional statistic alone would give the wrong scale. At successive depths, $C_{\operatorname{Ext}S}=U C_S$. Summing all endpoints gives $p_{t+1}(\operatorname{Ext}S,V,0)=p_t(S,V,0)/2$ for the enlarged compressed tape. Counting all tape outputs measures membership with the recorded prefixes distinguishable. Different chosen $S$ define different controls. The argument implements and tests the stated rule conditional on the ancilla and readout model; using the squared-coordinate formula as evidence for the independent origin of that very rule would be circular. **A two-path read with one extra qubit.** For selected words $w_0,w_1$ of the same length, prepare one label in $|+\rangle$. After each walk tick make a joint coordinate test that passes the outgoing directions prescribed by $w_j$ when the label is $j$. Retain coherence of the two passed alternatives and record the first failure. On the surviving branch project the label back onto $|+\rangle$. The effective yes operator is $(C_{w_0}+C_{w_1})/2$. Disabling one label’s route gives a singleton denominator at the same scale. Branch rejection and the other label result remain in the trial population. There is no growing tape, though the success probability of each prescribed path still decreases with length and the controller must store the words.

More generally, for $J\ge1$ distinct paths of positive length pad to $L=2^{\lceil\log_2 J\rceil}$ labels, disabling unused labels at the first test. The amplitude is $C_S/L$. Measuring the complementary path set gives its own weight. The three-tick example shows why subtracting that result from one can give the wrong answer.

On two same-sign routes with one common base endpoint and sheet difference $a$, let $\rho$ be the initial sheet, uncorrelated with the localized base, and $T$ the unit sheet shift. Relative to the singleton read the two-route ratio is $2+2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(\rho T^a)$. With all differences $a$ available, the real Fourier transform is invertible on conjugate-pair weights. In detail if $w_k$ is the weight of sheet character $k$, measuring $C(a)=\sum_k w_k\cos(2\pi k a/M)$ gives $(w_k+w_{-k})/2=M^{-1}\sum_a C(a)\cos(2\pi k a/M)$, including the correct self-paired zero term. This measures a sheet state’s relative response and distinguishes mixtures; it gives no observation of an independently named primitive root. Shifting every Fourier label along with the embedding leaves the physical experiment unchanged.
