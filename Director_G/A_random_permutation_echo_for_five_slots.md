# What a pooled five-slot echo certifies

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

The eight-label source groups equal-weight words of five binary slots and heralds an accepted group. How much of the incoming population can violate its symmetry assumption if the audit supplies only a mean error? Choose a uniformly random permutation of the slots, undo the preparation, and read the complete original blank ray. If the mean *kinematic* leakage is at most $\epsilon$, the mean nonsymmetric mass is at most $\min(1,4\epsilon/3)$. The factor $4/3$ is sharp for an arbitrarily heterogeneous source. The same four neighboring swap settings used in the earlier audit give a weaker bound from their pooled mean. [Original source, controls and pass map](An_equal_label_source_from_unknown_slots.md)

## A randomized return with a sharp ensemble bound

For each hidden type let $\psi$ be the normalized pure state of the complete prepared system. An inert reference can be included. The audit requires the matched inverse of the actual preparation, a one-dimensional complete blank, and actual permutations of five qubits leaving the reference unchanged. Sample the permutation independently of the type, from the same launch population as production. For $g\in S_5$ write $U_g$ for its slot permutation. Its kinematic echo leakage is $1-|\langle\psi,U_g\psi\rangle|^2$. Write $x=\|(I-P_{\rm sym})\psi\|^2$.

The five-qubit permutation action contains irreducibles of shapes $(5),(4,1),(3,2)$, dimensions $1,4,5$, with multiplicities $6,4,2$. These follow from the two-row Schur–Weyl decomposition, or from the weight-zero, one- and two-subset permutation modules and their complements. An inert reference changes only the multiplicities. On each irreducible carrier let $\rho_\lambda$ be the unnormalized reduced state obtained by tracing out its multiplicity space, and $w_\lambda=\operatorname{tr}\rho_\lambda$. Matrix-coefficient orthogonality gives
$$ \frac1{120}\sum_g|\langle\psi,U_g\psi\rangle|^2
=\sum_\lambda\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\rho_\lambda^2)}{\dim\lambda}
\le(1-x)^2+\frac{x^2}{4}. $$
Indeed the overlap is the sum of $\operatorname{tr}(\rho_\lambda U_g^\lambda)$, cross terms vanish under the group average, and each block purity is at most $w_\lambda^2$. The mean leakage $L(\psi)$ is at least $2x-5x^2/4\ge3x/4$. A normalized difference of two distinct one-hot words lies in one standard four-dimensional carrier and attains $x=1$, $L=3/4$. Mixing arbitrary fractions of those launches with symmetric launches attains the ensemble linear bound even at arbitrarily small mean leakage. The classical random choice requires one permutation per audited launch and no coherent control over a group register. Coherent symmetry projectors address a different measurement design. [Bacon, Chuang and Harrow on the Schur basis](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0407082) · [LaBorde, Rethinasamy and Wilde on symmetry tests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12758)

Either parity of permutations suffices. On $\Phi=\psi\otimes\bar\psi$, the return is the expectation of $U_g\otimes\bar U_g$. Its signed group average projects onto the alternating subspace of five doubled local slots. Each slot has dimension four, so this subspace vanishes. Uniform sampling of the 60 even words, the 60 odd words, or any setting-independent mixture of the two uniform samples therefore gives precisely the same mean.

There is also a single-swap option. Choose one of the four adjacent swaps uniformly and let $L_{\rm adj}$ be its mean kinematic leakage. On the doubled system let $Q$ be the invariant-space projector. The path interchange Laplacian $\frac14\sum_i(I-S_i\otimes\bar S_i)$ has gap at least $\gamma=(2-2\cos(\pi/5))/4=(3-\sqrt5)/8$ off that space. This follows from the spectral gap of the interchange process on the five-vertex path, since its regular representation contains all irreducibles. Taking the expectation on $\Phi$ gives $L_{\rm adj}\ge\gamma\|(I-Q)\Phi\|^2=\gamma L(\psi)$. Thus $\mathbb E x\le4\mathbb E L_{\rm adj}/(3\gamma)$. This estimate uses the existing four controls and the pooled leakage. Optimality of its constant for doubled product inputs remains open. [Caputo, Liggett and Richthammer, path interchange gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.1238)

## From the incoming ensemble to accepted labels

Let $T$ be the original contraction onto the accepted label and junk outputs, and $Y_t=\|T\psi_t\|^2$. When the conditional pass state is the normalized projected state, its weight outside the uniform logical-label ray is $e_t$. Set the joint wrong mass to zero for $Y_t=0$. Since $T$ sends every symmetric input into the target ray tensored with junk, $Y_te_t\le x_t$. If the measured per-type pass probability is $h_t$, the average wrong mass **among accepted launches** is $\mathbb E[h_te_t]/H$, where $H=\mathbb E h_t>0$. If the herald is norm-calibrated, $h_t=Y_t$. More generally a verified upper bound $h_t\le K Y_t$ gives
$$ \frac{\mathbb E[h_te_t]}{H}\le
\min\left(1,\frac{4K\epsilon}{3H}\right),\qquad \epsilon\ge\mathbb E L(\psi_t). $$
A calibrated lower bound on the population yield and an upper bound on mean kinematic leakage suffice. No individual type needs an identified leakage. This is a bound on wrong-ray squared mass. For a subsequent common ordinary measurement, trace distance to the normalized target projection is at most $\sqrt{e_t}$ when that projection is nonzero. With arbitrary junk at $e_t=1$, averaging gives a corresponding bound $\sqrt{\mathbb E[h_te_t]/H}$ on total variation. A nonlinear downstream score would need its own quantitative response calibration.

Two finite controls mark the boundary. First put $\psi=\sqrt{7/8}\,s+\sqrt{1/8}\,v$ with $s$ symmetric and $v$ an orthogonal standard-representation ray. For *every* permutation the absolute return amplitude is at least $3/4$. The blank mass is at least $9/16$ and all other cells together have at most $7/16$. A common strictly increasing power score $w^q$, $q\ge1$, can report nonblank with probability at most $(7/9)^q$, whether those cells are pooled before or after the power. Their observed frequency can approach zero while $x=1/8$. Exact zero faithfulness alone gives no finite calibration. The random assignment also matters. If the type can depend on the chosen permutation, it can supply one of that permutation's eigenvectors in the standard representation and pass with $x=1$.

Second take a normalized difference $v$ of two retained weight-two words in the same junk group. It is orthogonal to both the symmetric subspace and the target logical ray after passage. For $\psi=\sqrt{1-\delta}\,|00000\rangle+\sqrt\delta\,v$, $x=Y=\delta$ and $e=1$. The all-zero component is rejected, and the uniform permutation mean leakage tends to zero as $\delta\downarrow0$. For example a strictly increasing binary herald score $w^{q_\delta}$ with $q_\delta=1/\log(1/\delta)$ gives positive pass probability approaching $1/(1+\exp(1))$ even as $Y\to0$. It can accompany an independently norm-calibrated echo and the stipulated normalized pass-state update. All accepted outputs still miss the target. Measured yield alone cannot replace the bound on herald bias. These are model predictions, with no empirical estimate of the mean audit errors or herald response.
