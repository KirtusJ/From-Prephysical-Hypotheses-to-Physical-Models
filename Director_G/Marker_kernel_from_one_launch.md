# Calibrating a local marker from one sharp launch

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Launch the real signed Hadamard walk at site \(0\) in its right rail. Even this single preparation produces a dense set of local rail directions, already along the line \(x=t/2\) at times divisible by four. If the aggregate routing of a conserved marker is a universal continuous function of rail direction, its blank terminal records fix the function everywhere. A physical copy that transports both marker and signed field by the same permutation then fixes the joint terminal port and pointer records for the specified tagged flights. These are conditional results about an apparatus. The two routing laws tested below predict \(9/16\) and \(5/16\) at one finite detector.

## What the blank panels calibrate

Let \(D_t(x)=(u,v)\) be the raw right and left signed totals arriving at site \(x\). A tick sends \(u+v\) right to \((x+1,R)\) and \(u-v\) left to \((x-1,L)\). The sharp source has unit raw tally. The blank instrument has a conserved marker per run, faithfully read at the terminal port. For an untagged flight we use the score derived in the G1 candidate from that marker and a common scalar score shape. Its blank terminal probabilities are \(D_t(x,c)^2/2^t\). In particular these probabilities, the shared sharp source, the update of the signed field and the local movement of the marker are inputs here. G1 itself is a proposed physical specification. [Blank-instrument hypotheses and integer score derivation](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/4f3468b/Director_G/candidates/edge_record_walk/specification.md)

The oriented right successor has only one possible parent site. On fresh identical launches, divide its frequency in the \(t+1\) terminal panel by the sum of the two parent-rail frequencies in the \(t\) terminal panel. Independence of the pre-read dynamics from the planned terminal depth and conservation of the marker give the conditional right probability **aggregated over both incoming rails**,

\[
 h(u,v)=\frac{(u+v)^2}{2(u^2+v^2)} .
\]

This defines a frequency ratio only when \(u^2+v^2>0\). A zero denominator in a blank panel is a dark parent. The calibration uses two terminal ensembles and requires neither a parent-site read en route nor a trajectory-level assignment of both observations to the same specimen. The common power of two matters when comparing depths.

## A dense set of directions on one ballistic line

Write \(A_t(x)=2^{-t/2}D_t(x)\). Our Fourier convention gives \(U(s)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-is},e^{is})H\), with \(H(r,l)=(r+l,r-l)/\sqrt2\); inversion uses \(e^{isx}ds/(2\pi)\). Put \(k=\arccos(1/\sqrt3)\), \(H_0=3\sqrt2/8\), and \(z=(3/4,\ 1/4+i/(2\sqrt2))\). For \(t=4n,\ x=2n\) one has

\[
 A_{4n}(2n)=\frac{4}{\sqrt{2\pi t H_0}}\,
 \operatorname{Re}\!\left[e^{i(6nk+\pi/4)}z\right]+O(t^{-3/2})
 \quad(n\to\infty).
\]

To see the coefficients, take \(\omega(s)=\arcsin(\sin s/\sqrt2)\). The two eigenvalues are \(e^{-i\omega(s)}\) and \(-e^{i\omega(s)}\), and are separated on the whole circle. For the first band the phase per tick is \(s/2-\omega(s)\); its stationary points obey \(\omega'(s)=\cos s/\sqrt{1+\cos^2 s}=1/2\), hence \(s=\pm k\). At \(+k\), \(\omega(k)=\pi/2-k\) and \(-\omega''(k)=\sin k/(1+\cos^2 k)^{3/2}=H_0\). The phase \(2nk-4n\omega(k)\) is \(6nk\) modulo \(2\pi\). The plus-band projector applied to \((1,0)\) is \((U(k)+e^{i\omega(k)}I)(1,0)/(2\cos\omega(k))=z\).

The second-order stationary-phase term at \(+k\) is \(z e^{i(6nk+\pi/4)}/\sqrt{2\pi t H_0}\). The \(-k\) term is its conjugate. The second band has the translated saddles \(\pm k+\pi\) modulo \(2\pi\). Indeed \(U(s+\pi)=-U(s)\), and the inversion integrand picks up \((-1)^{t+x}=1\) on the occupied parity, with the band labels exchanged. These two replicas double the conjugate sum. The amplitudes and phases are smooth in neighborhoods of the four nondegenerate saddles. The next stationary-phase remainder is \(O(t^{-3/2})\); off those neighborhoods smooth cutoffs and integration by parts give \(O(t^{-2})\). This is an interior ballistic calculation. Sunada and Tate treat the oscillating asymptotics of line walks and separately analyze the ballistic boundary. [Primary asymptotic analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.1878)

The phase \(6nk+\pi/4\) is dense modulo \(2\pi\). Otherwise \(k/\pi\) would be rational, \(e^{ik}\) would be a root of unity, and \(2\cos k=e^{ik}+e^{-ik}\) would be an algebraic integer. Its square \(4/3\) is rational and nonintegral. The ellipse mapping \((\cos\phi,\sin\phi)\) to \(\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\phi}z)\) has matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}3/4&0\\1/4&-1/(2\sqrt2)\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant is \(-3/(8\sqrt2)\). Its image of the unit circle stays a positive distance from zero and meets every oriented ray. The \(O(t^{-3/2})\) error therefore perturbs the normalized direction by \(O(1/t)\) for sufficiently large \(t\). Every late tail of the irrational rotation is dense; the actual bare rail directions are dense on the unit circle. Multiplying back to raw integer tallies leaves the direction unchanged. This yields a calibration set on the line or on increasing rings with \(N>2(t+1)\) and the same routing law. One fixed circumference need not exhibit this sequence.

## From blank panels to pointer records

Assume a single function \(K\) gives the conditional fraction of a site's marker mass sent right, in each occupied sector of the actual pointer. It is independent of time, absolute position, previous controls and pointer value except through the two same-sector incoming tallies. Assume \(K\) is invariant under common **positive** rescaling, and its value on directions is continuous. A priori it may distinguish opposite rays. Blank calibration gives \(K=h\) on the dense set, hence \(K=h\) on the entire circle, including opposite rays. The left fraction is \(1-h\). This uniqueness needs the universal aggregate response. Even with that law, incoming-rail-dependent routing is underdetermined. For instance at \((2,1)\) the two incoming rail masses are in ratio \(4:1\). Right chances \(1\) and \(1/2\) on those two rails give the same \(9/10\) aggregate as using \(9/10\) on each.

Start the addressed marker and the signed field at the same sharp launch with blank pointer. A deterministic coordinate-controlled bit copy acts by the *same permutation* on marker and field on the complete port-and-tag space, preserving the tag on subsequent free ticks. More generally, one can interleave ideal deterministic port-and-tag permutations and free ticks, always forming the next split from the two incoming amplitudes at the same site and actual resulting tag. The future signed splitter for each tag must be specified independently of the individual marker route. Histories with different surviving physical records remain separate. A reused bit may bring old labels into a common current tag through different rails; any still-retained record must also be included in that sector. The marker leaves the signed dynamics undisturbed. Time depths and tally normalizations are aligned before interference.

Here is the induction for these operations. At depth \(t\) suppose marker masses at the complete input ports are \(D^2/2^t\). A shared label permutation preserves equality coordinate by coordinate. At each site/tag with signed pair \((u,v)\), its marker mass is \((u^2+v^2)/2^t\). Splitting by \(h\) sends \((u+v)^2/2^{t+1}\) and \((u-v)^2/2^{t+1}\) down the two outgoing oriented edges. Each feeds one named rail at the next site/tag. A dark input carries zero marker mass. This proves the joint law for every finite sequence in this class. It does not start by assigning a global tagged-instrument score. It starts with a known sharp marker, physical copying and a sector-independent local law. Which records have been preserved is part of the device.

## A finite discriminator and the reach of measurement

At tick three copy the bit \(b=\mathbf1_{x\ge0}\), preserving rail and position within each sector. The raw pair at \(x=1\) is \((2,1)\), so the marker mass of that parent with \(b=1\) is \(5/8\). Its unique right daughter \((b=1,x=2,R)\) at tick four has joint mass \((5/8)(9/10)=9/16\). A rival uses exactly the blank kernel while the bit is blank, uses \(h\) in tag zero, and fair routing in tag one. It shares the signed field, shared copy, and persistent tag marginal, but gives \(5/16\) at that same detector. The difference is \(1/4\) of all launches. The comparison uses a joint record over all sharp launches, with a calibrated port/flag read at the indicated clock time. Under the ideal alternatives the midpoint \(7/16\) distinguishes the two predictions whenever the absolute frequency error is below \(1/8\). A finite experiment must count misses and backgrounds, characterize flag/port response and report sampling uncertainty. No physical rates are supplied.

For this short test no extrapolation to an unsampled input direction is needed. Through output ticks four, five and six the 18 populated parent site/tag visits use ten distinct signed integer pairs. Each pair occurs exactly, including sign and magnitude, in a blank panel of depth at most six. Exact blank successor-to-parent ratios for these pairs reproduce all the stored joint port-and-tag probabilities. Word enumeration independently calculates the signed outcomes. This finite replay still presumes transfer of the corresponding local response to the tagged sectors.

The separate 144-point ballistic panel records the model's raw counts and finite asymptotic residual at every point. The largest gap in oriented angle is \(1.20282\) radians at 12 inputs and \(0.25169\) at 144. The first normalized spinor differs from its leading term by \(0.08845\) in Euclidean norm; the 144th differs by \(0.00007356\). Rare parent frequencies on the ballistic line are of order \(1/t\), so obtaining parent-normalized empirical ratios has a growing sampling cost. A finite calibration alone leaves alternatives: on an uncalibrated open arc take a continuous bump \(0\le g\le1\) supported there and positive somewhere with \(0<h<1\). Then \(h+g h(1-h)\) is a valid response that agrees at every calibrated point. It also works on the circle modulo sign if the unused arc is chosen there and the bump is lifted to both antipodes. Conversely, if an alternative has a separately justified Lipschitz bound \(L\) in angular distance, observations guarantee \(|K-h|\le e\) at the sampled points, and the covering radius is \(r\), then \(\sup|K-h|\le e+(L+1)r\). Here \(h(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)=(1+\sin2\theta)/2\) has derivative bounded by one. The oriented covering radius is half the largest angular gap. Continuity alone supplies no finite error threshold on this class.
