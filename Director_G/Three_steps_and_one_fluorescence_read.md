# Three walk steps and one fluorescence read

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Schmitz and colleagues measured the two coin levels of one trapped magnesium ion after a controlled three-step walk. The dark-state fraction was **0.259**, compared with **0.250** for the ideal walk with the ordinary read and **0.100** if the two ideal coin-sector masses are squared and normalized a second time. The coin read physically pools the oscillator states. This supplies an experimental comparison for a pooled **rail** flag. It leaves open the response of a device pooling **sites**. [Published experiment, Figs. 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.103.090504) · [Experimental methods and follow-up, §§3.2, 3.5 and 4.2](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/14/3/035012)

## Which population was measured

The authors initialized a single trapped ion in the bright $T$ level by optical pumping and cooled its axial motion near the oscillator ground state. Radiofrequency pulses supply the balanced coin and an optical dipole force displaces the oscillator conditional on that coin. For the terminal coin read, a resonant cycling transition fluoresces strongly on $T$ and negligibly on $H$. A photomultiplier integrates the 20 µs pulse. A separate setting swaps the levels with a π pulse before fluorescence to measure the formerly dark population. Both settings read one electronic sector across the whole motion. Comparing their reported rates as complementary requires the swap and the collection calibration to transfer between settings. The paper uses an additional pulse sequence and Fourier analysis for the motional populations. Those reconstructed positions enter none of the estimates below. A count of fluorescence photons is not a count of separate walks. [Published apparatus, pp. 2–3](https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRevLett.103.090504/fulltext)

| Setting | Authors’ pooled-coin result |
|---|---|
| One and two steps | Both channels $0.50\pm0.01$ at each step. |
| Three steps, optimized displacement duration | The 2009 journal text gives $T=0.741\pm0.003$, $H=0.259\pm0.002$, statistical uncertainties. The 2012 methods paper reports $0.741\pm0.002$ and $0.259\pm0.001$ at the precision setting with 60,000 measurements. The two publications describe the same experimental line. We use the central values. |
| Three steps, displacement duration about 2% either side of optimum | The populations return to approximately one half. The duration was tuned to maximize the coin imbalance. These off-optimum controls and the first two clocks also pass both stipulated response laws at exact balance. |

The PRL Fig. 3 scan states 1,500 experiments per point. The precision ensemble and scan have different denominators. Neither paper gives the per-trial bright/dark decisions, companion loss counts or a joint likelihood for the two estimates in the passages used here. We therefore do not convert the reported means into a binomial sample, add the two publications as independent data or assign a significance to the ideal differences. The 2009 preprint puts 1,200 experiments per scan point in Fig. 3. Its text gives statistical uncertainties of 0.003 and 0.001 on the bright and dark means. Its precision caption gives 0.002 and 0.001. The central values agree across versions. ## A one-parameter kinematic test

Assume one common forward motional unitary $V$ on $T$, its inverse on $H$, the same balanced rotation at all three ticks, and a sharp $T$ launch. Choose coin phases so the unnormalized rotation maps $(T,H)$ to $(T-H,T+H)$. For a motional input vector $v$ the three-step $H$ component is $(V+V^{-3})v/\sqrt8$. Indeed after two ticks the unnormalized $T,H$ components are $(V^2-I)v$ and $(I+V^{-2})v$. This holds for nonorthogonal translated states and does not identify motion with a lattice of orthogonal wells. For a density matrix $\rho$ define $c=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho V^4)$. The sector masses obey

$$ p_H=(1+c)/4,\qquad p_T=1-p_H. $$

The coherent-state specialization reproduces Eqs. (11)–(12) of the methods paper. Here the same amplitude calculation isolates the signed real overlap for an arbitrary motional state. [Three-step state and coherent-state ratio](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.0913#page=7)

The one-step mass is $1/2$ and the two-step $H$ mass is $(1+\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho V^2))/2$. Disjoint translated lattice sites give $c=0$ for a sharp launch. For an actual calibrated unitary displacement $V=D(d)$ and vacuum input, $c=\exp(-8|d|^2)$. Fix the two proposed binary readouts $G_1(p)=p$ and $G_2(p)=p^2/[p^2+(1-p)^2]$. With unchanged endpoint calibration, a single repeatable $c$ and dark reading $q=0.259$, inversion gives

| Readout | Required input mass $p_H$ | Required $c$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ordinary | 0.259 | 0.036 |
| Normalized square | 0.371547 | 0.486188 |

This inverse problem has actual countermodels. Put $Z=V^2$ and assign it spectral values $+z,-z$ with equal weight and $z=(1+it)/\sqrt{1+t^2}$. For $|c|<1$ choose $t=\sqrt{(1-c)/(1+c)}$. Then $\mathbb EZ=0$ while $\operatorname{Re}\mathbb E Z^2=c$. A unitary square root supplies $V$. Both balanced clocks can therefore coexist with either inferred three-step value. The measured comparison needs an independent bound on the realized translations to distinguish these descriptions. The additional pulse-duration data diagnose interference and motivate such a bound. They do not enforce a particular $c$ at the optimum from algebra alone.

## Hidden preparations and the size of a systematic shift

Suppose the source, the displacement or both vary between launches. Write $c_+=\max(c,0)$ and let $\varepsilon$ bound the absolute discrepancy in the mean between the actual calibrated dark read and the idealized per-launch prediction averaged over the incident ensemble. It includes any effect of conditioning on accepted trials, incorrect coin rotations or readout drift for which independent information supplies such a bound. Under the normalized-square alternative with this fixed readout,

$$ q\le \tfrac1{10}+\tfrac25\,\mathbb E c_+ +\varepsilon. $$

For $c<0$ monotonicity gives the first term. For $0\le c\le1$, subtracting $G_2((1+c)/4)$ from $(1+4c)/10$ gives $2c(1-c)(2-c)/[5(c^2-2c+5)]\ge0$. Mixtures at $c=0,1$ attain the bound at all intermediate means of $c_+$. Thus at the central published $q$, exact fluorescence and pulses require $\mathbb E c_+\ge0.3975$ even with hidden types. Under the ordinary rule the corresponding constraint is $\mathbb E c=0.036$. Fitting the nonlinear transform to the mean input would overlook the attainable mixtures.

For an example of an independently testable displacement premise, require each launch type to have $|d|\ge1$, a genuine $D(d)$, a sharp $T$ coin and average oscillator occupation $\mathbb E\bar n\le0.03$. For each type the vacuum deficit is at most $\bar n$ and the trace-norm distance to vacuum is at most $2\sqrt{\bar n}$. For example the latter follows by purifying the mixed state against a vacuum purification of overlap $\sqrt{\langle0|\rho|0\rangle}$ and contracting trace norm on discarding the reference. Testing the contraction $\operatorname{Re}D(4d)$ and averaging then gives $\mathbb E c_+\le e^{-8}+2\sqrt{0.03}<0.346746$. The square rule predicts $q<0.238699+\varepsilon$. Reaching the reported central value therefore requires over **0.02030** absolute additional discrepancy even under this deliberately loose ideal-shift bound. The reference expectation is in the standard oscillator normalization used in the methods paper. [Displacement model and ground-state preparation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.0913)

The authors report initial $T$ fidelity of at least 0.99, $\bar n<0.03$ and a displacement scale around one, alongside calculated squeezing and departure from the ideal shift. In §4.1 their model gives fidelities 0.97 and 0.90 against corresponding coherent states at positions 2 and 3. A mistimed pulse that separates paths intended to recombine can change the three-step signal even at small fourth-displacement overlap. The one-parameter formula requires the same conditional translations and coin phases to repeat. They tuned the displacement duration using the same coin imbalance. This supplies neither the per-type bound $|d|\ge1$ nor a common state-independent $V,V^*$ calibration over all three steps. An imperfect optical preparation cannot automatically be treated as a classical one-percent admixture. A quantum preparation fidelity is also distinct from readout error. Consequently the 0.02030 threshold is a conditional requirement for a future calibration or a more complete reconstruction of this experiment. It does not exclude the actual instrument independently of those premises. [Realized pulses, squeezing and optimization](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/14/3/035012)

The conversion from fluorescence to fraction also matters. In a two-output stochastic confusion model with symmetric flip probability $f$, the indicated fraction is $f+(1-2f)G(p)$. At $c=0$ the value $f=0.19875$ maps 0.100 to 0.259 and preserves the two earlier balanced values exactly. It would also move independently prepared endpoint controls by 0.19875, making it testable. For mean fluorescence the same example is an affine contrast reduction relative to the calibration. Here each readout law governs dark or bright sector selection, followed by a stable conditional photon yield in each sector. With a stable collector the brightness of separately prepared pure levels sets both endpoints and removes a common gain and offset; this requires the gain and collection response to transfer to the walk outputs, including their motion. If trials are instead retained with state-dependent acceptance, the retained dark fraction for incident dark rate $x$ is $r x/(r x+1-x)$ where $r$ is the dark/bright efficiency ratio. Converting $x=0.100$ to 0.259 takes $r=777/247\approx3.146$, which on its own would move a half-input away from one half. Neither process is inferred to have happened. The examples separate what a measured calibration could reject. [Fluorescence readout details](https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRevLett.103.090504/fulltext)

The positive result applies to reading an internal coin sector across a moving wavepacket. A site mask requires its own physical coarse detector or transfer measurement with calibrated labels, loss and backaction. The program’s cesium terminal-position result uses resolved wells, and its earlier trapped-ion control uses a qubit in one fixed trap. They answer different readout questions.
