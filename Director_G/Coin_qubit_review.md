# Coherent-return coin qubit: independent audit

I reviewed the proposed mirror-return construction on 13 November 2026 as a second Codex participant. I knew the proposed line, two-step return, longer excursion masks, Hadamard convention and departure gain before deriving the gate and the density test. I independently enumerated signed paths. The claims concern positive-time operations on the specified signed walk with coherent coordinate-membership records, normalized projective readout, and a selected nontrivial sheet pair.

## Physical paths and the success effect

Let \(H=2^{-1/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) in the order \(R,L\). A step adds its departure position to the cyclic sheet, applies \(H\), and shifts in its outgoing direction. Begin with an arbitrary coin at site \(x_0\). At steps \(1\le j\le2k\) retain membership in \(|x-x_0|=\min(j,2k-j)\), without recording the sign of \(x-x_0\), the coin, or the sheet. At the last step both arms have returned to \(x_0\). For \(k=1\), only the final home-site test matters.

The only two accepted direction words are \(R^kL^k\) and \(L^kR^k\). On the first arm the departure sum relative to \(x_0\) is \(0+1+\cdots+(k-1)+k+(k-1)+\cdots+1=k^2\). The other sum is \(-k^2\). Each arm has exactly \(k-1\) internal \(L\)-to-\(L\) transitions after the first coin action. Both carry the same sign \((-1)^{k-1}\). The outgoing coin is opposite the first arm. With sheet translation \(T\), the complete retained operator on coin and sheet is
\[
 K_k=(-1)^{k-1}2^{-(2k-1)/2}\,T^{2kx_0}\,
 X\begin{pmatrix}T^{k^2}&0\\0&T^{-k^2}\end{pmatrix}H,
\]
where \(X\) swaps the two coin directions. Each factor after the scalar is unitary. Thus \(K_k^\dagger K_k=2^{1-2k}I\) on the *whole* coin–sheet input, and on an extension by an arbitrary spectator. This includes entangled inputs and leaking sectors. A positive-time word at the same home site concatenates these operators and has success probability equal to the product of the displayed scalar probabilities, independently of the input. No inverse walk is needed to execute a forward word.

## Projective density and readout

Fix the mathematical convention \(T\mapsto e^{ih}\), \(h=2\pi r/M\) of odd effective order \(q=M/\gcd(r,M)>1\). Discard the global phase from \(x_0\). Write \(D(u)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{iu},e^{-iu})\) and \(J=XH\). A successful radius-\(k\) operation acts as \(G_k=D(-k^2h)J\), with \(J\) the Bloch-sphere rotation by \(\pi/2\) about \(y\). The radius \(q\) produces \(J\) exactly. Its projective inverse is \(J^3\); using a successful radius-one gate and this word gives \(D(-h)=G_1J^{-1}\) projectively. All inverses of these two generators are available through finite positive powers, since their projective orders are finite. If only the sheet order is known, radius \(M\) also produces \(J\) in every sector. This exact menu can be expensive.

There is a shorter *density* construction using only radii one and three. The closure of their positive-word semigroup inside the compact unitary group contains both inverses, because powers recur arbitrarily close to the identity (or reach it for finite order). There \(G_3G_1^{-1}=D(-8h)\), and eight is invertible modulo \(q\). It follows that the closure contains \(D(-h)\) and \(J\). This argument puts target gates in the closure and gives no efficient compilation bound. Using radii one and two in place of one and three fails at \(q=3\), where \(G_1=G_2\) and their powers stay in the subgroup generated by a single rotation.

For completeness, conjugating the primitive \(z\)-rotation by \(J\) supplies an \(x\)-rotation by the same Bloch angle \(\alpha=\pm2h\). Write \(A,B\) for the natural determinant-one lifts of those two rotations. The trace of \(AB\) is \(2\cos^2(\alpha/2)=1+\cos\alpha\), since the axes are perpendicular. Every conjugate of \(\cos\alpha\) has modulus strictly between zero and one: the underlying root has odd order \(q>1\). Its nonzero field norm has absolute value strictly below one, excluding algebraic integrality. The trace of an element of finite projective order in \(SU(2)\) is an algebraic integer, since its eigenvalues are roots of unity. The generated projective group is therefore infinite. An infinite closed proper subgroup of \(SO(3)\) has one-dimensional identity component: the Lie algebra has cross-product bracket, so a two-dimensional subspace cannot be a Lie subalgebra, and a compact zero-dimensional Lie group is finite. The full subgroup must preserve the unoriented axis of its identity component. Each of the two displayed odd-order rotations has only its own axis as invariant real line. Their axes differ. Thus the projective closure is \(SO(3)\).

In the chosen qubit representation a final direction read after a successful word measures the projector \(G^\dagger|R\rangle\langle R|G\), with conditional complement for \(L\). These projectors are dense on the Bloch sphere. Applying successful words to a fixed direction likewise prepares a dense set of pure coin states. Hence, for example, one can approximate three independent Pauli measurement axes for coin tomography relative to the same calibrated gate family. No explicit identification of an absolute imaginary axis follows. For a measured accuracy on a rare word, trial counts must account for its entire heralded probability and the cost of obtaining the sheet input. No density result here gives a uniform lower bound on yields as target accuracy increases.

## Real-sheet implementation and conditional sampling

In a physical real sheet plane a coin qubit is represented by a complex spinor and its conjugate at \(\pm r\). Gate words and coin projectors at the two tags are exact conjugates. Real initial data and real coordinate instruments give identical complete probabilities after tracing the sheet. Programming and measurement within the same relational representation therefore work in either orientation. An arbitrary coin tensor an unresolved sheet without the conjugate-spinor preparation need not undergo one common complex gate when the sheet is discarded. The trivial sector \(q=1\) has only \(J\) in this return menu, with no complex phase control. Nontrivial sectors other than the designated conjugate pair can undergo different gates. The retained excursion cannot purify them because every sector has precisely the same success probability.

For each *fixed* prescribed local word the no-success complement has total probability \(1-2^{\sum_j(1-2k_j)}\) on every qubit state. If a setting selects a different word, its normalization changes accordingly. The state-independent success effect licenses conditional Born statistics within this specified model. As an empirical matter the complete success/failure statistics must be retained. A Bell experiment with setting-dependent discards would also have to justify sampling and locality against its competing models. A native entangled source, separated parties, independent setting choices, and a detection-loophole-free Bell test do not follow from this single-coin gate theorem.

## Independent checks and adverse controls

- I enumerated all \(2^{2k}\) direction words for each input direction and \(1\le k\le6\), applying the time-specific coordinate masks and retaining the signed endpoint polynomial in \(T\). On all 300 radius/tag combinations at \(M=3,5,7,9,11,15\), both the predicted complex matrix and its scalar Gram matrix agreed to a largest entry discrepancy of \(8.90\times10^{-16}\).
- At \(M=5,\ r=1\), two radius-one gates starting from \(R\) yielded conditional final \(R\)-probability \(\sin^2(2\pi/5)=0.9045084972\). Reading the actual arm during the first return and later forgetting it gives conditional probability \(1/2\), since the first returned spinor has lost its off-diagonal term. The signed-arm membership mask is a substantive instrument specification.
- Omitting intermediate masks and accepting every four-step return home keeps six histories for each input direction, including paths that turn prematurely. Direct enumeration gave return probabilities 0.125, 0.57725425 and 0.29774575 at \(M=5,\ r=0,1,2\), respectively. The equal-success property across sectors is lost.
- At \(q=3\) I evaluated \(G_1\) and \(G_2\) and found maximum entry discrepancy \(5.2\times10^{-16}\), consistent with the exact equality \(4h=h\bmod 2\pi\). The two-radius counterexample does not affect the one-and-three density proof.

The preceding proof concerns a reachable, heralded, relational logical qubit under supplied real-coordinate instruments and score rule. It establishes neither deterministic gates at unit throughput nor a physical implementation of the masks. The numerical checks are finite mathematical controls; they do not calibrate a laboratory device.

## Subsequent audit of the radius process with no excursion rejection

I reviewed the proposed finite-ring controller after the preceding audit of the selectively retained mirror gates. Take a ring of \(2L\) sites with \(L\ge4\) divisible by the odd sheet order \(M\); for \(M=3\) one may take \(L=6\). At a meeting port \(x_0=0\) or \(L\), define radius as circular distance from the current port. After a meeting at the antipode this requires a new radial origin, or equivalently exchanging \(r\) with \(L-r\) in a fixed-origin instrument. At each tick, the instrument records only the radius, coherently preserving the two mirror sites, both direction possibilities at meeting sites, and the sheet. Restart the radial protocol at a boundary. The first radius is one with probability one for an arbitrary coin and any spectator, since the first Hadamard splits the coin into two orthogonal spatial arms.

After that first tick, at each interior radius the two occupied sites have exactly one incoming direction per arm, fixed by the arm index and the last radial increment. Continuing to either adjacent radius selects exactly one outgoing coin entry on each arm, with modulus \(1/\sqrt2\). This remains true on a step that reaches a boundary, since the two previously distinct arms arrive with *opposite* coin directions. Conditional on the radial transcript, the logical coin travels in orthogonal arm subspaces and acquires only known signs and sheet translations until the meeting. At the meeting these subspaces again are opposite coin directions at one site. The resulting operator for a complete first-boundary transcript of length \(t\) is \(2^{(1-t)/2}\) times a unitary monomial matrix followed on the right by \(H\). The monomial entries contain the two arms' sheet translations, or their phases in a tagged sector. The Gram effect of the full branch is \(2^{1-t}I\), using the complex adjoint within a character. Every possible syndrome is retained for feedback; the recorded *fine site* or coin along the interior would instead destroy this logical encoding.

The radius increments after the first tick form an unbiased nearest-neighbour walk until absorption at zero or \(L\). The duration expectation solves the elementary recurrence \(h(j)=1+(h(j-1)+h(j+1))/2\) with zero boundary values. Starting from radius one it gives \(L-1\) additional ticks, so the complete excursion has mean \(L\). Because \(M\mid L\), \(g(x_0\pm r)=\pm r\bmod M\) and the coin orientation can be kept in the same global \(R,L\) coordinates at either port. Complete branch matrices and durations therefore have the same distribution after every restart and their probabilities do not depend on the incoming logical state. The random excursion matrices are independent across restarts, although the running product of those matrices is history-dependent. Excursions commonly have unbounded finite length; the finite mean refers to each complete excursion.

**Hitting a desired gate.** Fix a target \(V\), a nonzero tolerance in projective unitary norm and a chosen nontrivial odd-order sector. Two possible returning excursions are the prescribed mirror paths of radii one and three (require \(3<L\)). Their probabilities are \(1/2\) and \(1/32\). They generate a dense positive-word semigroup by the proof above. From any current frame \(F\) at the home port, some finite word \(W\) of these returning excursions satisfies \(d(WF,V)\) strictly below tolerance. The same word works for an open neighbourhood of \(F\). From the other port, prefix a prescribed straight crossing of probability \(2^{1-L}\); it acts by a known unitary and ends at home. Apply the same density argument to the resulting frame. The compact state space \(PU(2)\times\{0,L\}\) admits a finite subcover. Hence some \(H<\infty\) and \(p_*>0\) give a uniform probability at least \(p_*\) of reaching the target at home within the next \(H\) excursions. Words do not have to be actively imposed: the complete random instrument produces each indicated sequence with that positive probability. The controller only monitors the transcript and stops when its known frame is acceptable. The chance of missing the target for \(jH\) excursions is at most \((1-p_*)^j\). For the count \(C\) up to this stopping rule, \(E[C]\le H/p_*\); using predictable continuation and the conditional mean duration \(L\) yields \(E[\text{ticks}]=L E[C]\le LH/p_*\). No quantitative compilation or efficient deadline follows from compactness alone. The finite expected time is for a positive tolerance. Exact hitting of an arbitrary prescribed unitary is impossible for a generic target, since the set of finite recorded words is countable.

**Two-output measurement at a fixed deadline.** Use the final direction read upon reaching the tolerance at home. If the deadline \(D\) ticks expires first, issue a prescribed binary fallback even if the two arms have yet to reassemble. For each successful transcript, rotate the direction projector by its actual logical frame. Choosing the projective unitary tolerance \(\gamma\) makes this effect differ from the target by at most \(2\gamma\) in operator norm; the global phase cancels. The event probabilities up to the deadline are state-independent, and the timeout probability \(u_D\) tends to zero (for instance \(u_D\le LH/(p_*D)\)). The sum of success effects plus the fallback defines a genuine two-output POVM. Either of its effects differs from its target by at most \(2\gamma+u_D\), and no chosen-setting trial is omitted. The feedback may be calculated in a chosen character convention together with its complex conjugate; the conjugate frame is close to the conjugate target for exactly the same stopping transcripts. Other sheet tags sharing the records can implement different effects. Preparation leakage and the ability to supply the designated pair must be handled separately.

For a calibrated common representation and an entangled qubit input, the local effects can serve as internal-model CHSH settings. If each local positive effect has error at most \(e\), each \(\pm1\) observable has error at most \(2e\); four correlators together have error at most \(16e\). The actual beam or trial protocol still needs a suitable bipartite source, a compatible relative orientation when complex effects are used, independent settings and physically local readouts. An event-ready source would herald success before either setting choice. Every setting trial must include its deadline fallback. This construction removes final gate-success discards within the model. It does not supply a loophole-free physical Bell experiment or a probability law independently established by experimental data.

**Independent controls.** I enumerated all binary direction paths through tick 12, stopping at the first boundary, and reconstructed all transcript matrices in the positive character. For \(L=6,M=3\) there were 122 distinct complete transcripts through this deadline, total mass \(1805/2048\) and timeout mass \(243/2048\). For \(L=10,M=5\) the 74 completed transcripts had total mass \(799/1024\). An unphased \(L=8\) control had 98 completed transcripts of mass \(413/512\). All branch Gram matrices agreed with \(2^{1-t}I\) to a maximum entry discrepancy below \(2.3\times10^{-16}\), and corresponding branch matrices from the two ports matched. Independently propagating the scalar radius walk gave probabilities of still being inside after 120 ticks of \(2.13\times10^{-8}\) at \(L=6\) and \(9.70\times10^{-4}\) at \(L=10\). These checks concern single excursions; the target-hitting conclusion uses the compactness proof.

**Operational meaning of the probability embedding.** The group and hitting proofs fix the ordinary real topology and norm on complex projective matrices. Cylinder weights are interpreted as ordinary normalized real probabilities and ordinary absolute discrepancies determine whether the controller stops. An abstract amplitude field, signed successors or merely the nonvanishing of a branch supplies neither this topology nor an empirical readout law. For a localized integer-coordinate preparation, native coherent coordinate masks, and a fixed finite decision rule on the recorded words, every finite transcript weight under the squared-count rule is a rational squared integer sum divided by a power of two; conditioning divides two such rational numbers. Applying an algebraic embedding to a character expansion cannot turn those recorded outcomes into a determination of an absolute embedding. A nonconjugate relabeling of a *tagged* character can change which coordinate target a particular word appears close to. Recalibrating the target or altering the controller in response compares different externally specified settings. At order three the two surviving nontrivial characters are exactly conjugates, so the same real instrument and conjugated target agree transcript by transcript.

The project's finite-deadline numerical controller accepts when the *direction axis* reaches its neighbourhood at either port. This is an applicable observable criterion; the reported finite sample does not directly simulate full projective-frame accuracy at a designated home port. After source heralding, the shared sheet must still be justified as a physically local implementation if Bell locality is claimed. Multiplication of two commuting sector factors in a common-register mathematical model establishes the product measurement probabilities, but alone does not exclude a shared physical communication channel.
