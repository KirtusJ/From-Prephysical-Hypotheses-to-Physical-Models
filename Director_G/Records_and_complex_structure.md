# What can the recorded walk prepare?

**From Prephysical Hypotheses to Physical Models**  
**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist  
Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

The weak-field walk admits a simpler preparation than coherent routing through auxiliary paths. Let it take three steps, observe the site and incoming channel, and repeat. The resulting records can concentrate the sheet wave on the real plane selected by the elementary holonomy. Within that plane, the real algebra generated by the walk and the records is a complex matrix algebra. An explicit joint walk then distinguishes two assumptions that matter for composition: sharing one sheet, and allowing two independent sheets.

This is a focused mathematical continuation of G8, G9 and OP-29. The root claim remains **UNRESOLVED**. The results below assume signed amplitude addition, normalization, a site/channel projection that leaves the sheet coherent, the Born probabilities of those projections, and a choice of when to read them. A physical mechanism for those assumptions remains an obligation.

## The sheet instrument obtained by waiting three steps

Use the update and Fourier convention of the [preceding construction](Signed_walk_and_weak_field.md). Write \(T|y\rangle=|y+1\rangle\). A three-step path from site \(x\) shifts the sheet by \(3x+a\), where \(a\) is the sum of its three departure positions relative to \(x\). A left-to-left coin transition changes the amplitude sign. Starting with incoming channel \(R\), the complete table is as follows. The entries are sheet operators with the common factor \(T^{3x}/\sqrt8\) omitted.

| Recorded displacement and channel | Sheet operator |
|---|---|
| \(+3,R\) | \(T^3\) |
| \(+1,L\) | \(T^3\) |
| \(+1,R\) | \(T+T^{-1}\) |
| \(-1,L\) | \(-T+T^{-1}\) |
| \(-1,R\) | \(-T^{-3}\) |
| \(-3,L\) | \(T^{-3}\) |

For example, \(RLR,LRR\) contribute the two constructive paths to \((+1,R)\). For incoming \(L\), the record \((-1,L)\) has operator \(-T-T^{-1}\), and \((+1,R)\) has \(T-T^{-1}\). The other four records still have a single path. These statements hold on the line and on a compatible spatial ring of length greater than six. The combined input coin and final displacement identify the constructive and destructive records on every block, including after a channel flip. The six squared norms sum to one for every sheet state.

On the Fourier character with \(h=2\pi r/M\), the two informative outcomes have probabilities \(C_r/2\) and \(D_r/2\), where \(C_r=\cos^2 h\) and \(D_r=\sin^2 h\). Each of the four remaining outcomes has probability \(1/8\). These probabilities are independent of site and incoming channel after relabelling the two informative outcomes. Reading only the site or reading after every *single* step defines a different instrument. A site/channel observation after every single step leaves one path per outcome, and every character has the same record law.

**A constructive record.** Start on one localized sheet. The first destructive record removes the constant mode and prepares a translated normalized dipole. Follow it by \(s\) constructive records. Up to a common scalar and a sheet translation, their operator is \((T+T^{-1})^s(T-T^{-1})\). No additional path labels, active shifts or coin resets are needed. The joint probability and posterior mass of the target pair \(r_*=(M-1)/2,-r_*\) for odd \(M\ge3\) are
\[
 P_s={1\over M2^{s+1}}\sum_r D_r C_r^s,\qquad
 W_s={2D_{r_*}C_{r_*}^{s}\over\sum_r D_r C_r^s}.
\]
The only surviving modes with largest \(C_r\) are the target pair, so \(W_s\to1\). For \(M=3\), the first destructive record is sufficient. At \(M=63\), a conservative 1,880-block word gives \(W_s=0.99999683\), with \(\log_{10}P_s=-572.07\). The previously reported shift filter had different normalization, path controls and preparation costs. Its success figures cannot be transferred to this instrument.

**Using all the records.** There is no need to wait for that exceptional word. Conditional on a sector, informative blocks occur with probability \(1/2\). Given an informative block, its destructive indicator is Bernoulli with probability \(q_j=\sin^2(\pi j/M)\). Here \(j\) is the smaller unsigned representative of \(2r\bmod M\), with \(0\le j\le(M-1)/2\). The target is \(j=1\). The conditional law is the same at every block, hence the indicators collected at successive informative blocks are iid within a sector. For a superposition the joint law is their classical mixture: all the record operators are diagonal in the sheet characters and distinct characters are orthogonal after the sheet is ignored. The posterior sector weights are exactly the Bayesian likelihood update. The localized-sheet prior gives weight \(1/M\) to \(j=0\) and \(2/M\) to each \(j>0\). This is an application of indirect quantum nondemolition measurement. Its assumed probability rule is explicit in the likelihood. [Bauer and Bernard, published analysis](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.84.044103), [Bauer, Benoist and Bernard, repeated measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.6045)

A finite performance guarantee follows without a simulation. The single-informative-block Hellinger affinity for classes \(i,j\) is
\[
 \sqrt{q_iq_j}+\sqrt{(1-q_i)(1-q_j)}=\cos(\pi|i-j|/M).
\]
Under class \(i\), the probability that a competing likelihood exceeds or ties its likelihood after \(k\) informative blocks is bounded by the \(k\)-th power of this affinity. Sum the square root of the two likelihoods' product over the event to obtain the bound, including cases with \(q_0=0\). A union bound over competitors gives conditional maximum-likelihood classification error at most \((M-1)\cos^k(\pi/M)/2\). The expected number of three-step blocks to obtain \(k\) informative ones is \(2k\).

At \(M=63\), 6,464 informative blocks suffice for a uniform classification-error bound of 0.01. Summing the binomial laws at all counts gives acceptance probability 0.03174595 for the decision \(j=1\) and mean target weight 0.99997425 over the accepted records. The second figure is the conditional probability of the target given acceptance under the specified prior. It is not a pointwise guarantee on every accepted word. A pointwise rule can compute and threshold the posterior on the observed word instead. Conditioning changes the sheet weights. Averaging over all records preserves the prior. A localized sheet therefore supplies only \(2/M\) target mass before selection. The measurement also leaves the **base** localized in a site and channel. To compare its subsequent motion with a single-band ray requires a justified band preparation on the base.

## A complex algebra generated on the selected real plane

Take a spatial ring of length \(N>6\) divisible by odd \(M\), and the \(d=2N\) site/channel labels. The statements about the area-two diamond below also hold on compatible smaller rings where its two paths are individually resolved by intermediate vertex projectors. Write \(F\) for the selected two-dimensional real plane of sheet functions. Translation restricted to \(F\) rotates by a fixed angle in an orthonormal sine/cosine basis. Let \(J\) denote a quarter turn on that plane, extended identically at each base vertex. Changing the orientation of the basis reverses its sign. The received edge weights were real.

**Result.** On \(\mathbb R^d\otimes F\), the unital real algebra closed under transpose generated by the lifted step \(U\) and the sheet-unresolved projectors \(P_v\) is isomorphic to \(M_d(\mathbb C)\) acting by realification. Its commutant on this real space consists of \(aI+bJ\). The central orthogonal complex structures in the represented algebra are exactly \(J,-J\).

**Proof.** Each edge block \(P_w U P_v\) is a nonzero scalar times a map between two vertex planes, given by a sheet rotation. It commutes with the common \(J\). Multiplying these edge blocks along individual paths isolates their transport. The two constructive three-step paths to (+1,R) have the same endpoints and sheet displacements differing by two. Multiply one path block by the transpose of the other, with the order chosen to close the path at the initial vertex. After removing the common scalar, the loop gives \(T^{\pm2}\) in that vertex corner. Its skew part, divided by the appropriate \(2\sin(2h)\), is \(J\) up to sign. This divisor is nonzero for the target on every odd \(M\ge3\). Since the underlying vertex graph is connected, paths and their transposes transport \(J\) to every vertex. For each ordered vertex pair the algebra contains a nonzero complex rotation in that matrix slot and its product with \(J\). Their real span is the full complex scalar slot. Summing the slots gives \(M_d(\mathbb C)\). Any commuting real map is vertex diagonal by the vertex projectors. Commutation with the full scalar slots forces one common complex scalar on every vertex. Imposing \(J'^2=-I\) on such a scalar leaves the two signs.

G9's obstruction for projections resolving every vertex of a connected real signed digraph still applies to its original observation menu. The new result concerns projections deliberately coarse in the sheet label, restricted to a sector reached with conditional probability. A sheet-localizing projector generally fails to preserve the plane. The real algebra closure also permits linear combinations and transposes. Algebra membership does not supply an experimental procedure that implements every element, unitary or positive effect. These are substantive limits on deriving the full quantum state and effect model. Other work has obtained complex structures under different inputs, including a common real ancillary system or a representation of spacetime symmetry. [Aleksandrova, Borish and Wootters](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.87.052106), [Moretti and Oppio](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09029)

**Orientation test.** For any sequence of real controls and real projective readouts, simultaneously conjugating the initial state and all complex sector matrices conjugates each record amplitude. Its squared norm stays fixed. In particular, with a real base preparation the entire readout law agrees on \(r\) and \(-r\), even when later real operations are chosen from earlier outcomes. A localized sheet followed by the preceding record filter has equal posterior weights on those two characters. It prepares a real plane without resolving an absolute orientation.

A relative orientation can have an observable consequence. In the complex description prepare \((|0,R\rangle+e^{i\alpha}|2,R\rangle)/\sqrt2\). After two steps, the competing paths to \((0,L)\) are \(RL\) and \(LL\). Their coin signs are opposite and their departure-position sums are 1 and 3. Thus \(p_h=(1-\cos(\alpha-2h))/4\). At \(M=5,\ r=1,\ \alpha=\pi/2\), the two field signs give 0.103054 and 0.396946. At \(\alpha=0\) the two probabilities agree. The extra resource is a phase reference that holds \(\alpha\) fixed when \(h\) reverses. Joint conjugation reverses both and leaves the data invariant.

**An effect that the generated algebra contains and the native records cannot measure.** On the line or an even spatial ring let \(C=(-1)^X\). It anticommutes with one walk step and commutes with every vertex projector. Every fixed record sequence has a Kraus product \(A\) of steps and projectors. Conjugation by \(C\) multiplies \(A\) by the sign \((-1)^{\text{total steps}}\), hence \(A^*A\) commutes with \(C\). Conditioning future observation times on earlier records preserves this property on every branch. Coarsening or randomizing over observed branches preserves it as well.

The two states \((|0,R\rangle\pm|1,R\rangle)/\sqrt2\) in the same character therefore yield identical probabilities for every such record sequence, yet projection onto the plus state gives respective probabilities one and zero. This projection belongs to the generated \(M_d(\mathbb C)\). Thus the full-record instrument menu is provably insufficient for tomography of the whole complex space even with arbitrarily many time settings. On a ten-site ring (\(M=5\)), the rank of the real span of all single-read effects \(U^{-t}P_v U^t\) through \(t=0,1,3,10,20\) was respectively 20, 30, 50, 102 and 102 at singular-value cutoff \(10^{-9}\), against 400 real parameters for all Hermitian matrices. These are finite-window numerical ranks. The parity witness proves the all-time obstruction independently of the cutoff. The additional phase-reference example above uses positions of the *same* parity and is consistent with this obstruction.

## Composition, relative orientation and a collision

Let two such planes have complex structures \(J_A,J_B\). In the ordinary **real** tensor product, \(Q=-J_A\otimes J_B\) is a symmetric involution. The \(+1\) subspace enforces \(J_A\otimes I=I\otimes J_B\). The \(-1\) subspace enforces the opposite equality. Choosing either provides a complex space of dimension \(d_A d_B\), with the second factor conjugated in the opposite case. Indeed, after fixing bases, each pair of local complex coordinates contributes two real dimensions to each eigenspace. A real product vector has equal squared projection onto the two sectors, because \(\langle a,J_Aa\rangle=\langle b,J_Bb\rangle=0\). Consequently the operation “take the ordinary real tensor product” does not fix the common complex orientation.

There is a direct witness of the missing composite effect. The normalized projectors onto the two \(Q\) sectors have identical statistics for every product of local real symmetric effects. Their difference is proportional to \(J_A\otimes J_B\), whose trace pairing with such a product vanishes. A measurement of \(Q\), if admitted, distinguishes the sectors perfectly. This gives an explicit obstruction to selecting the relative orientation from those local statistics. Real quantum tomography has related limits for composites. [Hardy and Wootters](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.4870)

For a connected graph of \(n\) factors, put \(J_i\) on the \(i\)-th factor and require \(Q_{ij}=-J_iJ_j=s_{ij}\) on chosen edges. The \(Q_{ij}\) commute. Their product around each cycle is \(I\), so a negative cycle product gives the zero joint subspace. Conversely, with all cycle products positive, choose at the vertices orientations whose pairwise products are the edge signs, by transport along a spanning tree. On complexifying each real factor its \(J_i\) has \(+i,-i\) eigenspaces. Only two conjugate global sign assignments survive in a connected graph. The joint subspace has real dimension \(2\prod_i d_i\). This is the signed-graph balance condition in the representation furnished by the walk. The arguments derive the constraint a consistent orientation must obey. They do not choose the edge signs from local records. [Harary, *On the notion of balance of a signed graph*](https://doi.org/10.1307/mmj/1028989917)

**A common-sheet construction.** On the product of two base rings, keep a single cyclic sheet. At departure from \((x,y)\), shift it by \(x+y\). Apply one Hadamard coin per walker and their respective conditional shifts. On a character this gives \(U_h\otimes U_h\), since the phase factor splits as \(e^{-ihx}e^{-ihy}\). On its real conjugate plane this is the real form of the ordinary complex tensor product. This construction can be iterated associatively for free factors by adding their departure positions. It assumes a common sheet, a common update and a joint configuration space. Retaining independent sheets is a different admissible construction with both relative orientations. Three-step propagation of the full real common-sheet walk at four orders reproduced the factorized joint probabilities.

A conditional interaction is available without putting complex edge weights on the product graph. Add \(g\,\mathbf1_{x=y}\) to the sheet displacement, for integer \(g\). On the selected character a collision contributes \(z=e^{-igh}\). Prepare each distinguishable walker in the equal position superposition at 0 and 2 with a coin state that the next coin sends entirely right. The walk sends them to positions 1 and 3. Up to local phases, the matrix of their joint amplitudes is
\[
 \frac12\begin{pmatrix}z&1\\ 1&z\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant has magnitude \(|\sin(gh)|/2\), and either reduced state's purity is \(1-\sin^2(gh)/2\). Thus \(g=1\) entangles these inputs on every tested odd order, whereas \(g=0\) and \(g=M\) give product outputs. For the weak-field pair and fixed \(g=1\) this one-step entanglement tends to zero as \(M\to\infty\). Contact interactions in coined walks are a published model class. The new calculation identifies how the assumed interaction is encoded in this shared real transport. Neither an interaction law nor a classical interacting limit has been selected by the primitives. [Ahlbrecht et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1051)

## Review and reproducibility

The prior's weak-field plane posed two distinct questions: whether its preparation needs auxiliary coherent routing, and whether the full observable and composition laws follow. The three-step table resolves the first under the declared projective instrument. The real algebra calculation gives a positive conditional answer for a common complex structure. The indistinguishable orientation sectors and two admissible composite constructions leave the original physical identification open.

The [checking program](check_record_selection.py) compares integer path enumeration with full lifted propagation, propagates the conditioned sheet vector separately from its Fourier weights, sums the complete binomial count distribution, and checks the two-particle density-matrix purity. The triangle synchronization control uses exact integer matrices. The remaining numerical calculations are binary64. No experimental data were collected and no cases were trimmed. The initial hand prediction for the phase-reference control failed. [All first-run phase residuals](results/orientation_oracle_first_run.json) remain alongside the [corrected checks](results/record_selection.json) and [every prescribed-branch intermediate value](results/record_branch_trials.csv). The corrected expression was derived from the two actual paths.

The mathematics and code received a same-participant self-check. The cited papers ground the existing mathematical and operational context. No independent referee has verified this extension. The mathematical-physics articles' inspected versions, publisher entries and available DOI metadata provide limited publication-status checks. A comprehensive correction and retraction search has not been performed.
