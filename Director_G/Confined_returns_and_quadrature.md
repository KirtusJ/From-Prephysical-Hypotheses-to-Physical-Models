# How much space and time does a circular coin take?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist  
Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Two returning arms can give a real walk a quarter-turn between its coin rails. When used before the force flight, the turn selects a direction of acceleration. This paper calculates the smallest spatial and temporal resources for a confined two-arm construction, and gives a lower bound for arbitrary coherent preparations with ancillas and heralds. Both statements refer to a specified sheet character, with the field the only source of complex phase in that representation. ## Area available to a returning path

Use the line with integer offsets $z_0=z_{2a}=0$, $|z_{t+1}-z_t|=1$, $|z_t|\le r$. Count departure sites, including the initial site and excluding the final site. Each tick has a supplied sign $f_t=\pm1$. In a character with field angle $h$, a route launched at $x$ gains phase $\exp[-ih\sum_t f_t(x+z_t)]$. Its reflected companion sees the opposite offset. Real, global $I,X$ pulses after a single balanced split implement these two deterministic words. The same pulses work on each occupied launch site by linearity. For $r\ge1$ define

$$B(a,r)=s^2+(a-s)(2s-1),\qquad s=\min(a,r).$$

For arbitrary field signs, $|\sum f_t z_t|\le B$. At each tick the offset is bounded by $\min(t,2a-t,r)$ rounded down to the parity of $t$. Sum this upper envelope. An outward ramp to $s$, $a-s$ bounces between $s$ and $s-1$, and a return ramp attain the sum. At radius one the area is $a$; without the wall it is $a^2$. Positive-length returns require positive radius and even duration. [Lean proof on actual nearest-neighbor paths](lean/ConfinedReturn.lean)

To remove the phase slope in $x$, require $\sum_t f_t=0$. Write $b=\lfloor a/2\rfloor$, $d=\lceil a/2\rceil$. The exact maximum one-arm compensated area is

$$A(a,r)=B(b,r)+B(d,r).$$

To see the upper bound, a closed path of span $D$ has equally many upward and downward crossings on each edge of its interval. Subtract the interval midpoint from every departure, which leaves the balanced sum invariant. One required up/down pair per edge contributes a total $\lceil D^2/2\rceil$ to the sum of absolute deviations. Each of the remaining $a-D$ pairs contributes at most $\max(1,D-1)$. For $D=1$ the bound is $a$, also its value at $D=2$ when attainable. From $D\ge2$ it increases up to $\min(2r,a)$, as direct subtraction shows. Substitution there yields $A(a,r)$. The empty path has zero area.

Follow an upper extremal lobe of length $2b$ and a reflected lower lobe of length $2d$, reversing the field at the join. If $d=b+1$, flip the field also on the first, zero-offset departure of the longer lobe. This balances the numbers of signs without changing the area. To realize smaller gains keep this maximizing word. Its sorted departures have adjacent differences zero or one. Assign plus to its upper half and move the selected indices, one adjacent step at a time, into the lower half. The area takes every integer from $A$ to $-A$ with parity $a$. The assertion allows either terminal rail. Requiring an R start and L finish can lower the maximum, from five to three at six ticks and radius two. For the two nonempty reflected lobes the R-start arm ends on R, and the calibrated reversal of *all* signs gives $L/R=e^{-2ihA}$. This rail convention matters in a subsequent flight. [Enumeration, pulse controls and exact finite outputs](results/confined_loop.json)

With $h=2\pi/M$, an exact quarter-turn requires $8\mid M$ and an admissible area congruent to $\pm M/8$ modulo $M/2$. Nearest-integer tuning gives a relative phase error at most $|h|$ when the half-length parity can be chosen. For the earlier unit-force scale $\ell=\delta^2,\ \tau=\delta^3,\ h=\delta^4$, write $R=r\ell$, $T=2a\tau$. Any returning pair has relative phase magnitude at most $2|h|(2a)r$. Thus $RT\ge\pi\delta/4$ for exact quadrature. Choose $r$ of order $\delta^{-3/2}$ and $a$ of order $\delta^{-5/2}$ with appropriate rounding. The compensated construction has $A/(2ar)\to1$, and both $R$ and $T$ shrink as $\sqrt\delta$. Its maximal base speed is $1/\delta$. Retiming the same per-hop phases to impose $\tau\ge\ell/c$ gives $RT\ge\pi/(4c)$. Physically integrating a potential over the retimed tick instead changes $h$ and leads to a different bound below. ## A heralded coin with arbitrary real controls

Take a real normalized initial state, including all memory in the tested sector. Between diagonal phase gates allow arbitrary real isometries and coherent real records. An accepted flag $P$ is real and commutes with the two circular-coin projections $Q_\pm$ on $(1,\pm i)/\sqrt2$. Deferred measurement includes first failures as orthogonal flags. At gate $t$, the **field-free** prefix state sees phases within an arc of half-width $\alpha_t$ after removal of a scalar phase. For the bounded field above one can take $\alpha_t=|h_t|r_t$. Let $p$ be the total accept probability and $\eta\le1/2$ the fraction of the opposite circular port among accepts. For $p>0$,

$$\sum_t\alpha_t\ \ge\
\arcsin\!\left[\sqrt{p/2}\,(\sqrt{1-\eta}-\sqrt{\eta})\right]. \tag{1}$$

Use the projective distance $\arccos|\langle v,w\rangle|$. One phase gate moves its field-free input by at most $\alpha_t$, since the real part of the centered overlap is at least $\cos\alpha_t$ for $\alpha_t\le\pi/2$. Beyond that the angular bound is automatic. Replace the field gates in order using field-free prefixes and actual suffixes. Isometry and the triangle inequality bound the distance to the field-free real output by the left side. If a prefix has exterior probability $q_t$, clipping its phases outside the arc adds at most $\min(\pi/2,2\arcsin\sqrt{q_t})$ to that gate's allowance, since the old and clipped vectors have overlap real part at least $1-2q_t$. [Geometry of the projective distance, Anandan and Aharonov](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.65.1697)

For every global phase, the accepted circular component of squared norm $p(1-\eta)$ has imaginary part of norm $\sqrt{p(1-\eta)/2}$ in the real basis. This holds for an arbitrary complex ancillary vector tensor the circular rail. The other component has imaginary norm $\sqrt{p\eta/2}$. Taking their difference in the triangle inequality gives the sine on the right of (1) as a lower bound on the imaginary norm. A real flag cannot enlarge this norm. Align the phase to maximize overlap with any normalized real comparison vector. That overlap is at most the norm of the real part, proving (1).

For deterministic perfect polarization the angle is at least $\pi/4$. For a rare herald the dependence on $p$ is sharp. Recombine two equal real paths phased by $\pm\alpha$ into amplitudes $\cos\alpha$ and $i\sin\alpha$. A real splitter retains a fraction $\tan\alpha$ of the first amplitude and all of the second in the accept flag. For $0<\alpha\le\pi/4$ the accepted spin is exactly circular, with $p=2\sin^2\alpha$. The splitter setting is a real calibrated resource. A circuit starting with an imaginary ancillary phase falls outside the real-input hypothesis. [Ancillary-state and all-port checks](results/quadrature_locality.json)

For a uniform physical potential $FY$, action scale $\varepsilon$, cell size $\ell$ and tick $\tau$, $h=F\ell\tau/\varepsilon$. Deterministic exact preparation from a real state confined to physical radius $R$ for duration $T$ therefore requires $RT\ge\pi\varepsilon/(4|F|)$. Here $R$ encloses the entire field-free state at each kick about a single center. For always-moving returned deterministic arms from one site with speed at most $c$, each arm's radius at time $t$ is at most $c\min(t,T-t)$. The integral gives $|F|cT^2/(4\varepsilon)\ge\pi/4$. Triangular arms attain this time bound, subject to phase rounding on a finite sheet. For the two-arm gate the calculation at each separate launch extends to a packet by linearity. A finite action scale forbids both sizes tending to zero at fixed force. The semiclassical limit permits both to shrink.
