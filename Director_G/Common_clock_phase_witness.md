# Comparing winding phases at a common site and clock

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A two-well walk can test a designated phase using the same sharp launch and the same detector at **tick four**. The control selects which ticks carry an unknown gain. For an ideal rail read, two settings suffice to test a pure target against arbitrary stable phase mixtures and fair counting. With unknown rail confusion or loss, one additional setting and a terminal rail exchange identify the phase distribution's first two normalized cosine moments. The raw frequencies then give a sharp interval for the counting fraction. [Two-clock experiment with a permanent gain](Two_well_phase_witness.md) · [Physical rail exchange](Separate_rail_scores.md)

## Two switch settings

Let $H=2^{-1/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $D_\beta=\mathrm{diag}(1,\beta)$ with $|\beta|=1$. Start at well zero on rail R. At each tick the spatial shift takes either outgoing rail to the other well. A gain-on step uses the arc gains $(1,\beta,1,-\beta)$ for RR, RL, LR, LL, respectively, and has rail matrix $D_\beta H$. A gain-off step uses $(1,1,1,-1)$ and rail matrix $H$. This switch removes the L-arrival gain while preserving the LL sign and balanced mixing. The off operation and its stability are additional apparatus premises. A different sign, a which-path mark, or a different coin on a switched tick can spoil the result.

All runs last four ticks. Let $h=0,1,2$ designate, respectively, gain-on ticks $\varnothing$, $\{1\}$, and $\{1,3\}$. In the one-gain program $\{3\}$ also works. Since $H^2=I$, the products in time order give $H D_\beta^h H$. Every word ends at well zero. On a coherent phase, the right-rail amplitude is $(1+\beta^h)/2$ and the left amplitude is $(1-\beta^h)/2$. On a fair-count launch each rail has probability $1/2$ under every program, including $h=0$. With $c$ the count fraction, let $\nu$ be the coherent subprobability on $x=\operatorname{Re}\beta$, with total $T=1-c$. The ideal R-rail probabilities obey
$$2p_h-1=J_h=\int T_h(x)\,d\nu(x),\qquad (T_0,T_1,T_2)=(1,x,2x^2-1). \tag{1}$$
Here $T_h$ is the Chebyshev polynomial, distinct from the scalar $T$. The last output has no gain in the stated schedules. The independent word calculation retains all intermediate exponents, including the cancelling middle power in the two-gain R output.

Fix $m=\operatorname{Re}\beta_*$ before obtaining the rates. The two switched settings $h=1,2$ give
$$S_m=\tfrac12+m^2-2m(2p_1-1)+(2p_2-1)/2
=\int(x-m)^2\,d\nu(x)+c(\tfrac12+m^2). \tag{2}$$
Thus $S_m=0$ holds exactly when all launches are coherent and lie on the designated conjugate pair. Any upper bound $S_m\le s$ bounds the count weight by $s/(1/2+m^2)$ and the coherent mass at $|x-m|\ge\delta$ by $s/\delta^2$. At a cubic unit ($m=-1/2$) the two right probabilities are both $1/4$. The h=0 run has $p_0=1$ for that pure ensemble and supplies an independent count check $c=2(1-p_0)$. All denominators count original launches, including missed detections when reports have more than two outcomes.

## What an unknown detector still measures

Assume the terminal apparatus has fixed response distributions $K_R,K_L$ given the true read rail, independent of the winding label, count/coherent choice, program and exchange. They may include dark responses, wrong rails and no report; assign each original launch one mutually exclusive outcome, including no-click. The outputs may be arbitrarily unequal. An independent terminal routing choice either sends the two output rails to the apparatus directly ($+$), or interchanges them ($-$) at the same well before classification. The exchange must act on the physical modes without changing the response bank or the four propagation ticks. An equally timed switch on both paths can supply this control. The initial sharp R rail can remain fixed. Alternatively start separately on known R and L and use the same detector, when the two preparations carry the same phase/count ensemble. Their true right probabilities are complementary by the displayed two-by-two matrix, and fair counts are still balanced. Terminal relabeling of already recorded clicks does not implement an exchange.

Write $q_h^\pm$ for the full vectors of observed frequencies, $B=(K_R+K_L)/2$, $E_h=q_h^+-q_h^-$. Then
$$q_h^\pm=B\ \pm\tfrac12(K_R-K_L)J_h,\qquad E_0=(K_R-K_L)T.\tag{3}$$
A common midpoint $(q_h^++q_h^-)/2$ across all settings is a testable consequence. If $E_0\ne0$, select any component with $E_{0,y}\ne0$. The signed ratios $r_h=E_{h,y}/E_{0,y}=J_h/T$ must be the same in every informative component. The normalized variance witness
$$V_m=(1+r_2)/2-2mr_1+m^2=\frac{\int(x-m)^2\,d\nu(x)}{T}$$
vanishes exactly when every coherent launch belongs to the designated conjugate pair. Positive $V_m$ bounds the fraction of coherent mass outside a $\delta$ cosine neighborhood by $V_m/\delta^2$. In finite data, the ratio needs a denominator distinguishable from zero. A response with tiny $E_0$ can require many launches. If $E_0=0$, either $T=0$ or the bank has identical response to the two rails; all program contrasts then vanish.

For compatible exact data with $E_0\ne0$, **all feasible coherent fractions** are given by
$$\max_{y:B_y>0}\frac{|E_{0,y}|}{2B_y}\ \le\ T\ \le\ 1. \tag{4}$$
An outcome with $B_y=0$ has $E_{0,y}=0$. Necessity follows by nonnegativity of $K_R=B+E_0/(2T)$ and $K_L=B-E_0/(2T)$. Conversely each $T$ in the interval gives two normalized response distributions since $\sum_y E_{0,y}=0$. Choose any conditional phase measure consistent with the first two normalized moments, allocate it mass $T$, and assign $1-T$ to counting. All observations in (3) are reproduced. When the endpoint in (4) is one the data force pure coherence. Otherwise positive counts are compatible even if $V_m=0$. A specific calibrated component with known nonzero $K_{R,y}-K_{L,y}$ gives $T$ directly. The all-off setting alone cannot calibrate that component: coherent paths return to their launching rail, but fair paths continue to split.

For example take the target $m=-1/2$ in the coherent population. Give the detector three outcomes, including a miss, and choose $K_R=(3/4,1/8,1/8)$, $K_L=(1/8,1/2,3/8)$ and $T=2/3$. The lower endpoint in (4) is $10/21$. Both $T=10/21$ and $T=1$ reproduce all six vectors with the same coherent target and suitable response columns. This ambiguity extends to any other balanced program whose fair read stays one half, under the same response assumption.

## Earliest common propagation clock

Consider one fixed duration, arbitrary switches of this gain at individual ticks, a single sharp rail at either well, and terminal rail measurement with optional physical exchange. Translation exchanges the names of the two wells; the occupied well at each clock is deterministic. For a calibrated read, a one-tick program always gives $1/2$. At two ticks the only R probabilities for a coherent sharp R launch are $1$ and $(1+x)/2$, depending on the first switch. These determine the count weight and first cosine moment at best. For $|m|<1$, a mixture on $x=1,-1$ can match the mean of the pure target. A target at $m=\pm1$ is selected at two ticks by an extremal probability in the one-gain program.

At three ticks the final gain drops out of a rail probability. If the first two gains are $\alpha,\gamma\in\{1,\beta\}$, the R-port numerator before division by $8$ is $|1+\alpha+\gamma-\alpha\gamma|^2$. It equals $4$ when either gain is off and $8-4x^2$ when both are on. The opposite sharp rail or an output exchange complements these probabilities. The target with $m=0$ gives $p=1$ in the both-on program, which excludes both count and all competing cosine values. For every other interior target the phase with real part $-m$ matches every possible response at tick three. Consequently the optimal common duration for selecting the full pure target under a calibrated rail read is two for $m=\pm1$, three for $m=0$, and four for all other $m$. This lower bound allows any number of settings at the chosen duration and assumes the controls just specified.

For unknown identical-across-settings response columns with $E_0\ne0$ at the four-tick reference, the normalized coherent target instead needs two ticks at either endpoint and four for every interior cosine. At tick three every informative contrast is proportional to a single number $\int(1-x^2)d\nu$, and its detector scale is unknown. Even for $m=0$, a pure interior alternative $x'\ne0$ can reproduce the data for some nonzero-contrast banks by increasing their contrast by $1/(1-x'^2)$ at the same midpoint. This proves the minimax lower bound for arbitrary informative banks; particular extremal observed probabilities can be more conclusive. Full pure-law selection with an arbitrary uncalibrated bank retains the fraction ambiguity of (4).

All programs reach the same physical site and read time. Randomizing program assignment independently of the preparation and detector before the first switched tick gives each setting the same preassignment population in expectation. Randomizing the terminal exchange after the flight can separately balance readout assignment. The assumptions still require the gain to stay fixed during each coherent flight, the switch to preserve the stipulated dynamics, reports to be mixtures of the true rail-conditional columns, and the detector distribution to apply to the ensemble sampled. A phase label correlated with a detector that has no rail contrast may leave unobserved mass. Selection here concerns the specified phase/count family and a physically designated generator. No frequencies from a realized walk entered the derivation.
