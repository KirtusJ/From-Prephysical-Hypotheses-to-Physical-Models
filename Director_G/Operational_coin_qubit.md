# Preparing and measuring a coin qubit with the walk’s own records

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist
Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research contribution by Director G (OpenAI Codex), 13 November 2026.

Can an integer signed walk perform measurements whose outputs require both quadratures of a complex qubit? In the model below a record of distance from a starting site suffices. It preserves the relative amplitude of two spatial paths. Reading which path was taken changes the answer. With three cyclic sheets two walkers can also produce an exact all-output CHSH value of \(134503435/67108864\). All quantities in this last prediction can be computed from signed paths. This gives a conditional operational model with an experimentally testable instrument specification. Its records leave the absolute probability embedding open.

## Two paths and three analyzer settings

The walk has site \(x\), direction \(R,L\), and cyclic sheet \(y\bmod M\). A tick translates the sheet by its departure site, applies \(H=2^{-1/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) to the directions, and moves one site in the outgoing direction. Write \(T\) for the unit sheet shift, \(X\) for the coin swap and \(Z\) for the signed direction read. Use a ring of \(2L\) sites with \(M\mid L\) and \(L\ge4\). Begin at port \(0\) or \(L\). At tick \(j\) of a prescribed \(2k\)-tick excursion, \(k<L\), read membership in the two sites at circular distance \(\min(j,2k-j)\) from the starting site. A single coherent membership projector covers the two arms.

The surviving words are \(R^kL^k,L^kR^k\). Their departure sums relative to the port are \(+k^2,-k^2\), respectively. After their first split, each arm has \(k-1\) negative \(L\)-to-\(L\) entries. They return with interchanged directions. Since the starting port vanishes modulo \(M\), the joint accepted operator on coin and sheet is
\[
 (-1)^{k-1}2^{(1-2k)/2}\,X\,\mathrm{diag}(T^{k^2},T^{-k^2})H.
\]
Its squared scalar is the success probability \(2^{1-2k}\) for *every* input, including an input entangled with another system. In the convention \(T\mapsto e^{-ih}\), \(h=2\pi r/M\), the normalized projective gate is \(U_k=X\,\mathrm{diag}(e^{-ihk^2},e^{ihk^2})H\). Opposite characters acquire conjugate gates. This procedure uses one fixed coin and changes the coarse spatial records. Controlling a walk to implement generalized measurements has also been studied with differently engineered coins. [Kurzyński and Wójcik](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.1800) · [Formal signed-path result](lean/CoinInterferometer.lean)

On \(M=3,\ r=1\) put \(G=U_1\). Measuring direction after zero, one or two successful applications of \(G\) gives axes \((0,0,1),(-1,0,0),(0,\sqrt3/2,1/2)\) in the usual Pauli convention. Their three conditional signed means determine the input Bloch vector through \(z=E_0,\ x=-E_1,\ y=(2E_2-E_0)/\sqrt3\). The four preparations \(R,L,GR,G^2R\) have Hilbert–Schmidt Gram determinant \(3/16\). The opposite orientation conjugates preparations and effects together. In one control, input \(GR\) followed by two further successful \(G\) gates gives output masses per prepared input, including analyzer failures, of \((7/32,1/32)\). Per initial localized direction trial, the preparation herald halves both masses. Reading the arm at each analyzer split instead gives per-prepared-input masses of \((1/8,1/8)\). Setting the sheet gain to zero reduces the rank of these three analyzer axes to two. These tests concern one coin at a meeting port. Access to arbitrary coherences across the full ring requires other instruments. [Signed-path and port tables](results/coin_qubit.json) · [Independent reading](Coin_qubit_review.md)

## Which rotations the excursions generate

Let \(q=M/\gcd(r,M)\) be odd and greater than one. Write \(J=XH\) and \(D=\mathrm{diag}(e^{ih},e^{-ih})\). Then \(U_k=D^{k^2}J\). The closure of the positive words in \(U_1,U_3\) contains their inverses, since powers in a compact group recur at the identity. It contains \(U_3U_1^{-1}=D^8\), hence \(D\) since eight is coprime to \(q\), and then \(J\). Projectively \(J\) rotates the \(z\)-axis to the \(x\)-axis. This gives two perpendicular rotations of angle \(\alpha=\pm2h\) and order \(q\).

Their product has determinant-one spinor trace \(2\cos^2(\alpha/2)=1+\cos\alpha\). All Galois conjugates of \(\cos\alpha\) have absolute value strictly between zero and one for odd order \(q>1\). Its nonzero field norm has absolute value below one, so \(\cos\alpha\) cannot be an algebraic integer. A finite-projective-order spinor has root-of-unity eigenvalues and algebraic-integer trace. The group is therefore infinite. An infinite proper closed subgroup of \(SO(3)\) preserves a line. Indeed its identity component must have one-dimensional Lie algebra, since cross products rule out two-dimensional Lie subalgebras and a compact discrete Lie group is finite. The full group normalizes that component. Two non-half-turn rotations about distinct axes have no common invariant line. Thus the projective closure is \(SO(3)\). The odd-order restriction matters. On the trivial character the prescribed returns generate only powers of \(J\). At \(q=3\), radii one and two alone give the same gate. Rational-angle perpendicular rotations have an independent mathematical literature. [Radin and Sadun](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9601202)

## Measuring every radius outcome

At each tick measure the complete circular distance from the current starting port. Record every outcome and restart the excursion at its first arrival at distance zero or \(L\). After the first tick, a possible distance history \(0=r_0,1=r_1,\ldots,r_d\) leaves exactly two arms at each interior distance. For arm \(a=\pm1\) the position is \(x_0+a r_j\) and its incoming direction is \(a(r_j-r_{j-1})\). Each possible next distance retains one outgoing edge on each arm, of the same modulus \(1/\sqrt2\). The arms occupy orthogonal cells, also at the final meeting where their directions are opposite. Thus each interior choice is fair, independently of the unknown coin. In particular the distance transcript contains no information about the input coin.

For a complete transcript of length \(d\) the scalar probability is \(2^{1-d}\). Divide the Kraus matrix by its positive square root. Row \(a\) of the initial \(H\) receives sheet translation \(T^{a\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}r_j}\) and a minus sign for each consecutive pair of left moves on that arm **after the initial split**. Put it in the row determined by final direction \(a(r_d-r_{d-1})\). The resulting matrix is unitary in every character. A remaining interior arm at a deadline still encodes the first split. Reading its side then measures the original \(X=H^\dagger ZH\), with a known assignment of the binary outputs.

The radius after the first split is a fair random walk from 1, stopped at 0 or \(L\). From any interior point there is a uniformly positive probability of absorption in at most \(L\) steps. Its crossing probability from \(r\) is \(r/L\) and its expected remaining duration is \(r(L-r)\), by the finite first-step equations. A complete excursion from the port takes \(L\) ticks on average and crosses with probability \(1/L\). Recentering at the other port gives identical gate laws since \(M\mid L\). A Lean proof checks the coordinates, both edge choices and the rational first-step tables. An independent dictionary implementation enumerates the entire first-excursion tree through tick ten from both ports. [Formal radius calculation](lean/RadialRecords.lean) · [Enumerated trees and sampled trajectories](results/radial_records.json)

**Feedback theorem.** Track the product \(F\) of the completed projective gates. Every prescribed radius-one or radius-three mirror return has positive probability. For any desired gate and positive projective tolerance, from each current \(F\) there is a finite word that takes it strictly inside that tolerance. The same word works on an open neighborhood of \(F\). Compactness gives finitely many such words, at most \(h\) excursions long, each having specified transcript probability at least \(p>0\). Check after each meeting. Given any previous misses, the chance of a hit in the next \(h\) excursions is at least \(p\). Thus the probability of missing \(nh\) excursions is at most \((1-p)^n\). The mean number of excursions is at most \(h/p\). The indicator of beginning the next excursion depends only on earlier records, so the mean tick count is at most \(Lh/p\). A specified final port is covered by adjoining its crossing bit to the frame, as proved in the accompanying simultaneous-control result. This argument supplies no practical bound on synthesis cost as the tolerance shrinks. For comparison, prior repeat-until-success circuits use measurement and corrective feedback with a supplied quantum gate library. [Paetznick and Svore](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.1074) · [Multiple planes and prescribed ports](Sector_control_and_probability_embedding.md)

For a hard deadline \(D_0\), announce a specified binary output on a miss. Its probability is at most \(Lh/(pD_0)\) by Markov's inequality. If the projective operator-norm error on a hit is at most \(\eta\), the measured projector differs from the target by at most \(2\eta\). The total, unconditional effect error is bounded by \(2\eta+Lh/(pD_0)\). Starting from a definite direction this also prepares any pure coin state to any given accuracy at meetings; mixing separate preparations with calibrated classical probabilities gives the Bloch ball in closure. The numerical controller checked a different and cheaper criterion, direction-axis distance at either port. With a 180-tick cap it hit 59 of 64 trials for \(X\), 64 for \(Y\), and 62 for the oblique direction \((2,3,1)/\sqrt{14}\). All outcomes, times and misses are retained. ## A finite two-walker correlation, with all analyzer outputs

Use \(M=3,\ L=6\), two walkers beginning at \(0,R\), and one localized shared sheet. Each tick applies both coins and the two self gains \(x_A+x_B\). With contact off, A reads the outcome \((-1,L)\) after three steps. Its sheet vector is a translated signed dipole, with zero constant component and total probability \(1/4\). Meanwhile B records each individual move. Three of its eight equally probable words also arrive at \(-1\). Require a recorded right hop by both walkers, probability \(1/4\). They are now at \(0,R\) with the same faithful real plane on every accepted branch. Apply one split to each walker, enable the additional gain \(g\,\mathbf1_{x_A=x_B}\) only at the next departure, then read the common return of each walker to zero without resolving either arm. The joint return probability is \(1/4\). All source tests precede the two binary analyzer choices. The six-tick, fully synchronous source succeeds with probability \(3/512\). An alternative accepts all pre-contact routing outcomes until both walkers reach \(0,R\) together. In eight ticks every same-parity site pair has positive probability of a simultaneous directed arrival. The minimum on this ring is \(1/4096\). Hence the alternative succeeds with probability \(1/16\) overall and has unbounded preparation time. Each measured routing word induces only a common translation and sign within each character. [All source words and timing](results/radial_bell.json)

After the source herald, in one faithful orientation the two-coin amplitudes in output order \((-a,-b)\), \(a,b=\pm1\), are
\[
 \psi_{-a,-b}=\tfrac12\exp[-ih(a+b+g\,\mathbf1_{a=b})].
\]
The other orientation and its analyzers are complex conjugates. For \(g=1\) the concurrence \(2|\det\psi|\) equals \(\sqrt3/2\). More generally it is \(|\sin(hg)|\). Local basis changes give Schmidt coefficients \(\cos\theta,\sin\theta\) with \(\sin2\theta=C\). Along Schmidt axes the correlation matrix is \(\mathrm{diag}(C,-C,1)\). Optimizing the two orthogonal directions proportional to the sum and difference of Bob's two unit axes gives \(2\sqrt{1+C^2}\), here \(\sqrt7\), in the closure of the analyzer family. The finite protocol below attains a smaller value with a specified cutoff. [Horodecki, Horodecki and Horodecki](https://doi.org/10.1016/0375-9601(95)00214-N)

Alice uses \(Z\) or \(X\), the latter read by the side after one split. Bob's second setting reads \(-X\) immediately by the same method. His first setting starts one radial excursion, with cap 24. For a meeting let \(d=\pm1\) be its last radius increment, \(s=\pm1\) the relative sign of its two arms, and \(j=\sum r_i\bmod3\) over departures. If \(t=(x,y,z)\) is the sum of the two rows of the source correlation matrix for Alice's \(Z,X\), the scores for reading the returned coin in \(X,Z\) are respectively
\[
 b_X=s\bigl(z\cos(4\pi j/3)+y\sin(4\pi j/3)\bigr),\qquad b_Z=d\,x.
\]
Here \(t=(-1/2,-\sqrt3/2,-3/4)\). Read the basis with the larger absolute score, break ties toward \(Z\), and multiply the answer by the sign of the selected score. If the deadline is reached between ports, read the side as \(-X\). This announces an output on **every** post-herald trial within 25 analyzer ticks. Once a party has read its output it can leave that result in a persistent classical record. With contact off, local steps and measurements commute across parties on each shared-sheet character. Ignoring any later complete local evolution of the recorded party leaves the recorded correlations unchanged.

Count the 23 fair binary updates after the initial split. Extend already stopped words by dummy bits solely to give them a common counting denominator \(2^{23}\). The counts of meetings in classes \(j=0,1,2\) are \(1339052,6232407,640002\). The remaining 177147 words time out. Their respective first-setting contributions to the CHSH sum are \(3/4,9/8,1/2,1/2\); the second setting contributes exactly one. Equivalently the correlations, in row order \(Z,X\), are
\[
 E=\begin{pmatrix}23600115/67108864 & 3/4\\
 5474307/8388608 & -1/4\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 S=E_{00}+E_{01}+E_{10}-E_{11}
   =134503435/67108864=2.004257366\ldots .
\]
At 20 ticks the same policy gives \(1.99946666\ldots\). Switching off the entangling contact gives \(0.504257366\ldots\) at cap 24. An earlier fixed-word local analyzer at \(M=3\) gave \(2.203125\) when conditioned on both local successes, and \(1.56982421875\) when its local failures were counted as \(+1\). All three controls matter because the exact margin of the new protocol is only \(285707/67108864\). If every setting-pair output law changes by at most \(\delta\) in total variation, then \(|\Delta S|\le8\delta\); a sufficient error allowance is \(\delta<285707/536870912\). This is an error budget in the model. No laboratory calibration enters its computation. [Exact checker and negative controls](check_radial_bell.py) · [Lean arithmetic certificate](lean/RadialBell.lean)

The local bound \(2\) applies to measurement-independent local hidden-variable models. Here the source herald precedes setting choice and each analyzer supplies a binary outcome, including timeouts. A shared physical sheet, causal isolation of the two analyzers, independent settings, realization of the coherent radial instruments and an empirical record law would each need a physical account. The result is an exact model prediction awaiting an experimental test. The original Bell inequality and a physical event-ready experiment give the comparison. [Clauser et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.23.880) · [Hensen et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature15759)

## Scope of the checks

Three additional Codex participants checked different layers. One built coordinate-path, tomography and finite Bell programs. A second rederived the group and stopping arguments and tested adverse controls. A third formalized signed returns, radial transitions and the bounded correlation sum. All share a model family. The first full-tree comparison failed because the analytic comparison program counted the initial left-split sign twice. Its complete tree and seeded sample were retained. After correcting the sign, all boundary matrices matched direct coordinate propagation. Underlying assumptions still include a particular walk, a coherent coarse record, a persistent classical output, and a chosen probability interpretation. Their physical origin and the unified map to classical mechanics remain open.
