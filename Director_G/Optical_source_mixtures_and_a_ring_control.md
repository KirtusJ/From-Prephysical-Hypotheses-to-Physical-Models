# When two optical preparation mixtures agree

Can the four target optical preparations in the [three-reading comparison](Classical_memory_for_three_half_ring_readings.md) be mixed in pairs to produce two operationally equivalent sources? With the 100-run pooled coincidence estimates, there is an exact intersection in two selected reader coordinates. The fourth reader resolves the intersection. This is a test of the interpretation of our four-cell score. The original experiment used eight primary preparations, four readers, a fitted three-dimensional state representation, and separately constructed secondary procedures. [Mazurek et al., article and supplement](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms11780) · [Pusey's four-preparation test and tomography assumption](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.98.022112)

## A conditional hidden-state distance

At the two-reader intersection weights above, any common classical terminal reader has a source-specific distribution of stored states as its input. The earlier bound $S\le 2+2d$ relates the four-cell score to the total-variation distance $d$ between those distributions. Optimizing the classical fit to all four readers revealed a second score $-E_{12}-E_{13}+E_{22}-E_{23}=2.505372842$ in one-based notation. It gives $d\ge0.252686421$. The optimized score was selected using these same data.

The lower bound is also attainable for the full empirical table. Assign probabilities to the 16 deterministic four-bit reply patterns for each of the four preparations and maximize the overlap of the two source mixtures. A stochastic reader can be represented by such a common randomization into deterministic patterns, so this search covers stochastic response laws as well. The checker uses numerical optimization to locate a support, then reconstructs a rational feasible model, verifies every outcome and overlap constraint, and checks exact agreement with the analytic lower certificate. Thus **0.252686421 is the minimum hidden distance for this point table and these weights**. The largest difference actually seen by the four readers for the same two mixtures is 0.0025207274. The hidden minimum is a requirement on the specified class of simulators; it is not a measured distinguishing probability or a lower confidence limit for the population.

## A ring control that adds a genuinely different reader

In the ideal eight-site walk, let $R$ be the sign of site parity, positive on even sites and negative on odd sites, on both rails. Each eight-tick frame preserves parity and the half-ring sign $Q$ is diagonal in position, so $R$ commutes with each of the three selectable half-ring observables $Q_i$. Consider the uniform states on the even and odd sectors, $\rho_\pm=(I\pm R)/16$. Every first half-ring measurement is fair on either preparation. Conditional probabilities after that first read depend only on its frame and answer as proved in the [three-reading analysis](Classical_memory_for_three_half_ring_readings.md). Induction therefore gives equal distributions for **every adaptive sequence** of the three instruments, including choices based on all earlier answers. One read of $R$ distinguishes the preparations perfectly. It still does so after any number of the half-ring measurements because they preserve each sector.

In particular a source could select the even sector for one preparation setting and the odd sector for another, then measure the selected half-ring observable to produce its fair sign. The resulting six preparations pass the ideal half-ring tests while their source averages are physically distinguishable. Uniform preparation over all 16 basis modes in every source arm would instead produce the common average $I/16$. Comparing even/odd frequencies after each source, with the hidden source sign averaged out, is a concrete control for this failure. Equal parity statistics would still leave other differences untested. The parity example concerns an effective walk with stipulated sites and detector operations. It shows how to test a proposed preparation equivalence without deriving an underlying spatial ontology.
