## Transfer into an actual ten-step position read

Let $T|j\rangle=|j+1\bmod8\rangle$. The clock here is the cyclic phase register. Elapsed walk ticks are a separate observable. Start at sheet 0, use a balanced path comparison with translation $T^4$, and retain the minus result. The unnormalized integer seed is $s_0=e_0-e_4$. The physical amplitude has an additional factor $1/2$, so the herald probability is $1/2$. Subsequent balanced comparisons have Kraus maps $(I\pm T)/2$. After $m$ plus outcomes write $s_m=(I+T)^m s_0$, $S=\langle s_m,s_m\rangle$ and $J=2\langle s_m,Ts_m\rangle$. The joint mass is $S/4^{m+1}$. A common translation or sign of $s_m$ does not change $S$ or $J$. These instrument laws use the squared coordinate readout. Repeated nondemolition readout and conditional phase kickback have independent treatments in quantum measurement theory. ## Recovering the reference without waiting for a rare word

On a base ring of eight sites use the fixed departure gains $(0,4,0,5,0,1,0,0)$. At centres zero and two the three-tick two-path comparisons have gain differences four and one. Reading site and direction distinguishes two informative exits and four single-path exits. The latter each have probability $1/8$ and produce a recorded translation. Further one-tick reads route to the next centre. On an informative exit the normalized action is the corresponding balanced comparison up to a tracked translation and sign. The total informative probability per attempt is $1/2$. [Actual local paths, signs and denominators](results/clock_feedback.json) · [Original three-tick protocol](Internal_phase_reference.md)

On the first informative half-turn, an addressed sign flip on the second occupied site can convert the even dipole into the odd one. The address must include the accumulated translation. On the odd seed, the two hidden conjugate pairs have equal prior mass. Conditional on one pair, an informative unit-shift result is plus with probability $a=(2+\sqrt2)/4$ or $1-a$ respectively. Conditional independence follows from the scalar action on each character and from the equality of the conjugate likelihoods. Use the difference between plus and minus counts as a score. Stop when its magnitude reaches a positive integer $m$. At either boundary the probability assigned to the losing class is exactly

$$
e_m=\frac{(3-2\sqrt2)^m}{1+(3-2\sqrt2)^m}.
$$

On reaching the high-frequency verdict the sign pattern $(-1)^j$ converts $k$ to $k+4$ and swaps the two conjugate pairs. This corrects the state while keeping the subsequent drive fixed. The biased walk on the finite score interval hits a boundary almost surely, with mean $\sqrt2\,m(1-2e_m)$ informative reads. This follows from the two absorbing-boundary probabilities and the drift $1/\sqrt2$ in the direction of the true class. At $m=4$ the corrected low weight is 0.9991342 and the mean is 5.64706 informative reads. Each stopped transcript has the same two pair weights. Its later Xue return therefore differs from the desired one by $(15\sqrt2/32)e_m$. [First-passage and finite-clock trials](check_clock_feedback.py)

## The time a localized phase clock needs

Take an $M$-site cyclic sheet ($M\ge3$). Suppose a microscopic tick can move the sheet by at most $v$ edges and all operations contributing to a preparation branch, including measurement updates, respect this support bound. From one localized sheet, by tick $n$ the branch is confined to an arc of at most $L=\min(M,2vn+1)$ sites for integer speed $v$. Extra coins or ancillary states are allowed. For the normalized projection onto the two frequencies $k=\pm1$ the following bound is sharp over states *supported on that arc*:

$$
W_{\pm1}\ \le\ \Lambda_M(L)=
\frac{L+\left|\sin(2\pi L/M)\right|/\sin(2\pi/M)}{M}.
$$

Restrict the two normalized Fourier columns to the arc. Their Gram matrix has diagonal $L/M$ and off-diagonal $M^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1} e^{4\pi i j/M}$ up to conjugation and a phase from the start site. Its largest eigenvalue is the displayed quantity. A leading restricted cosine or sine centred on the arc attains it. Tensoring the projection with the ancillary identity preserves its norm. Mixtures of individually bounded normalized branch states obey the same inequality. For arbitrary sets of $L$ sites, the trace gives the weaker bound $2L/M$. A direct nonlocal measurement onto the target plane could project out of the arc, which changes the microscopic premise. Fourier support uncertainty and causal discrete dynamics provide the mathematical context. [Donoho and Stark](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/statistics/cgi-bin/donoho/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/UPSR.pdf) · [Arrighi, Nesme and Werner](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.3975) · [Extremal-vector checks](check_clock_support.py)

For even $M$, strict majority requires $L>M/2$. At $L=M/2-1$ and $L=M/2$ the sharp capacity is precisely $1/2$. For odd $M$ the first strict majority occurs at $L=(M-1)/2$, where the capacity exceeds $1/2$ by $(\sec(\pi/M)-1)/(2M)$. To check the lower side, put $y=M/2-L$. The concavity of $\sin(2\pi y/M)$ on this half interval makes its ratio to $y$ smaller at $y>1$ than at one. For $y=1/2$ the inequality reverses. Monotonicity in $L$ also follows by inclusion of the support subspaces. The checker retains both parities, all tested arc lengths and the narrow odd-order crossings. [Trials and exact weak-force scaling parameters](results/clock_support.json)

In the constant-force model the unit sheet phase obeys $2\pi/M=|F|\varepsilon\delta^3$ and one flight tick takes $\tau=\varepsilon\delta^2$. Impose this same clock on bounded-speed sheet preparation, with fixed $F\ne0$ and fixed $v$. Along parameter sequences for which $M=2\pi/(|F|\varepsilon\delta^3)$ is an even integer, strict majority requires $2vn+1>M/2$. Thus the necessary physical time is at least $\pi/(2v|F|\delta)-O(\tau/v)$. At $v=1$, $F=2\pi$, $\delta=2^{-b}$, and either $\varepsilon=1$ or $\varepsilon=\delta$, the necessary number of ticks is at least $M/4$, giving $n\tau\ge1/(4\delta)$. For example $b=4$ gives an initial preparation cost of at least four flight-time units in both families. This excludes preparing this phase pair from one sheet in a uniformly bounded interval of that physical clock. The earlier envelope and Newtonian limits begin *after* the selected preparation. A model with long sheet jumps, a pre-existing extended reference or a different preparation clock must supply its own cost relation. ## Matching the order of the cost

For $M=2^\ell$, $\ell\ge3$, an addressed signed router can place a square wave on all $M$ sheets. From a blank ancillary bit, at stage $d=M/2,M/4,\ldots,1$ split with a balanced coin. One arm waits and the other advances $d$ unit steps. At every endpoint a permutation controlled by the address digit erases the route bit coherently. A minus on the highest split produces the two opposite half-cycle signs. This takes $M-1$ shift ticks and $O(\log M)$ additional parallel control layers. The resulting Fourier weights vanish for all even modes. Each odd mode has weight $4/[M^2\sin^2(\pi k/M)]$ by the geometric sum, so the initial target pair has weight $W=8/[M^2\sin^2(\pi/M)]\ge8/\pi^2$. Keeping distinguishable route labels would destroy the required coherence. Local address controls, their programmed signs and synchronized moving and waiting arms are supplied instruments. [Router and integer physical checks](check_clock_bandwidth.py)

At each scale $j=3,\ldots,\ell$, use $r$ odd repetitions of $(I\pm T^{M/2^j})/2$. Accept if plus wins at every scale. For $k=\pm1$ the probability of a minus at scale $j$ is $d_j=\sin^2(\pi/2^j)$. For each other **odd** $k$, let $j$ be the first scale at which $k\not\equiv\pm1\bmod 2^j$. At that scale $k\equiv2^{j-1}\pm1\bmod2^j$ and the probability of plus is exactly $d_j$. For a Bernoulli law of bias $d\le1/2$, the probability of a wrong majority is at most $[2\sqrt{d(1-d)}]^r$ by the exponential moment bound. Consequently the wrong joint acceptance is at most $(1-W)2^{-r/2}$. The true joint acceptance is at least $W(1-B_r)$, where

$$
B_r=\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\sin^r(2\pi/2^j)
\ \le\ \frac{2^{-r/2}}{1-[2\cos(\pi/8)]^{-r}}.
$$

These are uniform bounds over all dyadic clock sizes. At $M=4096$, $r=9$, direct summation gives 0.80688 accepted mass and 0.000525 wrong fraction among accepts. At fixed accuracy the cost per attempt is $M-1+r(M/4-1)$ unit shift ticks plus $O(r\log M)$ balanced comparisons and control layers. The probability of success stays positive as accuracy increases. For fixed purity close to one, the construction therefore matches the lower bound's linear order in $M$ on this carrier with the stated address controls. Repeating from a fresh square wave bounds the mean time by the per-attempt time divided by the acceptance probability. A strictly bipartite clock where both arms must hop one cell at every tick cannot implement the final **odd** relative delay at equal elapsed time. Dropping that comparison leaves $M/2\pm1$ indistinguishable from the target at earlier scales. Contamination by the constant mode is another adverse control since it passes every majority. A fixed independent probability $c<1/2$ of flipping a **recorded** probe sign changes the asymptotics if every scale uses the same repetition count. For $0<c<1/2$ a target then has a positive lower bound on its false-rejection probability per scale, and its all-scale acceptance tends to zero as $\ell$ grows. To repair this loss, use $r_j=r+2(j-3)$ comparisons at scale $j$. The largest corrupted target dark probability is $d'=c+(1-2c)\sin^2(\pi/8)$ and $\beta=2\sqrt{d'(1-d')}<1$. Conditional on the character, independent sign flips interchange the target and first-conflict probabilities in exactly the same way as before. The true rejection probability is at most $\beta^r/(1-\beta^2)$ by the geometric sum. The alternate acceptance probability is at most $\beta^r$. Larger $r$ gives any fixed accuracy uniformly over $M$. Summing $r_j M/2^j$ gives at most $M(r/4+1/2)$ shift ticks, with $O(r\log M+(\log M)^2)$ local comparison and record layers. The different allocation therefore also achieves linear time at fixed accuracy and fixed $c<1/2$. At $c=1/2$ every reported sign is independent of the true outcome.
