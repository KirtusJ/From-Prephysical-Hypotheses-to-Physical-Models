# Which binary probabilities can one three-tick walk identify?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Three comparisons on one forward walk determine the response of a binary position detector on every mass available at its third tick, including the heavy site of mass $5/8$. Each comparison substitutes a fair mixture of two terminal site masks for one fixed mask. No gate acts on the walker after the third tick except the terminal binary instrument. This gives a test with a single walker, one clock and no independent-copy premise. A source with a different tally law or a detector that resolves hidden paths can still reproduce the assigned rates. The claim concerns this finite set of records.

## The masks and the three comparisons

Launch at $(0,R)$ on an eight-well ring. Each tick applies the signed Hadamard (minus on $L\to L$) and streams by the outgoing rail. At wells $1,3,5,7$ the ordered rail numerators are $(2,1),(1,0),(0,1),(-1,0)$ with common denominator $\sqrt8$. Their kinematic site masses have numerators $5,1,1,1$. Every other site is dark. Use a detector that maps a programmed subset of sites to one binary flag and its full complement to the other. Pool both rails and the entire complement before applying a binary response. Summing outputs of a pre-existing site read after scoring them is a different physical instrument. [Previous multi-bank threshold comparison](One_threshold_on_several_walks.md)

Use the nested masks $M_0=\varnothing,\ M_1=\{3\},\ M_2=\{3,5\},\ M_3=\{3,5,7\}$. Set $M_{8-j}=M_j^c$, taking complements in **all eight wells**. The masks have mass $j/8$ for $j=0,1,2,3,5,6,7,8$. In particular, $M_5$ contains well 1 and the four dark wells. It implements the occupied heavy-site event if adding dark wells does not change the read. No union of whole sites at this clock has mass $1/2$.

Write $g_j$ for the reported probability of mask $M_j$ per original assigned launch. Use an independent fair interchange of the physical flag ports, decoding with the same bit. For complementary settings implemented by exchanging the assignments of the *same* partition, this enforces $g_{8-j}=1-g_j$ under a complete, stable two-port response. In particular, if the first physical port responds to mass $s$ by a common function $r(s)$, the decoded curve is $[r(s)+1-r(1-s)]/2$. Using the literal complementary partitions permits a narrower test of the specific hardware settings even without a universal mass-only curve. Distinct settings of equal mass still require a separate symmetry comparison before their rates can be identified.

Randomly assign launches from the same preparation to each target and comparison arm. The comparison rates can also be formed by averaging independently measured setting rates with the stated weights. The three equalities to test are
$$2g_1=g_0+g_2,\qquad 2g_2=g_1+g_3,\qquad 2g_3=g_1+g_5.$$
The ordinary probability rule predicts all three because the corresponding signed-walk masses satisfy the same relations. Each equality between rates is experimentally falsifiable. The randomized masks need only agree on this source. For instance, on a sharp input at site 3, $M_1$ always hits under an ideal coordinate read while the first fair mixture hits half the time. Thus agreement on this experiment does not establish operational equivalence on every preparation. [Operational equivalence and measurement context](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.71.052108)

If the empty mask is calibrated dark, put $a=g_1$. The first two rows give $g_2=2a$ and $g_3=3a$. The third gives $g_5=5a$, and complementarity gives $8a=1$. Thus $g_j=j/8$ on the entire stated menu. Neither continuity nor monotonicity of the detector enters this argument. If a detector depends on site identity, the proof selects only the named masks and their complements. Repeating the menu with the three light sites permuted covers all four-site-support subsets. Extending to different choices among nominally dark wells requires invariance under those additions or direct tests.

There is a finite-error identity for the heavy mask. Let $e_1,e_2,e_3$ be the **signed** left-minus-right residuals in the three displayed equations, and $c=g_3+g_5-1$. Elimination gives
$$8(g_5-5/8)=-2g_0-2e_1-e_2-3e_3+5c.$$
Thus simultaneous upper limits $g_0\le b$, $|e_j|\le d_j$, $|c|\le h$ give $|g_5-5/8|\le(2b+2d_1+d_2+3d_3+5h)/8$. A reported midpoint-minus-mixture difference is half the displayed residual. Use a joint confidence region or simultaneous bounds for the reused setting rates. Count every assigned trial, including every unresolved outcome, with lower and upper hit probabilities given respectively by confirmed hits and by confirmed hits plus unresolved. A detector stuck on one physical port gives decoded rate $1/2$ for **every** mask. It passes the three rows and fails the empty-mask calibration.

The common power-two binary response $g_j=j^2/[j^2+(8-j)^2]$ gives $25/34$ at $j=5$ and doubled residuals $-3/50,-36/425,-96/425$. All three differ from the ordinary zeros. Conversely a freshly drawn equal mixture $r_\pm(s)=s\pm s(1-s)(2s-1)/2$ gives the ordinary average at every mass. Both curves are complementary, strictly increasing and endpoint-sharp. Their derivatives lie between $1/2$ and $3/2$, and their opposite deviations at $5/8$ are $15/512$. This finite panel identifies the **observed** response under the comparison premises. Selecting each latent response needs additional evidence such as repeatable correlated source types with independently sampled outcomes. Neither route identifies a separately named phase embedding on rational native transcripts. ## What an existing site histogram can test

There is a direct control on the interpretation of the single-bank test. Suppose a device first reports one definite site from any distribution on the four occupied sites, and the operator applies the masks offline. Let the three light-site frequencies in a chosen order be $u,v,w$, and the heavy-site frequency $H$. The first two doubled residuals are $u-v$ and $v-w$. Averaging over all permutations of the light sites gives zero for both by construction; the third residual becomes $(5-8H)/3$. Such an averaged histogram passes exactly when $H=5/8$, even if the three light sites have unequal rates. Keeping settings separate retains the information needed to detect that inequality. The binary gate experiment calls for the selected mask to **change what the detector receives**.

As an illustration, the two retained arms of the published cesium walk give offline heavy-site fractions $107/201$ and $115/196$. Their corresponding permutation-averaged third residuals are $149/603$ and $5/49$. Those values use the retained detected arms. Displaced atoms, unreported launch losses, and the absence of a switched binary detector prevent interpreting them as incident rates of the proposed experiment. The counts provide a four-site comparison on actual records and make clear which further measurements would be needed. ## What the joint records add

The multi-bank experiment also tests the physical preparation of its banks. Suppose three possibly correlated flags have individual kinematic masses $p_1,p_2,p_3$ and pair intersections $q_{12},q_{13},q_{23}$. These numbers may come from calibrated controls. Merely observing agreement of reported pair frequencies presupposes an appropriate instrument before interpreting them as kinematic masses. Put $Q=q_{12}+q_{13}+q_{23}$ and $h=\Pr_{\rm kin}(111)$. The eight word masses are $h$ for all hits, $q_{ij}-h$ for exactly the pair $ij$, $p_i-q_{ij}-q_{ik}+h$ for a single $i$, and $1-\sum_i p_i+Q-h$ for no hits. Consequently the feasible interval is exactly
$$L=\max(0,\max_i(q_{ij}+q_{ik}-p_i))\ \le h\le\
U=\min(\min_{ij}q_{ij},\,1-\textstyle\sum_i p_i+Q),$$
where $j,k$ are the other two indices and the minimum on pairs uses $i<j$. Every interval point defines a nonnegative complete diagonal preparation when $L\le U$. These finite consistency calculations are instances of sharp event bounds from partial intersection data. [Prékopa's binomial-moment bounds](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.38.2.227)

The three threshold masses $s_j=\Pr_{\rm kin}(K\ge j)$ satisfy $s_2+s_3=Q-h$. The pair calibrations guarantee a threshold rate at most $2/3$ for **every** compatible preparation and common nondecreasing complementary response exactly when $Q-L\le1$. If $s_2+s_3\le1$ then $G(s_2)+G(s_3)\le1$. Otherwise $s_2>1/2$. A monotone complementary curve may have $G(s_2)=1$ and $G(s_3)\ge1/2$, while $G(s_1)=1$. Setting values on the finite grid and interpolating continuously preserves the counterexample. [Separated outer banks and a correlated middle](Three_walks_with_a_correlated_middle_record.md)

For three banks of mass $5/8$ whose **every pair is independent**, the triple mass ranges from $10/64$ to $19/64$. At the lower endpoint the word weights are $9/64$ at 000, $15/64$ at each double-only word and $10/64$ at 111. The thresholds are $(55,55,10)/64$. A continuous complementary detector can place a plateau of height $1/2$ between $10/64$ and $54/64$, hit zero at $9/64$, and reach one at $55/64$. The recorded threshold average is then $5/6$. Its two-bank diagonal reduced states on every pair are identical to those of independent Bernoulli flags at $5/8$. The original $2/3$ bound for three **product** banks is valid, but even complete tomography on each pair of these diagonal flags could miss this change in the three-body source.

There is a same-clock repair with unequal masks. Give the first two banks masses $3/4$ by selecting the heavy site and one light site at each. Give the third mass $3/8$ using the three light sites. Their mean is still $5/8$. The pointwise bound $s_2+s_3\le q_{12}+p_3$ follows by counting the eight words (the excess on the right is precisely the mass of 001). Thus $q_{12}\le5/8$ at each responding type suffices, including independence of the first two and even a positive covariance up to $1/16$. This comparison retains the $7/102$ minimum gap between the heavy-site square-score rate and the three-bank reference ceiling. It needs no three-way product. A population-average covariance does not warrant this per-type bound.

The same three predicates can even be defined on one walker, using $A=\{1,3\}, B=\{1,5\}$ and $C=\{3,5,7\}$. Every occupied site belongs to one or two of them. The thresholds now reduce to masses $1,7/8,0$, so a uniform choice reports $[1+G(7/8)]/3\le2/3$ for a common complementary detector, without monotonicity. This construction presupposes the calibrated dark support or a mass-only read for the certain and impossible masks. One can instead program the full and empty masks on those two settings. Unlike an unknown-input probability reference, this comparison is tailored to the specified source. The three midpoint rows above determine the full set of tested rates on the same carrier.
