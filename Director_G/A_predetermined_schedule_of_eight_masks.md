# Two fixed detector laws tested on a predetermined cycle of masks

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A 256-tick Hadamard walk admits eight whole-site masks whose masses are known for every input coin. Each site belongs to exactly five masks. A block that presents each mask once can therefore test two specified detector laws without a random bit for setting assignment. Under the ordinary law the predicted mean hit rate is exactly $5/8$. Under a binary detector that scores each port by the square of its mass and normalizes the two scores, it is $0.735281069747006$ to the displayed precision. On 200 complete launches a fixed count threshold bounds both decision errors below 4.74% under the conditional probabilities stated here. No physical trials are reported. ## What is fixed before collecting data

Use the [eight bins](results/shifted_site_mask.json) of distance shells on the line. The sharp origin, two-rail Hadamard flight, tick, and the routing of both rails into one unresolved binary flag are part of the apparatus. Every untabulated radius is in bin zero. Setting $j$ passes bins $j$ through $j+4$ modulo eight. Decide the number of original incident launches, the setting on each launch and the hit threshold before observing any read. Use each setting the same number of times. Counts in the table below allow any such ordering. Cycling every eight shots gives a more informative comparison in the presence of drift.

For any completed offline site label $I$, the eight bits $1\{I\in M_j\}$ sum to five, even when the result lies outside the walk's predicted support. All eight can be scored after that single read. Their average is identically $5/8$, regardless of site law, launch drift or order. It carries no eightfold sample-size gain. If a launch produced a site whose identity was lost, every possible site completion gives the same averaged score. If it could have produced no site record at all, its score per incident launch is undefined until the no-record outcome is specified. With $m$ resolved sites and $N-m$ unexplained losses, their hypothetical complete-site contribution per incident run lies in $[5m/(8N),5/8]$ if a missing run scores either no site (zero) or a complete site. Record which interpretation the acquisition supports.

In the physical arm the setting controls the flag **before** the read. At setting $j$ the passing mass is $s_j I_2$ as an effect on the input rails. Thus the mass is $s_j$ for every input coin, including a history-dependent coin correlated with a hidden detector type. The eight numbers range from $0.6205319956$ to $0.6280289140$, with mean exactly $5/8$. Under the two hypotheses the conditional hit probabilities at that setting are respectively $s_j$ and $F(s_j)=s_j^2/[s_j^2+(1-s_j)^2]$. These hypotheses concern the eight tested masses and the associated instrument. An independent reference coin, a random order of settings and detector continuity are unnecessary. A drifting trajectory law or a different mask-sensitive coupling can change the predicted probabilities. Sorting already resolved site outcomes into two bins can reproduce the ordinary mean and supplies no evidence by itself that the device pooled a coherent flag.

## Finite exposure with adversarial loss or corruption

Let $T$ be the number of physical hits that the specified complete-read model would generate in the $N$ assigned launches, and $H$ the number actually labeled hit. Up to $u$ original reads may be unresolved and up to $c$ other retained labels may be flipped. The failure mechanism can inspect outcomes and settings before choosing which records to damage. For $u_{\rm obs}$ unresolved on the run,

$$\max(0,H-c)\le T\le \min(N,H+u_{\rm obs}+c).$$

The two entries in the interval are possible endpoint counts when enough hit or missed labels are available; for any given $T$ the extremal possible $H$ are exactly $\min(N,T+c)$ and $\max(0,T-u-c)$. A bound on metadata losses does not control a changed source or a detector disturbance that prevents any underlying complete-read comparison. An unlogged incident count does not establish an exposure.

Use the decision “square if $H\ge k$, ordinary otherwise” **within the stated pair of hypotheses**. Its worst-case probabilities of a false square and a false ordinary are respectively $\Pr_0(T\ge k-c)$ and $\Pr_1(T<k+u+c)$. For each individual error the damaging mechanism can attain the tail by flipping retained misses to hits, or erasing and then flipping hits to misses. These statements use the integers $u,c$ as verified caps. If the loss cap is exceeded or the corruption bound has no support, the tabulated guarantee is unavailable.

For the table, condition each underlying binary outcome on the scheduled setting and on all previous physical outcomes. When its conditional probability equals the specified deterministic $p_i$, the chain rule gives the count generating polynomial $\prod_i(1-p_i+p_i z)$. Identical probabilities across settings are unnecessary. To certify the two tails the checker rounds each ordinary $p_i$ *up*, and each square $p_i$ *down*, to multiples of $1/65536$. Coupling each Bernoulli draw to the same uniform number orders their sums and bounds both tails in the adverse direction. The resulting rational polynomials are multiplied with integers. The same one-sided tails continue to hold when, conditional on every realized history, the ordinary hit probability is at most its rounded bound and the square probability at least its rounded bound. Drawing a fresh uniform number at each trial couples the conditional binary outcome to the bounding outcome pointwise, even when the actual probabilities vary with history. The reported plans are sufficient exposures for this count rule. They do not claim an optimal sequential or likelihood-ratio test. ## What a public schedule leaves open

The offline score can be exact while the physical data do not concentrate. For example, take a single shared uniform $V$ and report $X_i=1\{V<F(s_{j_i})\}$ on every occurrence of a setting. Every setting has exactly its stipulated square probability when viewed in isolation across repetitions of the entire experiment. Over 98.75% of these experiments nevertheless report entirely ones or entirely zeroes, however many eight-shot cycles are added. This violates the conditional-probability premise of the exposure guarantee.

There is also a distinct issue with inference about an unrestricted detector population. If hidden type $A_i$ has an unknown response $g_{A_i}$, it may track a public schedule even with perfectly calibrated coin dynamics. A detector on scheduled shot $i$ can agree with the identity at $s_{j_i}$ and have a different increasing response between its observed masses; even full agreement on the schedule constrains only the tested incidences. For comparison, if every shot in a block has one common curve $g$, the balanced mean is exactly $\sum_j g(s_j)/8$. A different curve may govern the next block. More generally, if the within-block curves satisfy $\|g_{i+1}-g_i\|_\infty\le v$ at each successive pair, the difference between the scheduled average and the eight-setting average for the fourth shot's curve is at most $2v$. The distances in time index from shot four sum to sixteen over the eight shots. This bound is pathwise and needs a balanced block at that time scale. The source-independent shell masses remove arbitrary drift of the two input rails. Randomization or separate drift information plays a different role when estimating an unknown population curve. The observation rule and its proposed physical realization remain open.
