# Preparing every signed sheet multiset with whole-position masks

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

On the cyclic staircase, one sharp launch can prepare arbitrary numbers of words of each sign and **absolute sheet residue** on each of the two rails at a named terminal well. The filters see only positions and keep at most four wells per tick. For sheet order $M\ge5$ the usual ring of $4M$ wells suffices. At orders three and four the proof below uses $8M$ wells. No sheet measurement, arbitrary source array or individually selected history is used.

This changes a premise of the earlier probability tests. If the endpoint scores are nonnegative and stationary, and one unrestricted extra tick multiplies the score of every position product event by a common constant, the finite-ring walk now forces that constant to be two and the score to be a mixture of path count and squared cyclic-character sums. Even dependence of the score on well and arrival rail is initially allowed. The weights within this family still require discrimination by records. The earlier block classification supplies the algebra once the needed multisets can actually meet. ## The register and the programmed delays

A walk begins in R at site $x_0$ with sign $+$ and residue zero. From a departure at site $x$, both daughters gain $\lfloor x/2\rfloor\bmod M$. R moves right, L moves left and only L after L reverses sign. The ring circumference $N$ is a multiple of $2M$, so the residue gain is well defined across its seam. At tick $t$ write the physical well as $x=x_0+t-2d\bmod N$. A right step holds the deficit $d$ and a left step increases it by one. For every word of length $T$,

$$j_{\rm end}=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lfloor(x_0+t)/2\right\rfloor-\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} d_t\pmod M.$$

The first sum is the same for every word. Controlling how long a word spends at a particular deficit therefore controls its absolute terminal residue.

Choose a width $H\ge4$ coprime to $M$. Retain the wall at deficit zero as a positive singleton and the wall at $H$ as an accumulator on R. A pulse opens the intermediate positions of one word on successive ticks, together with the persistent walls. Use either $L^H R$ or $LLR L^{H-2}R$. In the first word there are $H-1$ sign reversals. The second has $H-2$. The single dwell at deficit two is at least two positions from either wall. Deficits 1 and $H-1$ open once each, so exactly one singleton leaves the source during a pulse and no stored word enters the intermediate path. Landing on L at $H$, it takes R there on the final pulse tick, joining the previously stored words. The singleton itself survives on R. All positions refer to whole wells.

For each requested deposit reserve a slot of length $\ell+M-1$, where $\ell$ is its pulse's word length. Take $s$ wall-only ticks before the pulse and $M-1-s$ after. Increasing $s$ by one replaces a departure at $d=H$ by one at $d=0$ for this particular deposited word. Its scheduled intermediate deficits have simply moved one tick later. The final clock stays fixed, so its residue increases by $H$. Already stored words coast through the slot as before, and singleton lineages that deposit in later slots remain at zero. No other target residue changes. Compute the final residue $j_0$ at delay zero including every subsequent coast and transfer. Choose the unique $s\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ satisfying $Hs=j_{\rm target}-j_0\bmod M$. For $M\ge5$ use $H=M-1$; at $M=3,4$ use $H=4,5$ respectively. ## Storing two rails and choosing the well

Build the future R output first at $H$. To move the whole stock to $H+2$, retain $\{0,H+1\}$, then $\{0,H+2\}$ twice. The moving stock takes LLR and changes every sign once. The reservoir stays on R. Now build the future L output at the emptied $H$ while also keeping the wall $H+2$. The gap of two deficits prevents either wall from feeding the other. After the final pulse one may coast by retaining $0,H,H+2$. Finally keep $\{H,H+2\}$ to drop the source, then $\{H+1,H+2\}$ and $\{H+2\}$. The inner stock takes LL after its coast and changes sign once; the outer stock stays on R. The merger hence delivers the two prescribed multisets, each generated with the opposite pulse sign to compensate its single transfer reversal.

The terminal site is $x_0+T-2(H+2)\bmod N$. Set the coast padding to the unique integer in $[0,N-1]$ that gives the desired terminal site *before* computing the delays. For $m$ total requested words, with lengths $\ell_i$ for their sign-compensated pulses, the total depth is $6+\mathrm{padding}+\sum_{i=1}^m(\ell_i+M-1)$. The construction includes empty and mutually cancelling bins. Its horizon may vary with the request, so a common response law across horizons is essential.

In one tick, all candidate outgoing deficits lie in $[0,H+3]$. For even $N$ this interval embeds without aliasing when $N/2>H+3$. This proves the stated ring bounds and explains why no restriction on depth from wraparound is needed. On the original 12-site ring at order three this wiring can put a history into an intended empty preparation. On the original 16-site ring at order four that empty test passes, but the joint all-residue test fails. These are failures of this particular wiring. The checker retains the surplus and missing bins, sign-compensation and timing mutations, and a receipt for each item of a worked schedule. ## From the attained words to an endpoint law

Let $G=\{\pm1\}\times\mathbb Z/M$. A multiset at each oriented port $v$ is scored by an arbitrary function $F_v$ of its nonnegative signed-residue multiplicities; here “signed” labels gains, and multiplicities themselves are ordinary counts. Assume $F_v(0)=0$, nonnegative finite scores, and some positive score. Add the scores across terminal ports. Suppose that appending one unrestricted walk tick to *every* finite position product event multiplies its score by a common $k$. The assembly above applied to $(U,0)$ or $(0,U)$ isolates any multiset on either rail of every well. A free tick from such an event therefore gives an equation on the two daughters for that very multiset.

Fix $U$ and form the lifted finite directed walk on $(v,g)$ with $g\in G$ and transition $(v,g)\to(w,g e_{vw})$, where $e_{vw}$ includes the staircase shift and the possible L-after-L sign. Let $h(v,g)=F_v(gU)$, with $g$ multiplying every gain label. The one-input equation says that the two successor values sum to $k h(v,g)$. Every lifted state has two incoming and two outgoing edges. Summing gives $k=2$ whenever the sum of $h$ is positive; choose $U$ with a positive score. Since $k$ is common it is two everywhere.

The lift is irreducible. Its base is strongly connected. At $(0,R)$ the two closed words LR and RLLR have respective gains $(+,-1)$ and $(-,-1)$ modulo $M$. Indeed their departure positions are $0,-1$ and $0,1,0,-1$, and only the latter contains an L-after-L. Powers and products therefore realize the entire finite gain group at that port. A base route to the origin, the chosen loop and a route out reach any target lifted state. At factor two the field $h$ is harmonic; a maximum on this finite lift propagates to every accessible state. Thus for *every* $U$ all $F_v(gU)$ agree. Spatial and rail homogeneity and invariance under a simultaneous shift of all gains follow from the available preparations and the continuation premise.

Write the common score as $F$, $J$ for simultaneous sign reversal and $T_b$ for a cyclic shift by $b$. A jointly prepared pair $U,V$ at a single well has daughters $T_b(U+V)$ and $T_b(U+JV)$ with $b=\lfloor x/2\rfloor$. Their scores discard the common shift by the invariance just proved. Thus for all signed-residue multisets

$$F(U+V)+F(U+JV)=2F(U)+2F(V).$$

Adjoining one cancelling pair of residue $j$ increments $F$ by a constant depending at first on $j$. Translation makes this constant independent of $j$. After subtracting its linear count contribution, the score descends to the integer differences $D_j=n_{+,j}-n_{-,j}$. The block identity becomes the parallelogram identity on $\mathbb Z^M$, giving a real symmetric quadratic form by polarization. Its invariance under a cyclic shift makes the matrix circulant. Nonnegativity on balanced pairs gives a nonnegative count term. Scaling any integer $D$ by increasingly large integers, then using density of rational vectors, makes the quadratic part positive semidefinite. Diagonalization gives the normalized form

$$F(U)=a\,|U|+\sum_{s=0}^{\lfloor M/2\rfloor}b_s
  \left|\sum_{j=0}^{M-1} D_j\,e^{2\pi i sj/M}\right|^2,\qquad
  a,b_s\ge0,\qquad a+\sum_s b_s=1.$$

Normalization divides by the common positive singleton score, which is positive by the decomposition and nontriviality. Each $b_s$ combines conjugate frequencies; zero and, when present, Nyquist are singletons. Conversely each term satisfies the unrestricted extension law for all position events by per-site count doubling or the square identity. The theorem permits mixtures, nonfaithful characters and pure counting. It gives the actual preparable domain of the earlier algebra. It does not extend the family by assumption to a sheet-resolved detector or a measurement that stores an internal path record. On the infinite line the same circuit fits without aliasing. To place it arbitrarily far to the left, precede it by a deterministic word ending on R as in the signed position-mask construction, translate the subsequent schedule, and include the known prefix sign and sheet shift in the requested labels. With $k=2$ supplied, the lifted random walk with two equally likely successors projects to the recurrent simple symmetric walk. It visits the finite oriented gain fiber above zero infinitely often. Irreducibility, proved by the same loops, therefore makes the lift recurrent. A finite nonnegative harmonic $h$ obeys $h(i)\ge \Pr_i(T_j\le n)h(j)$ by stopping at the first hit of $j$ or time $n$. Letting $n$ grow and reversing $i,j$ gives equality. The rest of the classification follows. In contrast, if $k$ is unknown on the line, $F_{(x,r)}(U)=z^x|U|$ with $z>0$, $z\ne1$, obeys all the same preparation comparisons with $k=z+z^{-1}>2$. Positivity alone cannot give the finite-ring normalization argument on this infinite carrier. [Deterministic line prefix](Position_mask_preparation.md)
