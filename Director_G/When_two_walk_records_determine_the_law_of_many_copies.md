# When two walk records determine the law of many copies

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Two banks each run the same reflecting walk and report a whole word of position readings. Record whether the words agree, and how much kinematic mass the model assigned to each displayed word. These data suffice to test every fixed number of joint displays **if the displays remain exchangeable and consistently extendible to arbitrarily many banks**. A rival built from nine banks passes all two-bank tests, forbids a particular ordinary triple, and cannot be extended to a tenth bank. Neither construction requires a power law for the detector.

## An observable score for a proposed word law

Fix the reflecting walk, its source, the clocks, cut locations and the measurement protocol. Let \(p=(p_w)_{w\in\mathcal A}\) be the vector of squared masses computed on this walk, with \(d=|\mathcal A|\) possible whole words. Include a missed or invalid display among the outcomes with its stipulated mass, keeping the original exposure. Let \(Y_i\) be the actual display on bank \(i\). We make no probability assumption about a single quantum read. We use ordinary probability calculus for the already classical joint displays. Bank order is distinct from the time order of bits *within* one word.

For two distinct banks drawn uniformly without replacement, take the expectation of the bounded score
\[
 \mathbf 1_{\{Y_1=Y_2\}}-p_{Y_1}-p_{Y_2}+\sum_w p_w^2.
\]
Write \(\mu_w\) for a sampled bank's display rates, \(c=\Pr(Y_1=Y_2)\) and \(S=c-2p\cdot\mu+\|p\|_2^2\). Sorting the observed bank outputs and scoring them only after both displays are committed prevents the scoring program from changing a word. Repeatability across batches and the uncertainty of an estimated \(S\) need separate checks. The statements below concern population rates.

For \(N\ge2\), consider an actual joint block and its random empirical histogram \(H_N\) of whole words. The exact identity
\[
 \mathbb E\|H_N-p\|_2^2=S+\frac{1-c}{N}
\]
follows by expanding \(\|H_N\|_2^2\) into \(N\) equal-index and \(N(N-1)\) distinct-index terms. This identity applies to uniform sampling from any block. Interpreting it for a specified smaller-bank experiment requires that experiment to have the same law as its randomly sampled counterpart. Identical labels on bank hardware alone do not ensure this. A common distribution over hidden apparatus states is allowed.

Suppose the family of measured joint laws \(Q_k\) on ordered lists of banks is exchangeable and consistent under deletion for every required size. Equivalently for this argument, each \(Q_k\) is the ordered random-\(k\) marginal of the same larger blocks and the pair measurements use their ordered random-pair marginal. Sample \(k\le N\) indices with replacement from a block. Conditional on its displays, the resulting tuple has law \(H_N^{\otimes k}\). Couple the indices to sampling without replacement, resampling the tuple if any index repeats. The two samples can differ only on a collision of indices, whose probability is \(1-(N)_k/N^k\), where \((N)_k=N(N-1)\cdots(N-k+1)\). A separate coordinatewise coupling gives \({\rm TV}(H_N^{\otimes k},p^{\otimes k})\le k\,{\rm TV}(H_N,p)\). Cauchy–Schwarz and the identity above now give
\[
 {\rm TV}(Q_k,p^{\otimes k})\le
 \min\!\left(1,\ 1-\frac{(N)_k}{N^k}
      +\frac{k}{2}\sqrt{d\left(S+\frac{1-c}{N}\right)}\right).
\]
The comparison by sampling with and without replacement is classical. [Diaconis and Freedman, *Finite Exchangeable Probability*](https://statistics.stanford.edu/technical-reports/finite-exchangeable-probability)

If compatible extensions exist to unbounded \(N\), then \(S\ge0\), and \(S=0\) **if and only if** \(Q_k=p^{\otimes k}\) for every fixed \(k\). Take \(N\to\infty\) for the forward implication. The converse follows by computing \(S\) on \(p^{\otimes2}\). In particular, finite one- and two-word population statistics can determine all these joint laws without postulating independent detector draws. A finite extension on its own can give a negative \(S\). For example, shuffle exactly \(64p_w\) copies of each word in a block of 64 whenever these counts are integers. Then \(H_{64}=p\) identically and \(S=-(1-c)/64\) although sampling without replacement differs from the product.

## Six walk ticks and the exact nine-bank rival

Use sixteen wells, sharp well 8 and right rail. Write the high bit for well at most 5 after tick three and the low bit for well at least 12 after tick six. Each comparison preserves the signed walk inside the recorded bin in the reference dynamics. Adding signed paths within each final word and directed mode and then summing squared amplitudes gives \(p=(29,27,8,0)/64\) in binary order. The ordinary independent-launch equal-word rate is \(817/2048\). Following independent fair directions at every microscopic turn gives \(q=(49,7,8,0)/64\). It predicts \(S=\|q-p\|_2^2=25/128\) for independent banks. An independently calibrated pair score could distinguish these models on directly displayed words. The earlier echo protocol used three protected returns on this flight to identify a common terminal exponent. [Walk and protected-return experiment](Returning_a_protected_position_word.md)

Here is a different law for the displays. Call word \(10\) rare. With probability \(9/16\) mark two distinct banks uniformly from nine, and otherwise mark none. Marked banks show \(10\). Each unmarked bank draws independently from \(00,01\) in proportions \(29:27\). The 36 possible two-bank masks each have weight \(1/64\). Each bank therefore has rare probability \(8/64\) and every prescribed distinct pair has joint rare probability \(1/64\). The rare indicators of two distinct banks are independent, and the independent common-word draws make their *full word law* exactly \(p^{\otimes2}\). Consequently the score is zero and every two-bank experiment restricted to those same displays agrees. Three specified banks can never all show \(10\), whereas the ordinary probability is \(1/512\).

Nine is the **largest** possible exchangeable extension of this particular triple law. In any size-\(N\) extension the number \(K\) of rare words has \(K\le2\) almost surely, since the triple-rare probability vanishes on each of finitely many triples. The one- and two-bank marginals require \(\mathbb E K=N/8\) and \(\mathbb E K(K-1)=N(N-1)/64\). Pointwise \(K(K-1)\le K\) for \(K\le2\), giving \(N\le9\). Feasibility at nine is witnessed by the actual construction. The [Lean certificate](lean/ExchangeableWord.lean) checks the nine-bank mask incidences, the pointwise inequality and the implication for arbitrary positive integer \(N\).

More generally, a finite audit through m−1 banks can miss an m-bank correlation for every integer m ≥ 2. Choose a bounded function \(f\) of the word with \(|f|\le1\), \(\sum p_w f(w)=0\), nonzero with positive \(p\)-probability. Multiply the product probability of each size-\(m\) tuple by \(1+\theta\prod_{i=1}^m f(Y_i)\), with \(0<|\theta|<1\). The altered table is normalized, permutation invariant and positive on the same support. Summing over even one coordinate kills its perturbation. All proper marginals agree with the product although the size-\(m\) table differs. For \(m\ge3\) the zero-score theorem rules out arbitrary extension of this altered table. A classical readout can produce these rivals from a preloaded random table and a permutation of its slots. Establishing which such memory and coordination the actual apparatus permits is a physical question.

## A route from the prepared banks to the score

One can replace an assumed numerical value of \(S\) with an additional measurement. Prepare \(N\) independent copies of the specified *kinematic* word source. Reversibly compute a single flag for the event \({\rm TV}(H_N,p)>\epsilon\) into a blank, leaving the word registers intact. Here \(H_N\) inside the circuit denotes the histogram of its basis-word tuple. For rational \(p,\epsilon\) the circuit uses integer counts and comparisons. Its squared mass is the sum of \(p^{\otimes N}\) over those tuples by product amplitudes, irrespective of how a detector responds. In that kinematic table \(\mathbb E\|H_N-p\|_2^2=(1-\|p\|_2^2)/N\). The flag mass is at most \(d(1-\|p\|_2^2)/(4N\epsilon^2)\).

Two operational premises now have separate roles. First, a flag of vanishing squared mass on this family must have vanishing observed rate as \(N\) grows, for each fixed positive tolerance. A common pooled binary response continuous at the zero endpoint suffices. The apparatus must keep its calibration as the number of residual registers grows. Second, reading that pooled flag must agree in rate with evaluating the same predicate on the actually displayed, complete word tuple. This comparison ties the independent flag experiment to the direct detector, which can otherwise use a different normalization, grouping or response. If \(\eta\) bounds the *observed* probability of an atypical tuple, then sampling from the displayed block gives
\[
 {\rm TV}(Q_k,p^{\otimes k})\le 1-(N)_k/N^k + k\epsilon +\eta .
\]
Indeed on good blocks the conditional product coupling costs at most \(k\epsilon\), and on bad blocks its cost is at most one. With compatible extensions, taking \(N\to\infty\) and then \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) gives every fixed product law. An inaccurate tally/flag agreement adds its measured absolute rate error to \(\eta\). If emitted direct words are instead independent draws from \(q\ne p\) while the pooled reader is ordinary, the two reported atypical rates tend to one and zero respectively for \(\epsilon<{\rm TV}(q,p)\). Endpoint checks by themselves leave this failure possible. [Single-threshold reference](One_threshold_on_several_walks.md) · [Caves and Schack on the limits of frequency-operator arguments](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0409144)

The theorem uses different banks for different *launches*. Repeated reads on one walker condition its later state and are already represented inside one word. At each carrier size one may test a fixed schedule this way. Where the kinematic word table converges to the classical law of one reflected velocity per launch, convergence of the corresponding direct-word score to zero under unbounded compatible extension transfers those finite-word probabilities, provided the alphabet is fixed. This conclusion concerns displayed words. It leaves the after-measurement state, uniformity at growing word depth, and the physical origin of the prepared banks and coherent position writes open. Native coordinate preparations with a globally relabeled cyclotomic embedding can have identical \(p\) on every word, and all the bank scores above then agree.
