# A protected fourth port and balanced pair mixers

**Question.** Can late record comparisons select the ordinary probability embedding on an entire continuum when the apparatus has only one fixed mixer angle? The preceding [three-mode miss probe](Two_rotations_and_an_early_position_write.md) fixes one split relation. Its four-port apparatus admits a stronger conditional test if any pair can be addressed coherently before detection.

**Apparatus and test.** Start each trial with one sharp occupied real mode. Allow finite words of the same signed two-mode Hadamard on ordered pairs of four modes, acting as identity on the others. After preparation designate port 0 as protected. Choose independently between a late identity and one late mixer on a pair among ports 1,2,3. Score all four ports at a common terminal clock. Require that the protected port's original-launch frequency agree across each such pair of settings for every prepared word and every active pair. The single-mixer comparison is the entire family of observations required; equivalence under longer words follows by applying it successively to their prefixes. Clock matching, coherent routing and stationarity over word lengths are apparatus assumptions.

**Identification.** Let each squared real coordinate w receive one stable continuous finite score f(w), with f(0)=0 and f(w)>0 for w>0, and normalize once over the four ports. Balanced pair mixers on at least three addressable modes generate a dense orbit of every real unit vector. This uses the real case of the [Bouland–Aaronson beamsplitter universality theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.6718), which includes nontrivial real two-mode gates. It applies twice here: on four modes for preparation, then on the three unprotected modes for rearrangement. The theorem concerns amplitudes; it supplies no record probabilities.

Continuity and positivity of the normalizer extend the tested single-mixer equalities from the preparation orbit to every real unit vector. Fix any protected squared coordinate s strictly between zero and one. Its positive numerator stays fixed through all active operations, so the sum of active scores is invariant. The dense three-mode orbit and continuity extend this equality to the full active sphere of radius sqrt(1-s). In particular, compare active squared coordinates (x,y,0) and (x+y,0,0) with x+y=1-s. The result is f(x)+f(y)=f(x+y) whenever x+y<1. Continuity extends it to x+y=1. Repeated addition fixes f on rational subdivisions of the unit interval up to the factor f(1), and continuity fixes f(w)=w f(1) throughout. Conversely this score gives invariant protected frequency under every allowed active operation by norm preservation.

The result replaces the positive-power hypothesis with an experimentally defined family of protected-frequency comparisons. Each setting is finite, while identification across the continuum uses the whole family and continuity. For any finite collection of words a continuous positive score can agree at every tested mass and differ on an untested open interval. A detector that samples the protected port irreversibly before the late setting also evades the common-terminal-normalizer model. No such comparisons have been measured here. To transfer the result to the fixed eight-well walk one still needs a coherent compilation of the addressed mixers with spectator isolation, or independent evidence that its native controls generate the two required orbits.

## Exact compilation on the controlled eight-well ring

Encode the four ports as right movers at wells 0, 2, 4 and 6. Assume site- and tick-selectable identity, rail exchange X and the signed Hadamard H, each applied before the ordinary one-site stream S. These straight, turnaround and balanced commands are also used in the [routed local-write model](Local_writes_and_dyadic_site_scores.md). This compilation needs no extra sheet, herald, phase knob or selective loss. It concerns the underlying two-rail ring with no intermediate sheet writes.

For any ordered pair a,b of distinct port wells put d = ((b-a) mod 8)/2, taking d in {1,2,3}. Number ticks from zero. At ticks 0 and 8 exchange rails at b. At tick d apply H at well (a+d) mod 8. Use identity coins everywhere else and stream on all 16 ticks. At the mixing time the original a right mover is on the right rail and the original b right mover is on the left rail at that same well. Writing U = S^d X_b for this initial routing, the remaining operations after the mixing coin implement its inverse because S^8 is identity: the first eight ticks finish with S^(8-d) after H, then the rail exchange and eight further streams return the output to its encoded ports. The result is U-inverse H U. The chronological definition above fixes the sign of the reflected b amplitude. Every other physical basis mode returns exactly to itself. The bypass is 16 straight ticks.

In the late test each protected right mover follows x(t) = x(0)+t modulo eight; it misses both exchange gates and the mixing coin. This proves isolation in the stipulated circuit. It does not establish causal isolation from an external controller or detector. The [symbolic tick checker](check_four_port_walk.py) propagates all 16 physical basis columns for all 12 ordered port pairs with exact signs and balanced factors. At block boundaries the four ports occupy distinct wells, so ordinary site grouping suffices when empty sites are dark. Any finite preparation or active rearrangement in the identification proof now has a coherent fixed-duration-per-gate realization on this controlled ring.

The spatially homogeneous ring with its Hadamard permanently on is a different control regime. Its free powers commute with translation by two wells, which cycles the four encoded ports. An individual addressed mixer fails to commute with that cycle even on the encoded subspace, so waiting alone cannot implement it there. This leaves a specific control dependency for the detector experiment, alongside the outstanding frequency tests. For a related established use of switched interference and loop routing in a different architecture see [Motes et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.4007).

## A four-tick terminal read outside the switch's causal cone

Before assigning the late setting, permute the encoded ports so the ordered target wells satisfy b = a-2 modulo eight. Every required transposition is available by replacing the meeting H of the 16-tick construction with X. This scheduling is shared between the late alternatives. Turn at b at tick 0 as before. At tick 3 choose identity or H at a+3, stream once and read immediately. The outgoing ports from a,b are at a+4 on R and b+4 on L. Each spectator starting at c is at c+4 on R. Thus all four wells are distinct in both settings. Both spectator reads are three ring edges from the setting event with only one tick elapsed. The checker verifies the swap compilation, both early panels and the distances. With an independent switch choice and a one-edge-per-tick bound on **all** influences on the local original-launch read, its marginal law must be invariant. The walk stream alone bounds propagation of amplitudes; a global normalizer, faster controller or postselection on joint clicks need not obey this locality premise.

### Removing the common scalar score

Suppose a preparation has a real unit amplitude vector z. Let p_i(z) be an arbitrary continuous normalized four-outcome probability panel. Assume the vector suffices for the read across preparation histories, permutations and early read routings once the physical ports are mapped back to logical labels. This includes rail and clock calibration. Assume a dark label has p_i=0 when z_i=0. No common response or ratio form is required. For each protected label i the causal comparisons impose p_i(Hz)=p_i(z) for each addressed mixer H among the other three entries. Density and continuity, as above, make p_i constant on the entire sphere at fixed z_i. Hence p_i depends only on z_i. Flipping a single coordinate leaves the other three probabilities fixed; normalization makes its own probability even. Write g_i for its dependence on the squared coordinate.

For any two labels i,j and a third label k, compare panels placing mass x at i or at j with the same remaining mass 1-x at k. Normalization and the dark-label condition give g_i(x)=g_j(x). Call this common function g. Now compare the filled masses (x,y,1-x-y,0) with (x+y,0,1-x-y,0). Normalization gives g(x+y)=g(x)+g(y), including the endpoint. Since g(1)=1, rational subdivision and continuity yield g(x)=x. The common local score is therefore a conclusion in this model class. The proof uses real preparations and position records only. State sufficiency, continuity, exhaustive counts, dark calibration and finite-speed locality still have independent content. In particular an early/late or L/R-dependent detector cannot be assigned a single p_i merely by renaming its output. For the distinct classical theorem on measures over subspaces see [Gleason (1957)](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~nwallach/gleasonq.pdf).

## A shortest finite test against a discontinuous probability embedding

The compiled menu admits an explicit rival on every state reachable from a sharp port. Its coordinates lie in the real field Q(sqrt(2)). Apply the field automorphism sigma that changes the sign of sqrt(2) to each coordinate, then report p_i(z) = sigma(z_i)^2. This is a single state-dependent rule, positive on occupied ports, dark on empty ones, with sum one because sigma preserves sums and products. Every spectator switch equality holds exactly: the untouched amplitude is identical before and after the switch. The rival even has a local amplitude propagation on this menu. Replace the balanced block H by -H on its addressed pair, leaving bypassed modes, exchanges and streams unchanged, then square the propagated coordinates. This is the four-port specialization of the program's [field-embedding question](Native_preparations_and_probability_embeddings.md). The previous identification theorem rules out a continuous extension agreeing with these rates and the switch comparisons on the dense reachable orbit. Countability alone would give no such conclusion.

Use logical ports 0,1,2,3 in this paragraph, starting in port 0. Apply H on ordered pairs (0,1), (1,2), (0,1), in that order. Write h=1/sqrt(2). The second panel of amplitudes is (h,1/2,1/2,0), and the last is ((1+h)/2,(1-h)/2,1/2,0). The ordinary rates are ((3+2 sqrt(2))/8,(3-2 sqrt(2))/8,1/4,0). The rival exchanges the first two rates. Thus a single ordering comparison between the first two ports, with matched efficiencies, distinguishes the two pure field embeddings. Their predicted signed differences are +1/sqrt(2) and -1/sqrt(2). For a launch-independent mixture with ordinary fraction t the difference is (2t-1)/sqrt(2); an ordering alone then selects only a majority. No counts have been collected.

Three balanced gates are minimal for a sharp input and a final port read, even allowing intervening permutations. After one effective gate there are two coordinates of equal magnitude h. A second gate either misses both, mixes both, or mixes one with zero. In every case each individual terminal amplitude is a rational number or a rational multiple of sqrt(2), whose square is unchanged by sigma. The third gate in the displayed sequence brings these two arithmetic types into the same port. In physical wells 0,2,4 the three mixers each use the verified 16-tick implementation, giving a 48-tick prescribed test with a common final clock. It probes one explicit rival without assuming continuum stability; it cannot exclude all discontinuous or history-sensitive alternatives.

### Single-launch amplification and stability at a sharp preparation

On three logical ports the two embeddings can approximate independently prescribed real rotations with the **same word**. To see this, take the closure of the pairs (U,sigma(U)) for orientation-preserving gate words, including port permutations. Both projections are SO(3): the real beamsplitter universality theorem above applies separately to H and to its conjugate -H as addressed two-mode blocks, and a sequence converging to a rotation eventually has positive determinant. The closure is compact, so the projections are onto. The kernels in each factor are closed normal subgroups. SO(3) has only the trivial and full closed normal subgroup: its Lie algebra is simple by the cross-product bracket, and any discrete normal subgroup of a connected group is central, with trivial center here. The [two-factor Goursat lemma](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.0024) therefore gives either the full product or the graph of a continuous automorphism (continuity follows from compactness of the graph).

A continuous automorphism of SO(3) preserves trace: its differential preserves the negative Killing form and the cross-product bracket and so is a rotation, whose conjugation agrees on the connected group. To exclude such a graph take W to be the three-mixer word above and Z to reverse the sign of port zero alone. Conjugating a two-port swap by H realizes a single sign reversal on its second port. The rotation Z W Z W-inverse is a product of two plane reflections with normals e0 and W e0. Its trace is -1 + 4 W_00^2 by expanding the two reflection matrices. The conjugate trace differs since W_00^2 = (3+2 sqrt(2))/8. This proves full product closure. In particular words exist whose ordinary output tends to the launch port while the rival output tends to an orthogonal port. Exact disagreement at unit versus zero probability is impossible at finite length: a zero algebraic coordinate is zero in both embeddings. Any fixed positive admixture of this rival violates continuity even at the single sharp launch. This excludes that family under a weaker stability premise; general uniqueness still uses the broader hypotheses above.

For a finite check, the following ordered-pair word was selected by a numerical search and then evaluated exactly. From a sharp port zero use, chronologically, 01 02 21 10 12 10 20 21 02 20 01 21 10 20 01 02, where ij denotes H on ports i,j. The final port-zero amplitude is -15/32 - 3 sqrt(2)/8. The two predicted return probabilities are 513/1024 plus or minus 45 sqrt(2)/128, approximately **0.998161** and **0.003792**. Each step on coefficient pairs x+y sqrt(2), u+v sqrt(2) replaces them by (y+v)+(x+u)sqrt(2)/2 and (y-v)+(x-u)sqrt(2)/2. The four-port compiler uses 256 ticks and one final read. This is a model comparison with no repeated launches inside the prescribed word, no intermediate conditioning and no claim of minimum length. Accumulated gate error and the return detector's efficiency would need calibration before interpreting physical counts.

### Classifying detectors continuous in both embeddings

A larger state-sufficiency hypothesis allows the detector to depend on the pair (z,z') = (z,sigma(z)). Assume its exhaustive probability panel extends continuously to the closure of these pairs in the product of the ordinary real unit spheres. This is an additional regularity hypothesis: allowing both coordinates admits detectors discontinuous in z alone. Use sharp-launch dark calibration and the protected marginal equalities for every active mixer and active swap. For the swap comparison use X instead of H at the late meeting; either spectator is at the same three-edge distance. Assume the same logical read calibration across these routings.

The closure is the entire product of four-coordinate unit spheres. Indeed the three-port product result applies on each coordinate triple, leaving the fourth fixed. The group closure on four ports consequently contains every coordinate-plane rotation in either embedding with identity in the other. Successive plane rotations reduce any real unit vector to the sharp vector, so the paired preparations are dense in the product. The active triple alone acts transitively on each pair of spheres of complementary coordinates at fixed (z_i,z'_i), including a zero-radius sphere. Extending the measured generator equalities by continuity and composing them now makes p_i depend only on that local pair.

Normalization after independent sign reversals in either vector makes the dependence even in each argument. Denote it by g_i(x,y) on squared coordinates. Dark calibration on a sharp preparation in a different port gives g_i(0,0)=0. Put (x,y) in port i, its coordinatewise complement in a third port k, and zero pairs elsewhere. Moving (x,y) from i to j leaves the k term fixed; normalization gives g_i=g_j. For their common function g, distribute pairs A and B across two ports and their complementary remainder (1,1)-A-B across a third. Comparing with the merged pair A+B yields g(A+B)=g(A)+g(B) whenever both sums are at most one. Applying this on the two axes and then adding gives g(x,y)=t x+u y by rational subdivision and continuity. Nonnegative probabilities and g(1,1)=1 imply u=1-t and 0<=t<=1. Conversely all these panels satisfy the hypotheses. Thus **one constant mixing fraction** describes the whole two-embedding class, even though no mixture law or shared one-port score was imposed initially. Stability in z at the single sharp launch then selects t=1 by the amplification result above. An additional inaccessible state variable, or failure of extension to the paired closure, lies outside the theorem.

There is a parameter-free cross-check between the two finite prescriptions already given. Let D be the port-zero minus port-one rate for the three-mixer preparation and R the port-zero return rate for the sixteen-mixer preparation. Substitution gives **R = 513/1024 + (45/64) D**. A common detector preparation and efficiency calibration across the two clocks is essential. Violation would contradict at least one assumption of this whole classified family. Agreement at these two settings cannot certify the family on other words. No count data are asserted.

### A finite record comparison selecting a pure embedding

Use the three-mixer word W above. Let p be its port-zero return rate. For a second setting run W, reverse the sign of port zero, then undo W before the final read. The sign reversal uses H X H on a pair whose second port is zero. Thus the echo fits nine 16-tick blocks in the four-port ring. Pad the direct setting with straight eight-tick cycles to match its clock, subject to verifying that padding preserves the read. For either pure embedding the unobserved return amplitude of the echo is 1-2r, where r is the direct return probability in that embedding: expand W-transpose (I-2|0><0|) W at entry 00. No intervening position read occurs.

Write r_+, r_- = (3 +/- 2 sqrt(2))/8 for the two direct rates. In the classified family p = t r_+ + (1-t) r_-. The echo frequency E averages (1-2r_+)^2 and (1-2r_-)^2 with the same weights. Direct subtraction gives the exact identity

    E - (1 - 2p)^2 = 4 t(1-t) (r_+ - r_-)^2 = 2 t(1-t).

The ordinary and conjugate echo rates are respectively (9 - 4 sqrt(2))/16 and (9 + 4 sqrt(2))/16. Consequently an observed zero gap selects a pure embedding within this family, and p > 3/8 then selects the ordinary one. These are two finite settings. The underlying family classification still assumes the full protected comparisons and paired-state regularity. In finite data, if the true gap is bounded above by epsilon < 1/2 and p > 3/8, the conjugate fraction is at most (1-sqrt(1-2 epsilon))/2. This is a deterministic implication for the true rates; sampling uncertainty must enter both input bounds.

There is an independent record interpretation of the nonlinear term. Pair independently reset direct launches and assign +1 to equal return bits and -1 to unequal bits. The mean is (1-2p)^2. This statistic can be checked against the direct marginal before comparison with the coherent echo. If an unobserved embedding is shared by both launches, the parity mean can equal E for an interior mixture: conditional independence within each embedding gives the average of the two squares. Reset independence must therefore be tested or the direct marginal used explicitly. The protocol asks for an echo-versus-record equality as data; locality alone does not impose it. It specializes the earlier [composition analysis](Probability_from_record_composition.md) without requiring a coherent gate between launches.
