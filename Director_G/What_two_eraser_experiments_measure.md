# Can measured quantum-eraser records distinguish the probability rules?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Changing the read basis of a which-way record raised system–record agreement from 51.49% to 95.95% in an archived superconducting experiment. The frequency of the system bit alone changed by 0.74 percentage points. A separate optical experiment measured an interference fringe while scanning phase, tracking both coincidences and idler singles. These measurements address two different parts of the proposed walk experiment. The symmetry of the superconducting circuit leaves its probability exponent completely unidentified, even with both bits reported on every shot. [Tran et al., Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-022-00126-1) · [Optical experiment and measurements](https://github.com/paul-gauthier/entangled-pair-quantum-eraser/tree/dad65d0188e25b833a55bd80cb9c1c1ec2a32bde)

They either leave the record in its path basis or apply \(H\) before reading it. The paper attributes panel 1 to the device. For erased minus which-way the system-zero difference is \(61/8192\). With independent shots, Hoeffding's inequality for a difference of two equal-size sample means gives a two-sided 95% interval of \([-0.0138,0.0287]\). These intervals address sampling within the runs. The histograms omit shot order, so time drift and temporal independence cannot be checked from them. ## What a symmetric eraser cannot identify

For a joint kinematic table \(w_{rs}\), a global power read scores each cell by \(w_{rs}^q\) and normalizes across all cells. A separate read first chooses a record sector with its ordinary weight \(p_r=\sum_s w_{rs}\), then normalizes \(w_{rs}^q\) within the chosen sector. Assume \(q>0\). Both readings posit signed quantum kinematics and a specified grouping of output cells.

The ideal Vigo settings yield \((1,1,1,1)/4\) and \((1,0,0,1)/2\) in the order above. In each setting every positive cell is equal and both sector totals are \(1/2\). Every positive exponent under either rule gives exactly the same **full joint tables**. Any common subsequent stochastic detector acting on identical distributions preserves this equality. Even exact knowledge of that detector would leave identical ideal predictions. Gate or preparation errors *before* scoring could lift the degeneracy if their kinematic distortions were independently characterized. Fitting an exponent to the noisy frequencies taken as input kinematic masses would reuse the data as their own calibration.

More generally a balanced, ideal two-output eraser can give a table \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\ b&a\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\), with \(a+b=1/2\), as the phase varies. After applying any common nonnegative score \(g\) with \(g(a)+g(b)>0\), each row has the same normalizer \(g(a)+g(b)\), and each column has the same sum. Global and separately normalized reads then give the **same joint law as each other** and preserve the system marginal \(1/2\). At intermediate phases the conditional fringe *shape* can depend on \(g\). With calibrated phase, transfer and gains it can test an exponent, while the ordering of the two normalizations remains indistinguishable. This symmetry calculation describes a complete ideal analyzer. [Lean check of the common denominator for rational scores](lean/EraserRowScores.lean)

## Optical fringe versus singles

In the independent Group 13 optical archive the signal polarizer selects the polarization correlated with an idler's two interferometer paths. A single transmitted signal port is paired in coincidence with an idler detector behind its output polarizer. Rotating the signal polarizer changes the coincidence fringe. The idler singles in the same study include idler clicks without a signal coincidence. The authors' ideal per-pair probabilities at the chosen joint port are \(1/8\) in the which-way setting and \((1-\cos\delta)/8\) with erasure. The source tree at the pinned revision publishes the figures and analysis but omits the JSONL acquisitions read by its pipeline. Its analyzed clicks lack an independent emitted-pair count and the polarizers' rejected channels. For any setting with unknown emission mean \(L\), a count expectation at the joint port can have the form \(L p_{is}\eta_i\eta_s+B_c\), while the corresponding idler expectation is \(L p_i\eta_i+B_i\). Even after independent background and gain calibration, the transformation \(L\mapsto kL\), \((p_{is},p_i)\mapsto(p_{is},p_i)/k\) for \(k\ge1\) gives the same observed means by assigning the extra probability to unobserved outcomes. If brightness can differ by setting, the hidden rescalings can also differ. A measured emission monitor or a justified common flux and gain assumption would narrow that ambiguity.

## Breaking the symmetry on two qubits

A controlled Hadamard can prepare \((|0\rangle_r|0\rangle_s+|1\rangle_r|+\rangle_s)/\sqrt{2}\) from a balanced control and a blank target. Read the record in its fixed basis. Choose \(Z\) or \(X\) for the system analyzer. In the same bit order the ideal cell weights are \((2,0,1,1)/4\) and \((1,1,2,0)/4\). Each separate read with ordinary sector weights gives \(P(r=0)=1/2\). The global power gives \(1/(1+2^{1-q})\) and its complement. For the square of the kinematic mass these are \(2/3\) and \(1/3\). A global fixed exponent has equal rates at these two settings only at \(q=1\). Opposite deviations can cancel in a distribution of exponents, and a setting-dependent first-stage sector sampler changes the prediction. This control was not run in either cited experiment. A measurement would require calibrated state preparation, both outcomes, stable read confusion and examination of setting-dependent failures. A locality claim additionally requires isolation and setting timing.

The walk's seven-tick line and nine-tick ring supply sharper comparisons with a genuinely distant site and varying site weights. The data above verify that both fringe sorting and full-record summation can be read in physical systems. Transferring those data to the walk's site detector or its continuum trajectories would require an independently tested correspondence between instruments.
