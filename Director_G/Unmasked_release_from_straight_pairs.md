# An unmasked flight from a position-only direction preparation

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist
Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Stop applying the straight-pair filters immediately after the complete switching word. The unmodified physical Hadamard walk can then release a slow packet with asymptotic probability one half, conditional on the prepared source. The other half moves ballistically on this scale. The stopping rule, the gauge from the encoding, and the physical sheet character all affect the result.

## The physical release state

Use a microscopic sheet of order $N=4M$, a base ring of $4N$ wells, and character convention $z=e^{-2ih}$ with $h=\pi s/N$. Start at physical well $2a$ and a sharp rail. Keep $m$ consecutive logical two-tick switches with $m$ even. This preparation takes $4m$ physical ticks. On each logical step only straight native pairs survive. Its probability in the specified physical sector is $2^{-2m}P_m(4h)$, using the logical probability for the field slope $4h$. A known physical character $s=\pm1$, or a prior physical selection with controlled contamination, is needed for the weak-field flight below. Selecting a logical unit alone also admits the lifts $s\equiv\pm1\bmod M$. The slope $h$ on those lifts can be of order one. [Encoding and finite signed histograms](Straight_pairs_and_direction_records.md) · [Native physical-sector selector](Native_multiscale_selector.md)

At the encoded site $X=2x$ the real coin sign and physical sheet gain differ from their logical values by the endpoint character $(-z^{-1})^{(a+2m-x)/2}$, up to a common factor. With the negative spatial Fourier exponent this means that the physical spinor's scalar Fourier profile at momentum $k$ is the logical profile at

$$q=2k+\pi/2+h.$$

The proportionality includes a translation by the original home and a global phase. The state occupies one rail, equal to its launching rail, and one spatial class modulo four. Its Fourier modulus has period $\pi/2$. Starting at $a=0$ for simplicity, the exact unnormalized shape on that rail after division by the straight-pair factor is $\prod_{j=1}^m\sin(q+(2j-1)4h)$. Its four large peaks are centered at $k_\ell=\ell\pi/2-(2m+1/2)h$ modulo $2\pi$. The four equal-width Fourier tiles centered on those peaks have equal mass. The peaks approach the four different momenta $0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2$. [Two-step switch multiplier](Field_reversal_during_preparation.md)

## Fixed-action flight

Choose $F>0$, $s=\pm1$, $\delta=(\pi/(NF))^{1/3}$, physical site pitch $\delta$, and physical tick duration $\delta^2$. Let $m\delta^2\to\lambda>0$. The clock for the unmasked flight starts at release. The measure carried by an individual tile has initial mass $1/4$. In its local coordinate $p=(k-\ell\pi/2)/\delta$ the normalized shape tends in squared norm to a real Gaussian proportional to $\exp[-2\lambda(p+2sF\lambda)^2]$. Indeed the corresponding logical coordinate is $2p+h/\delta$ about its edge; the initial logical center is shifted by $-4sF\lambda$. The field-on source survival factor relative to $P_m(0)$ tends to $\exp(-16F^2\lambda^3/3)$. Both statements follow by expanding the same product of cosines on compact charts and using the Gaussian bound for its tails. Position is measured relative to the last valid encoded home $Y_0=2a\delta$, on a local lift. [Field-on normalization and chart estimates](Field_on_source_and_release.md)

The tiles at $\pm\pi/2$ have zero group velocity at their carrier. They are parity replicas of one another, as are the tiles at $0,\pi$. These partners may be coherent. Their integrated masses for smooth macroscopic position observations add because a smooth position characteristic translates momentum by only $O(\delta)$. In the two slow tiles a sharp R or L rail has limiting half-weight in each smooth band. The scaled momentum has mean $-2sF\lambda$ and variance $1/(8\lambda)$; its conjugate position variance is $2\lambda$ and the symmetrized covariance is zero. In the forced-envelope limit the two band Hamiltonians are $bp^2/2+sFY$, $b=\pm1$. The position detector therefore sees two slow Gaussian distributions of mass $1/4$ each at elapsed time $T>0$, with

$$
\mu_{\pm}=Y_0\pm F(2\lambda T+T^2/2),\qquad
\sigma_T^2=2\lambda+T^2/(8\lambda).
$$

The position distribution here is the sum of the slow contributions, of total mass $1/2$. It is a weak limit against continuous compactly supported functions for the whole unnormalized-on-release-window spatial law. The full law on the macroscopic line is not tight. ### Where the other half goes

At $0$ and $\pi$, the free Hadamard group velocities are $\pm1/\sqrt2$ physical sites per tick. For an R launch their aggregate masses are $(1+1/\sqrt2)/4$ at positive velocity and $(1-1/\sqrt2)/4$ at negative velocity. An L launch exchanges these two weights. Their sum is $1/2$. They follow from the RR entry $(1+\omega'(k))/2$ of the positive-frequency spectral projector with $\omega(k)=\arcsin(\sin k/\sqrt2)$ and the replica relation $U(k+\pi)=-U(k)$.

For the velocity assertion work on the lifted line. At time $n\delta^2\to T$, the support cone of the native preparation and flight fits on the ring because $m+n=O(\delta^{-2})$ and $N$ is of order $\delta^{-3}$. Along the accelerated momentum path the total sweep is $O(n|h|)=O(\delta)$. Moving the eigenframe overlaps and the two endpoint frames to a single chart costs $O(n|h|)$ in norm by the uniform gap estimate. Keep the accumulated eigenphases exactly. In the characteristic function for $X/n$, translating momentum by $\xi/n$ changes their signed sum by $\pm\xi\omega'(k_\ell)+o(1)$ on each compact tile chart. The initial envelope translate has norm error $o(1)$ since $(1/n)/\delta\to0$, and the field-dependent shift of the chart is also $O(\delta)$. Remove smooth cutoffs after this calculation by the initial Gaussian tail estimate and unitarity. Thus the fast mass concentrates at those two nonzero velocities. Cross-band matrix elements have vanishing overlap for a momentum translation tending to zero, and distinct tiles have separated supports. ## A position aperture for the slow packet

At fixed $T>0$ choose $R_\delta\to\infty$ with $\delta R_\delta\to0$, for instance $R_\delta=\delta^{-1/2}$ in the physical position coordinate. Keep only wells with $|Y-Y_0|\le R_\delta$. The slow components are tight, so their lost squared norm tends to zero. The fast components satisfy $|Y-Y_0|/(\delta n)\to1/\sqrt2$ in probability, so their retained norm tends to zero. The projected vector is consequently $o(1)$ in norm from the freely evolved slow spectral component. Its acceptance conditional on the successful encoded preparation tends to $1/2$. The selected state contains both slow curvature bands. A later unmasked flight of bounded duration preserves the norm estimate. Keeping a permanent record of which individual well passed would destroy the asserted post-aperture state.

The physical flight uses $n$ native ticks with no per-flight acceptance penalty $2^{-n}$. The preparation factor $2^{-2m}$ remains. The final aperture and all earlier passing schedules are whole-position product events on the original microscopic walk; after the sheet sector is specified, the square-character term of its classified endpoint law supplies these probabilities. A pure counting input, or a mixture with poorly calibrated physical lifts, need not have the same release law.

For an upstream physical selector with error $\eta$, the rarity of this particular successful word does not by itself cause unlimited contamination. In the nonaliasing regime, Hölder bounds $P_m(4h_s)$ by the same zero-field $P_m(0)$ for every character. At fixed $\lambda,F$ the target yield divided by $P_m(0)$ tends to the strictly positive constant above. The factor $2^{-2m}$ is common. Thus an incident wrong character fraction $\eta=o(1)$ is still $O(\eta)$ after preparing the source. In contrast, $s=1+M$ has the same logical preparation probability as $s=1$ at every $m$; the encoding alone cannot suppress this physical lift.

For a finite test use $N=16$, $M=4$, start at $(0,R)$ and take two switching blocks. The eight-tick source succeeds with probability $1/128$ at both $s=1$ and $s=5$. After three unmasked ticks the port $(5,R)$ has signed counts $-1,-1$ on residues 8 and 9 and zero on every other residue. Conditional on the source it reads $[1+\cos(2\pi s/16)]/8$, about $0.24048$ and $0.07716$ respectively. The physical ambiguity has an observable consequence at an ordinary terminal port.

## Separating the packet before an ordinary flight

The spatial selection can have vanishing *macroscopic* delay. At fixed action take $n_*=\lfloor\delta^{-3/2}\rfloor$ free ticks and $R_\delta=\delta^{-1/4}$. The elapsed physical time tends to zero while a fast ray moves a physical distance of order $\delta^{-1/2}$. Translation by $\xi/n_*$ in the velocity argument above remains negligible relative to the physical Fourier width, since $n_*\delta\to\infty$. The slow envelope stays tight at these vanishing times. This aperture therefore prepares the slow component with conditional probability $1/2+o(1)$ before any prescribed positive flight time.

On the shrinking-action scale instead take physical pitch $\delta^2$, tick duration $\delta^3$, $\delta=(\pi/(NF))^{1/4}$ and $m\sim\delta^{-5/2}$ even. The logical forcing remains weak during preparation, since $m^3(4h)^2\to0$. Each slow carrier has microscopic momentum width of order $m^{-1/2}$, hence scaled momentum width $O(m^{-1/2}/\delta)\to0$, and its momentum shift $O(mh/\delta)\to0$. The comparison binomial envelope has scaled position width $O(\delta^2\sqrt m)\to0$ after doubling the sites. Norm convergence to that envelope suffices for bounded position reads. The two slow bands then follow $Y_0\pm FT^2/2$ at fixed elapsed time $T$ by the weak-field packet estimate. Every fixed positive-radius aperture about $Y_0$ at the earlier $n_*=\lfloor\delta^{-5/2}\rfloor$ passes the slow packet and rejects the fast packet, since $\delta^3 n_*\to0$, $\delta^2 n_*\to\infty$ and $n_*/\sqrt m\to\infty$. The position-amplitude assertion here follows by the same contraction argument, leaving both slow bands coherent inside the passing aperture. From the specified physical target sector the probability per original source attempt of making the packet and passing this early spatial test is asymptotic to $2^{-2m-1}/\sqrt{\pi m}$. The later unfiltered flight does not turn the preparation into a polynomial-cost experiment.

At $N=16{,}384$, $F=1$, $m=76$ and elapsed time nearest to one in the fixed-action experiment, the central aperture accepted $0.49960$ of the prepared source. The right-moving fast fraction was $0.42667$ against the predicted limiting $0.42678$ for an R launch.
