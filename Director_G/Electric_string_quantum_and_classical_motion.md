# Quantum binding and classical motion from a local electric string

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A local walk in one dimension can generate a two-body potential without reading both particle positions at each tick. Add one binary electric-flux register on every bond, toggle that register when an occupant traverses it, and apply the same phase to each energized bond. For a pair prepared in the vacuum Gauss sector, the energy is exactly the string tension times their separation. This paper derives the two-body quantum evolution and the ensuing classical collision in the same model. It also identifies a different clock on which the flux and matter update has a finite propagation speed. The construction assumes the link degrees of freedom, their energy, coherent local gates and a position readout. Microscopically the two charges carry the same nontrivial \(\mathbb Z_2\) charge; a pair is neutral. The two endpoints have opposite forces. ## Reducing the local circuit

Scalar sites are spaced \(b=a/2\), with two sites in each cell of pitch \(a\). A full matter step \(K_a\) uses the two alternating nearest-neighbor bond layers of the [occupation walk](Interacting_occupation_walk.md). Use the determinant of each addressed one-body gate as its double-occupation amplitude, as selected by the [four-mode composition test](Occupation_contact_from_composition.md) under the stated record-equivalence premise. Work on the line. With particles at \(i<j\) and vacuum flux on the far left, the constraint \(E_l\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}E_{l-1}=n_l\) fixes \(E_l=\mathbf1_{i\le l<j}\). On a matter hop the only differing bit is the link on the traversed bond. Different paths with common initial and final occupations therefore leave the same field configuration. The map \(L|i,j\rangle=|i,j\rangle|E(i,j)\rangle\) is an isometry. Each dressed bond rotation intertwines with the ordinary occupation gate through \(L\). After **both** layers, the product of local phases \(e^{-i\tau\sigma b E_l/\varepsilon}\), with \(\sigma>0\), gives one reduced cycle

\[
 W_a=D_a\,\bigwedge\nolimits^2 U_a,\qquad
 D_a(i,j)=e^{-i\tau \sigma b(j-i)/\varepsilon}.
\]

Here \(U_a\) is the one-particle cycle; \(L\) is a change of description, not a nonlocal gate in the schedule. Occupation reads intertwine as well, so the full dressed circuit gives the reduced model's complete occupation transcripts, including an intermediate read. Bare partial trace over the links can erase the off-diagonals in the matter density; using that diagonal state for a subsequent **dressed** hop would give the wrong interference law. A far-away source change that alters a nearby string bit changes the local initial data too. Identical local reduced states within a backward causal cone give identical local records. With four ordered occupied sites the energy counts the first and third intervening gaps. Treating it as a sum of six pair potentials would change the many-body model. ## A strong quantum limit with the field switched on

Fix \(m,\sigma,\varepsilon>0\). Take the same rational one-particle rotations as in the hard-point calculation, with cosines/sines \((3/5,4/5),(4/5,3/5)\), and the same orientation convention in its complex Bloch chart. Real occupation gates are related by the fixed odd-site phase gauge, which commutes with the electric multiplier. At \(k=0\) the positive-energy eigenvalue is \(e^{-i\gamma}\), \(\cos\gamma=24/25\), and its angle has expansion \(\omega(k)=\gamma+\kappa k^2/2+O(k^4)\), \(\kappa=12/7\). Set \(\tau=m\kappa a^2/\varepsilon\). Remove the two-particle rest phase by multiplying each cycle by \(e^{2i\gamma}\). On scalar antisymmetric wave functions on the full \((x,y)\)-plane the limiting generator is \(-iH/\varepsilon\), where

\[
 H=-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2m}(\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2)+\sigma|y-x|.
\]

Restriction to \(x<y\) multiplied by \(\sqrt2\) gives the Dirichlet problem there. This statement holds for the scalar envelope; at finite \(a\) two different modes in one cell can both be occupied. The field kick can mix the free Bloch bands. Simply restricting every evolved state to its initial band would assume part of the conclusion.

Here are the consistency and stability details. Let \(J_a\) send each scalar Fourier variable \(\xi\) to its normalized positive-band spinor \(u(a\xi)\), multiplied by a smooth cutoff \(\chi(a\xi)\) equal to one near zero and supported strictly inside the isolated band chart. Use normalized Fourier sampling on the grid and take the antisymmetric tensor map. This is a contraction and \(\|J_a f\|\to\|f\|\). It specifies the initial data; no band measurements are made during the flight. Write \(G_a=e^{2i\gamma}\bigwedge^2 U_a\). On smooth compactly supported antisymmetric \(\phi\),

\[
 \left\| \frac{G_a-I}{\tau}J_a\phi-
 J_a\frac{-iH_0\phi}{\varepsilon}\right\|\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad H_0=-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2m}\Delta .
\]

In Fourier coordinates this follows from the expansion above and dominated convergence; on the chart the difference quotient is bounded by a constant times \(\xi_x^2+\xi_y^2\). For the potential term identify a cell amplitude with its piecewise constant interpolation, using the \(L^2\) normalization, and retain both sublattice indices. For a core vector, the interpolated \(J_a\phi\) tends to \(u(0)\otimes u(0)\,\phi\) even after multiplication by \(1+|x|+|y|\). Indeed derivatives in Fourier space of \(\chi(a\xi)u(a\xi)\) are bounded with each derivative contributing a factor \(a\); the inverse transforms converge in the Schwartz seminorms for a Schwartz input. Sampling those smooth spinor functions and replacing each sample by a cell constant preserves weighted \(L^2\) convergence. For a general \(L^2\) vector one obtains ordinary unweighted convergence by density and contractivity. In particular this latter conclusion applies to \(V\phi\), \(V=\sigma|y-x|\), without differentiating the cusp.

At lattice sites the two sublattice offsets change \(V\) from its value at a cell origin by at most \(\sigma a/2\). Use \(|e^{-iz}-1|\le |z|\), weighted convergence on \(\phi\), and dominated convergence on the limiting spinor to obtain

\[
 \left\|\frac{D_a-I}{\tau}J_a\phi-J_a\frac{-iV\phi}{\varepsilon}\right\|\to0 .
\]

The full quotient follows by writing it as \((D_a-I)J_a\phi/\tau+D_a(G_a-I)J_a\phi/\tau\); the extra \(D_a\) tends strongly to the identity on the already convergent free-derivative vector. The real nonnegative locally Lipschitz potential of at most linear growth defines an essentially self-adjoint Schrödinger operator on compact smooth full-plane functions. Exchange symmetry projects this core onto the antisymmetric core. In particular one may use smooth antisymmetric functions crossing the diagonal; demanding support away from the wall would impose a stronger derivative condition on an operator core.

To see why the core estimate suffices, first take a continuum trajectory from the generator domain. Its time derivative is continuous in norm and its values are continuous in graph norm on compact time intervals. Approximate it by a piecewise linear graph-norm path whose finitely many vertices belong to the core: choose a small time mesh, then approximate those vertices to graph-norm error small compared with the mesh. Its residual \(\partial_t\phi+iH\phi/\varepsilon\) is small away from the finitely many corners, by uniform continuity of the original derivative and \(H\phi\). On each segment the consistency estimates are uniform by linearity; a time cell containing a corner has a bounded integrated residual. Telescoping with the unitary lattice cycles bounds the error by the time integral of this residual plus a term tending to zero. Density extends the result to all initial data. Thus the demodulated \(n\)-step state approaches \(J_a e^{-it_nH/\varepsilon}f\) in norm uniformly on \(t_n=n\tau\le T\). This is a comparison in the dressed Hilbert space. In a separate periodic diagnostic the fixed-action generator residual on a low-momentum column fell from \(5.27\) to \(0.218\) between 16 and 256 cells. Freezing the source spinor before taking the difference quotient gave \(6.96\) at 256 cells. ## Bound states and the classical meeting

Put \(R=(x+y)/2,\ r=y-x>0\). The center of mass moves freely with mass \(2m\); the internal Hamiltonian is \(-(\varepsilon^2/m)\partial_r^2+\sigma r\) with a zero trace at \(r=0\). For \(\ell=(\varepsilon^2/(m\sigma))^{1/3}\) its normalized eigenfunctions are proportional to \(\operatorname{Ai}(r/\ell-\lambda_n)\), where \(\operatorname{Ai}(-\lambda_n)=0\), and its internal energies are \(\sigma\ell\lambda_n\). The decaying Airy solution and the boundary condition give this quantization directly. The first three dimensionless energies from a separate tridiagonal Sturm count in a box of length 18 at mesh \(18/721\) were \(2.33805,\ 4.08778,\ 5.52024\). These are continuum checks; strong convergence of finite-time evolution alone does not imply spectral convergence of all lattice quasienergies. [Box sizes and computed levels](results/string_band.json)

Take an antisymmetrized product of coherent packets centered at distinct classical positions \(q_1<q_2\), with momenta \(p_1,p_2\) and widths of order \(\sqrt\varepsilon\) in position and momentum. Its Wigner measure on the full plane has two equally weighted exchange-related atoms; their cross term vanishes because the phase-space centers are separated. The potential has the conical form \(\sigma|g|\) for \(g(x,y)=y-x\). The propagation theorem of Fermanian-Kammerer, Gérard and Lasser applies after a constant mass/time rescaling to the standard kinetic convention. Its excluded glancing set is \(r=0,\ p_y-p_x=0\). The conserved relative energy \(m\dot r^2/4+\sigma |r|\) is strictly positive on these rays, so any encounter is transverse. The theorem transports both atoms through the crossing; sorting their positions gives one classical trajectory even at the instant of coincidence. Positive potential and conservation of energy give tightness of momentum marginals, and finite-time position tightness follows from the initial second moment and the kinetic bound. Thus these are probabilities of bounded continuous sorted-position records, including the collision. [Fermanian-Kammerer, Gérard and Lasser, Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.5047#page=4)

Before first contact \(R(t)=R_0+(p_1+p_2)t/(2m)\) and
\[
 r(t)=r_0+\dot r_0 t-\sigma t^2/m,\qquad
 t_{\rm hit}=\frac{m}{2\sigma}\left(\dot r_0+\sqrt{\dot r_0^2+4\sigma r_0/m}\right).
\]
At a meeting the relative velocity reverses in the ordered coordinates. Between meetings each endpoint has constant inward force of magnitude \(\sigma\). The separation therefore oscillates between zero and the turning point set by the relative energy. The limit established here is first \(a\to0\) at fixed \(\varepsilon\), then \(\varepsilon\to0\). Strong convergence lets one choose a sufficiently fine diagonal mesh for each prescribed packet and action; it supplies no explicit uniform mesh rate through the cusp. The finite open-chain and relative-coordinate runs measure the model at actual lattice times and retain their finite-box errors. [Motion and independent continuum checks](results/string_motion.json)

At action \(\varepsilon=1/16\) and 2,048 relative cells with zero total momentum, the probability of a separation of at least one was 0.05426 near the first collision and 0.99728 after rebound. A separate odd-wave continuum solver gave 0.04978 and 0.99761 at those same lattice times. At an intermediate rebound read the probabilities were 0.52571 and 0.65665. The final phase-sensitive norm discrepancy was 0.944. A full open-chain test allowing nonzero center-of-mass velocity gave a mean separation of 1.564 at time 1.358 against the continuum value 1.546, with center-of-mass displacement error 0.052. The folded finite box in the relative calculation is an auxiliary numerical geometry. The open-chain calculation uses the unique unwrapped link string. ## Reading two energized links with the walk

The Gaussian launch used for the open-chain test can itself be approximated with addressed coherent controls. Let \(u,v\) be its two single-particle band orbitals restricted to \(N\) scalar sites. Orthonormalize the two columns in their span; their normalized antisymmetric product changes at most by a common phase. A complex Givens rotation on rows \(j-1,j\) reduces entries \((z,w)\) in the first column to \((h,0)\) with \(h=(|z|^2+|w|^2)^{1/2}\): use the rows \((\bar z,\bar w)/h,(-w,z)/h\) when \(h>0\). Clear that column bottom up and then clear the second column on rows 1 through \(N-1\). Orthogonality makes its row 0 vanish. Up to a common phase, the wedge is now the adjacent pair at sites 0 and 1. Starting from the local neutral pair source and applying the inverse sequence therefore prepares both orbitals and the correct link string in every branch. At most \(2N-3\) addressed nearest-neighbor rotations are needed. This is the two-column case of unitary synthesis; the dressed link toggle and determinant contact carry it into the Gauss sector. [Reck et al. on two-mode synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.73.58) · [Dressed source tests](results/string_source.json)

A direct propagation used 13 rotations for an eight-site target with Gaussian-integer wedge coefficients and 17 for a ten-site pair of positive-band wavepackets. The full dressed errors were below \(6.1\times10^{-16}\). The measured version chose a fixed phase on the cleared row, giving inverse gates with determinant \(-i\). Replacing that determinant on double occupation by one changed the sharp position distributions by total variation 0.343 and 0.185. Both inverse echoes returned with unit probability to numerical precision; dephasing the strings before reversal reduced those probabilities to 0.046875 and 0.03282. [Source, inverse and controls](check_string_source.py)

For any desired norm accuracy one may truncate two line orbitals on a growing interval, then apply the construction. Linear independence persists for sufficiently small tail error. The normalized wedge depends continuously on its two columns wherever its norm is positive. Rational points are dense in the unit three-sphere of \(SU(2)\) parameters by stereographic projection. Approximating each adjacent gate to operator error \(\delta\) changes the final dressed state by at most \((2N-3)\delta\), since the adjacent determinant-one lift is a direct sum of single-occupation blocks and identities. Subsequent unitary flight preserves that error. This requires independently programmed amplitude and phase controls, aligned references for complex entries, and time for launch. The fixed autonomous flight rule alone does not prescribe the program. A general correlated two-particle state can require a source beyond a single wedge; in particular the zero-total-momentum numerical comparison has a different preparation.

In a finite array put a real phase plane on each bond. On the aligned code each quarter turn \(J_l\) restricts to a single common \(J\), with \(J^2=-I\). An electric-bit-controlled rotation through angle \(\theta\) then acts on each flux configuration by \(\exp(\theta J\sum_l E_l)\). These operations act on distinct links and their own planes in parallel. Starting with independent blanks on a chain of \(N\ge2\) such planes, the earlier deterministic contact preparation takes \(\lceil N/2\rceil\) nearest-neighbor attachment layers under its gate and timing assumptions, before loading phase-sensitive data. The alignment has a preparation cost even though the kick is parallel. Uniform alignment throughout an infinite line is an idealization. For finite flight and localized initial data, one can align a finite interval containing the relevant causal cone, at a cost that grows with its length. A four-site trial distinguishes the prepared references. Create an adjacent dressed pair at sites \((0,1)\), split the right occupant on bond \(1,2\) with a balanced real gate, and swap sites 2 and 3. All hops toggle the traversed link. The two branches have occupations \((0,1)\) and \((0,3)\), and energized links \(\{0\}\) and \(\{0,1,2\}\). Rotate each energized link's plane by \(R_l=(3I+4J_l)/5\), undo the swap and splitter, and read the right occupant. Use identity on vacuum and the gate determinant on double occupation. Reversing one rotation exchanges the two oriented predictions. These measurements distinguish preparations of a relative phase standard.
