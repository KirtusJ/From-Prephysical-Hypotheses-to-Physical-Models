# Which hidden site copies can the walk diagnose?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A direction detector can report the right rail while also keeping a secret copy of the well. How can the walk detect that copy if the detector has memory? Consider a sharp launch, one attempt at $m$ consecutive rail changes (one read after each pair of Hadamard ticks), and a detector whose hidden full site copies follow an arbitrary joint distribution. Deliberately adding unread, persistent site records gives an exact answer at zero field. With the permanent field and a localized sheet, some distinctions disappear, but the rate for one long direction word still tests whether the walk can produce its predicted later records. ## A finite experiment at zero field

The $d=m-1$ internal boundaries form $D=\{1,\ldots,m-1\}$. At boundary $j$ the $j$th rail projection has just passed. A full copy retains the site in an unused external slot and preserves the rail and sheet. The detector itself would copy at $X\subseteq D$, drawn before the attempt. We may add copies at any fixed $F\subseteq D$; the resulting site dephasings act at $X\cup F$. Assume the law of $X$ is independent of the setting and the path, the external copies introduce just these operations, and all other branches have the stated squared-coordinate dynamics. Correlations among the hidden bits are unrestricted. Every launch, including first failures, counts in the denominator. A new slot at the final pass can affect flight without affecting the success just observed.

Write $a_j=\binom{2j}{j}4^{-j}$, $a_0=1$. For $K\subseteq D$ let $W(K)=\prod_i a_{\ell_i}$ where $\ell_i$ are consecutive segment lengths cut at $K$ with endpoints $0,m$. Each unread site record leaves a mixture of sharp wells on the passing rail, every one with the same next segment survival. The zero-field direction amplitude gives $a_j$ by the integral of $\sin^{2j}q$. Use the infinite line or a ring on which the integrals apply to the segments (in particular $2m<4M$ on the native ring). Measure $Y(F)=\mathbb E W(X\cup F)$. Marginal readings in short windows can match an independent detector while long records differ. Here forcing is paired with the very word length being calibrated. [Field-off word and stationary counterexample](Hidden_site_records_and_preparation_time.md)

**Identification.** Define
$$
 b(U)=\sum_{V\supseteq U}(-1)^{|V\setminus U|}W(V),\qquad
 C(U)=\frac{1}{b(U)}\sum_{F\supseteq U}(-1)^{|F\setminus U|}Y(F).
$$
Then $C(U)=\Pr(X\subseteq U)$ and every $b(U)>0$. The point probability at $V$ is $\sum_{U\subseteq V}(-1)^{|V\setminus U|}C(U)$. To see the first equality, pair terms which differ on a bit of $X\setminus U$. Their two unions with $X$ are identical, so they cancel. The histories with $X\subseteq U$ contribute $b(U)$. This works for an arbitrary finite law. The last inversion is ordinary finite inclusion-exclusion. [Rota, Möbius functions](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/rota1.pdf)

For positivity, view $a_j$ as the chance of a return to zero of an auxiliary simple symmetric walk at step $2j$. The product $W(U)$ is the chance of returns at all the designated boundaries and the endpoint. Subtraction of the other possible return times leaves consecutive *first* returns of lengths $2\ell_i$. Thus $b(U)=\prod_i f_{\ell_i}$. The return generating function $A(z)=(1-z)^{-1/2}$ and the first-return decomposition give $\sum_{j\ge1} f_j z^j=1-1/A(z)=1-\sqrt{1-z}$. Therefore $f_j=a_j/(2j-1)>0$. The auxiliary walk proves the coefficient identity, with no additional detector assumed in the physical walk. [First-return calculation](https://www2.math.uu.se/~takis/L/McRw/mcrw.pdf)

The all-clear fraction $\Pr(X=\varnothing)$ needs just the alternating contrast divided by $f_m$. A uniform independent random mask supplies a single unbiased score $2^d(-1)^{|F|}{\bf1}_{\rm all\,pass}/f_m$ per original launch. Addressing all settings is folded into the randomization; the signed score can have large variance. For unrestricted priors, knowing the constant fully forced rate $Y(D)=2^{-m}$ still leaves every other deterministic-setting mean with a nonzero coefficient. Indeed the invertible transform maps the simplex of full-support laws onto a set with relative interior in that affine hyperplane. A small perturbation of any one other rate, with the rest fixed, changes the all-clear probability. The last copy can be included by extending the same setting-independent source by one switch and calibrating its first $m$ internal boundaries.

## A limit on statistical recovery

For $m\ge2$, compare the two exogenous laws uniform on even and odd internal-mask parity. They are disjoint. If any boundary is forced, averaging pairs of latent masks differing only there gives identical rates. At the unforced setting the difference in the two rates is $2^{2-m}f_m$. Their midpoint is the independent fair-copy probability; its generating function is $(1/2)/(\sqrt{1-z/2}-1/2)$, giving $S_m\sim(2/3)(2/3)^m$ from its leading pole. For the two Bernoulli success outcomes, the one-launch relative entropy is at most the squared rate difference divided by $p(1-p)$ for the comparison rate $p$. Since $f_m\sim(2\sqrt\pi m^{3/2})^{-1}$, this is $O(6^{-m}m^{-3})$. The likelihood chain rule, also for adaptively chosen settings on independently reinitialized trials, bounds the relative entropy after $N$ launches by $N$ times that amount. Pinsker's inequality therefore requires $\Omega(6^m m^3)$ launches for fixed separated size and power on this pair using only the declared Boolean outcome. Additional measured ports define another experiment. [Independent-copy survival generating function](Hidden_site_records_and_preparation_time.md)

When the question is how much a released walk may differ from the ideal, a single unforced rate is enough for a one-sided bound. At zero field any internal cut in $t\ge2$ switches has survival at most $a_1a_{t-1}$: merging adjacent segments increases the return probability and log-convexity of $a_j$ maximizes a two-piece product at an endpoint cut. The ratio to $a_t$ is $t/(2t-1)$, attained by one such cut. If $Y_t(\varnothing)\ge(1-\epsilon)a_t$, the probability of any internal copy is at most $(2t-1)\epsilon/(t-1)$. This bound is sharp up to the trivial cap one, by mixing the clear detector with a single endpoint copy. Taking $t=m+1$ includes the final $m$th detector action on a flight launch. Transfer from zero field to another gain schedule requires a common latent law. The field-on experiment in the companion note avoids this change of schedule.

## What the calibration assumes

At $m=3$ a device that always copies after the first switch has unforced survival $3/16$. A device choosing no copies with probability $1/3$ and both with probability $2/3$ gives the same rate. Their four forced rates in order $\varnothing,\{1\},\{2\},D$ are $(3/16,3/16,1/8,1/8)$ and $(3/16,7/48,7/48,1/8)$. An intervention-sensitive rival can use the second law only at the unforced setting and the first law elsewhere. It then agrees on the entire table while changing the latent unforced all-clear fraction from zero to $1/3$. A persistent fair counter also gives the same survival readings as copying at every boundary. The inferred law concerns copies inside the stated squared instrument; it cannot in general apportion a fully copied component and counting. For instrument dependence of quantum memory more generally, see [Taranto et al., *Quantum Markov Order*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.122.140401).

The exact checker uses signed rails and unread position tags. It tests all 255 masks for one through eight switches from both launch rails, 40 input laws and 57 cases of omitted settings. The Lean file proves factorization of the contrast, an integer-scaled range bound and inversion of the finite cumulative weights.
