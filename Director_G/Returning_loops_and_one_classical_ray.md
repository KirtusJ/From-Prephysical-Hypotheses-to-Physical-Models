# Preparing one accelerating ray with two returning paths

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A real direction filter on the signed force walk prepares a packet that can follow either of two accelerations. A second preparation, before the flight, can select one acceleration without measuring which side the walker later reaches. Split the packet into two paths, let them enclose opposite signed areas, and return them to their starting sites at the same tick on opposite coin rails. The relative phase becomes a coin polarization. Conjugating the field reverses both the effective force and the selected band curvature, so the two conjugate components move to the same side. The construction uses a calibrated relative field magnitude and specified coherent instruments. It leaves the choice of carrier, the record law and their physical origin open. ## A real circuit that gives the coin a relative phase

Use a line with rails $(R,L)$, the real Hadamard $H$, and a signed cyclic sheet of order $M$. Each tick adds the base departure coordinate to the sheet. In a chosen character the multiplier at site $x$ is $e^{-ihx}$. Start with any base packet entirely on rail $R$. The first tick uses $H$. Continue straight for $a-1$ ticks, turn both arms with $X$, then continue straight for $a-1$ ticks. The stream moves on every tick; the pulse schedule is independent of the starting site. From each initial site $x$, the words $R^aL^a$ and $L^aR^a$ both return to $x$. Their departure sums are $2ax+a^2$ and $2ax-a^2$ and their final rails are $L$ and $R$. Consequently a scalar input packet $v$ becomes, in $(R,L)$ order,

$$v(x)e^{-2iahx}e^{iha^2}\,
       (1,e^{-2iha^2})/\sqrt2. \tag{1}$$

There is no position projection or route record in this circuit. The relative-area comparison is invariant under common changes of sheet origin at the launch; the final coin phases are defined relative to the same coin convention used for the flight. An independent rephasing of the two final rails must also transform the subsequent Hadamard. The signed sheet operator, its action on a whole packet and reduction on a ring with $M$ dividing its circumference are checked in Lean. The Fourier and band arguments below use that operator. [Returning-loop proof](lean/LoopCoinPreparation.lean) · [Complete signed output rows](results/loop_coin_flight.json) · [Discrete gauge fields on walks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10850)

Choose $a$ nearest to $\sqrt{\pi/(4|h_{\rm ref}|)}$ for a small nonzero reference field. Then $2a^2|h_{\rm ref}|=\pi/2+O(\sqrt{|h_{\rm ref}|})$. With fundamental $|h_{\rm ref}|=2\pi/M$, any two $2a$-tick base-return words have relative area at most $2a^2$: the displacement at time $j$ is bounded by $\min(j,2a-j)$ and these bounds sum to $a^2$. Thus the leading cost $\sqrt{M/2}$ ticks is sharp for accumulating an unwrapped quarter turn with two such words. The two final rails are part of the intended output. If a single scalar endpoint on the same rail is required, a recombiner is additional.

There is a more accurate finite-order control. Choose an integer $d$ nearest to $M/8$. Choose an $R$-starting, $L$-ending return word of signed departure area $d$ and its complementary word of area $-d$. On the line, at $2a$ ticks the first word has attainable areas $a^2-2j$ for $0\le j\le(a-1)^2$: each interchange of adjacent middle steps changes the area by two, and all inversion counts in that range occur. The two words require the same turn/straight pulses. For sufficiently large $M$, $a=\sqrt{M/8}+O(1)$ suffices after matching parity. This gives angular error at most $2\pi/M$. Exact quadrature from two equal-time integer-area returns for the fundamental character requires and, permitting sufficient depth, admits $8\mid M$. The relative area is even; a quarter turn requires it to equal $\pm M/4\bmod M$. Different signs and modular wraps can give a better circular approximation than the nearest positive unwrapped area. The small-order records include all rounding ties and both objectives. [Pulse synthesis and modular controls](results/return_loop_control.json)

One may also preserve the original Hadamard at every streamed tick. Coherently copy the first outgoing rail to a private bit. At each later tick, read only whether the new direction is the prescribed multiple of that bit, retaining one relative word and its complement. On success the last rail is opposite the first and can clear the bit reversibly. The two retained histories have equal signed amplitudes up to a common sign: in the $2a-1$ internal transitions the number of turns is odd, hence the total number of stays is even, and switching $R,L$ leaves the parity of the $LL$ count unchanged. The initial $R$ split is positive on both rails. The joint success probability is $2^{1-2a}$, independent of the character and input packet because the final rails are orthogonal. A measurement of the actual rail values or a surviving private copy destroys the relative coin phase. The programmable-coin implementation has unit success conditional on its input; the fixed-coin implementation pays the displayed heralding cost and requires coherent relative-direction comparisons. ## Removing the momentum kick

Equation (1) shifts the initial base momentum by $-2ah_{\rm ref}$. A second schedule removes this shift exactly. Take $a=2b$ and the same two triangular return paths of length $4b$, but apply the departure gain with signs $-,+,+,-$ in the four successive blocks of $b$ ticks. The common $x$ coefficient cancels. On the right-first path the departure heights of the triangle have total $4b^2$ and the first and last blocks together contribute $b^2$. Its signed sum becomes $4b^2-2b^2=2b^2$. Its partner has the opposite sum. Thus the two outputs return to the same base packet with $L/R=e^{-4ih_{\rm ref}b^2}$ at every site. Choosing $b$ nearest to $\sqrt{\pi/(8|h_{\rm ref}|)}$ again produces a quarter turn asymptotically. This variant needs two field switches around the middle positive interval and a specified reference sign at its boundaries. ## From the coin to the trajectory

Let $\varepsilon=\delta\to0$, one base site represent $\delta^2$ in position, and one streamed tick represent $\delta^3$ in flight time. Put $h_{\rm ref}=s F_{\rm ref}\delta^4$ and $h_{\rm flight}=s F_{\rm flight}\delta^4$, where $s=\pm1$ indexes the conjugate sectors and $F_{\rm ref}\ne0$, $F_{\rm flight}$ are fixed real numbers. The signed $F$ is a potential slope; the positive-curvature classical force is $-F$. Begin with the previous coherent direction-only preparation using even $m\sim\delta^{-5/2}$ blocks from $R$, ending on $R$. Its two momentum peaks are at $\pm\pi/2+o(\delta)$ with scaled width $O(\delta^{1/4})$; the initial scaled position width tends to zero. One can keep the reference field on with constant sign throughout those preparation blocks, or alternate its sign. The latter has an exact profile at every finite order and was used in the finite flight calculations. Previously supplied preparation controls and yields enter the joint experiment. [Direction filter](Base_preparation_and_two_rays.md) · [Alternating-sign preparation and Hölder bound](Field_reversal_during_preparation.md)

For $k_*=-\pi/2$ the positive-real-part band of $U(k)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-ik},e^{ik})H$ has curvature $+1$ and eigenvector $(1,-i)/\sqrt2$. Its orthogonal vector has curvature $-1$. At the other peak $U(k+\pi)=-U(k)$, so the same spinor has the same dispersion curvature, with the spectral band name exchanged. After tuning the loop, the unwanted curvature has vanishing weight. Indeed band projections are continuously differentiable on both charts, the momentum concentrates at their edges, and the spinor in (1) approaches the matching edge eigenvector in norm. The common kick is $O(\delta^2)$ in lattice momentum, or $O(\delta)$ in scaled momentum, for the fixed-sign loop. It vanishes exactly for the compensated schedule. The loop acts without changing the position profile at its final time.

On each selected chart the earlier spinor estimate gives the envelope Hamiltonian $b_{\rm curv}p^2/2+sF_{\rm flight}Y$ with $b_{\rm curv}=\operatorname{sgn}(s F_{\rm ref})$. For a zero-centred packet the characteristic estimate of that paper makes its position converge weakly, at any fixed flight time $T$, to

$$Y(T)=-\operatorname{sgn}(F_{\rm ref})F_{\rm flight}T^2/2. \tag{2}$$

More explicitly, truncate each of the two momentum peaks on a fixed scaled-momentum chart as in the two-ray proof. The discarded norm tends to zero. The approximating chart envelope has vanishing deviations of $Y$ and $p$ from zero. Multiplication by the loop's spatial phase translates $p$ by the above known amount. Differentiating the smooth band projection adds $O(\varepsilon\delta)$ to the position-operator bound, while the orthogonal component has vanishing norm. The uniform spinor error on the chart tends to zero because $\delta^2/\varepsilon\to0$. For fixed $\xi$, the operator $e^{i\xi Y}$ translates lattice momentum by $O(\varepsilon\delta)$; for small $\delta$ the two compact chart supports do not overlap under that translation. Their characteristic functions both tend to $e^{i\xi Y(T)}$. The sheet trace sums the conjugate sectors with the same limit. This proves a single position law irrespective of their relative weights. It assumes those two effective field magnitudes and the stated projection and dynamics. It does not establish variance convergence from weak convergence. [Spinor and position estimates](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md) · [Related extended initial states](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0976)

Holding $F_{\rm ref}$ fixed and reversing only $F_{\rm flight}$ reverses the acceleration. Repreparing with both signs reversed gives the same acceleration as before. Erasing the phase by retaining orthogonal path labels gives equal curvature weights in the limit. Unmodified balanced history counting with no inter-path interference also fails to produce the selected Newtonian response. For a different bounded phase multiplier $r$ on the same device with a positive reference tuned to its fundamental unit, the limiting curvature weights at the negative edge are $(1\pm\sin(\pi r/2))/2$, and the possible positions are $\mp rF_{\rm flight}T^2/2$ when $r$ multiplies the fundamental field. For example, the $r=\pm3$ pair moves in the opposite direction and with three times the acceleration of the $r=\pm1$ pair. A simultaneous conjugation changes both signs and preserves the whole positional prediction. This formula uses bounded effective phases after the parity alias is removed; applying it to raw indices growing with $M$ without a chart estimate is unwarranted. [Matched field calibration and the aliases](Force_calibration_across_scales.md)

At $M=32768$ near $T=1.5$ the ideal negative position is $-1.12612$. The computed means were $-1.40679$ for the fixed-sign loop and $-1.10490$ for the compensated loop. Their target-curvature weights were 0.99853 and 0.99874. The fixed-sign position RMS about the ideal ray remained 0.68981, so a high band weight alone would be poor evidence of position concentration at that finite scale. A conjugation check at order 111 initially gave different curvature diagnostics by $1.27\times10^{-7}$. Splitting the two momentum-grid chart-boundary points evenly restores symmetry; the actual position laws agreed throughout. All such boundary weights and the individual off-target rows are retained. The two-arm excursion adds $O(\delta)$ physical time on this scaling. Its maximum **intermediate physical displacement stays of order one** because $a=O(\delta^{-2})$ and the site unit is $\delta^2$. The microscopic speed in these units is $1/\delta$; no scale-independent signal-speed bound follows. Previous direction filtering, route erasure, apparatus reach, sheet realization of the gain, and the empirical force and time standards still have to be accounted for. Electric quantum walks have been implemented with individual atoms under controlled pulses; the preparations and relative-stride tests here have not been identified in that experiment.
