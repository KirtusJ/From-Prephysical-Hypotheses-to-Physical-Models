# Which Tally Ring coins are changes of phase convention?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Cross-review by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

[Tally Ring version 0.1, first proposed attack](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/e4f8b728e659d7a8eee1e99bb7f9ace171883e0d/Director_Z/candidate/Z2_Tally_Ring/FORM.md) asks whether the single accessible number already classifies the balanced coins under phase symmetries. It does for the evaluated coins at fixed ring size. The physically allowed masks and fork are still needed to show that inhabitants can *measure* the invariant and at which tick. The unitary condition and squared-amplitude read remain stipulations T3 and T6. This finding addresses the candidate's claim to novelty and does not refute its predicted weights.

## The orbit of a sharp-start coin

Write $a,b,c,d$ for the **unit complex values of the character** on the four gains. The coin is $2^{-1/2}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&c\\ b&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and $ad=-bc$. Put $u=bc/a^2$. Multiplying the step by $a^{-1}$ and changing the relative phase of the R and L basis vectors by $D_R/D_L=a/c$ gives the canonical coin
$$2^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\u&-u\end{pmatrix}.$$
Indeed the R,L entry becomes $1$ and the L,R entry becomes $bc/a^2$. These transformations commute with the position masks up to the unitary basis change and leave the initially sharp coordinate and all terminal port projectors fixed. A uniform per-tick phase cancels in every squared terminal weight at that tick.

For any $N$th root of unity $\zeta$, multiply the state at site $x$ by $\zeta^x$. This is single-valued on the ring. After streaming, the R row picks up $\zeta$ and the L row picks up $\zeta^{-1}$. Removing the common phase $\zeta$ now acts on the canonical coin as $u\mapsto\zeta^{-2}u$. The squared $N$th roots form the entire group of $M$th roots, where $M=N/\gcd(N,2)$. Complex conjugation takes $u$ to $\bar u$. Real position projections and the sharp-start ray commute with conjugation. Every time, named site and rail can thus stay fixed. A reflection that permutes ports is unnecessary.

It follows that two balanced coins related by those transformations have the same $\operatorname{Re}(u^M)$. Conversely two unit complex numbers with equal real parts are equal or conjugate. If $\operatorname{Re}(u^M)=\operatorname{Re}((u')^M)$, conjugate one coin when needed. The ratio of the two remaining parameters is an $M$th root. A periodic ramp realizes it; undo the canonicalization to map one evaluated coin to the other. This gives all of D3, D5 and the effective-coin indistinguishability in D6 by symmetry. It does not identify the underlying groups. The explicit fork in D4 proves that inequivalent orbits actually give different readings with the allowed masks at tick $M+1$. The lower bound for earlier ports requires a separate argument about path lengths. Symmetry alone gives neither fact. [Mask theorem and fork proof](../Director_Z/papers/drafts/G10_subsection_what_masks_read.tex)

A smaller transformation group poses a different question. If each change of convention must belong to a fixed finite character image $\mu_L$, the available ramps satisfy $\zeta\in\mu_{\gcd(N,L)}$. For $N=4$ and an image consisting only of signs, every allowed ramp squares to one and conjugation fixes each sign. The coins with $u=1$ and $u=-1$ have identical $\operatorname{Re}(u^2)=1$ and remain distinct under the restricted changes of convention. A ramp with $\zeta=i$ maps one effective coin to the other. It changes the mathematical representation; the seven stipulations need not implement that ramp as an intervention. Equality of the observations is valid even in its absence.

Phase-gate invariance on the line and its breakdown on a cycle were previously studied for discrete-time walks. The calculation here classifies the residual periodic rephasings, conjugations and global phases for the stipulated equal-modulus coins, and checks the status of a specific claim about the candidate. [Banerjee et al., Phys. Rev. A 78, 052316](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.78.052316)

The [finite check](check_tally_ring_symmetry.py) uses integer root exponents to apply transformations to all four coin entries, compares the reachable classes to real-part equivalence for each submitted ring, and tests the restricted four-site case. Run from this directory using Python 3.9 or later.
