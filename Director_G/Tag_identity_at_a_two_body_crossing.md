# What a reversible tag identifies at a two-body crossing

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Reading the same position register at several times can distinguish passage from reversal in a *one-particle* two-ray flight. For two identical occupants, even a complete time sequence of positions records unordered pairs. The positions can be the same whether the incoming tags follow their ranks or change sides. On the program's three-mode occupation walk, two extensions of the native contact make different predictions for a tagged return, yet agree on every record of the original carrier. A positive bound transfers high-probability **occupation** statements from one of the tagged extensions to the original walk, including at a transverse crossing. ## One microscopic contact, two ways to transport a tag

Work on an open line with exactly two occupied scalar sites $x<y$. The occupation gates on adjacent pairs preserve the vacuum, act by the specified two-mode unitary on one occupant, and multiply the doubly occupied state by a phase $g$. The earlier determinant choice is $g=1$ for the scattering matrices below. Attach two internal states in **spatial order**, with $\alpha$ at the left occupied site and $\beta$ at the right. A hop into a neighboring empty site carries its occupant's internal state along. On a doubly occupied addressed bond compare the following reversible extensions:

- Apply $gI$ to the two tags. They remain attached to their spatial ranks.
- Apply $gS$, where $S(\alpha\otimes\beta)=\beta\otimes\alpha$. The adjacent occupants exchange their internal states.

Both implement exactly the original walk on common identical tags $\alpha\otimes\alpha$. This equivalence includes any sequence of previously specified occupation-only measurements. On a single occupied bond with orthogonal tags, the last gate can leave the occupation and all previous position records unchanged while exchanging the two final tag readings with certainty. The original two-state-per-site occupation carrier has no degree of freedom that decides which extension a physical tag follows. Internal transport, reversible preparation, coherent contact and a calibrated tag read are new apparatus assumptions. The contact SWAP exchanges *information* between neighbors. Its occurrence alone does not establish that any individual object passed through the other. Partial distinguishability in passive multiparticle networks also uses overlaps of internal states. The choices here are explicit local gates on the binary occupation walk. [Tichy, interference and internal-state overlaps](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.91.022316)

## Three forward layers expose exchange

Launch the native pair at $\{0,2\}$ with normalized separable tags $\alpha,\beta$. Let $\gamma=|\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle|^2$. For example either tag can be reversibly prepared from a blank internal mode before launch. On bonds $01,12,01$ apply the three native gates in order. Their diagonal and off-diagonal amplitudes are $(3,-4i)/5,(4,3i)/5,(3,-4i)/5$ respectively. The $g=1$ return to $\{0,2\}$ receives two amplitudes, $36/125$ on a history with no doubly occupied gate and $-80/125$ on a history with one. In the SWAP extension the return tag vector is
$$\frac{36\,\alpha\otimes\beta-80\,\beta\otimes\alpha}{125}.$$
The position return therefore has probability $(7696-5760\gamma)/15625$. The $I$ extension gives $1936/15625$ at every overlap. At $\gamma=0$ a resolved return in the SWAP extension has unswapped and swapped tag order with absolute probabilities $1296/15625$ and $6400/15625$ per launch. A changed tag can thus be read in this instrument even when the two final positions are exactly the launch pair. At $\gamma=1$ the occupation responses coincide. This is a measurement prediction conditional on the score and tag controls. If the initial internal states are not orthogonal, their two final assignments are not perfectly discriminable by a terminal internal read.

For the three possible occupied pairs listed as $\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\{1,2\}$, the original $g=1$ numerators are $(2025,1936,11664)$ and the $g=-1$ numerators $(2025,13456,144)$, all over 15625. The tag SWAP leaves the $\{0,1\}$ rate unchanged and transfers mass between the two other outcomes. Its total variation distance from the original three-bin law is exactly $(1-\gamma)1152/3125$. The return event alone attains this distance. Resolving the occupation at an intermediate time is a different experiment and can destroy the two-history cross term. ## An occupation-word transfer bound

The construction admits an exact decomposition at every record depth. On the tag space put $\Pi_\pm=(I\pm S)/2$. Every untagged gate or occupation-only pointer commutes with these projectors, and on their ranges the tagged contact acts by $+g$ and $-g$. For the initial tag product the weights are $\|\Pi_\pm(\alpha\otimes\beta)\|^2=(1\pm\gamma)/2$. Set $p=(1+\gamma)/2$. For **any whole occupation-record event** $E$, with the same pointer timetable and gates in both scalar walks,
$$P_{\rm tag}(E)=p P_g(E)+(1-p)P_{-g}(E).$$
Here the signs change every doubly occupied gate, while one-occupant gates are the same. To see this at the path level, write a record amplitude as $aI+bS$. Each tagged contact swaps the two coefficients and multiplies both by its scalar phase. Thus $a+b$ and $a-b$ are precisely the two scalar branch amplitudes. Their orthogonal tag sectors add when the tag is ignored. This remains true for an identical record-dependent control policy in the two sector walks when that policy acts only on occupation data. Initial spatial and tag preparations must factor with the tag in the same spatial-rank convention on every occupied pair. Postselecting a tag read can mix the two sectors and changes the calculation. For any such event $P_g(E)\le P_{\rm tag}(E)/p\le2P_{\rm tag}(E)$. In particular, if with high probability the **entire** tagged occupation word stays near the unordered classical trajectories, its untagged counterpart inherits that same property when the tag-insensitive event, controls and source satisfy the decomposition. The comparison remains meaningful for an orthogonal readable tag. The total variation between the two complete occupation-word laws is $(1-p)\,\mathrm{TV}(P_g,P_{-g})$. If the experiment selects a successful preparation $A$, let $Y_{\rm tag}=P_{\rm tag}(A)$ and $Y_g=P_g(A)>0$. Then
$$P_g(E\mid A)\le\frac{Y_{\rm tag}}{pY_g}\,P_{\rm tag}(E\mid A).$$
An increasing ratio of yields can spoil a conditional transfer even when the tagged conditional failure probability shrinks. The converse transfer from the original walk to a strongly tagged walk additionally needs control of the $-g$ sector; the three-layer data show a finite difference.

The earlier hard-point limit establishes concentration of the **sorted** two-particle positions at each specified time on the determinant occupation carrier. It does not supply a measured joint word for that two-body carrier. Ordered rank curves can be called the rebounding pair and the same unordered locations can be described by freely passing centers. If a four-read, tag-resolved word selects one continuation, the tag-insensitive pushforward records only the pairs of sites. It can transfer a bound on escape from those pairs under the stated conditions, but the event “the left launch's tag arrived on the right” has no outcome in that pushforward. Assigning the tagged secant impulse to a particle in the untagged system would require an independently specified persistent observable with the same identity and disturbance bounds through contact.
