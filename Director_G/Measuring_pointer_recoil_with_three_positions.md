# Measuring a position meter's recoil from three readings of one walk

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

An intermediate position reading can change the next position by an amount that depends on an unknown launch velocity and on the meter's impulse. Three positions on the same launch separate those contributions. Compare the speeds on either side of the middle reading. After correcting for the uniform force, their difference gives the **distribution** of the middle recoil in the one-band limit. The launch can have a broad momentum distribution and the two meters can be entangled. All recorded triples enter the comparison. [Preceding single-pointer result](One_position_pointer_and_the_classical_ray.md)

Braginsky and coauthors give the underlying free-mass three-position filter in §II.B.3 and Appendix A. The walk result here transfers the full filtered distribution through two invasive reads of a discrete, two-rail carrier and identifies the resolution limit in which it measures recoil. Benoist, Fraas and Fröhlich establish semiclassical tracking under repeated indirect detection in a different scaling. [Braginsky et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0109003) · [Benoist, Fraas and Fröhlich](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09558)

## What the three records measure

The carrier is the line walk in its prepared positive band with the calibrated Hamiltonian $P^2/2+FY$. Measure two translated pointers at attained times $t_0,t_1$ and the final native walker position at $t_2$. Write $h=t_1-t_0$, $k=t_2-t_1$, with both intervals tending to fixed positive values. A pointer couples instantaneously by $G(z_i)\mapsto G(z_i-Y)$ in physical position units. It then remains isolated. The position reads use the complete squared-coordinate instrument, with no selection or feedback. For each observed triple calculate

$$D=\frac{Y_2-Z_1}{k}-\frac{Z_1-Z_0}{h}+\tfrac12 F(h+k).$$

Represent the input pointers in dimensionless coordinates $U_i$ with $Q_i=-i\partial_{U_i}$, physical coordinate noise $N_i=w_iU_i$ and momentum $\Pi_i=(\varepsilon/w_i)Q_i$. Initially they have a common normalized profile $G(U_0,U_1)$ independent of the walker. The joint profile need not factor across the two meters. The position coupling leaves $Y$ fixed, adds $Y$ to the output pointer coordinate and changes the walker's $P$ to $P-\Pi_i$. In the quadratic envelope the characteristic function of $D$ is **exactly**

$$\langle G,\exp[iu\{N_0/h-(1/h+1/k)N_1-\Pi_1\}]G\rangle,\qquad u\in\mathbb R.$$

Indeed, let $X,P$ be the walker immediately before the first coupling. The three commuting records, pulled back to the input, are

$$Z_0=X+N_0,\quad X_1=X+h(P-\Pi_0)-Fh^2/2,\quad Z_1=X_1+N_1,$$
$$Y_2=X_1+k(P-\Pi_0-Fh-\Pi_1)-Fk^2/2.$$

Substitution gives the displayed characteristic function. Commutation follows also by reading the two isolated pointer coordinates and the walker coordinate at the end. These identities can first be read on Schwartz functions, then as identities of their Weyl exponentials for every normalizable input. The mass is in the walk's units. In physical units a velocity change must be multiplied by the calibrated mass to obtain momentum. The surviving middle meter quadrature contains noncommuting $N_1,\Pi_1$. Treating them as two independent ordinary errors can give an incorrect distribution at finite width. The integer polynomial identity after clearing denominators was checked separately. [Lean proof](lean/RecoilPositionFilter.lean)

Let $w_0,w_1\to0$, assume $\varepsilon/w_i$ stay bounded, and put $\varepsilon/w_1\to\lambda$. Strong continuity of Weyl translations and modulations now gives

$$D\ \Rightarrow\ -\lambda Q_1,\qquad
   \Pr(Q_1\in dq)=\left(\int|\widehat G(q_0,q)|^2\,dq_0\right)dq.$$

There are no moment assumptions. The observed histogram requires only the three positions, their clocks and the force correction. The Fourier density predicts what that histogram will approach for a stipulated translation instrument. Its comparison with counts tests the model. Using that same distribution on a different instrument, or inferring a pre-existing momentum kick correlated with the individual first read, requires further evidence.

## Transfer to the signed walk

Write site pitch $a=\varepsilon\delta$, tick duration $\varepsilon\delta^2$, and take $\varepsilon,\delta\to0$ with $\delta^2/\varepsilon\to0$. At $t_0$ require an encoded unit scalar wave in one oriented band supported in a common compact interval of scaled momentum, or a vanishing norm error from such waves. The envelope can vary with the scales, with arbitrary phase and spatial extent. Neither a known launch velocity nor concentration on one ray enters the following argument. [Uniform full-spinor propagation estimate](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md)

Fourier transform both pointers, initially truncating $|q_0|,|q_1|\le R$. The discarded norm goes to zero as $R$ grows. In each retained fibre the first and second translations multiply the actual lattice spinor by the site phases $\exp[-i(\varepsilon/w_i)q_iY/\varepsilon]$. The corresponding compact-band momentum shifts are bounded uniformly in the scales for fixed $R$. The old and newly shifted band sections differ by at most $2\delta|(\varepsilon/w_i)q_i|$ in norm at each such coupling. Between shifts use the full-spinor estimate on the enlarged chart, once per flight interval. Its error is uniform in the encoded input, and tends to zero there. These norm estimates integrate in the joint pointer variables even when their amplitudes have correlated phases. Intermediate pointer projections can be delayed until the end since each past meter is left untouched.

For the terminal exponential of the three recorded coordinates, the data factor translates scaled walker momentum by $u\varepsilon/k$, up to its sign convention for translation on functions. The two pointer factors are ordinary Weyl translations in $q_0,q_1$. On the truncated final state replace the smooth final spinor by its constant central value at norm cost $O_R(\delta)$. The resulting lattice expectation equals the continuum expectation in the common chart for all sufficiently small $\delta$, by the site-pitch Fourier shift. Only a bounded characteristic function is compared. Returning the omitted pointer tails costs at most twice the discarded norm in each experiment. Taking the scale limit first and then $R\to\infty$ proves the weak convergence. The proof allows mixed pointer preparations with a fixed density operator by linearity and trace-norm approximation. A first meter with unbounded $\varepsilon/w_0$ can leave the one-band chart even though its recoil cancels algebraically in the quadratic model.

## Two ambiguities and a finite check

* With only the middle pointer and a later position reading, a deterministic change of its recoil can be absorbed in the incident momentum. At the time of coupling replace the data by $\exp(i cY/\varepsilon)\psi(Y)$ and the pointer in physical coordinates by $\exp(i c z/\varepsilon)g_w(z)$. After the coupling the two data phases cancel exactly at every reported $z$, leaving the global scalar $\exp(i c z/\varepsilon)$. All subsequent data-position statistics conditioned on that read are identical, including those at different elapsed flight times on separate experiments. The trade is exact on the line and for a site phase on the lattice; a generic boost of a band-encoded lattice wave can require a vanishing band-section correction to implement within that preparation class. A preceding position read distinguishes two different launch momenta that persist through the first uninterrupted flight interval. If an external device delivers the compensating boost $+c$ immediately before the middle coupling, its data phase cancels the middle pointer phase at every pair of earlier and middle records, even after conditioning. All three records, and indeed all later position records, are then identical in the two designs. Inferring meter recoil alone requires excluding or independently calibrating such a time-local impulse.
* Force and timing matter. An unknown impulse at the second coupling enters the contrast with the same coefficient as the meter's recoil. If the value of $F$ used in the reduction is wrong by $\Delta F$, the result is displaced by $\Delta F(h+k)/2$. A common output offset cancels, while independently drifting read origins need calibration. Narrow bins suffice when all recorded positions differ from their tabulated values by bounds tending to zero and both time gaps stay positive. This result supplies a test of read disturbance relevant to P9 and P11 under the named controls. It leaves the physical origin of the readout law open.

The checker first enumerates all $7^3$ joint cells of each of twelve finite experiments with one correlated two-pointer state. Under the cyclic quadratic shear the modular second difference has the same histogram on three distinct sources. A unit phase winding of the middle pointer shifts that histogram by exactly minus one bin to floating precision. The bare one-tick coined walk gives a source-dependent histogram; two tested sources differ in total variation by 0.00744. This finite control prevents promoting the quadratic cancellation to an identity of the microscopic tick. A separate two-rail periodic calculation on both bands measures one harmonic of the three final coordinates at attained intervals, using two launch momenta and three refinements. It checks the filtered characteristic function against the finite-width quadrature and retains its discrepancy from the limiting impulse characteristic.
