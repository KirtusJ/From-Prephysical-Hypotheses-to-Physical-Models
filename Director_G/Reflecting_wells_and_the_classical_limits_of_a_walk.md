# Reflecting wells, a position-only probability test and two classical limits

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Six wells with reflecting ends can expose the three single-mode masses that the [reciprocal ring](A_reciprocal_sheet_connection_on_six_wells.md) hides from an eleven-tick well-only detector. The detector on the interval reads the final well directly. On larger versions of the same interval, keeping the walk coherent gives a mixture of specular classical flights at times proportional to its length. Recording position after every tick gives reflected diffusion at times proportional to the square of its length. The probability test and both limits use the same specified step. The limits assume ordinary probabilities for the records.

## The endpoint operation

Label wells \(0,\dots,L-1\) and rails \(R,L'\), with \(L'\) the left-going rail. Start at one well on a named rail. At every well and tick use the same real Hadamard coin \(H(r,l)=(r+l,r-l)/\sqrt2\). In the interior, shift each output one well along its named direction. At either endpoint an attempted step outside the interval stays there for that tick and reverses rail with amplitude \(+1\). The shift permutes the \(2L\) directed modes. A detector need only report the well. The endpoint residence time and reflection phase are part of the model. Elster et al. implemented an optical Hadamard walk with programmable transmission and reflection and studied three vertices through six steps. Their data concern a different graph schedule and do not measure the six-well histogram below. [Original experimental report, pp. 2–3](https://publicatio.bibl.u-szeged.hu/5961/1/srep13495_u.pdf)

At tick eleven a \(0,R\) launch gives the following complete table. The common signed amplitude divisor is \(\sqrt{2048}\). The final column counts all choices of outgoing directions at the eleven coins, including outward proposals that reflect.

For this test a launch may use any common finite exponent \(q\ge0\). Within each well it groups the occupied modes as it chooses, raises each group’s total squared mass to \(q\), and normalizes over the whole interval. Empty cells stay dark. The distribution of exponents and partitions may vary arbitrarily across launches. Additional alternatives select a maximal cell or count direction words fairly. At \(q=1\) the reported probabilities are the squared mass column divided by 2,048.

For full incident frequencies \(P_x\) write \(a=81,b=225,c=576\), \(\lambda=\log(c/b)/\log(c/a)\), and score \(D=\lambda P_0/a-P_3/b+(1-\lambda)P_4/c\). Each scored well has only one occupied rail. Multiplication by the positive power normalizer gives \(\lambda a^{q-1}+(1-\lambda)c^{q-1}-b^{q-1}\), nonnegative by weighted arithmetic–geometric mean, with equality precisely at \(q=1\). Fair counting gives positive score since \(\lambda>1/3\) and \(463/(3\cdot81)>341/225\). A maximal-cell detector always returns well 5, whose largest rail mass 676 exceeds every competing site total. Hence \(D=0\) and \(P_5=680/2048\) together eliminate every alternative in the stated mixture. A single phase error can invalidate the calculation. Replacing only the left wall’s reflection amplitude by \(-1\) gives site numerators \((121,145,5,425,1156,196)\) instead. Keep every incident trial and no-click outcome when comparing predictions with data. ## Unfolding the interval and its reflection phase

At a fixed clock, unfold the interval to one parity of a cycle of length \(2L\). The two lifts of \(x\) are \(j=x\) and \(j=2L-1-x\), which have opposite parity. Choose the parity of the initial lift \(j_0=x_0\), alternating it after every tick. On \(j<L\) retain the rail order. On \(j\ge L\) exchange the rails. Then an outward proposal at either wall advances on the cycle while leaving \(x\) unchanged. Each active parity has exactly one lift per well, with both rails. Counts follow this bijection of words for any \(L\).

The amplitudes require signs. Set \(h_j=1\) on the reflected half and zero elsewhere. In traveling-rail coordinates the reflected coin is \(XHX=-ZHZ\), where \(X\) exchanges rails and \(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\). Change the sign of the left component at sites with \(h_j=1\). Substitution in the coin-then-shift step gives the same Hadamard matrix at every site, followed by reciprocal link signs \((-1)^{h_j}\) on \(j\leftrightarrow j+1\). For example the right hop from \(j\) acquires the factor \((-1)^{h_j}\), and the left hop from \(j+1\) acquires the same factor. Multiply both components at \(j\) by \(u_j\), where \(u_0=1\) and \(u_{j+1}=u_j(-1)^{h_j}\) through \(j=2L-2\). Every link is then positive except possibly the wrap, whose sign is the product \((-1)^L\). The signed permutation gives an exact step-by-step equivalence between the interval walk and the corresponding alternating parity sectors. For even \(L\) the unfolded cycle is periodic. For odd \(L\) it carries one negative reciprocal edge. At \(L=3,t=4\) the reflecting-well numerators are \((2,1,13)\) over 16. Omitting the negative edge in the unfolded walk gives \((2,5,9)\) over 16.

In the six-well example, the old ring-with-sheet experiment and the reflecting interval have the same occupied twelve-cycle modes up to these permutations and signs. They have different physical reporting maps. An interval record \(x\) identifies its active lift at that clock. A six-ring record \(j\bmod6\) pools two occupied lifts at tick eleven. This explains why changing the boundary can replace the terminal sheet sensor. It does not recover a lost sheet label from pre-existing ring data.

## The coherent limit of the same step

Take even \(L\to\infty\), \(x_0/L\to y\in[0,1]\), and \(t/L\to\tau<\infty\). Use a sharp site and a fixed unit coin vector \(u\) in the first half. Write \(z_x=(x+1/2)/L\). Under ordinary weighting the final \(z_x\) converges in distribution to
\[
   F(y+\tau V), \qquad F(s)=\operatorname{dist}(s,2\mathbb Z).
\]
Here \(V\) is drawn by sampling momentum \(k\) uniformly on \([0,2\pi)\), then a band \(b\) with probability \(\langle u,P_b(k)u\rangle\). The velocity is \(v_b(k)=\omega'_b(k)\) for the eigenvalue \(e^{i\omega_b(k)}\) of \(U(k)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{ik},e^{-ik})H\). The fold \(F\) reverses macroscopic velocity at 0 and 1 and covers repeated wall encounters at every fixed \(\tau\). This is a weak limit of one-time position distributions. The infinite-line group-velocity theorem supplies the Fourier method. Periodic wrap on the growing finite graph is accounted for in the following calculation. [Grimmett, Janson and Scudo, *Weak limits for quantum random walks*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.69.026119)

For a fixed nonnegative integer \(m\), set \(\delta=\pi m/L\). On the unfolded cycle use the Fourier transform with positive sign and momenta \(k=\pi n/L\). The position mode \(\mathbb E e^{i\delta J_t}\) is \(e^{i\delta x_0}\) times the grid mean of \(u^\dagger U(k)^{*t}U(k+\delta)^t u\). The eigenphases can be chosen as \(\omega(k)=\arcsin(\sin k/\sqrt2)\) and \(\pi-\omega(k)\). The two bands stay separated, and their projectors and the first two phase derivatives are bounded and smooth. Cross terms contain \(P_b(k)P_c(k+\delta)=O(\delta)\) for \(b\ne c\). For a diagonal term Taylor expansion gives \(t[\omega_b(k+\delta)-\omega_b(k)]=t\delta v_b(k)+O(t\delta^2)\). The grid average thus tends to the stated uniform momentum integral. Exactly at finite \(L\), \(\cos(\pi m z_x)=\cos(\delta(j+1/2))\) at either lift, so taking real parts gives the moments of \(F(y+\tau V)\). Cosine polynomials are dense in continuous functions on the unit interval, proving weak convergence.

For the \(R\) launch \(v_\pm(k)=\pm\cos k/\sqrt{1+\cos^2 k}\) and the respective weights are \((1+v_\pm(k))/2\). Indeed \(U'=iZU\) gives \(v_b=\operatorname{tr}(ZP_b)\). The \(L'\) launch reverses the sign in the weight. A sharp launch has a spread of velocities. Concentrating on one macroscopic ray would require a different preparation. The calculation establishes neither a joint trajectory law under intermediate coherent observations nor a general Hamiltonian or physical time scale.

### Preparing one reflected ray

A single ray can also be prepared within the stipulated controls. Take a nonzero continuously differentiable bump \(g\) of compact support, \(0<y<1\), and widths \(w_L\to\infty\), \(w_L/L\to0\). On wells of the same parity as \(x_0\) prepare the normalized two-rail amplitudes
\[
   g((x-x_0)/w_L)\,e^{-ik_0x}u_b(k_0).
\]
Give all other sites zero amplitude. Here \(u_b(k_0)\) is a unit eigenvector of \(U(k_0)\). This preparation needs spatial phase coherence and a specified coin polarization. On the chosen source parity its unfolded support lies entirely in the first half and the physical rail order is unchanged.

Multiplication by the parity selector gives momentum peaks at \(k_0\) and \(k_0+\pi\). To see concentration without assuming a packet shape in momentum space, translate the envelope by two sites. After the carrier phase is removed, its norm difference is \(O(1/w_L)\) by the mean value bound and normalization. Parseval then bounds the spectral mean of \(|e^{2i(k-k_0)}-1|^2\) by \(O(w_L^{-2})\). Off any fixed neighborhoods of the two peaks this weight is bounded below. The same coin polarization selects matching branches at the two peaks because \(U(k+\pi)=-U(k)\). The eigenphase on the matching branch changes by a constant \(\pi\) locally, so both peaks have the same velocity \(v_0=v_b(k_0)\).

Apply the preceding fixed-\(m\) Fourier calculation with the initial momentum amplitudes at \(k\) and \(k+\delta\) in place of the constant source coin. Their summed product in absolute value is bounded by one using Cauchy–Schwarz with normalized grid measure. Bounded projector derivatives still give an \(O(\delta)\) cross-band error. Continuity near the two peaks removes the opposite-band contribution in the limit and replaces both diagonal phases by \(e^{i\pi m\tau v_0}\). The remaining initial shifted overlap is the initial position mode, tending to \(e^{i\pi m y}\). Thus the position law concentrates at \(F(y+\tau v_0)\) for each fixed \(\tau\), including wall contact. The result describes free motion with specular velocity reversal for this prepared band. Its proof permits packet dispersion on microscopic scales. Measuring a sequence of positions along the ray would be a different instrument.

The [packet replay](check_reflected_packet.py) uses a Gaussian of width \(\sqrt L\) sites, cut off by the ends of the interval, on the even wells around \(y=1/4\). Its exponentially small boundary tails give the same concentration and translation estimates as the compact bump. At \(k_0=\arccos(1/\sqrt3)\) choose the band of velocity \(+1/2\). The ray reaches the right wall at \(t/L=3/2\) and returns to \(5/8\) at \(9/4\). For \(L=64,128,256,512,1024\) the mean absolute distance from the wall at impact is respectively \(0.10492,0.07604,0.05385,0.03803,0.02687\) interval lengths. At \(L=1024\) and the return time the mean distance from \(5/8\) is \(0.02906\). Selecting the orthogonal coin instead gives velocity \(-1/2\) and a predicted return-time position of \(7/8\), with mean distance \(0.02905\) from that prediction. ## Recording the well after every tick

Start again on one well and rail. Conditional on every observed well history the rail is sharp: at an interior site the incoming direction is determined by the preceding well, and an endpoint repetition denotes an outward proposal and its reversal. From a sharp rail the next Hadamard sends equal squared weight to its two outputs. Recording the well after each shift therefore gives the fair Markov chain. Its interior moves are \(x\mapsto x\pm1\) with probability \(1/2\) each. At an endpoint half the weight stays and half moves inwards. Its eleven-tick numerators are exactly the direction-word column. Leaving the direction unobserved at the final read does not undo those earlier records.

For fixed \(m\), the chain takes \(\cos(\pi m z_x)\) to \(\cos(\pi m/L)\cos(\pi m z_x)\). The formula also holds at either endpoint by reflection about the half-grid wall. Thus at \(t/L^2\to s\) its \(m\)-th cosine moment tends to \(e^{-\pi^2m^2s/2}\cos(\pi m y)\). These are the moments of the heat kernel on \([0,1]\) with reflecting (Neumann) boundaries and diffusion coefficient \(1/2\). At ballistic times \(t=O(L)\) the recorded chain instead stays at \(y\) in the same weak limit. The different predictions depend on which records the apparatus actually writes. Neither stochastic diffusion nor the specular velocity ensemble by itself supplies the program’s full quantum and classical correspondence.
