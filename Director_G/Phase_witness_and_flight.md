# From a phase witness to the distribution of a fall

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

The two-well phase test has a shrinking separation between neighboring charges at high sheet order. For the designated unit winding this separation gives a sharp $M^{-4}$ scale in the **unamplified squared-deviation score**. A score smaller than this scale transfers the unit-winding prediction to the program’s real, rested-packet flight, provided the same generator and source distribution govern both devices. The preparation succeeds on a decreasing fraction of launches. Its specific direction record permits transfer at the same score scale even after conditioning. At the boundary scale a fixed zero-force population survives and changes the limiting fall. ## What the calibration identifies

Use the stipulated mixture at a known sheet order $M\ge4$. Its fair-count weight is $c$ and its coherent weights are $b_k$ for $\beta_k=e^{-2\pi i k/M}$. The weights sum to one including count. From a sharp right launch let $p_2,p_3$ be the calibrated right-rail probabilities on two wells at ticks two and three. All trials use their original exposures. Set $m_M=\cos(2\pi/M)$ and $x_k=\cos(2\pi k/M)$. The two-well result gives
$$W_M=2(1-p_3)-2m_M(2p_2-1)+m_M^2
=c(1+m_M^2)+\sum_k b_k(x_k-m_M)^2.$$
Use the true score or a justified upper confidence bound. A detector that observes different phase populations with different visibilities may report a reweighted score. Transfer below requires a bound for the **incident** population.

Call the mass outside $k=\pm1$, including count, $B_M$. The smallest folded-cosine separation from the unit pair is $\Delta_M=1-\cos(2\pi/M)=2\sin^2(\pi/M)$. Indeed the next folded angle after $2\pi/M$ is $4\pi/M$ and its separation is $2\sin(3\pi/M)\sin(\pi/M)\ge\Delta_M$ for $M\ge4$. The remaining folded cosines are no larger. The zero winding attains $\Delta_M$. Also $1+m_M^2\ge\Delta_M^2$ since $m_M\ge0$. Therefore
$$B_M\le \min(1,W_M/\Delta_M^2),\qquad
\Delta_M^2\sim 4\pi^4/M^4. \tag{1}$$
An upper bound $W_M=o(M^{-4})$ suffices for $B_M\to0$. The scale refers to accuracy of the combined score. Using separate absolute error bars $e_2,e_3$ on the two probabilities adds $4|m_M|e_2+2e_3$ to its upper bound. This observation makes no optimal claim about statistical cost or other experiments that deliberately amplify the phase.

## How many unamplified launches distinguish a fixed amount of contamination?

Now restrict the available data to fresh independent launches, each reporting the right/left bit at **one** of the two clocks. The null has all its weight on $k=\pm1$. The alternative has $B_M\ge\eta$ for a preassigned $0<\eta\le1$. Fix a maximum error probability $\alpha<1/2$ for each hypothesis. The number of original launches needed in the worst case is of order $M^8$ at fixed $\alpha,\eta$. This sampling statement concerns an ideal recorded rail at a known clock; intermediate measurements, applied phase powers and other readouts give different experiments.

For $M\ge5$ let $\theta=2\pi/M$, $a=1-\cos\theta$, $b=\cos\theta-\cos(2\theta)$. Both are positive. The distribution placing masses $b/(a+b)$ at $k=0$ and $a/(a+b)$ at $k=2$ has mean cosine exactly $m_M$ and variance $ab$. Its mean is the weighted interpolation of $m_M+a$ and $m_M-b$. Its second centered moment is $(ba^2+ab^2)/(a+b)$. Combine it with the null in proportions $\eta,1-\eta$. The alternatives and the null then have **identical** tick-two probabilities. Their tick-three probabilities differ by $\eta ab/2$, with the alternative smaller. Here $a\sim\theta^2/2$, $b\sim3\theta^2/2$, and $ab/2\sim6\pi^4/M^4$. Both tick-three probabilities tend to $1/2$.

Let $p,q$ be the null and alternative tick-three probabilities. For sufficiently large $M$, $q(1-q)\ge1/8$. The inequality $\log z\le z-1$ gives the one-bit relative entropy $\mathrm{kl}(p,q)\le(p-q)^2/[q(1-q)]\le2\eta^2 a^2b^2$. If the next clock is chosen using the actual previous outcomes and private randomization, the same policy occurs under both hypotheses. Its conditional probabilities cancel in the transcript likelihood ratio. Each realized tick-two choice contributes zero expected log likelihood and each tick-three choice contributes $\mathrm{kl}(p,q)$ under the null. Thus the transcript divergence is at most $2\eta^2 a^2b^2\,\mathbb E_0 N_3$, also for an almost surely terminating policy with finite expected launch count. Data processing down to its terminal decision gives divergence at least $\mathrm{kl}(1-\alpha,\alpha)$. Consequently $\mathbb E_0 N\ge \mathrm{kl}(1-\alpha,\alpha)/(2\eta^2a^2b^2)$. This lower bound applies to adaptive clock allocation as well as a fixed budget. [Kaufmann, Cappé and Garivier, change of measure for stopped sampling, Lemma 1](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v17/kaufman16a.html)

For the converse, the null has $W_M=0$ and the entire alternative has $W_M\ge g=\eta\Delta_M^2$. Use equal fixed sample sizes at both clocks and threshold $\widehat W_M$ at $g/2$. If both absolute frequency errors are strictly below $g/12$, their contribution to $|\widehat W_M-W_M|$ is strictly below $g/2$ because $4|m_M|+2\le6$. Taking $N_j=\lceil72g^{-2}\log(4/\alpha)\rceil$ independent launches per clock makes the probability of a larger or equal error at either clock at most $\alpha$ by the binomial Hoeffding bound. Since $g^{-2}$ is of order $\eta^{-2}M^8$, this proves the matching power of $M$. The constants of the lower and upper bounds need not coincide. A comparison against the zero-winding sector alone can be easier because its tick-two probability shifts too. [Hoeffding, 1963](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830)

## Transporting the generator

The intended flight on $4M$ wells adds $g(y)=\lfloor y/2\rfloor\bmod M$ at every **departure**, before a real signed Hadamard step. The diagnostic applied the same cyclic generator on each left arrival. The two protocols need a common assignment from a physical increment to $\beta_k$ and the same distribution of count and character laws before preparation. An independent random allocation of identically prepared launches between the protocols implements the comparison if this assignment and distribution are stable. An unknown remapping of sheet labels between protocols can turn unit diagnostic charge into any flight charge. Comparing two independently tuned generators leaves (1) a statement about the diagnostic only.

On occupied parity $p=(y\bmod2)$,
$$\beta_k^{g(y)}=e^{ih_k p} e^{-ih_k y},\qquad h_k=\pi k/M.$$
The extra scalar is common to that slice and factors through all rail projections. The equality holds across the $4M$ seam and for a sharp launch all histories at any given tick have one spatial parity. Retain relative parity phases if preparing both parities coherently. By contrast, putting $\beta$ on left arrivals alone on a **line** gives the same factor $\beta^{(t+y_0-y)/2}$ for every word from $y_0$ to a final well $y$. Even adding a separate fixed scalar potential to that line step leaves this an endpoint gauge in sharp-source position and rail records. Observing a ring holonomy therefore supplies no independent derivation of the sheet staircase, physical force unit or Hamiltonian.

With a real source and real controls, global conjugation sends every sector-$k$ path amplitude to its sector-$-k$ conjugate. Real rail projections, terminal positions and real classical decisions on stored outcomes preserve this identity. If $Q$ is the unit sector’s probability law for these records, then $P=(1-B_M)Q+B_MQ_{\rm other}$ for some probability law on all reported outcomes, hence $\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le B_M$ at *every* clock. Independent complex controls or a fixed complex source require a separate comparison. For example, start the two-well diagnostic on rail $R$ and apply a known $i$ phase on $L$ just after the first step. The second right-rail amplitude is $(1+i\beta)/2$. At $\beta=\pm i$ the probabilities are respectively zero and one. Both sectors give the same unmodified two-well score. The added quadrature reference can distinguish their orientations.

## Conditioning on the complete preparation record

Start on $(0,R)$ and retain the alternating string of $r$ switches: after each pair of ticks, require the opposite rail to the rail at the beginning of the pair. Position and sheet remain unresolved. Let $A_k$ be the probability of this entire word in sector $k$, $a=A_1=A_{-1}$, and $A_C=2^{-r}$ in the fair-count model. Only the second direction in a pair is prescribed. Counting both directions as prescribed would give the wrong survival denominator. For even $r$ with $2r<4M$, the exact staircase word gives
$$A_k=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\prod_{j=1}^r \sin^2(q+(2j-1)h_k)\frac{dq}{2\pi}
\le A_0=\binom{2r}{r}4^{-r}.$$
The ring average agrees with the integral in this degree range. Hölder's inequality gives the comparison with $A_0$. For counting, $\binom{2r}{r}=\sum_j\binom rj^2\ge 2^r$ gives $A_C\le A_0$. These estimates are properties of this preparation. Other rare records can increase an incident error by an arbitrarily large factor. [Word amplitude, ring normalization and Hölder bound](Same_flight_sheet_selection.md) · [Earlier conditioning estimate for the uniform force](Force_calibration_across_scales.md)

The unnormalized competing accepted mass is
$$L=c2^{-r}+\sum_{k\ne\pm1}b_k A_k\le B_M A_0.$$
When $(1-B_M)a>0$ the fraction of accepted trials in competing sectors is exactly $L/[(1-B_M)a+L]$. Thus **every subsequent real flight record on the accepted ensemble** differs in total variation from the unit flight by at most
$$\frac{B_M A_0}{(1-B_M)a+B_M A_0}. \tag{2}$$
Conditioning here keeps all histories with the complete prescribed rail record and uses the physically correct acceptance rate for each law.

Fix a common calibration $F_1>0$ and set $\delta=(\pi/(MF_1))^{1/4}$, $\varepsilon=\delta$, $\Delta Y=\delta^2$, $\Delta t=\delta^3$. Choose $r$ nearest even to $\delta^{-5/2}$, followed by $n$ unfiltered ticks with $n\delta^3\to T>0$. Then $2r+n=o(M)$. The previous unit preparation theorem gives $a\sim A_0\sim1/\sqrt{\pi r}$, so (1) and (2) give vanishing conditional contamination when $W_M=o(M^{-4})$. The actual measured position relative to the prepared origin consequently has the same weak limit
$$\tfrac12\delta_{-F_1T^2/2}+\tfrac12\delta_{+F_1T^2/2}.$$
This is the distribution at one specified elapsed time. The finite ensemble has two band curvatures, and concentration at a single signed acceleration requires a further band preparation. The bound transfers bounded observations and convergence in distribution; moment convergence requires its own tail estimate. [Prepared two-ray theorem and its conditions](Base_preparation_and_two_rays.md)

The scale in (1) is sharp for an inference from this score alone without further restrictions on the populations. Give $k=0$ a fixed weight $w\in(0,1)$ and put the rest at $k=1$. Then $W_M=w\Delta_M^2$ and the post-preparation zero-charge mass tends to $w$ since $A_0/a\to1$. Its field-free rested component tends to a point mass at zero. The limiting flight has this additional mass at zero and unit rays of weight $(1-w)/2$ each. A source that is allowed to vary with $M$ can pass a weaker accuracy criterion while retaining this discrepancy. Count and the zero-charge coherent sector both have zero-radius limits in this flight; the score penalizes them differently.

## Which Schrödinger prediction is transferable?

The earlier plus-band theorem starts with a separately supplied common orientation, one accepted spectral band, normalized envelope supported in $|p|\le P$, centre $k_*=-\pi/2$, and the known parity/carrier demodulation where applicable. In its **line uniform-force geometry** choose $\Delta t=\varepsilon\delta^2$, $\Delta Y=\varepsilon\delta$, and an oriented phase gradient $h=F\varepsilon\delta^3$. For fixed bounded time $t=n\Delta t$, set $J=P+|F|t$ and require $\delta J\le1$. The spinor error relative to $i\varepsilon\partial_t\psi=-(\varepsilon^2/2)\partial_Y^2\psi+FY\psi$ is bounded by
$$E(t)=2|F|t\delta+t\delta^2J^4/(6\varepsilon)+t|F|J\delta^2/2.$$
For fixed $P,F,t$ this vanishes at $\varepsilon=1$, or at shrinking $\varepsilon$ with $\delta^2/\varepsilon\to0$. For an initially encoded smooth coherent packet concentrated at $(Y_0,p_0)$ in the theorem’s shrinking-action regime the position marginal concentrates at $Y_0+p_0t-Ft^2/2$. Neither the compact plus-band encoding nor its orientation follows from the two-well terminal frequencies. The analytic estimate was made in a momentum chart on the line. Applying it to a finite ring or to both initial spatial parities requires a justified encoding and seam/parity comparison. The preceding *localized two-ray preparation* supplies its own finite-ring comparison. [Encoded spinor estimate and point-packet hypotheses](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md)

For a deployment that **does** transport the same calibrated generator, has the specified oriented unit sector and actually matches this line geometry, the unit-sector probability error for any common effect is at most $E(t)$ by the trace-distance bound for normalized pure states. Convexity adds at most the weight outside that oriented sector. For a declared signed mixture on one fixed externally oriented plus-band packet the two unit signs generally correspond to different uniform gradients. Its conditional prediction is the corresponding mixture of the $+F$ and $-F$ responses with their own demodulations and error bounds. The folded witness can bound the other sectors and count. It cannot supply the relative weights or convert that mixed probability statement into a spinor close to one oriented Schrödinger solution. Quantum probabilities, the particular unitary step and the physical clock and length assignment are premises throughout. [Envelope construction in a coined walk, de Valcárcel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0976)
