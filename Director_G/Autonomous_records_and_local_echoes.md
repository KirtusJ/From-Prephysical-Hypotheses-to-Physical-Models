## One fixed step and one calibration table

Write the physically resolved signed counts at the target as $x_i$, where $S=\sum_i x_i^2=2^t$ after $t$ balanced steps from a localized coordinate. The normalized vector $x/\sqrt S$ describes amplitudes. Denote by $p_i$ the unknown probability of reading coordinate $i$ at that phase of the undisturbed preparation. A coherent program can carry this vector forward without reading it. Sampling $p_i$ directly means reading at this phase on an identically prepared launch. A single destructive trajectory does not sample all intermediate marginals.

Use $K=2^k$ tags, a data coordinate and, where needed, a private bit for idle wires. Begin with the target in tag zero and all other tags blank. Repeat the balanced mixing of each occupied tag cell with its own blank copy, doubling the number of tags at each round. Suppose the mass of a two-port component is locally conserved and a balanced splitter with only one occupied input divides that mass equally. These hypotheses apply for each such cell even when there are other occupied components elsewhere. After $k$ rounds each copy of cell $i$ has probability $p_i/K$. Consequently each tag has total $1/K$ by ordinary normalization. Marginal fairness of a separate setting bit would give only the tag totals, which are insufficient to establish the per-cell statement.

Hold the target tag fixed, including its private wires. In other tags install reference rows of norm $S$. Include a row of $S$ unit entries and for each needed signed numerator $n$ a row with one $n$ and $S-n^2$ unit entries. Padding and extra tags are allowed, including all-negative reference rows as a way of checking the two signs independently. Scale all rows by $1/\sqrt{KS}$. The construction applies to either sign of $n$, including a negative unit. If $n=0$ the port is handled by the sharp-zero argument below. Each active row is obtainable by converting $x$ to a localized input and preparing the desired row, because same-parity pairing reduces the squared integer norm by two at each normalized Hadamard layer. A common depth $2t$ suffices for the conversion, counting the mathematical matching layers; the signed middle permutation can be included in a matching. An even number of idle Hadamards on a private bit returns that bit to blank. These are synchronized transformations on disjoint tag blocks. The protected tag and target cell probabilities are conserved by the physical wire construction below. [Column synthesis and finite references](Protected_records_and_coordinate_weights.md)

An autonomous clock for a finite list of steps $V_0,\ldots,V_{L-1}$ is the one time-independent operator
$$
 W(|j\rangle\otimes v)=|j+1\bmod L\rangle\otimes V_jv .
$$
For the forward schedule take the actual target preparation, tag splits and reference conversions. Appending the adjoints in reverse order closes the orbit, with no intermediate measurement. The product around the full loop is the identity on the complete bank; its cyclic shifts are the identity by invertibility. Signed matchings give every vertex two incoming and two outgoing arcs of magnitude $1/\sqrt2$. The clock changes once per tick in a fixed direction. Its transition table programs the apparatus; no external decision between two calibration routes is made during the run. A different target can require another finite graph. Completion of a cycle is a statement about the unmeasured amplitudes, not a postulate identifying readout probabilities before and after it.

If equal **signed** entries of the simultaneous table have equal probabilities, write $q(n)$ for their common probability at this one phase. Every unit cell in the positive reference has $q(1)=1/(KS)$. The other reference rows have total $1/K$, so $q(n)+(S-n^2)q(1)=1/K$ and $q(n)=n^2/(KS)$. The protected target has $q(x_i)=p_i/K$. Thus
$$
                             p_i=x_i^2/S .
$$
No equality is needed between two measurements made at different clock phases just because their visible amplitude vectors happen to coincide. The next sections replace the full-table equal-entry assumption by local tests. ## Conservation along the actual edges

For two consecutive unmeasured record marginals $p,p'$ assume a nonnegative array $F_{uv}$ on the actual directed edges, with row sums $p_u$ and column sums $p'_v$. The array need not be unique. It may depend on the coherent state and the clock phase. Geometric proximity alone is weaker than support on the specified edges. If membership in $A$ at the input agrees with membership in $A'$ at the output along every supported edge, summing the same array over those edges gives $p(A)=p'(A')$. This argument iterates over changing masks and successive, separately chosen arrays. For a complete two-input, two-output balanced block the coupling exists exactly when the input and output totals agree. The product $p_u p'_v/m$ on that block is one choice for total $m>0$; the zero-total block uses the zero array. The squared amplitudes satisfy this conservation law by orthogonality. A general unitary-locality existence theorem for the squared rule and related stochastic mappings are available in the literature. Here edge support is used in the converse argument. [Mister, Arayathel and Short](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08569) · [Aaronson](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0408035)

A local implementation arranges logical wires in a line, with a private two-valued routing coordinate on each wire. To exchange adjacent wires by a classical permutation $P$, one balanced tick applies $P H_r$ and the next applies $H_r$ to every wire. Here $H_r$ mixes the two private values and commutes with $P$. Every first-tick edge out of wire $u$ enters $P(u)$, and the private bit is restored after the second tick. Thus a wire's full marginal is transported individually, even during the exchange. Route a chosen matching to adjacent slots, apply the ordered balanced gate, and restore the wire layout. At a logical gate, the union of both input wires of a pair stays in its output union; an idle data wire can be mixed only on its private bit. The masks used for the target, tags and copied settings obey the edge-membership condition at every such microstep. Unlike the preceding two-orientation router, this construction requires a private balanced port and an adjacent wire switch; it avoids assuming persistence through a routing block whose internal edges leave the mask. The geometry here is the computational wire line. Identifying it with particle position or microscopic spacetime is a further requirement. [Compiled line and all matching columns](check_causal_wires.py) · [Local edge and moving-mask checks](results/causal_wires.json)

One need not assume a probability of zero for every vanishing table entry. Start with a sharp localized source. A zero-amplitude output from two zero inputs has probability zero by local conservation. A nonzero single input to a balanced pair gives two nonzero amplitudes. With two nonzero inputs, a zero output is a destructive port; its zero probability is precisely the local dark-port assumption. Induction over the signed and permuted balanced steps now covers every empty coordinate. Forward equal splitting requires a local calibration for a single occupied amplitude and a dark input. If the component has zero probability both output probabilities are zero by conservation. No division by a zero total is used.

## From a two-port echo to equality of two old records

Compare two scalar cells with equal nonzero signed amplitudes. Route their wires together and apply $H$. One output is dark and the single bright output carries their entire mass $m=p_a+p_b$. Opposite amplitudes interchange the bright and dark ports. A further $H$ restores the original **amplitudes** and, by the forward fair response to a single occupied input, gives each final cell probability $m/2$. There are two distinct sufficient assumptions for the inference that the *original* cells also had probability $m/2$.

- A local reciprocal-flow law says that on this convergent comparison, the row of the reversed joint array from the bright port returns equal mass to its two inputs. For $m>0$ its entries are necessarily $p_a$ and $p_b$ because the other column is zero. Reverse fair splitting therefore forces $p_a=p_b$. When $m=0$ equality already follows. The premise concerns the transpose of the **same** joint array. The existence of some separately prepared fair reverse experiment does not identify its array with this transpose.
- A forward echo law says that the direct marginals of the two named input cells survive the complete local $H,H$ echo, after restoring their wires. This plus forward single-arm fairness gives the same conclusion. Requiring only preservation of the pair's sum adds no condition. If the pulse is compared with a separately chosen idle run, identifying their prefix probabilities is an operational assumption. Within the fixed clock schedule one may instead require invariance from the pre-probe cells to their corresponding post-echo cells on that occurrence. The echo hypothesis need only apply to the equal-amplitude pairs, on the clean ports used for calibration.

Within one nonempty two-port component with squared-rule marginals, a compatible joint array is the product of its normalized input and output marginals times the component total. On a bright/dark test it satisfies reverse fairness, and on a single-arm splitter it satisfies forward fairness. An isolated fringe test can check input/output sign conventions and sharp endpoints while still allowing nonlinear responses. For example, $s=(a^2-b^2)/(a^2+b^2)$ and $u=2ab/(a^2+b^2)$ interchange under $H$. A two-mode assignment $P_0=(1+G(s))/2$ before the coin and $(1+G(u))/2$ after it restores its two-mode probabilities under $H^2$ for any odd $G:[-1,1]\to[-1,1]$ with $G(1)=1$. The quartic choice gives $G(s)=2s/(1+s^2)$. Regrouping modes that have interfered with *different* partners is what makes the full-circuit echo discriminating. [Isolated pair sign and restoration controls](results/causal_wires.json)

The local rules have a clear scalar scope. Equality of two **sums** over an unresolved internal register need not produce one dark port. The apparatus must route the individual complete cells or ensure equality of the two full internal vectors (up to a common sign). The private routing bit is uncomputed at the probe. The Lean proof gives the two local implications separately. [Local echo and moving-mask formal statements](lean/AutonomousRecords.lean)

There is one fixed downstream setting construction even if the table has many thousands of equal-cell pairs. Split **each** table cell against its own blank setting bit with a fair $H$, after the preparation being tested. Each child has half its parent cell's probability. Sort the members of each class of equal signed nonzero amplitudes in a prescribed coordinate order, using the known target and schedule. Connect successive members in a path. Put its first, third and subsequent odd-numbered edges in one setting, its even-numbered edges in the other. Different classes are disjoint, and each setting is a matching on its participating cells. Idle unpaired wires preserve their total individually. Apply the local comparison to all assigned pairs in each setting. By reverse fairness, or by the local echo test, every joined pair of children has equal original probabilities. Fair duplication transfers this equality back to its parents. Chaining the edges equalizes the entire class. Singleton classes impose no comparison. One additional coherent setting bit suffices at any finite size. It is not a hidden assumption that a marginally fair setting is independent of the data; the conditional per-cell split is an operation in the graph.

The two settings need not single out a favored Fourier embedding or rely on comparing square roots. They pair exactly equal *signed integers* in a known preparation program. All required control tables can be computed before the run. When the target's amplitudes are themselves unknown to the designer, a different finite resource gives a choice-independent construction: enumerate all candidate pairings of coordinates up front. Using disjoint matchings that cover every unordered pair, and testing the dark/equal implication only on pairs that meet its amplitude hypothesis, suffices. For $D$ cells, pad to even $D'$ if necessary and use the $D'-1$ rounds of the round-robin pairing schedule: write $D'=2h$ and use labels $\infty$ and $\mathbb Z/(2h-1)$. In round $s$ pair $\infty$ with $s$, and pair $s+j$ with $s-j$ for $1\le j<h$. The round of any two finite labels is their half-sum modulo $2h-1$. Every unordered pair occurs once. Pad the setting count to a power of two and split it by successive fair balanced gates. This eliminates the need to identify equal-cell *locations* before running the comparison. To include references for an unknown target of known depth, install one reference for each possible signed integer $n$ with $n^2\le S$. Converting an *unknown* $x$ by $U_x^{-1}$ would still require its preparation circuit. For the theorem here, any specified native sequence from a localized source comes with its reversible preparation word, even if its counts have not yet been calculated.

For the two-setting construction based on known classes, there is a finite discrepancy bound. Write $b_a$ for the mass of each original table cell. On an equal-amplitude pair $a,b$ in one setting the component begins with $(b_a/2,b_b/2)$ and finishes with two copies of $(b_a+b_b)/4$. If unpaired cells retain their masses, the total variation distance between the complete resolved pre- and post-echo records, on the same wire and setting labels, is
$$
         D_{\rm echo}=\tfrac14\sum_{(a,b)\text{ in the class paths}}|b_a-b_b|.
$$
Consequently a measured upper bound $D_{\rm echo}\le\delta$ bounds the largest within-class spread by $4\delta$. Interpreting empirical frequencies this way requires the equivalent launches and a statistical bound for the population distance. For the round-robin version with $L$ equally weighted settings, a tested equal pair contributes $|b_a-b_b|/(2L)$ to resolved total variation, and unequal pairs can also move. The two-setting factor does not transfer unchanged. A three-cell control gives $1/12$ with its setting recorded and $1/24$ after the setting is forgotten. Reporting only the bright/dark marginal can also hide discrepancies.

## A periodic countermodel and a discriminating read

The squared-coordinate assignment is consistent with all of these local rules. To see the extra content of reciprocity, keep the quantum signed amplitudes, but distribute each incoming pair's *actual total* $m$ over its two outputs in proportion to $|a+b|^4$ and $|a-b|^4$. Empty pairs pass zero weight onward. This is a context-dependent evolution of marginal weights. It has a sharp dark port, a fair split of a single input and an edge-supported coupling; the law is covariant under the corresponding local signs and output exchanges. It can be exactly periodic on an autonomous clock from a sharp source. It need not respect the reciprocal or forward echo premise. Simply normalizing fourth powers across the whole bank creates a different countermodel: it respects equal entries but can violate even pair conservation, changing a protected mass from $1/2$ to $4/5$ in the line check. Using the same amplitude-blind Markov kernel for all preparations also fails: $(1,1)/\sqrt2$ and $(1,-1)/\sqrt2$ have the same input cell probabilities but concentrate in different output cells after the same $H$. [Signed and stochastic controls](results/causal_wires.json)

In the line implementation two named wires have identical signed amplitudes $+1/4$ after 216 microticks from a localized source. Under the squared rule the input masses on wires 3 and 7 are $(1/16,1/16)$. The intermediate bright/dark pair is $(1/8,0)$ and the echo restores $(1/16,1/16)$. Under the pair-local fourth-power rule the same wires have $(5/656,1/16)$. The bright port takes $23/328$, and each post-echo cell gets $23/656$. The idle and echo outputs differ by $9/328$ in total variation under this rule. Both alternatives take 230 ticks from the common launch and are the exact same amplitude map on all 16 physical modes. The opposite-sign example also separates the models. The single-occupied and empty pair controls restore their probabilities; an unequal-amplitude control gives a larger discrepancy that lies outside the equal-pair premise. Every read, including a failed discrimination on an earlier 186-tick schedule, is retained. These predictions specify a finite model-selection test. Count only whether the reported output is wire 3, using the same resolved wire definition at the common final tick in the two settings. Take equal numbers $n$ of independent, identically prepared launches at each setting. With the specified source the squared law predicts an echo-minus-idle difference of zero. The pair-local quartic law predicts $d=9/328$ on that wire. Classify at the midpoint $d/2$. If each sample proportion is strictly within $d/4$ of its own prediction the decision is correct. Hoeffding’s inequality and a union bound give an error bound $4\exp(-n d^2/8)$ for this binary decision, allowing equality at the cutoff among the failure events. Thus $n\ge\lceil 8\log(4/\alpha)/d^2\rceil$ launches per setting suffice for error at most $\alpha$ under either specified law. At the five-percent level this conservative sufficient value is 46,562 launches per setting. This is a sufficient statistical budget conditional on the ideal source, detectors, clock and independence. Loss, systematic drift or uncalibrated port errors need their own treatment. No experimental launches were made. [Hoeffding’s bounded-sum inequality](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830)

## Does microscopic time reversal imply reverse fairness?

For a prescribed finite phase orbit with rational record marginals, an explicitly reversible fine-grained model can realize even the asymmetric counterexample. For each balanced input/output block choose a nonnegative supported coupling with the required row and column sums. Within a two-by-two block one can fill the smaller remaining row or column amount at each step. If all marginals are multiples of $1/D$, so is every entry of this coupling. Choose $D$ as the least common multiple of the denominators over the entire periodic table. At each phase partition the $D$ microstates into port fibers of sizes $Dp_u$. Divide each fiber into subintervals of lengths $DF_{uv}$ and match these intervals bijectively to the corresponding pieces of the destination fibers. This gives a bijection from each phase to its successor, hence a permutation $P$ on the phase-labeled states. The period of the observed orbit need not be the period of each individual microstate. The longest hidden cycle in the quartic example takes 6,467 complete clock laps. The fibers at a given clock phase are fixed named observables.

Double the orientations and use $P$ on the forward sheet and $P^{-1}$ on the backward sheet. The involution $J$ exchanging the sheets obeys $J V J=V^{-1}$ for this enlarged dynamics $V$. Uniform counting on the doubled space is invariant under both $J$ and $V$. Conditional on the forward sheet and a fixed phase, every port has its desired marginal. Conditional on the opposite sheet at the succeeding bright port, the reversed transition samples the very same edge counts in transpose order. A deterministic bijection has supplied exact time reversal, including the invariant measure. It imposes no equality between the two incoming fiber sizes.

On the concrete 14-phase cycle, six signed matchings from a localized launch prepare equal normalized amplitudes $2/8$ on cells 1 and 2. The quartic weights are $5/656$ and $41/656$. A comparison in the order $(2,1)$ makes cell 2 bright at the next phase. Its reversed predecessor proportions on cells $(1,2)$ are $(5/46,41/46)$; the fair forward split following the bright port gives $(1/2,1/2)$ by the splitter law. All amplitudes and forward record probabilities return to the localized source after the echo and reversed six preparation layers. The least common denominators for these 14-phase marginals are $D=22{,}304$ for the quartic model and $D=32$ for the squared model. Including 14 phases and both orientations gives 624,512 and 896 microstates respectively. The checker exhausts both permutation spaces. At the quartic bright fiber there are 170 microstates whose predecessor was port 1 and 1,394 whose predecessor was port 2. Forgetting propagation orientation at this bright phase gives a half-mixture of the forward and reversed conditional responses, $(7/23,16/23)$ in that port order. A sufficient microscopic condition for reverse fairness *would* be a measure-preserving exchange of the two predecessor subsets conditional on the same bright ensemble. For uniform discrete weights such an exchange exists exactly when their cardinalities agree. The time-reversal involution alone supplies no such exchange; the two cardinalities exhibit the obstruction. The construction describes a fine-graining of this finite orbit. It presupposes its coherent amplitude schedule and supplies no common microscopic dynamics for arbitrary input superpositions, independent setting protocols, or the formation of a physical pointer. Both temporal orientations coexist; conditioning on a directed preparation is part of the operational comparison. ## What the calibration establishes

The autonomous graph can use the complete original four-tick preparation on eight sites with five sheets as its prefix. At its target phase the port $(0,R)$ contains the counts $(2,1,0,0,0)$, while the other 75 coordinates also enter the normalization. The base calibration cycle has 28 ticks, 17,920 vertices and four tag rows; the common table occurs at tick 14. The signed values $-1,+1,2$ have table masses $1/64,1/64,1/16$ respectively. The earlier target cells have four times those probabilities, giving the herald $5/16$. A separate 22-tick graph checks an odd depth and a negative nonunit amplitude. The versions with the deferred setting fork, comparison and return are separate 32- and 26-tick cycles. They have 40,960 and 3,328 clock-labeled vertices respectively. The 52 comparison edges on the five-sheet case yield a full-rank system for 55 individual occupied cells after including the four tag totals. The odd-depth case uses 23 edges and has rank 26. Inserting separately normalized fourth-power weights within each tag instead gives resolved echo distances $153/8960$ and $9/640$, while squared weights give zero in both. This insertion is a controlled test of a candidate table law; it is distinct from evolving pair-local fourth powers all the way from the source. Each base graph returns exactly to its initial state without a read, including a test with superposed clock phases. The clean setting, clock and local reciprocal or echo premise are supplied features of these models. The duration of the comparison after the target does not relabel its original physical tick. For a finite walk experiment with persistent measurement pointers, the same construction can be appended after each selected finite horizon; the squared law fixes the coordinate marginal of that *occurrence* on the enlarged clock. To use it across different horizons of the original experiment requires a common-prefix assumption. If horizon and calibration choices made after a fixed preparation cannot alter its earlier undisturbed marginals and a copied reading agrees with the direct named record, the finite cylinder laws transport to the original protocol. Ordinary countable additivity and control of any preparation stopping time remain necessary before passing to its measured classical limit. For the previously established two-ray limit these conditions accompany the stated dynamics, initial packet, clock and scaling hypotheses. Neither an independent readout on a different device nor an arbitrary context-dependent assignment to it is tested by this inference. The localized model's probabilities remain rational under every simultaneous cyclotomic relabeling. An absolute embedding, a physical origin for the signed dynamics, and a physical warrant for reciprocal or echo fairness remain open.
