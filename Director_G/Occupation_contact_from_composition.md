# Selecting the contact phase by comparing gate sequences

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

The two-particle rule in the hard-point walk can be fixed by two comparisons on four neighboring modes. The comparisons use localized input occupations and count a single output pattern in each circuit. They allow arbitrary complex contact phases, including different phases on the two outer bonds. If operations with the same one-particle amplitude map are required to give the same records in these comparisons, the unique contacts are the determinants of the individual gates. An interacting walk can fail this requirement while preserving its one-particle dynamics and binary exclusion. The comparisons specify an experiment that could distinguish the two possibilities. ## Two measured comparisons

Use modes \(0,1,2,3\) in that order on a line. Each gate is number preserving and acts as the identity on the vacuum of its bond. Its action when both local modes are occupied is a scalar of modulus one. On the left and right bonds the one-particle matrix is

\[
 R=\begin{pmatrix}c&-h\\ h&c\end{pmatrix},\qquad c,h\in\mathbb R,\quad c^2+h^2=1,\quad ch\ne0 .
\]

Denote the two unknown double-occupancy phases by \(g_0,g_2\). On the middle bond use the one-particle swap \(S\), with unknown double-occupancy phase \(s\). All gate actions are stable across repeated uses and independent of distant spectators. The same outer bond can have its own phase throughout; no equality between \(g_0\) and \(g_2\) is imposed. A different one-body axis, a fluctuating private flag, or a swap used once with different phases on its return is outside this model.

Call one application of \(R\) on each disjoint outer bond \(A\). Let \(B\) mean \(S,A,S\) in time order. On one particle the occupied position is a two-bit address \(00,01,10,11\). The middle swap exchanges its address bits, so \(A=I\otimes R\), \(B=R\otimes I\); the two operations commute exactly. This identity concerns the four coordinates of **one** particle, without assuming a tensor product law for two particles. In the sequences below letters list operations in chronological order. The condition to test is equality of two particular *probabilities* after substituting \(AB\) for \(BA\) while holding the source, subsequent operations and occupation labels fixed.

1. Launch particles at \(0,1\) and read occupancy at \(0,2\). For \(AB\) the amplitude vanishes. For \(BA\) it is \(-d(1+s)\), where \(d=c^2h^2>0\). The two histories that reach this port differ by whether the middle swap encountered two occupied modes. Equality forces \(s=-1\).
2. Launch at \(0,2\), append the same \(A\) to both sequences, and read \(1,3\). With the calibrated \(s=-1\), the amplitudes of \(ABA\) and \(BAA\) are \(d(g_0+g_2+2)\) and \(4d g_0\). Thus the common intensity must be \(16d^2\). The exact difference of the two output probabilities, reference minus tested sequence, is

\[
 d^2\big[2|1-g_0|^2+2|1-g_2|^2+|g_0-g_2|^2\big].
\]

Consequently equality fixes \(g_0=g_2=1\). Conversely these three phases give the exterior-power rule on every local bond. Taking exterior powers respects multiplication, so the full occupation maps of \(AB\) and \(BA\) agree in every number sector. After that construction all subsequent fixed occupation instruments also have equal transcripts. The necessity proof used only two labelled ports. At \((c,h)=(3/5,4/5)\) or \((4/5,3/5)\), the reference intensity of the second test is \(331776/390625\). For two negative rotation contacts it is zero in the tested sequence. If \(ch=0\) these ports carry no information. [All paths and zero ports](results/contact_exchange.json) · [Lean evaluation of the two rational settings](lean/ContactComposition.lean)

For reproducibility, before calibrating the swap the tested amplitude in the second experiment is \(d[g_0+g_2+c^2(1+s^2)-2h^2s]\); the reference is still \(4d g_0\). These expressions follow by expanding the single-occupation stay/hop rows on the two bonds, multiplying by the local contact on two occupied modes and by one on zero occupied modes. The checker stores the full polynomials in \(c,h,g_0,g_2,s\) at all output ports of the four columns. With real portable signs the same conclusion follows from a return test comparing a commutator with a matched inverse sequence. Both programs use the same 20 elementary strokes and give the identity on one particle. That version implements the inverse rotation with independently supplied local sign flips; it relies on \(g^{-1}=g\) for the unknown real contacts. The forward tests above do not need that premise. **Finite discrepancies.** If the actual probability at the first forbidden port is at most \(\delta_s\), then \(|s+1|\le\sqrt{\delta_s}/d\). Suppose the absolute difference of the second pair of probabilities is at most \(\delta_R\). In \(ABA\), switching \(s\) to \(-1\) changes each of the two middle swap operators in norm by \(|s+1|\), hence the final unit state by at most \(2|s+1|\). The trace distance of two pure states is bounded by their vector distance. The \(BAA\) reference port is independent of \(s\). Therefore the bracket in the displayed equation is at most \((\delta_R+2\sqrt{\delta_s}/d)/d^2\). With exact swap calibration the sharper bound is \(\delta_R/d^2\). These are bounds on underlying model probabilities; using counts from an experiment also requires sampling uncertainties and control over source, detector and single-particle gate errors.

## Phase strokes, general gates and the remaining premise

An overlapping three-mode comparison also fixes the action of a single-site phase on its neighbor's occupied configurations. Give \(P\) on \(01\) the one-particle block \(\operatorname{diag}(u,1)\), \(|u|=1\), and double phase \(g\). Give \(12\) a mixing gate \(R\) as above and a final analyzer with single-particle block \(R^{-1}\). On one particle \(P\) commutes with this \(R\). On the two-dimensional subspace \(\{|01\rangle,|02\rangle\}\), mode \(0\) is always occupied, \(P=\operatorname{diag}(g,u)\), and \(12\) sees exactly one particle, including at the inverse analyzer. Starting at \(|01\rangle\), the wrong-port probabilities for the two orderings followed by that analyzer are zero and \(c^2 h^2|g-u|^2\). Equality forces \(g=u\). Reflect the layout to handle a phase on the other site. These tests may use a phase orientation already calibrated by relative interference. Conjugating the entire apparatus preserves all port probabilities. [Three-mode complex-phase controls](results/contact_exchange.json)

On a longer line one can realize a gate acting on each desired bond by a calibrated copy in a four-mode window; at boundaries this requires sufficient neighboring or auxiliary modes and tested orientation conventions. Disjoint phases on the two sites multiply. With calibrated diagonal phases and proper rotations a synthesized \(2\times2\) unitary \(U=D_L R D_R\) consequently has \(g=\det U\). The factorization follows by removing the phases of the two entries of a column; orthogonality fixes the other column up to one phase. A primitive alternative implementation of the same \(U\) needs its own interchangeability evidence. With raw vacuum amplitude \(v\) instead of one, first divide the entire gate by \(v\). In the original convention the rule is \(g_{\rm raw}=\det U_{\rm raw}/v\). The local determinant condition is the number-preserving instance of the matchgate determinant condition. [Jozsa and Miyake](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.4050) · [Brod and Galvão on inequivalent parity-preserving gates](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.1863)

Under the two record requirements, the occupied-bond rule needed for the earlier scalar Dirichlet and ordered hard-point limits is now derived. The rule concerns gates generated by those local controls. It does not constrain independently allowed three-body gates. Single-particle probabilities alone cannot certify single-particle **amplitude** equality, and amplitude equality alone cannot enforce implementation equivalence on a many-particle system. The alternate contact walk violates the record requirements and supplies a specific countermodel. ## Calibrating the probabilities on the signed carrier

The derivation for arbitrary complex contact phases used the squared readout law. For the real sign alternatives and rational rotations at denominator five, the same tests have a separate realization in the program's protected-coordinate apparatus. At each rotation prepare three selector bits by balanced Hadamard gates. On a single occupant three selector labels apply a signed stay, four a signed hop with opposite signs for the two directions, and the last label goes to an orthogonal junk flag. On zero or two occupants five labels apply the identity with the prescribed sign and the rest go to junk. On each selector label this is a reversible signed permutation with the flag included. Apply the inverse Walsh gates. At selector zero and clean junk the block on the whole bond is \(5/8\) times the target operation. Each rotation uses fresh registers, and all old fail registers remain distinct. This makes for a single coherent circuit with a single terminal read. A gate later in the program never mixes the success and failure flags of a previous stage.

The unnormalized numerator of every all-pass port is an integer over \(8^K\) for \(K\) synthesized rotations. Six balanced Hadamard layers per rotation account for the denominator. The [protected-record comparison theorem](Protected_records_and_coordinate_weights.md) assigns the complete coordinate probability \(n^2/64^K\) from signed-permutation covariance, complete preparation equivalence, ordinary marginalization, and persistence of a coherently protected subset. The apparatus must supply those comparisons on the configuration and selector coordinates, including spectator padding as needed. Under these hypotheses the occupation-test joint probabilities follow without a separate Born postulate for these particular ports. These requirements are additional to the four-mode gate-word condition; the local occupation update alone does not establish them.

For either program in the first comparison \(K=4\), and in the second \(K=6\). On all passes the joint occupation probability is \((25/64)^K\) times its ideal value. Keeping the first failing rotation as a separate outcome gives unconditioned mass \((25/64)^j(39/64)\) after exactly \(j<K\) passes. Both compared words have the same compressed one-particle map on the all-pass record, the same success scale and the same first-failure weights. The coherent junk output within a failure event can still differ. Here the equivalence tested is specifically that of the matched all-pass ports; equivalence of the complete enlarged devices on all failure inputs is a separate condition. At the second port for the determinant model, the exact joint probability is \(331776/390625\) times \((25/64)^6= (5/8)^{12}\). A discrepancy measured in raw all-pass joint probabilities must be divided by the corresponding success scale before applying the ideal-port error bound. Discarding the failures would obscure this cost.
