# What fixes the phase between an operation and its idle arm?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist  
Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

The [eight-sheet experiment](Finite_clock_purity.md) gives two possible probabilities at a fixed port, $(2\pm\sqrt2)/4$. Its interference comes from a coherent arm that experiences one more Hadamard than the other. An unknown relative sign between the two arms can absorb the whole difference. Here the walk supplies a rational comparison operation with a quantitative precision and success guarantee. A published controlled-Hadamard experiment supplies an independent check of the fixed-apparatus interpretation. The same printed data illustrate why a test of a prescribed gate need not distinguish a simultaneous transformation of all preparations and analyzers.

## Measuring the relative phase

Let $\sigma=\pm1$ denote the algebraic sign of a Hadamard in the held real basis. Let $\Gamma$ be the normalized overlap of the shallow and deep path markers including their relative propagation phase. Summing both markers and all sheet sites at the final unit-stroke comparator gives $P_+=1/2+\sigma\mathrm{Re}\,\Gamma/(2\sqrt2)$. For an odd stroke $d$ the signed contrast is $\sigma\,\mathrm{Re}\,\Gamma\,\cos(\pi d/4)$; for an even stroke it is $\cos(\pi d/4)$. A change in the relative arm phase can therefore change the interpretation of the unit port without changing the populations of the shallow and deep arms. Complete route marking gives $P_+=1/2$. If $\mathrm{Re}\,\Gamma\ge v>0$ is independently known, the two unit-port predictions are separated by at least $v/\sqrt2$. [All physical output bins, phases and markers](results/bypass_phase_reference.json)

If each use of the apparatus is $\operatorname{diag}(\sigma H,e^{i\phi}I)$ on its addressed subspaces, $(\sigma,\phi)\mapsto(-\sigma,\phi+\pi)$ multiplies it by a global minus. Every full $H$ also changes by a global minus. Fixed invocations, coordinate records, and adaptive classical choices preserve every transcript under this transformation. A coherent bypass around that *entire invocation* would introduce another phase standard. If every coherent route instead experiences the same Hadamard depth, all finite coordinate probabilities are rational under both embeddings. An explicit completion of the last idle modes with a Hadamard on a fresh spectator gives a bright probability $3/4$ under either sign. Reading spectator zero first succeeds with probability $3/4$ and gives conditional brightness $5/6$ under either sign. [Synchronized source control](check_bypass_phase_reference.py)

A separate phase sensor can reside on the same apparatus. For a fair, common fluctuating sign $g=\pm1$, the eight-sheet unit contrast is $\sigma g/\sqrt2$. A two-H interferometer around $\operatorname{diag}(1,g)$ on otherwise matched paths measures $g$. Its sign product with the bypass read has expectation $\sigma/\sqrt2$ under conditional independence and verified phase transfer. Two uncalibrated bypass copies instead have product mean $1/2$ for the same shared fair fluctuation. Independent fair phase errors give zero. The difference between shared and independent fluctuations is a property of the joint physical preparation.

## A synchronized rational comparison operation

Let $X,Z$ be signed permutation involutions, $X^2=Z^2=I$, with $XZ=-ZX$. For a Pythagorean triple $a^2+b^2=c^2$ use $U=(aZ+bX)/c$. A sequence with positive $a,b$, $|a-b|=1$ and $c\to\infty$ comes from $u+v\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^t$, $m=u+v$, $n=v$, and $(a,b,c)=(m^2-n^2,2mn,m^2+n^2)$. The irrational symbol here abbreviates an integer recurrence $(u,v)\mapsto(u+2v,u+v)$. The operator distance to $H=(Z+X)/\sqrt2$ satisfies

$$\|U-H\|^2=2-\sqrt2(a+b)/c
=\frac{1}{c^2(1+\sqrt{1-1/(2c^2)})}.$$

Take the least dyadic $K\ge a+b$. In an index register prepare $K$ equal positive amplitudes by a Walsh transform. In the acted subspace assign $a$ indices to $Z$ and $b$ to $X$. In the idle subspace assign $c$ indices to $I$. On the other indices reversibly toggle a validity bit. Invert the Walsh transform and test only the joint index-zero, flag-zero subspace. All paths have equal Hadamard depth. Its accepted map is $(c/K)(U\oplus I)$ on any input, including any attached spectator. For the displayed family, $1/8<p=(c/K)^2\le25/49$. Indeed $K<2(a+b)\le2\sqrt2 c$ and $c^2/(a+b)^2=c^2/(2c^2-1)\le25/49$. With $K=O(1/\|U-H\|)$ the program needs $O(\log K)$ synchronized Hadamard layers per call, plus reversible comparisons, routing and their physical time. [Exact signed program and both signs through the eight-sheet port](results/rational_bypass.json) · [Linear combinations of unitary operations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.5822)

A coarse failure does not consume the unknown logical input. Write the coherent implementation as $W|0,v\rangle=\sqrt p\,G(v)+\sqrt{1-p}\,B(v)$ in the good and bad subspaces. Since the successful singular value is independent of $v$, $W^\dagger G=\sqrt p\,|0,v\rangle+\sqrt{1-p}\,A(v)$ and $W^\dagger B=\sqrt{1-p}\,|0,v\rangle-\sqrt p\,A(v)$, with $A$ orthogonal to the input ancilla subspace. After failure, apply $W^\dagger$, read only whether the ancilla has reset, apply $W$ and read success again. This pair succeeds with probability $2p(1-p)$, and either failure returns $B(v)$ up to phase. After $1+2l$ calls the residual failure probability is $(1-p)[1-2p(1-p)]^l$. The expected number of calls is $1+1/p<9$. Resolving individual failure indices can expose the acted/idle data and invalidate this recovery. [Full branch checks](check_rational_bypass.py) · [Marriott and Watrous on inverse-verifier amplification](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/Papers/QuantumArthurMerlinGames.pdf)

An equal-time comparison of two such compiled reflections on a fixed target basis state has rational contrast $(aa'+bb')/(cc')$. Comparing instead to a separately held candidate $+H$ or $-H$ with controlled phase gives contrast $\pm(a+b)/(\sqrt2 c)$. Thus arbitrarily accurate rational standards exist and an independent gate can disagree with them at finite precision. For the $(3,4,5)$ approximation the eight-sheet circuit gives rational plus-port probability $5177/6250$. Every finite standard, including its retry records, still gives the same rational data under both field embeddings. The two signs of the compiled program define distinct rational sequences. Identifying a separately specified irrational gate with one sequence's operational limit requires continuity in the topology defined by those comparisons.

### Success weighting and transfer to another port

Under the usual channel model the two complementary truth tables bound the process fidelity. The authors print a lower bound of $0.8971$; direct subtraction from their printed central scores gives $0.8973$. We retain both and use the smaller for numerical transfers. The guarantee requires either a common success probability or the success-weighted averages for a trace-decreasing map. With unknown input-dependent efficiencies, the normalized columns alone need more care. [Hofmann's two-basis bound](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.94.160504) · [Mičuda et al. on success weighting](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.4863)

There is a lower bound from the unweighted central averages alone for a **single** trace-decreasing completely positive map with trusted preparations and analyzers. For its normalized successful Choi state $\rho$ let $P_A,P_B$ project onto the two correct truth-table subspaces on input-reference and output. The input reference uses the conjugate basis. These projectors commute and intersect in the ideal Choi ray: the overlap of any corresponding correct input-output vectors between the mutually unbiased bases is $1/4$ with the natural output phases, so their cross-Gram matrix has rank one. Write $f$ for the mass in the intersection and $z$ for the mass correct in $B$ alone. Put $a=1-F_A$, $b=1-F_B$, where the $F$'s here are equally weighted averages of the four individually normalized input columns, each with positive success. Applying the printed figures in this calculation assumes this averaging convention and confinement to the two-qubit input and output spaces. Pooled counts with unequal efficiencies require separate success information.

To show $z\le a$, purify $\rho$. Split its $A$-wrong residual into input columns with squared norms $q_i e_i$; $\sum_iq_i=1$ and $\sum_i e_i=4a$. On each input column, projection onto $B$-correct contracts norm by a factor at most $1/2$ by mutual unbiasedness. Triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz give $\sqrt z\le\sum_i\sqrt{q_i e_i}/2\le\sqrt a$. Let $\delta=1-f-z$ be all $B$-wrong mass. For one input of $B$, its total Choi weight is at most $(\sqrt f+\sqrt{3z})^2/4+\delta$. Indeed the correct component has one ideal amplitude and three orthogonal deviations: each of the four coordinate evaluations has ideal projection norm $1/2$ and complementary projection norm $\sqrt3/2$ within the $B$-correct subspace. The rest contributes at most $\delta$, since the input event commutes with $P_B$. Thus $\delta\le 4b\max_i q_{B,i}$. For any $s>0$ and $4b<1$ this yields

$$ f\ \ge
\frac{1-a[1+b(3+3/s)/(1-4b)]}{1+b(1+s)/(1-4b)}.$$

Taking $s=0.47$ gives $f\ge179860054/211515275\simeq0.85034$. This is a central-value implication, with no claim of an optimal bound or a joint confidence interval. Reducing both scores by their stated errors gives about $0.84151$ as a sensitivity check. Mixtures of an ideal channel and two distinct one-basis-only Weyl errors attain $f=F_A+F_B-1=0.8973$ at these two scores. The channel can be scaled to arbitrarily small overall success without changing any conditional table. [Exact fractions and alternative bounds](check_optical_bypass_calibration.py)

For the input $|+\rangle|0\rangle$ the ideal control-plus probabilities after the same controlled gate are $(2\pm\sqrt2)/4$ for the two held signs. This Ramsey measurement was not in the two tables. A conditional transfer is possible: for a common trace-preserving error channel relative to the ideal, with process overlap $f$, the output fidelity on *each* pure input is at least $(4\sqrt f-3)_+^2$. In a basis starting with that input, apply the triangle inequality to the vectors of diagonal Kraus entries in the trace formula for $4\sqrt f$. The other three columns have norm at most one. For a normalized successful Choi state of a trace-decreasing map a weaker bound is $(4f-3)_+$: if that input has normalized Choi weight $w$, Cauchy–Schwarz on the other columns gives $4f\le(\sqrt{w f_\psi}+\sqrt{3(1-w)})^2\le f_\psi+3$. A binary measurement with ideal plus probability $p$ and actual $u$ then has $\sqrt{f_\psi}\le\sqrt{pu}+\sqrt{(1-p)(1-u)}$. At $f=0.8971$, using the success-weighted or common-success calibration above, these two assumptions give lower plus-probability bounds $0.24332$ and $0.21452$, respectively. The weaker unweighted fidelity bound does not by itself exclude the alternate Ramsey value through this inequality. A later coherent bypass around an *entire* calibrated channel would require a new global phase reference. The published experiment concerns its two-qubit device. Its scores are not measurements on an eight-sheet walk or a derivation of the underlying probability law.
