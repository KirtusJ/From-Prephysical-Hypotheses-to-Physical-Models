# Reading which half of the ring contains the walker

The eight-site Hadamard walk admits a temporal comparison with one binary spatial measurement. Assign $+1$ to sites 0 through 3 and $-1$ to sites 4 through 7. In each arm of the comparison, read this same bit at two selected clocks among 0, 8 and 16. The ideal probability that the two readings differ is $3/4$ for each pair, for **any initial position-and-coin state**. All three pair probabilities cannot arise from one undisturbed joint history of the bit. This supplements the [single-origin study](What_a_return_to_one_site_can_identify.md) and uses the [published walk convention](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/26/4/313).

## A pair of readings versus a history of three

For any three binary labels, either zero or two of their three pairs disagree. If the three pair experiments sample the same histories and reading the bit leaves those histories unchanged, the three disagreement probabilities therefore sum to at most 2. Shared preparation and common, consistently calibrated readouts matter here. The inequality allows any dependence between the three labels.

In the ideal measurement, use a binary projector onto each half, preserving superpositions of sites and coin states within each reported half. Let $U$ be the signed walk step, $Q$ the diagonal half-ring sign operator and $Q_t=(U^t)^\dagger Q U^t$. The exact 16-dimensional matrices obey

$$Q_0+Q_8+Q_{16}=0,\qquad Q_iQ_j+Q_jQ_i=-I\quad(i\ne j,\;i,j\in\{0,8,16\}).$$

The [checker](check_half_ring.py) constructs each matrix from the existing two-rail walk recurrence, translated to all eight input sites. Squaring the sum of any two observables and using the third proves the second identity from the first. In the eigenspace of the earlier result $a=\pm1$, the projected later observable is $-a/2$ times the identity on that eigenspace. The later result disagrees with $a$ with probability $3/4$, for either earlier answer with nonzero probability. Thus the pair-only sum is $9/4$. This realizes the established three-observable temporal quantum bound for binary Lüders measurements. More explicitly the sum is $9/4-\operatorname{Tr}[\rho(Q_0+Q_8+Q_{16})^2]/4$. [Budroni and Emary on temporal quantum bounds](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.050401)

When the detector reads all three clocks on the same launch, the endpoint bits differ if exactly one of the two successive comparisons flips. The conditional flip probability remains $3/4$ in each branch. Hence the endpoint disagreement falls to $2(3/4)(1/4)=3/8$. Asking at clock 8 changes the unconditioned endpoint statistic even when its answer is ignored. A mechanism with measurement-induced disturbance can give context-dependent pair records. A full intermediate position measurement followed by discarding the fine-grained output is a different instrument. Figure 8(b) of the Cairo paper used terminal position reads through eight steps. Its data do not give these pair rates. Tests for temporal correlations have been implemented in other quantum walks with other measurement schedules. [Cairo experiment](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/26/4/313) · [Four-step cesium experiment](https://journals.aps.org/prx/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevX.5.011003)

## What the single terminal position maps admit

Start at a known site with the coin in an equal incoherent mixture of its two rails. Let $p_t(x)$ denote the distribution of displacement after $t$ ticks. In a stationary trajectory description the displacement of the second three-step segment has the same distribution as the first. No independence of segments is required. A trajectory cannot reach the antipode in six steps if its two three-step displacements are both at distance one. Thus $p_6(4)\le 2\Pr_3(\text{distance}>1)$. The ideal values are $5/8$ on the left and $1/2$ on the right. The two radial probabilities hold for every coin localized at the source. The complete directional distributions below use a mixed coin.

Through clock five the mixed-coin position tables admit an exact stationary nearest-neighbor realization. Choose the periodic step word RLLLL with probability $5/8$ or RLL with probability $3/8$. Independently pick a uniform cyclic phase and, with probability one half, reverse the direction of every step. The checker tabulates all eight displacement probabilities. At clock six the violation of $1/8$ forces one of the tested panels to have total-variation error at least $1/24$, since discrepancies of at most $\epsilon$ in each panel can change the inequality by at most $3\epsilon$. A stationary mixture of LR, LLLR and LLLLR with respective weights $1/6,5/18,5/9$, the same uniform-phase rule and the same fair reflection attains that bound across every clock through six. The first-failure result applies to the mixed coin. The oriented position distribution of the published pure preparation is different.

The displayed Cairo bars give near-site mass about 0.5224 at clock three (sites 1 and 7) and antipode mass about 0.4555 at six. Counting all other reported sites as outside the near pair leaves ample room under the inequality. Moreover, a separate circuit launched at a specified source does not measure the second half of an uninterrupted stationary trajectory. [Published figure transcription](results/cairo_cycle_figure8.json)

One counterexample isolates the stationarity premise. Prepare at phase zero on each launch. Sample one of six twelve-step routes with integer weights 2, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1 out of eight, trace it, then retrace the steps backwards. The checker specifies the six words. Repeating gives finite reversible, nearest-neighbor trajectories that reproduce **all eight ideal mixed-coin terminal position probabilities at every clock**, including after translation to a different launch site. Their starts are synchronized. Their middle three-step segments can have a different distribution from their first three steps. The construction matches a family of single terminal position reads and supplies no common noninvasive interpretation of the three half-ring comparisons.
