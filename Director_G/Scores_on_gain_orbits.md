At each tick a walker at either rail of $x$ can go to $(x+1,R)$ or $(x-1,L)$. A left move from the left rail acts by a permutation $T$ of a finite gain alphabet. The other three transitions preserve the label. A position mask admits a set of wells at a given tick and passes both rails there. At a terminal rail, $U$ counts paths of each gain as a nonnegative integer vector. The raw score of a position record is the sum of the scores of its actual terminal rails and sites. Extending that record by an unrestricted tick is assumed to multiply the total by a common positive factor. This is a record-consistency hypothesis. Position masks prepare both incoming multisets on the generating cycle from a single sharp source on the line and on a ring with $N/\gcd(N,2)\ge6$. This statement concerns the reachable orbit. ## Algebra for any finite permutation

Let $T$ have $k$ cycles, of which $e$ have even length. For each cycle $i$ let $n_i(U)$ be its total count. In each even cycle choose one origin, and let $z_i(U)$ be its total on even positions minus its total on odd positions. Applying $T$ simultaneously reverses every $z_i$. Suppose real scores with $F_R(0)=F_L(0)=0$ obey
$$
 F_R(U+V)+F_L(U+TV)=2F_R(U)+2F_L(V)
$$
for all pairs of multisets on the whole alphabet. Then $F_R=F_L=F$, and the complete family is
$$
 F(U)=\sum_{i=1}^k a_i n_i(U)+z(U)^\mathsf{T} A z(U),
 \qquad A=A^\mathsf{T}\in\mathbb R^{e\times e}.
$$
Its dimension is $k+e(e+1)/2$. The score is nonnegative on all multisets exactly when every $a_i\ge0$ and $A$ is positive semidefinite.

Taking $V=0$ identifies the two rail functions, and taking $U=0$ gives $F(TV)=F(V)$. Write the equation again with $TV$ in place of $V$ and subtract. The common term $F(U+TV)$ cancels, leaving $F(U+V)=F(U+T^2V)$ for every context $U$. Consequently a single atom can be replaced by its two-step translate without changing the score. All atoms on an odd cycle are interchangeable. On each even cycle just the two parity classes remain.

On this collapsed alphabet a single odd-cycle atom and a balanced pair of even-cycle atoms are both fixed by $T$. For any such $C$ the equation implies $F(U+C)=F(U)+F(C)$. Take $a_i=F(C)$ for an odd atom and $a_i=F(C)/2$ for an even balanced pair. Subtracting these multiples of the cycle totals leaves a function unchanged by adding any of these generators. Removing them to exhaustion shows that the remainder depends only on the integer differences $z$. All $z\in\mathbb Z^e$ occur since both signs have nonnegative representatives. For their score $G$ the remaining equation is
$$
 G(x+y)+G(x-y)=2G(x)+2G(y).
$$
Here $G(0)=0$, $G(-x)=G(x)$ and $G(2x)=4G(x)$. Set $B(x,y)=[G(x+y)-G(x-y)]/4$. Symmetry follows from evenness. Two applications of the displayed equation give $B(x+u,y)+B(x-u,y)=2B(x,y)$. Swapping $x$ and $u$ gives a second identity with the sign of the second term reversed, by oddness of $B$ in its first argument. Addition proves $B(x+u,y)=B(x,y)+B(u,y)$. Thus $G(x)=B(x,x)=x^\mathsf{T}Ax$ with $A_{ij}=B(e_i,e_j)$. The formula satisfies the original equation by direct substitution. The fixed atoms and pairs determine the linear coefficients, and polarization determines $A$, proving the dimension. [Quadratic functional equation on groups](https://data.math.au.dk/publications/pp/2004/imf-pp-2004-8.pdf)

Nonnegativity forces the $a_i$ conditions by the same fixed tests. For a prescribed integer $z$, scale any nonnegative representative by $N$. A negative $z^\mathsf{T}Az$ would outweigh its linear count for large $N$. Scaling rational vectors to integers and using continuity of a finite matrix gives the positive-semidefinite condition on all real vectors. Conversely both terms are then nonnegative. An independent sign reversal of only one even cycle is additional symmetry and generally changes a cross term. The walk reverses all even-cycle differences at once.

For translation by an element of order $r$ in a cyclic alphabet of size $q$, $k=q/r$ and $e=k$ for even $r$, zero otherwise. This proves the multi-coset dimension conjecture in the draft, for the **algebra on all multisets**. A sharp source at the identity with this one translation reaches just its own orbit. To apply the larger algebra as a physical classification requires preparations that put atoms from different orbits together at the two rails. Within the reachable orbit an odd $r$ gives counting alone; an even $r$ admits counting and the square of the alternating residue sum. Increasing the number of roots on this one arc does not by itself give a complex phase response.

## When the site and multiplier can also be inferred

Suppose position masks can prepare arbitrary $U,V$ on the two rails at each site in the stated domain. One may append a terminal site restriction to remove other sites at that same clock. On a finite ring suppose each rail/site score is finite and nonnegative, some score is positive, and the continuation factor $\kappa>0$ is common to all these records. For a given $U$ consider the finite directed lift with states $(x,r,j)$, $j$ modulo the order of $T$. Attach the value $H(x,r,j)=F_{x,r}(T^j U)$. Preparing only the specified rail gives the one-input equation $\kappa H=\sum_{\mathrm{two\ successors}}H$, where precisely the $LL$ arc increments $j$. Each lifted vertex has indegree and outdegree two. Summing and choosing a $U$ for which the nonnegative sum is positive forces $\kappa=2$. This argument allows a different $U$ for proving positivity and for comparing the functions.

The lift is strongly connected: the base walk connects both rails and all sites, and the closed word $LLRR$ based at any right rail raises $j$ by one and returns to the same right rail and site. A base path through a right rail together with these loops adjusts any desired phase offset on an otherwise fixed route. The finite maximum principle now makes $H$ constant across this lift, for each fixed $U$. This gives independence of site and rail, and invariance under a **simultaneous** translate of $U$. Substituting the recovered common score into the two-input law supplies the algebra above. If only the source orbit has been prepared, this argument classifies scores only on that orbit.

For the line, if $\kappa=2$ is supplied, the two equiprobable successors project onto the recurrent simple symmetric walk. The lift is irreducible by the same loop. Starting over site zero, infinitely many visits to that site in a finite rail/phase fiber force at least one recurrent state, and irreducibility makes them all recurrent. A nonnegative finite harmonic $H$ satisfies $H(i)\ge\Pr_i(\tau_j\le n)H(j)$ by stopping at $n$ or the first visit to $j$. Let $n$ increase and exchange $i,j$ to get equality. If the line's multiplier is free, $F_{x,r}(U)=b^x|U|$ with $b>0$ and $b\ne1$ gives $\kappa=b+b^{-1}>2$ for every position record. A genuinely finite spatial ring removes that example. These results classify all **scores under the stated law**. The generating even cycle still permits both counting and the alternating square with independent nonnegative coefficients. An observed interference port can reject pure counting; an explanation of why the world realizes one particular mixture requires additional evidence. No unitary normalization or Born probability was used to solve the score equation.
