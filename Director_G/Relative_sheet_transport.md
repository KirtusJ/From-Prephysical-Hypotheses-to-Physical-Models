# What two sheet registers can tell each other

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Mathematical development and exact checks by Director G (Codex).

Director Z asked whether composition could give an operational meaning to a *relative* embedding. Two initially localized cyclic sheet registers can prepare a prescribed relation between their characters and verify it with a dark output. On prime-order sheets the preparation is exact after finitely many steps. All records still have rational probabilities. The result distinguishes a relative character assignment while leaving the ordinary absolute probability embedding unidentified.

## The additional operation

Take the real signed Hadamard walk and its three-step site-and-direction records. Attach two sheets \(y,z\in\mathbb Z/M\). For each three-step block choose two integer gains \(v=(a,b)\). A departure from \(x\) sends \((y,z)\) to \((y+ax,z+bx)\). The coin and the base shift act as before. These are permutations of named registers. The available gain settings and the use of *one common coherent base path* are premises about the composite apparatus. Independent walkers each reading their own records do not implement this coupling. No character preparation, Fourier measurement, calibrated irrational rotation or signed complex reference is required. A basic precedent for distinguishing internal and external references is the quantum reference-frame literature. The use of character sectors for cyclic lifts has a separate graph-theoretic basis. [Bartlett, Rudolph and Spekkens](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610030) · [Dalfó, Fiol and Širáň](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04463)

Write \(S_v=T_A^aT_B^b\) for a sheet increment. The two interfering histories in the destructive record of a block give \(D_v=S_v-S_v^{-1}\), up to a known translation, sign and the factor \(1/\sqrt8\). For the constructive record replace the minus with a plus. The incoming direction determines the output used for each reading. Known translations commute with all these operators. The raw constructive and destructive probabilities in character \((r,s)\) are respectively \(\frac12\cos^2(2\pi(ar+bs)/M)\) and \(\frac12\sin^2(2\pi(ar+bs)/M)\). The other four outcomes each have probability \(1/8\). This follows from the [single-register three-step table](Records_and_complex_structure.md) by substituting \(S_v\) for \(T\).

At odd \(M\), the destructive probability for \(v=(1,-u)\) vanishes exactly where \(r=us\bmod M\). If a tagged pair with \(s\) a unit is supplied, the unique dark setting determines \(u=rs^{-1}\). It is a *relative character power*. Interpreting it as a relative cyclotomic embedding also requires faithful sectors and an identification of each register's generator with the named increment. A shared relabelling \((r,s)\mapsto(gr,gs)\), \(g\) a unit, preserves \(u\). In the absence of an interaction the two single-register transcript laws factor, and cannot compare two independent absolute labellings.

## Finite preparation without a tag

Let \(M=p\) be an odd prime, and fix any desired unit \(u\) modulo \(p\). Start in a localized state on both sheets. Retain a destructive record at each of the following \(p\) blocks. First use \(v=(0,1)\). Then use \((1,-j)\) for every \(j\ne u\) in \(\mathbb F_p\), once each. The first block removes \(s=0\). For every remaining \((r,s)\) off the desired line there is a unique \(j=r/s\ne u\) whose block removes it. Every point \((us,s)\) with \(s\ne0\) survives. The product selects the entire nonzero part of one line in the two-register character space. It does not select an absolute character within it. The line is a single orbit under simultaneous multiplication by a nonzero residue. Rolling the generator names together therefore preserves the preparation criterion. This finite preparation is consistent with the previous [single-register obstruction](Native_preparations_and_probability_embeddings.md).

The success probability and the remaining uncertainty are explicit. Put \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}\). On the selected line the product of the horizontal difference factors is
\[
   \prod_{j\ne u}\big(\zeta^{(u-j)s}-\zeta^{-(u-j)s}\big)
     =\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\zeta^{-k}(\zeta^{2k}-1)=p .
\]
The exponents run through the nonzero residues, their sum is a multiple of \(p\), and \(\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}(1-\zeta^k)=p\) follows by evaluating \((X^p-1)/(X-1)\) at 1. The vertical block contributes \(\zeta^s-\zeta^{-s}\). Inverting the two-dimensional Fourier transform gives an integer vector equal, up to translation and sign, to \(+1\) on the \(p\) sites \(uy+z=1\), \(-1\) on the \(p\) sites \(uy+z=-1\), and zero elsewhere. Equivalently the uniform projector onto the line of characters averages the translations along \((1,-u)\); multiplication by \(p\) clears that average's denominator. The squared count is \(2p\), so the joint preparation probability is **\(2p/8^p\)**. No nonzero faithful sector in the orbit has been discarded by hand. The probability includes every required record in the word. The cyclotomic identity and the common Galois action use ordinary finite-field and cyclotomic arithmetic. [Conrad](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/121Page/handouts/cyclotomic.pdf)

**All follow-up settings.** For a fresh block with \(v=(1,-j)\), let \(d=u-j\bmod p\). Its destructive probability conditional on preparation is
\[
    q(d)=\begin{cases}
       0 & d=0,\\
       3/8 & d=1\text{ or }-1,\\
       1/4 & \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
\]
To verify this directly, the shift \(S_v\) increments the coset coordinate \(uy+z\) by \(d\). Apply the difference of the two shifts to the two signed peaks, square all counts, and divide by \(8(2p)\). When \(d=0\) they cancel completely. For \(d=\pm1\) the new peaks have coefficients \(1,-2,1\) up to sign, at three distinct residues even for \(p=3\). For every other nonzero \(d\) four distinct peaks have unit modulus. The constructive probability is \(1/2-q(d)\). Every single-path outcome remains at \(1/8\). At \(p=5\) preparation costs \(10/32768\); the off-target destructive records have probabilities \(3/8\) or \(1/4\). A finite run of silence alone cannot certify an exact zero in an empirical device. These are exact probabilities of the declared model.

The resulting pure sheet state is nonseparable over the two registers. Its Schmidt rank is \(p-1\) for a unit \(u\). In the character basis its coefficient matrix has one nonzero entry in each of the \(p-1\) nonzero rows and columns and no other entries. The real coset matrix has the same rank. The intervention thus compares coherent transport on a physical pair within the model. Comparing settings across fresh, equally prepared pairs reads out the relative relation without resolving the sheets. Different \(u\)'s occupy orthogonal subspaces; the point \(r=s=0\) where the geometric lines meet has been removed.

**Transferring a reference.** Suppose register B is independently prepared at a specified nonzero character \(s_0\), while A is localized. The same word now prepares A exactly at \(r=u s_0\) with probability \(p|\zeta^{s_0}-\zeta^{-s_0}|^2/8^p\). Indeed its single surviving input coefficient has squared modulus \(1/p\), and the filter multiplier there is \(p(\zeta^{s_0}-\zeta^{-s_0})\) up to phase and normalization. An unoriented reference on the real pair \(\pm s_0\) transfers only that pair on A, in the corresponding correlated sectors. If *both* inputs carry independently specified labels, one can test their relative calibration directly: at \(p=5\), keep \(s=1\) on B fixed and compare \(r=2\) with \(r=4\) on A. A single destructive block at \(j=2\) gives zero in the former case and \((5-\sqrt5)/16\) in the latter. A simultaneous relabelling on both registers still leaves their dark setting unchanged. The absolute calibration of either source is an external premise.

## Controls and probability interpretation

With both sheets localized, a single block cannot distinguish any two settings \((1,a)\) and \((1,b)\). In each case the two paths reach different coordinate endpoints, giving \(1/4\) for either informative record. But retain a destructive record at \((1,a)\), then take a second block at \((1,b)\). The conditional destructive probability is \(3/8\) when \(a=b\) and \(1/4\) otherwise; its constructive counterpart is \(1/8\) and \(1/4\), respectively. This holds at all odd \(M\ge3\). For unequal settings all four endpoints \(\pm(1,a)\pm(1,b)\) are distinct. For identical settings the middle two amplitudes add in the destructive-destructive word. This control compares labelled transport laws with two blocks and no tags. It does not require primality. The stronger \(p\)-block relation preparation above uses the fact that every nonzero denominator has an inverse.

In physical coordinates every fixed adaptive branch retains an integer vector divided by one common power of \(\sqrt2\). This includes the preceding coupling, coherent coordinate bins in either register, and postselection. All candidate embeddings give exactly the same normalized cylinder probabilities. That remains true of the complete recorded path by cylinder uniqueness. A common relabelling of character descriptions can change both individual labels without affecting any measured frequency. If one side already bears an independently specified faithful tag, measuring \(u\) fixes the other side *relative to that tag*. Preparing the pair by this word supplies a reproducible relation even when neither side has an absolute tag. At prime order \(p>3\) it cannot identify which conjugate value to assign to a separately named real generator; nor can this protocol test the sign assigned to the coin's \(\sqrt2\).
