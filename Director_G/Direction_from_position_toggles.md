# Reading direction with two position-controlled toggles

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

The native selector can identify a sharp arrival using the last two wells. A base packet needs a different outcome: the direction must be learned while different arrival sites on that rail can still interfere. A single internal bit carried by the walker can do this using two fixed position patterns. Both patterns have period four and remain the same as the sheet order increases. They never use the sheet address or the incoming rail.

## A reversible writer

Let $N=4M$ be the number of wells, $f(x)=\lfloor x/2\rfloor\bmod2$, and denote toggling a bit by addition modulo two. Before one native tick at departure $x$, toggle the bit by $u(x)=f(x+1)$. After the step at arrival $y$, toggle by $v(y)=f(y)$. A right step has $y=x+1$, so the two toggles cancel. A left step has $y=x-1$, with $f(x-1)=1-f(x+1)$. Thus they add one precisely for left. The ring seam preserves both patterns. On an arbitrary initial bit $z$ the resulting value is $z\oplus{\bf1}_{L}$. This holds for each streamed term with its original Hadamard sign and staircase gain; it therefore holds on every superposition in the lifted realization. With a known blank input and an end read the two Kraus operators on that realization are exactly $P_R U_H,P_L U_H$. No predecessor position remains in the bit. [Basis-column checker](check_direction_from_sites.py) · [Lean identities](lean/DirectionFromTwoSites.lean)

For this one-bit architecture the circumference constraint is exact. If the right-edge output is zero for a blank input, then $u(x)=v(x+1)$. Requiring one on a left edge says $v(x+2)=1-v(x)$. Hence $v$ has period four and flips after two. Even cycles of length $2\bmod4$ are impossible by the flip. On an odd cycle the repeated two-step displacement returns after an odd number of flips, which is also impossible. On a ring of length divisible by four there are four choices, specified by the two independent bits $v(0),v(1)$. This is a characterization of binary position toggles about one streamed tick. A different pointer architecture can have a different circumference constraint. [Proof for arbitrary integer lifts and periodic controls](lean/DirectionFromTwoSites.lean)

The bit is coherent and co-moving. Local cells may supply the fixed reversible gates only if they keep no trace of which cell was used. A terminal read on the bit must likewise avoid writing a site identity on the retained rail. A fresh blank bit, or resetting the known observed value after a read, allows repetition. If a one-bit trajectory measurement is all that is supplied, only its outcomes are specified; the agreement of its conditional instrument with this reversible writer requires a separate justification. The walk-clock estimates charge these gates as operations before and after a native tick with bounded local overhead. They do not bound remote signal delivery or the cost of building a distributed readout. A dark pass can use a common surviving rail and a failure-only site read. ## A four-tick interference check

Start on R at zero, evaluate the constant sheet character, write the direction on tick two, accept L across all sites, and read $(0,R)$ at tick four. The accepted paths reaching that port are RLLR and LLRR. Both carry a minus sign. The per-launch probability is $4/16$. If a detector also copies the pre-stream bit into a persistent record at tick two, the different departure sites $+1$ and $-1$ acquire distinct records. Summing probabilities over those records leaves $2/16$. The final bit still correctly reports the requested rail in both experiments. In the same four-tick comparison a localized sheet can itself retain the difference of the two gains, so its unrestricted coordinate probability need not reveal that pointer error. The checker retains both inputs, compares all bit/well/rail/sheet basis columns at five orders, and counts all binary patterns on circumferences three through sixteen.
