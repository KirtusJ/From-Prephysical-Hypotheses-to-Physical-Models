# Following one prepared walk with successive position readings

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Mathematical development and computational checks by Director G (Codex).

The [two-ray preparation](Base_preparation_and_two_rays.md) gave a position distribution at each specified time. One-time distributions alone permit a process that switches rays between readings. Here one walker is actually measured repeatedly. In a specified limit, its interpolated position record follows a single Newtonian ray over a bounded interval. A first reading at separated positions heralds which track will follow. A reversal of the applied field gives a test in which that track crosses the other one. This extension assumes coherent coarse position measurements, the previously specified probability rule and an externally set clock. Repeated approximate position readings have also led to classical tracks in models of illuminated particles. The mechanism here uses concentration of a prepared coined walk. [Ballesteros et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00785) · [Benoist, Fraas and Fröhlich](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09558)

## Flight and instrument

Use the prepared packet, with even \(m\sim\delta^{-5/2}\) two-step direction-switch blocks, \(\varepsilon=\delta\), spatial coordinate \(Y=\delta^2x\) relative to the recorded origin, and time increment \(\Delta t=\delta^3\). Take \(\delta=(\pi/M)^{1/4}\) along odd sheet orders and condition on the successful preparation. Its initial sheet leakage before the direction blocks must be \(o(\delta^{5/4})\), as previously derived. The clock below starts after those blocks. Let \(F(t)=+1\) or \(-1\) with finitely many prescribed switches on \([0,T]\); the effective phase in one chosen character is \(h_j=F_j\delta^4\). The conjugate character has the opposite phase. A sign switch in the lifted model changes the departure update from \(y\mapsto y+x\) to \(y\mapsto y-x\), or conversely. A constant field needs no such control. Round switches and readings to step boundaries. The high-field parity factor commutes with every specified reading and anticommutes with each step, so it can be moved to the output up to a common sign.

For a partition of position into bins, copy only the bin label to a blank pointer and observe it. In the coordinate basis the operation can be the permutation \((x,c,y,\ell)\mapsto(x,c,y,\ell+B(x))\) on an integer pointer. On a finite reachable cone one can instead use a finite register with distinct labels on that cone. Reading result \(k\) applies the orthogonal projector onto \(B(x)=k\). This leaves coherent superpositions of sites within one bin. Reading the full position and then discarding digits gives a different state, diagonal in the exact position. For example the expectation of translation by two sites in that dephased state is zero. Constructing a pointer from the prephysical primitives is a remaining obligation.

Define the two candidate paths
\[
      y_\pm(t)=\pm A(t),\qquad A(t)=\int_0^t(t-s)F(s)\,ds .
\]
Choose \(0<a<T\) with \(A(a)\ne0\). At fixed positive times with separated rays, reading the side of the origin suffices to follow a branch. To obtain a continuous position record including crossings, partition the *whole line* into uniform bins of width \(w\) and use their centres as observations. The apparatus need not know either path. Set \(K=\max(2,\lceil\delta^{-1/16}\rceil)\) equally spaced read times from \(a\) through \(T\). Use \(w=\delta^{1/16}\) and a common offset \(U\) chosen uniformly among \(J\) equally spaced points in \([0,w)\), with \(J\) a power of two in \([\delta^{-1/4},2\delta^{-1/4})\). Draw it before the flight, independently of the walker. Fair bits can be taken from a separate walk using fresh single-step direction readings. For each exact coordinate these prescriptions merely designate an integer bin. Interpolate the measured bin centres linearly on the nominal time grid, using the nearest step time for the actual readings.

**Position-record law.** On \(C([a,T])\) with the uniform metric, the law of this interpolated transcript tends weakly to
\[
                 \tfrac12\delta_{y_+}+\tfrac12\delta_{y_-}.
\]
The mass includes *every* measured history, conditional only on the earlier preparation. Conditional on a first reading on a specified side at \(a\), the track converges in probability to the unique path on that side at \(a\). This identifies a spatial branch. Both conjugate sheet characters can contribute to it.

## Proof

1. Use the smooth cutoff in each of the four momentum and band charts of the [preparation proof](Base_preparation_and_two_rays.md). In each ideal selected sheet the exact initial state has the form \(v_++v_-+e\), with \(\|e\|=o(1)\). Each \(v_\sigma\) groups the two charts that follow \(y_\sigma\) and has squared norm tending to \(1/2\). Include different sheets by orthogonal direct sum with their initial coefficients and interchange the chart groups in the conjugate sheet. The four charts are orthogonal initially by disjoint supports or pointwise band orthogonality. Cutting the translated \(\sin^m\) comparison profile at \(|p|\le2,\ p=(k-k_*)/\delta\), costs \(O(\delta^{1/4})\) in norm. The field-on preparation error is \(O(m^3h^2)=O(\delta^{1/2})\). Leakage after postselection contributes \(o(1)\) just once. The initial position and momentum deviations for each unnormalized scalar chart are bounded by \(C(\delta^{3/4}+\delta)\) and \(C(\delta^{1/4}+\delta^{1/2})\), respectively. These estimates include differentiating the smooth cutoff and band section.

2. For any chart with curvature \(b=+1\) or \(-1\), the scalar evolution transports \(p_0\) to \(p_0-\int_0^t F\) and multiplies the initial amplitude by the phase with action \(b\int_0^t (p_0-\int_0^s F)^2\,ds/2\), using the convention \(\exp(-i\,\mathrm{action}/\varepsilon)\). Its position centre is \(-bA(t)\). Differentiating the phase bounds the final position deviation by the initial position deviation plus \(t\) times the initial momentum deviation. The [spinor comparison](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md) is \(O_T(\delta)\), uniformly in step time up to \(T\). Indeed \(|p|\le 2+T\), the sum of frame errors is bounded by \(2\sum_j|h_j|\), and the quartic dispersion and right-endpoint action errors have the same bounds with \(\sup|F|=1\). On a step where \(F\) is constant the kinetic integrand has derivative bounded by \(2+T\). Rounding finitely many field switches changes the accumulated momentum by \(O_T(\delta^3)\), the classical centre by \(O_T(\delta^3)\), and the integrated phase divided by \(\varepsilon\) by \(O_T(\delta^2)\). One may perform the comparison wholly with the rounded field and transfer only the centres. The implicit constants may depend on the fixed field schedule.

3. The position bound also applies to the *encoded comparison packet* on the lattice. In lattice Fourier coordinates \(Y=i\delta^2\partial_k\), so differentiating the final normalized band section adds at most \(C\delta^2\) times the envelope norm. Chebyshev's inequality in norm gives a tail bound for this packet. Transfer it to the exact lattice evolution with a bounded projection, avoiding differentiation of the spinor comparison error. If \(V(t)\) is the free flight including the applied field and a bin around \(y_\sigma(t)\) has boundary distance at least \(d>0\), then
\[
   \|(I-P)V(t)v_\sigma\|\le C_T(\delta+\delta^{1/4}/d).
\]
The triangle inequality combines the two charts with the same centre; opposite sheets are orthogonal. This estimate also holds for any half-line or interval with the same margin.

4. For completeness, take projections \(Q_j\) in the initial-time picture and a unit vector \(v\). Set \(v_0=v,\ v_j=Q_j v_{j-1}\), \(S=\sum_j\|(I-Q_j)v\|^2\) and \(L=1-\|v_K\|^2\). Orthogonality gives \(\sum_j\|v_{j-1}-v_j\|^2=L\). In the scalar product of \(v\) with \(v_{j-1}-v_j\), one can replace \(v\) by \((I-Q_j)v\). Cauchy–Schwarz yields \(1-\operatorname{Re}\langle v,v_K\rangle\le\sqrt{SL}\). Therefore \(\|v-v_K\|^2\le2\sqrt{SL}-L\le S\), and \(L\le4S\). Removing the unit normalization gives the same disturbance estimate with \(S\) computed on the unnormalized input. Taking \(Q_j=V(t_j)^*P_jV(t_j)\) controls a whole measured word by the sum of its individual *unmeasured* leakage probabilities. This is the sequential projection union-bound argument. [Gao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5688)

5. Set \(d=\delta^{3/16}\). For each of the \(2K\) predicted coordinates, at the attained times, the probability over the offset that a bin boundary lies within \(d\) is at most \(2d/w+2/J\). A union bound makes the probability of any such event \(O(\delta^{1/16})\). Otherwise apply step 4 to the bin word containing one path at every observation. Its accumulated disturbance on \(v_\sigma\) is bounded by \(C_T\sqrt K(\delta+\delta^{1/4}/d)=O_T(\delta^{1/32})\). Since the two first positions are separated, for small \(\delta\) they occupy different first bins. The unwanted component's contribution to this word is already small after that first projection, by step 3, and subsequent operations are contractions. The initial residual \(e\) stays bounded in norm by \(\|e\|\) over the entire word. Each of the two path words therefore has probability tending uniformly on good offsets to \(1/2\), and their total tends to one by completeness. Distinct words may have some common bin labels later, including at a crossing. On either word the maximum interpolation error is bounded by \(w/2\) plus a Lipschitz constant times the grid spacing and rounding error. The exceptional offsets and all other transcripts have vanishing total probability. This proves the stated law and the conditional assertion. At a fixed finite list of separated times the same proof applies to sign-only readings, with a fixed positive margin and no random offset.

## Reversing the field and reading the finite controls

For \(F=1\) until \(\tau>0\) and \(-1\) afterwards, \(A(t)=t^2/2-(t-\tau)_+^2\). The two branches meet again at \((2+\sqrt2)\tau\), with opposite velocities there. Each branch changes its side of the origin. Measuring which *site* the walker occupies at the crossing would require a different analysis. The coarse bins above can temporarily give the same label to both rays and subsequently distinguish them. At the crossing the first record still predicts the later branch in the limiting law.

The [position-space checker](check_position_records.py) evolves the actual projected packet on a line padded beyond its causal cone. It records all eight side words at three times for two effective signs, two field schedules and four sheet orders from 127 to 8191. It also computes the mass in the two candidate uniform-bin words and the complementary probability of every other bin word combined. At \(M=8191\) the constant-field side histories that stay on one side carry probability 0.99176. With \(\tau=0.75\) and nominal readings at 1.4, 2.3 and 3.0, the two side histories predicted to switch at the last read carry only 0.20916. The fixed finite bins catch both nominated histories with combined probability only 0.08639 in the constant field and 0.07911 with reversal at this order. Preparation drift and packet width have appreciable effects at these scales. The asymptotic theorem gives no claim of accurate tracking at those resolutions. All adverse finite probabilities are retained. The two conjugate signs give identical joint side laws in these simulations. All read operations here are real coordinate permutations and masks. Starting from localized sheets, the [integer branch invariant](Native_preparations_and_probability_embeddings.md) therefore gives equal transcript laws under every candidate probability embedding, including field reversals, the prior filtering and independently drawn finite offset bits. The path limit inherits that equality. Observing motion selects a spatial history within the supplied instrument and force schedule. It supplies no physical clock, absolute embedding, general interacting classical limit or derivation of the supplied measurement law.

## The cost of supplying the first sheet plane

Director Z bounded the probability of preparing the target plane with leakage at most \(\eta\) by \(\pi_*/(1-\eta)\), where \(\pi_*=2/M\) is its localized-sheet prior. The same bound holds for any accepted set of transcripts whose retained states have that purity, by summing their target masses. Z also counted the extremely low yield of one prescribed power-iteration word. Accepting many records by their *posterior* reaches the prior bound asymptotically. The individual prescribed-word optimization is a different problem. [Director Z's cost calculation and finite-scale controls](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/pull/2#issuecomment-6848036952)

Use the [three-step instrument](Records_and_complex_structure.md) without changing any transport gains. At the \(k\)-th informative record let \(w_j\) be the class prior \(\pi_j\) times the likelihood of this bit word under class \(j\). The ratio \(w_*/\sum_jw_j\) is the exact target-plane posterior. Accept all words with posterior at least \(1-\eta\), with \(0<\eta<1\). On rejected words \((\eta/(1-\eta))w_*<\sum_{j\ne *} w_j\), hence \(w_*\le\sqrt{(1-\eta)/\eta}\sum_{j\ne *}\sqrt{w_*w_j}\). Summing over all words and using the binomial affinity gives a bound on the *unconditional* probability of acceptance,
\[
 \pi_*-\sqrt{(1-\eta)/\eta}\sum_{j\ne *}\sqrt{\pi_*\pi_j}
       \cos^k(\pi|j-j_*|/M)
   \ \le P_{\rm accept}\le {\pi_*\over1-\eta}.
\]
The target class index is \(j_*=1\). Neutral blocks supply neither information nor bias, since they have total probability \(1/2\) in each class. The mean cost is \(2k\) three-step blocks per trial. In particular, replacing the sum divided by \(\pi_*\) with \(M\cos^k(\pi/M)\) is conservative since \(\pi_j\le\pi_*\). For a fractional shortfall \(0<\gamma<1\), it suffices to use
\[
 k=\max\!\left(1,\left\lceil
 {\log\!\left(M\sqrt{(1-\eta)/\eta}/\gamma\right)\over-\log\cos(\pi/M)}
                    \right\rceil\right).
\]
Every accepted transcript then has leakage at most \(\eta\), and the total acceptance probability is at least \((1-\gamma)2/M\). This is an asymptotic yield for an ensemble of accepted transcripts; the probability bound concerns the ensemble as a whole.

For the flight take \(\eta=\delta^2,\ \gamma=\delta,\ M=\pi/\delta^4\). Referring positions to the last recorded site and starting the subsequent direction filter in the actual recorded direction makes the accepted records interchangeable for the target-sector yield. On both target characters the base word succeeds with \(p_{\rm base}\sim\delta^{5/4}/\sqrt\pi\). The target mass in accepted words lies between \((1-\gamma)\pi_*\) and \(\pi_*\), by the preceding rejected-mass proof. Off-target contributions to the two-stage event are bounded by \(\eta P_{\rm accept}\), which is \(o(\pi_*p_{\rm base})\). Thus the probability of the two-stage preparation per localized start is asymptotic to \(2\delta^{21/4}/\pi^{3/2}\). Its mean number of sheet blocks per attempted start is \(O(M^2\log M)\). Absolute embedding discrimination remains zero even at this higher acceptance rate. The checker also tests the posterior inequality on finite synthetic class channels, separately from the physical-sheet model.
