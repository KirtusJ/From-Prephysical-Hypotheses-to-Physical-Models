# Two local routing switches test a freshly fluctuating site score

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

An original-launch position read can have the ordinary distribution even if a detector draws a new nonlinear score on every launch. How far can the walk identify that score? On a programmable signed walk, two independently switchable packets force almost every score to be affine on the tested grid **strictly below half occupancy**. One detector reading is made per launch. The hidden score may change before the next launch. For continuous scores the half-occupancy boundary can be included, and the freedom above it can be characterized exactly. This is a restriction on a specified detector family. The comparisons have not been performed on a physical walk.

## Nine local steps from a sharp source

Use a 24-site ring with a position detector at every site and a sharp walker at 0 on rail R. A line with a fixed finite bank containing all terminal outputs gives the same argument. Instrumenting infinitely many empty wells with a positive pedestal would make the normalizer diverge. Prepare $m$ protected bits with real balanced Hadamards from their blank state. This gives $N=2^m$ orthogonal routing labels of equal squared amplitude $u=1/N$. Choose $2\le k<N/2$. The first $k$ labels take the eight directions RRLLLLLL and the next $k$ take RRRRLLLL. They reach $-4$ and 0 respectively on L. The remaining labels take RRRRRRRR, reaching 8 on R. Straight or exchange permutations controlled by the unchanged label realize these routes reversibly from the specified input. This assumes local label-controlled switches and coherent preparation. The symbols $u$ and $s$ below refer to kinematic masses, before a probability rule is applied.

At each of the two local wells choose one setting just before tick nine. In setting S exchange the rails for one fixed member of the arriving group and leave the other members on L. In setting P leave them all on L. Stream once. The local output pair has masses $((k-1)u,u)$ or $(ku,0)$ in the two settings. Its two positions are $(-5,-3)$ in the first apparatus and $(-1,+1)$ in the second. The spectator at 9 receives $s=1-2ku>0$ in every setting. No final path from either switch can reach the spectator in one tick on the stipulated graph. A physical comparison requires late choices, isolated control lines, a fixed detector bank and the same distribution of prepared launches in every setting.

Record the original-launch spectator probability $r_{SS},r_{SP},r_{PS},r_{PP}$ under independent assignment of the local settings. The ordinary rule predicts $s$ in every setting. Our test uses only
$$ C=r_{SS}-r_{SP}-r_{PS}+r_{PP}=0. $$
The expected equality follows if both remote choices preserve the spectator probability at all four settings. Arithmetic on four measured rates does not require measurements of an unobserved variable shared by two launches.

## Why the signs cannot cancel

On one launch let the detector use a finite nonnegative score $f$, positive at each occupied mass, the same at every well. Its position probabilities are $f(w_i)/\sum_j f(w_j)$ with the kinematic masses already pooled by site over rail and label. The score distribution may be arbitrary and must be common to all four assignments. Allow a positive empty-well score. The number of instrumented wells remains fixed when a packet changes shape.

Write $Z$ for the all-S normalizer. Replacing one split packet by a pooled packet adds
$$ d=f(ku)+f(0)-f((k-1)u)-f(u). $$
The denominators are $Z,Z+d,Z+d,Z+2d$, all positive. At fixed $f$ the four-rate contrast is
$$ C_f=\frac{2f(s)d^2}{Z(Z+d)(Z+2d)}. $$
Thus exact zero after averaging forces $d=0$ almost surely. No componentwise invariance, persistence across launches or known absolute spectator frequency was assumed. For $N=8,k=3$, the squared site-mass rule gives spectator rates $2/7,2/9,2/9,2/11$, hence $C=16/693$. A concave score can give the opposite signed change on switching a single packet. Its four-rate contrast still has the same sign. Statistical independence of the source choices from the detector population and identical per-type *responses* at both local apparatuses are essential. For different changes $d_1,d_2$, the numerator in the common-denominator expression becomes $f(s)d_1d_2(2Z+d_1+d_2)$ and can be negative.

For finite errors put $x=d/(Z+d)$ and $r=f(s)/(Z+d)$. Positivity gives $|x|<1$ and $C_f=2rx^2/(1-x^2)$. A simultaneous upper bound $C\le e$ on the underlying rate combination gives
$$ \Pr(r\ge b,\ |x|\ge\delta)\le \frac{e(1-\delta^2)}{2b\delta^2},\qquad b>0,\quad 0<\delta<1. $$
An inefficient spectator weakens the bound. A fixed positive spectator efficiency may be included in $r$, using the observed per-launch rates and a setting-independent efficiency for that type. Reconstructing counts by conditioning on a click somewhere in the bank requires an additional loss model. A shared additive background cancels in $C$ but changes how a measured dark rate constrains $f(0)$.

## The boundary and the unidentifiable part

At each $k$ with $2\le k<N/2$, the zero contrast gives $f(ku)-f(0)=f((k-1)u)-f(0)+f(u)-f(0)$. Finitely many such comparisons imply the score is affine on $0,1/N,\dots,(N/2-1)/N$ for almost every type. A zero probability at a calibrated empty well forces $f(0)=0$ almost surely. On nested dyadic grids with one type population, countable intersection and the common interior mass $1/4$ (for $N\ge8$) fix a single type-specific slope $a>0$ at all dyadics below $1/2$. Continuity gives $f(w)=aw$ on $[0,1/2]$. Monotonicity alone gives the interior by squeezing. With monotonicity and an additional ordinary heavy-site frequency in the table $(1/2,1/4,1/4)$, the lower bound $f(1/2)\ge a/2$ makes that site's conditional probability at least $1/2$; equality on average forces the endpoint for almost every type. An empty spectator at $2ku=1$ has no discriminatory power for a dark detector. A component with positive pedestal can still contribute there.

There is an exact converse for the class with continuous scores, a calibrated dark response and the programmability just specified. Suppose ordinary position probabilities hold on every finite normalized table. Below and at half occupancy the result above gives $f(w)=aw$ almost surely, simultaneously in $w$. For $1/2<w<1$ a table has at most one dominant site of mass $w$. All minor sites together contribute $a(1-w)$ to its denominator. Ordinary statistics are then **equivalent**, for each such $w$, to the single condition
$$ E\!\left[\frac{a}{f(w)+a(1-w)}\right]=1. $$
Necessity follows from a two-site table $(w,1-w)$; sufficiency follows by multiplication by each minor mass and complementing for the dominant site. A finite set of dyadic preparations gives constraints on the tested grids. If every dyadic mass can be tested with one common population of continuous scores, the condition extends to each interior real $w$ by local dominated convergence, since the integrand is at most $1/(1-w)$. A sharp table by itself requires only $f(1)>0$. Fatou's lemma for the limit from below gives $E[a/f(1)]\le1$ for continuous scores satisfying the interior condition. If the scores are also nondecreasing, $f(w)\ge a/2$ above half, so bounded convergence yields equality of that expectation.

Two different strictly increasing continuous scores that pass every such one-launch experiment are given in the [fluctuating-score construction](Fluctuating_site_scores_and_paired_launches.md). They agree with the ordinary score below half. Above half their reciprocal normalizers are $1\pm h(w)$ with equal type weights and a small smooth perturbation supported on the upper half. Every minor-bin rate averages to its ordinary value. Without endpoint regularity even $1/2$ may escape: set $h=0$ except $h(1/2)=1/2$. Take $f_\pm(w)=w$ except $f_\pm(1/2)=1/(1\pm1/2)-1/2$. If just one half occurs its minor bins average correctly. If two halves occur they exhaust the table and each type gives the same equal split. This establishes sharpness for the unrestricted single-read class.

Routing labels remain in this experiment. A detector resolving and normalizing within each protected label can follow its single occupied position and pass every setting with any within-label exponent. The protocol also admits an ordinary fair label sampler. It addresses the identifiability of scores applied to whole-site masses, and it does not identify the physical origin of squared amplitudes. The [single local write](Local_writes_and_dyadic_site_scores.md) covers masses up to one for a *stable* score. The [paired acquisition](Fluctuating_site_scores_and_paired_launches.md) extends that result to a persisting type under additional composition assumptions. The present half-occupancy result uses neither assumption. Causality arguments for linear *dynamics* starting with the quantum trace rule address a different inference.
