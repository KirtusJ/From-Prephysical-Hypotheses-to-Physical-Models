# Calibrating a binary site detector with a spreading walk

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

At a fixed clock, pass a randomly selected set of wells to one detector port and the complement to the other. Alternatively, record a site first and apply the same independent mask to its label afterward. The latter procedure can be programmed to hit with probability $p$ for **any** complete distribution of site records. For the former, the walk's spread can place the coherent mass delivered to the first port within a controlled distance of $p$ on every mask choice. Comparing the two rates at two programmed settings then bounds the binary detector response at a specified mass. The detector may have jumps. The preparation uses one forward walk, without a supplied balanced multi-bank source or a reversed flight. [Earlier three-tick comparison](A_binary_record_on_one_three_tick_walk.md)

## One random origin, two operations

Use the real signed Hadamard walk from a sharp site and right rail on the line, or on a ring long enough that the light cone has no wrap-around aliases. At tick $t$, let $w_i=n_i/D$ be each site's summed squared rail mass, where $D=2^t$ and $n_i$ is the sum of two integer squares. The integer recurrence preserves $\sum_i n_i=D$. This is the source kinematics under test. The argument does not interpret the $w_i$ as ordinary detection frequencies.

Order the sites from left to right. Put their successive boundaries $a_i=\sum_{j<i}n_j$ on the residue circle of length $D$. For a choice $0\le k\le D$, choose an integer origin $R$ uniformly and include site $i$ in the mask precisely when $(a_i-R)\bmod D<k$. The programmed fraction is $p=k/D$. Thus every site, including a zero-weight site at a repeated boundary, is included with exactly this probability. A boundary of $D$ uses residue zero. As a function of site order the aperture is one consecutive block, possibly split into two intervals at the cut. Uniform random starts on cumulative-size intervals also occur in systematic sampling. [Unequal-probability systematic sampling](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1966.10480902)

* In the offline comparison, read a complete site label $I$ independently of $R$ and score the single bit $[I\in M_R]$. Conditioning on *any* $I$ gives hit probability $p$. One may draw $R$ after the position record. Count losses and unresolved sites on their incident denominators. For incomplete readouts the certain hits and possible hits give separate bounds.
* In the physical comparison, set the aperture **before detection**. Coherently route all selected sites to one flag port without identifying the site, retain other coordinates as unmeasured degrees of freedom, and read the two pooled ports. Let $G_t(s)$ denote the decoded hit rate at selected mass $s$, assumed nondecreasing across the implemented settings at this clock. Its values lie in $[0,1]$. It may include an independently randomized physical port interchange. The source law and detector population must be independent of the origin and comparison setting. Label-sensitive routing or a read that scores each site separately before combining them can give the offline law without performing this measurement.

Let $S_R(k)=\sum_{i\in M_R}w_i$ and $H_t(k)=\mathbb E_R G_t(S_R(k))$. The assignments imply $\mathbb E_R S_R(k)=p$ exactly. Moreover, for **every** origin,
$$ |S_R(k)-p|\le b_t,\qquad b_t=(\max_i n_i-1)/D. $$
To see this, extend the cumulative boundaries periodically along the integer line. Let $T(v)$ be the first boundary at or after $v$. The selected mass in units of $1/D$ is $T(R+k)-T(R)$, because a site contributes its width when its left boundary is in the half-open interval. Each forward roundoff $T(v)-v$ lies between zero and $\max_i n_i-1$. Their difference gives the bound, including endpoints $k=0,D$ and repeated dark boundaries.

Full $t$-bit randomness is unnecessary for a coarser grid of settings. If $L=2^a$ divides $D$ and $k$ is a multiple of $D/L$, take $R=u D/L$ for uniformly assigned $u=0,\ldots,L-1$. A translated half-open integer interval of length $k$ contains exactly $kL/D$ of these origins, so **each site's inclusion probability remains exactly $p$**. The per-origin mass bound also persists. $L$ determines the spacing of tested settings. This simpler sampler need not cover every possible physical mask.

## A same-clock bound, including detector jumps

For a desired mass $x$, choose $k_-,k_+$ so that $S_R(k_-)\le x\le S_R(k_+)$ at every chosen origin. Then
$$ H_t(k_-)\le G_t(x)\le H_t(k_+). $$
The average rates on the two outside settings directly bracket the middle read. In particular, if the measured physical rates are within $\eta$ of the offline programmed fractions there, choose a grid point at or just below $x-b_t$ and another at or just above $x+b_t$. Full or empty masks supply the ends when needed. On the $1/L$ grid this yields $|G_t(x)-x|\le\eta+b_t+1/L$. Exact extrema of $S_R$ can give closer bracketing settings. Finite data require simultaneous statistical limits on the rates used; agreement at one pair of settings only calibrates the masses it brackets. One may select the settings from the computed kinematics before observing the counts.

For example, at $t=512$ on the line, the heaviest site has mass below $0.046077$. With $L=1024$, a $5/8$ programmed mask and the stable normalized power-two detector $G(s)=s^2/[s^2+(1-s)^2]$, all detector inputs lie between $5/8-b_t$ and $5/8+b_t$. Thus even the worst origin gives a physical hit probability above $0.65$. Averaging all 1,024 origins gives **0.73487884** against the exact offline rate **0.625**. This one-setting comparison uses ten classical bits per mask. It predicts a gap exceeding 0.1098 per complete incident read. The two arms must share the source and coordinate convention.

The same clock has an actual contiguous target $x=\sum_{j\le310}w_j\simeq0.6270233$. With *all* $D$ origins the largest programmed fraction that keeps every selected mass at most this $x$ is about $0.58109708$. The smallest fraction keeping every mass at least $x$ is about $0.67036872$. These are programmed settings; their measured hit rates would bound $G_t(x)$. The [checker](check_shifted_site_mask.py) weights intervals between mask changes rather than visiting $2^{512}$ origins. It keeps even the smallest site masses. The experiment tests the shape of the pooled read and retains the possibility that the signed source itself is inaccurate.

For the unwrapped localized Hadamard source the clock can be increased to make $\max w_i$ arbitrarily small. Grimmett, Janson and Scudo give an absolutely continuous weak limit for the kinematic position divided by $t$, supported on a bounded interval. If an atom retained mass $\varepsilon>0$ along a subsequence, its scaled position would have a convergent subsequence in $[-1,1]$. Each fixed closed neighborhood of the limit point would then have limiting mass at least $\varepsilon$, forcing an atom. Hence $\max w_i\to0$. Applying this theorem to the squared coordinate norm uses it as a property of the unitary solution. The experimentally tested premise is how the apparatus responds to this norm. A fixed small ring can have revivals. [Grimmett, Janson and Scudo, Hadamard walk and weak limit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0309135)

If a mesh of settings agrees with the offline rates within $\eta_t$, the above bound holds across the clock's menu. Decreasing $\eta_t$, increasing $L$ and spreading the source would therefore force uniform agreement at the clocks tested under the stated response assumption. The monotone curve may change from clock to clock. Extrapolation back to a different short flight still requires a detector-stability test. With a common calculated profile, individual detector types may fluctuate and correlate with other hidden properties; their independently sampled average is the curve being constrained. If each type's actual site distribution lies within total variation $\rho$ of the programmed one, expanding the deterministic brackets by $\rho$ controls its mass error. A type-specific target-bin mass or an unbounded change of source across settings requires further calibration.

## Keeping the offline control when the input rail is unknown

At a known launch site, the signed Hadamard walk assigns a common mass to each complete distance shell for **every** initial two-rail state. The effect of the pair of output sites at $+r,-r$ is a scalar times the identity. At the origin the single site's effect is scalar as well. Order the shells by increasing radius and apply the shifted-boundary construction to their masses. Each particular site inherits its shell's exact inclusion probability $p$. For each realized mask the summed physical mass is also the same for all initial coins. A fluctuating input coin, including one correlated with a detector type, therefore changes neither quantity. The successor bound uses the largest shell in place of the largest site. A random mask is still assigned independently of each launch, and all shells it passes still have to feed one coherent binary flag. [Reflection identity and its domain](Position_masks_for_an_unknown_input_coin.md)

For a hidden detector type $A$ with a nondecreasing response $g_A$, write $G(s)=\mathbb E_A g_A(s)$. Every mass $S_R$ in this shell experiment is common to all types, so the physical mean is $\mathbb E_R G(S_R)$. The two-setting bracket above now applies to this population curve even when the input coin and detector type are dependent. It gives no such transfer to a target site whose mass changes with that type. At tick 512 the 1,024 origins pass each site at exactly 640 settings. Across all origins the selected mass ranges from $0.57147165$ to $0.67838781$. The power-two detector predicts a mean hit rate of $0.73468154$ against the offline $0.625$ for any input coin.

A smaller menu uses eight bins at tick 256. Sort the shells from heaviest to lightest, breaking ties by increasing radius, and put each into the currently lightest bin, breaking ties by lowest bin number starting at zero. Choose one of eight equally likely starts and pass five successive bins cyclically. Each individual site is passed at exactly five starts. Direct addition of the shell weights gives the eight selected masses, all in $[0.62053199,0.62802892]$. Averaging the power-two response over precisely those eight masses gives $0.73528107$, a predicted excess of $0.11028107$ per complete incident read. Thus three classical random bits suffice to compare an exactly known offline rate with a narrow range of physical masses for any launch coin. This is a finite certificate for this walk and clock. The bins may combine separated radii, so it calls for a more programmable aperture than a contiguous block of shells.

## Two adverse controls

**Independent random site bits can miss a finite-clock alternative.** At three ticks the occupied masses are $(5,1,1,1)/8$. Include every site independently with some probability $p$. Assign a continuous monotone complementary response the values $(0,1/12,1/6,1/4,3/4,5/6,11/12,1)$ at mass numerators $(0,1,2,3,5,6,7,8)$, interpolating linearly. Conditional on exactly one of the four occupied sites being selected, the mean response is $1/4$. Two-site subsets pair with their complements and give mean $1/2$. Three-site subsets give $3/4$. Thus the physical and offline mask averages equal $p$ for *every* inclusion probability, while the heavy-site read is $3/4$ instead of $5/8$. The three asymmetric midpoint comparisons resolve this particular ambiguity at the same clock. [Three-tick proof](A_binary_record_on_one_three_tick_walk.md)

**Geometric arc length need not track mass.** Put weight $1/h$ at each of $h$ neighboring wells on a circle of $N$ wells, with $h\to\infty$ and $h/N\to0$. A uniformly shifted *spatial* arc of a fixed positive fraction $p<1$ of the circle selects every well with marginal $p$, but at most $2(h-1)$ origins cut the packet interior. Almost all masks select its entire mass or none of it. Any endpoint-sharp detector then gives asymptotic rate $p$, regardless of its behavior at interior masses. The cumulative-weight rule avoids this ambiguity at the cost of specifying the source profile. A basis-label sampler that chooses a site with its kinematic mass and passes the membership bit also reproduces every mask mean. Distinguishing that sampler from a detector acting on a coherent pooled flag requires interventions that test coherence. These results concern the measured probability curve on accessible preparations. The absolute embedding of a separately named algebraic phase remains open.
