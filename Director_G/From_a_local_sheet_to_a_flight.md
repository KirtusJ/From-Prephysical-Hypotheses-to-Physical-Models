# From one localized sheet to an accelerated packet

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Start one walker at a known site and rail, with its physical sheet localized at address zero on an $M$-cycle. Use that same sheet to select the fundamental character pair, prepare the base packet, and drive its flight. On the square law for the lifted coordinates, the first source has exactly $2/M$ weight in the pair $\{1,-1\}$, for $M\ge3$. A fast test can retain almost all that weight without measuring a sheet address. Its choice of which winding is called one uses an independently calibrated generator. A coordinate probability law and the stipulated preparations remain premises. [Refinement measurements](Refining_the_sheet.md) · [Staircase force transfer](Refinement_and_free_fall.md)

## Sheet selection and its clock

Put $\delta=(\pi/(MF))^{1/4}$ for a fixed $F>0$. One site represents $\delta^2$ in length and one ordinary tick lasts $\tau=\delta^3$. On each sheet probe choose a uniform independent integer $l$ between zero and $M-1$. A balanced split, deterministic straight/turn controls and a final balanced recombination of two returning paths supply an observed sign $Y$. The sheet winding difference of the paths is $l$. Both output records are kept, the rail sign is decoded using the known incoming rail, and one return step restores the sharp base at the same home. The known rail can initialize the next probe. Unresolved sheet amplitudes remain coherent within a given record. At fixed character $k$, the conditional contrast is $\mathbb E[Y\mid l,k]=\cos(2\pi kl/M)$. These path controls cost at most $2\max(4,\lceil\sqrt{M-1}\rceil)+4$ ticks per supplied lag. [Routed sheet test](Routed_sheet_filter.md)

Use the rational score $Z=Y[1-4\min(l,M-l)/M]$. The routed test's cyclic second-difference bound gives conditional mean at least $2/5$ in the target pair and at most $1/9$ elsewhere. Accept when the mean of $r$ independent scores at fixed $k$ is at least $1/4$. Hoeffding's inequality on $[-1,1]$ bounds either type of conditional error by $\beta=e^{-25r/2592}$. An alternative score $Y\cos(2\pi l/M)$ has means $1/2$ and zero by Fourier orthogonality, and gives $\beta=e^{-r/32}$. The rational score needs only counted lags and rational arithmetic. Neither score supplies an optical phase reference. Write $q=2/M$, $A$ for acceptance and $\eta_A$ for the posterior unwanted weight. Then

$$
q(1-\beta)\le P(A)\le q+(1-q)\beta,\qquad
\eta_A\le\frac{(1-q)\beta}{q(1-\beta)+(1-q)\beta}.
$$

For example, take $r=\lceil(2592/25)\log(M/\eta)\rceil$ with $\eta=M^{-\gamma}$, $\gamma>0$ fixed. Then $P(A)=(2/M)[1+O(\eta)]$, $\eta_A=O(\eta)$, and the sheet test takes $O(\sqrt M\log M)$ ticks, or $O(M^{-1/4}\log M)$ in flight time. Known final rail $L$ can be routed to $R$ at a recorded adjacent launch site, or the direction schedule can be reflected. If both possibilities are compared in position, register this site as $Y_0$; a one-site offset vanishes on the flight scale. The guided excursion can still require physical radius $O(\sqrt M\,\delta^2)=O(1)$ and has available link speed $1/\delta$.

Supplying the lags has a cost. For example, a balanced native step from a sharp rail, a site-and-rail read, and a directed return give a fair bit at a known site independently of the character. Put $b=\lceil\log_2 M\rceil$ and reject a $b$-bit number if it exceeds $M-1$. At most $J$ batches for each of $r$ probes give a total timeout probability at most $r2^{-J}$, independent of the label. The completed lags remain uniform. Taking $J=O(\log M)$ with a sufficiently large coefficient makes timeout $o(1)$, multiplies all quoted selection yields by a common $1-o(1)$, and adds at most $2rbJ$ carrier ticks. This count assumes that recording and applying each bit and the selected routing instructions fits the stated clock up to bounded factors. If the schedule must instead be distributed afresh to each well, its delivery time must also be charged.

For comparison there is a selector using only ordinary signed Hadamard ticks and a full **base** read after each block of three. From a sharp input there are four single-word records of probability $1/8$ each and two interference records of total probability $1/2$. Their constructive/destructive labels can be recovered from the observed entrance rail and exit port. Conditional on an informative block the destructive probability at folded label $d$ is $q_d=\sin^2(\pi d/M)$. For either launch parity, exchanging the two path interiors changes the staircase gain by one; an incoming rail flip exchanges the two interference signs. Thus the successive informative indicators are independent conditional on the persistent label. The affinity of the resulting Bernoulli laws for $d,j$ is $\cos(\pi|d-j|/M)$. A maximum-likelihood test for the target $d=1$ on $s$ informative blocks bounds each pairwise error by this affinity to power $s$. For $s=CM^2$, summing the bounds over non-target labels gives at most $2\sum_{u\ge1}\exp(-C\pi^2u^2/2)$. This bounds the target miss probability and, weighted by the localized priors $1/M$ or $2/M$, bounds false acceptance divided by $2/M$. Hence $O(M^2\log(1/\eta))$ informative records suffice for acceptance of order $2/M$ and conditional error $O(\eta)$. They cost six native ticks each in expectation. Conversely, $q_0=0$ whereas $q_1\sim\pi^2/M^2$; with $o(M^2)$ such records, both hypotheses give an all-constructive informative transcript with probability tending to one. An order-one distinction needs order $M^2$ trials on this instrument. Its expected physical time already grows as $M^{5/4}$ for fixed discrimination quality. A known final position supplies the release origin; returning to a prescribed home would take additional routing. ## A higher-yield source with sheet-addressed routing

A stronger apparatus can increase the target weight before the phase-preserving selector. From one localized sheet prepare a real vector on $L=2^{\lfloor\log_2 M\rfloor}$ consecutive addresses, amplitude $+1/\sqrt L$ on its first half and $-1/\sqrt L$ on its second half. The base finishes at a known well and rail. At stage $j$ two guided paths return to that well with sheet-gain difference $2^j$ and opposite rails. Their translated intervals are disjoint and adjacent. A switch conditioned on the physical sheet interval aligns both branches onto one rail; at the last doubling a relative minus supplies the sign change. The disjoint address supports permit this reversible routing. The $O(\log M)$ stages use $O(\sum_j\sqrt{2^j})=O(\sqrt M)$ streamed ticks if the interval-dependent switches, signs and base routing fit the assigned slots. These operations require access to sets of sheet addresses. [Addressed sheet preparation](Addressed_sheet_preparation.md)

A Fourier sum gives the pair mass

$$
q_{\rm addr}=\frac{8\sin^4(\pi L/(2M))}{ML\sin^2(\pi/M)}
      \ \ge\ \frac4{\pi^2}.
$$

Indeed $\sin^2(\pi x/2)=(1-\cos\pi x)/2$ is concave on $1/2\le x\le1$, where its endpoint values equal $x$, so $\sin^2(\pi x/2)\ge x$. Use $x=L/M$ and $\sin(\pi/M)\le\pi/M$. At dyadic orders the exact weight is $8/[M^2\sin^2(\pi/M)]$. Translating the block changes only Fourier phases.

Replace $q=2/M$ by $q_{\rm addr}$ in the selection inequalities. A desired posterior error $\eta$ now needs only $r=O(\log(1/\eta))$ probes, uniformly in $M$. With no base retries the completed fraction is $(q_{\rm addr}+O(\eta))p_0[1+o(1)]$ for the direction yield $p_0$ below. With base retries it is $q_{\rm addr}+O(\eta+\delta^{1/4})$. At fixed tolerance the selector costs $O(\sqrt M)$ ticks, versus $O(\sqrt M\log M)$ from the localized sheet. For a pure limiting ray take $\eta\to0$; $\eta=M^{-\gamma}$ costs $O(\sqrt M\log M)$ in both cases, with different per-launch yields.

With an additional complete sheet-address read after each rejection the same walker can retry the sheet stage. The read leaves a known localized address $y$. Translate the next preparation's control intervals by $y$; selector probabilities are unchanged. This avoids transporting the register to address zero, provided its controller can obtain $y$ and program the switches at the charged cost. Rehoming the known base rail and site is also charged. Each attempt succeeds conditionally with probability at least $(4/\pi^2)(1-\beta)$ times the independent probability of completing its lag samples. If this is at least $s_0>0$, $J_{\rm sh}$ attempts leave timeout mass at most $(1-s_0)^{J_{\rm sh}}$. The posterior on first success satisfies the same bound as in a single attempt because that bound holds after each earlier failure and reset. For $\eta=M^{-\gamma}$ take $J_{\rm sh}$ proportional to $\log M$. All sheet attempts then cost at most $O(\sqrt M\log^2 M)$ ticks, or $O(\delta\log^2 M)$ in physical time, with the preceding bounded lag sampler. A bounded-speed sheet read or a serial controller may require more time. Keep each sheet timeout in the final record.

## Direction selection on the retained sheet

From the sharp accepted base, take $m$ two-tick blocks, choosing the closest even $m$ to $\delta^{-5/2}$. After each block read only the outgoing rail, coherently across sites, and retain a switch. Under constant staircase gain, character $k$ sees linear phase slope $h_k=\pi k/M$ on its occupied parity. From one initial site the success probability $p_m(k)$ is the Fourier mean of $\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}|\sin(u+(2j+1)h_k)|^2$. Set $p_0=\binom{2m}{m}4^{-m}$, the zero-field value, and let $p_*=p_m(1)=p_m(-1)$. Hölder's inequality gives $p_m(k)\le p_0$ for **every** label. On the $4M$-ring the same inequality applies to the discrete mean: the mean of each shifted $\sin^{2m}$ is its constant term when $2m<4M$. In the target sector $m^3h_1^2=O(\delta^{1/2})$ gives $p_*/p_0\to1$, while $p_0\sim\delta^{5/4}/\sqrt\pi$. [Proof and signed-schedule controls](Field_reversal_during_preparation.md)

Let $B$ denote successful direction selection. Even for a sheet posterior concentrated on the most troublesome other label,

$$
\Pr(k\notin\{1,-1\}\mid A,B)
 \le \frac{\eta_Ap_0}{(1-\eta_A)p_*+\eta_Ap_0}.
$$

Thus $\eta_A=o(1)$ suffices; dividing the unwanted input by the small direction yield would overestimate its contribution. The comparison requires a sharp known base in every accepted selector record. When the sign of the staircase gain alternates between successive two-tick blocks, or is set to zero during base preparation, all labels have yield $p_0$ exactly and the sheet weights do not change. For the constant field, one-pass full success satisfies

$$
\Pr(A,B)\sim\frac{2}{M}\frac{\delta^{5/4}}{\sqrt\pi}.
$$

The direction procedure takes $2m\tau=O(\delta^{1/2})$ before release on the successful branch. Its failures and the much larger rejected sheet population are part of the initial ensemble.

A second schedule recovers the factor $p_0$ in yield. After $A$, disable the gain while preparing the base. Upon any direction failure read the *base* site, return through nearest-neighbour links to home, reset the rail and retry. Neither the detector nor routing writes to the retained sheet. The known sharp base after the initial sheet read is a product with its conditional sheet state; all these gain-off operations act on the base alone. Each successful retry makes exactly the same base packet and leaves the sheet posterior unchanged. With $p_j=\binom{2j}{j}/4^j$, the expected number of active two-tick blocks per attempt is $\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}p_j=2mp_m$. Including retries gives $4m$ expected moving preparation ticks and at most $2m$ expected return slots. A bounded number of slots per read, reset and route step keeps the total expected slot count $O(m)$. This discrete estimate applies at the present $m$ without the separate scaling used for the four-tick force plate. [Early-abort clock and return proof](Renewal_launch_and_classical_records.md)

Set a direction-stage deadline of $\delta^{1/4}$ physical time. Markov's inequality gives timeout probability $O(m\tau/\delta^{1/4})=O(\delta^{1/4})$, conditional on $A$. Retain TIMEOUT as an output. Release immediately on completion and measure flight time from that record. A common release clock would also require a coherent hold or calibrated delay. The total completed fraction now approaches $2/M$ in ratio, which is optimal to leading order for any nondemolition filter with vanishing posterior error from this uniform sheet source: $\Pr(\mathrm{complete})(1-\eta_{\rm final})\le2/M$. With one localized sheet, the accepted mass still tends to zero. Independent full trials with fresh localized sheets would require a mean asymptotic to $M/2$ trials per completion.

## The ensuing quantum propagation and terminal position

For the usual real outgoing rail, the two curvature signs have equal limiting weights. Ordinary field-on propagation for $n\delta^3\to T>0$ then puts the terminal distance from $Y_0$ at $FT^2/2$ in probability. To select a single side, start the sharp-rail preparation on $R$ so that even $m$ ends on $R$. Act next on the *extended* base packet by the compensated returning loop: take two complementary triangular paths of length $4b$, switch the field with signs $-,+,+,-$ in blocks of $b$, and use $b$ nearest to $\sqrt{\pi/(8h_1)}$. The loop returns each site to itself, removes the common momentum shift and creates $L/R=e^{-4ih_k b^2}$. The staircase parity phases are the same on both arms. At the target these spins approach the two opposite circular polarizations, selecting curvatures $b_{\rm curv}=\operatorname{sgn}k$. The additional time is $O(\sqrt M\,\delta^3)=O(\delta)$ with real programmable routing. A fixed bounded flight time uses $n=O(M^{3/4})$ ticks. In all these stages the coherent base excursions from home are $o(M)$ sites on the controlled schedule, including $2m=O(M^{5/8})$ within any direction attempt, so the finite ring reproduces the unwrapped packet flight.

On a compact momentum chart the target spinor follows the Schrödinger envelope with Hamiltonian $b_{\rm curv}p^2/2+\operatorname{sgn}(k)FY$, with scaled action $\varepsilon=\delta$. The norm error over bounded $T$ vanishes by the forced-envelope estimate; chart truncation costs vanishing norm for the prepared packet. Both conjugates now predict the same terminal position $Y_0-FT^2/2$. Tracing the retained sheet makes the terminal law a mixture of orthogonal sectors, so the total variation distance to its target-sector prediction is at most the posterior unwanted weight. For any fixed tube containing that position in its interior, the probability of completion **and** tube arrival divided by $2/M$ tends to one in the gain-off retry protocol. In the constant-field one-pass protocol the corresponding divisor is $(2/M)p_0$. These are rates per original launch and a statement about one final-time record. The quantum norm approximation is for a shrinking-action sequence; a fixed-action experiment would retain wavepacket spreading. [Norm estimate](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md) · [Two-peak preparation](Base_preparation_and_two_rays.md) · [Calibrated one-sided flight](Returning_loops_and_one_classical_ray.md)

With addressed sheet retries followed by gain-off base retries, the unconditional probability of hitting the tube on the original square-law ensemble tends to one. The missing probability is bounded by the sheet timeout, the base timeout, posterior leakage and target-sector tube error, each tending to zero. The combined preparation and polarization deadline is $O(\delta\log^2 M+\delta^{1/4}+\delta)$ in the stated control clock. This strengthens the $2/M$ nondemolition yield by spending address-dependent operations that change the sheet's character weights. In either protocol the position comparison is relative to the registered release origin, or to one common home when the base is rehomed across attempts.

## A persistent counting component changes the per-launch yield

Suppose a fraction $c$ of launches follows path counting throughout. This extension requires fair branching at every $H$ **conditional on the complete prior record**, deterministic transport through each straight, swap or addressed permutation, and settings chosen independently of the type. It also requires the projective reset and conditional control laws used above on the square component. Fixed-walk terminal classification alone does not establish transfer to these instruments. In counting, whichever arm reaches the analyzer, its two outgoing rails have equal conditional weights, even at lag zero. The return preserves a known sharp base input. Thus every score $Yf(l)$ has conditional mean zero and lies in $[-1,1]$. The one-sided Hoeffding bound for the $1/4$ threshold is $\alpha_C\le e^{-r/32}\le\beta$. [Count and square records](Counting_and_character_records.md) · [Selector and controls](Routed_sheet_filter.md)

For one completed lag schedule put $Q=(1-c)W$, where $W=2/M$ for the localized sheet and $W=W_M$ for the addressed source. Per original launch the accepted **target** mass is at least $Q(1-\beta)$, while accepted nontarget mass, including count, is at most $(1-Q)\beta$. For $Q>0$ the counting fraction among acceptances is at most $c\alpha_C/[Q(1-\beta)]$. An independent timeout in sampling the lags multiplies both joint yields by its completion probability.

A sheet-address read resets the square component to a localized sheet on each rejection. It leaves a persistent counting walker in the counting component. For repeated identical, translated attempts, if the type-specific probabilities of passing are $a_C,a_Q$, respectively, then after $j$ failures the counting fraction among survivors is $c(1-a_C)^j/[c(1-a_C)^j+(1-c)(1-a_Q)^j]$. This generally increases when $a_Q>a_C$. After at most $J$ attempts the **unconditional** probability of a counting launch passing is at most $cJ e^{-r/32}$, also when attempt lengths vary but this bound holds after every failure. With the addressed source, $J\to\infty$, $J e^{-r/32}\to0$ and the vanishing square-component errors above, the probability of preparation and tube arrival per original launch tends to $1-c$; the timeout probability tends to $c$. For a fixed finite $r$, a count attempt has strictly positive pass probability: all lags zero and all corrected rails positive is one passing event. Unlimited independent retries at that $r$ eventually admit even a counting walker. Redrawing the walker and its type on failure is another physical source.

Conditional fairness can be challenged using one hidden fair bit $B=\pm1$ retained for the run. Report $Y=B\,\operatorname{sign} f(l)$ every time, with $\operatorname{sign}0=1$. Each prescribed lag has fair one-probe rail marginals. At $M=4$, $f(l)$ takes $1,0,-1,0$ on the four settings. With fresh uniform lags the mean score tends to $B/2$, so half the launches eventually exceed the threshold. This rival violates the conditional counting law and identifies the record assumption that supplies the exponential bound.

## A finite-action quantum flight from the same source

There is a different scaling of this same circuit in which the terminal probability density retains a finite width and spreads. Set $\varepsilon=1$, $\delta=(\pi/(MF))^{1/3}$, site pitch $\delta$, tick duration $\delta^2$, and take even $m$ with $m\delta^2\to\lambda>0$. Use the same sheet selector and gain-off direction preparation. The latter's one-attempt survival is now $p_0\sim\delta/\sqrt{\pi\lambda}$. The retry protocol takes $4m$ moving ticks on average, or $4\lambda+o(1)$ units of time, plus at most $2m$ expected return slots and the stated read/reset costs. A success at the first attempt already needs $2\lambda+o(1)$ time. A deadline $D$ gives timeout at most $C\lambda/D$ for large orders, given the assumed controller clock. This permits fixed finite accuracy at finite deadline or almost-sure release at finite mean time. A diverging deadline drives this bound to zero. With polynomially shrinking sheet error, the addressed sheet preparation and its retries take $O(M^{-1/6}\log^2 M)$ time in these units, and the polarization loop takes $O(M^{-1/6})$. Their maximum physical radius can grow as $O(M^{1/6})$. This fixed-action preparation has a finite intrinsic duration even when its sheet controls become fast.

Use coordinates relative to the release home. On one occupied spatial parity the two Fourier valleys a distance $\pi$ apart are exact replicas up to the parity sign. Fold one full period of length $\pi$ around $q_*=-\pi/2$. With $\widehat\psi(q)=\sum_x e^{-iqx}\psi(x)$, its norm measure is $dq/\pi$ after folding, so the scaled wavefunction is $\sqrt{\delta/\pi}\,\widehat\psi(q_*+\delta p)$ with measure $dp$. On finite rings the integrals are the corresponding grid sums; the scaled grid spacing $\pi/(2M\delta)$ tends to zero, and the source and flight have finite propagation inside the ring at each order since $m+n=O(M^{2/3})$. Up to its constant phase, the normalized gain-off packet on rail $R$ has folded scalar shape $\sqrt{\delta/(\pi p_0)}\cos^m(\delta p)$. It converges in $L^2(dp)$, with zero extension outside the folded interval, to

$$
G_\lambda(p)=(\lambda/\pi)^{1/4}e^{-\lambda p^2/2}.
$$

Pointwise convergence follows by expanding $\log\cos(\delta p)$, and $\log\cos u\le-u^2/2$ supplies an integrable squared bound. At the chosen valley the curvature polarizations are $u_b=(1,-ib)/\sqrt2$, $b=\pm1$. The incident rail $R$ has equal squared projection on both. The replicated valley has the same polarizations and exchanged eigenvalue labels because $U(q+\pi)=-U(q)$. It contributes no extra normalized weight after folding.

In target sheet character $s=\pm1$, after removing the separate known carrier eigenphase on each band, the two envelopes evolve with Hamiltonians $bp^2/2+sFY$. On every fixed compact $p$ interval the forced spinor error vanishes with $\delta$ at fixed time $T$. A smooth cutoff of $G_\lambda$ followed by removal of that cutoff controls the full tails in norm. In the terminal position characteristic function at wavenumber $\xi$, the shift in lattice momentum is $\delta\xi$. Opposite local band eigenvectors at those shifted arguments have overlap $O(\delta|\xi|)$ on the compact chart; the omitted tails have vanishing contribution in the cutoff limit. Thus summing the two physical rails eliminates cross-band interference in this macroscopic observable even though the pure incident spinor is coherent. No temporal averaging is required.

The initial envelope has position variance $\lambda/2$, momentum variance $1/(2\lambda)$ and zero symmetrized covariance. The linear potential shifts the mean while preserving the freely evolved covariance. More explicitly its momentum evolves by $p\mapsto p-sFT$; the quadratic dynamical phase of curvature $b$ shears the position according to $Y\mapsto Y+bTp-bsFT^2/2$. Evaluating the Gaussian characteristic function gives an equal mixture of two normal densities centred at $Y_0\pm FT^2/2$, each with variance

$$
\sigma_T^2=\frac{\lambda+T^2/\lambda}{2}.
$$

The compensated loop above, now tuned with $h_1=F\delta^3$, instead prepares curvature $b=s$ up to vanishing norm error, on both parity replicas. Both conjugates then give **one Gaussian**, centred at $Y_0-FT^2/2$ with the same $\sigma_T^2$. This quantum spread and the previous semiclassical concentration describe different choices of action, packet width and clock on one specified microscopic carrier. [Forced spinor propagation](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md) · [Free Gaussian and native two-rail records](Folded_field_and_native_gaussian.md)

One coherent read beyond position uses a balanced comparison of a base packet with itself translated an even $j$ sites while keeping the rails and sheet. Choose $j\delta\to\ell$ and correct the output contrast by the known carrier sign $(-1)^{j/2}$. Summing both rails gives the real part of the base-translation overlap. Its limiting value is

$$
e^{-\ell^2/(4\lambda)}\cos(FT\ell).
$$

Indeed the squared momentum density translates by $-sFT$ along the exact force characteristic, independently of the coin rotations. The expectation of $e^{i\ell p}$ in the shifted Gaussian then yields the expression above. A position-diagonal mixture has zero translation overlap at any nonzero $j$. The overlap read requires a coherent translation and recombination between arms, including a common elapsed clock and no uncontrolled force difference during analysis. Measuring the base position first destroys the comparison.

Twenty finite spinor flights use $M=128,512,2048,8192$, both conjugate sectors, a zero-field control and the exact compensated loop angles. At $F=1$, target time $0.75$ and unit position wavenumber, the unpolarized characteristic-function error for $k=1$ was $0.02670,0.10140,0.04179,0.03953$, respectively. The errors are nonmonotone. For the loop preparation the corresponding final error was $0.001982$, and for the even-translation contrast it was $0.000307$. In their geometry the free-evolution phase differences cancel for constant gravity; the optical pulse phases supply the measured signal. These are empirical examples of calibrated transport and recombination.
