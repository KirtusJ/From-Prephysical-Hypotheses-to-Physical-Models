# One adjacent swap stores the first direction in site parity

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A single walker, launched at one site, can carry its first direction as a readable bit through any number of free ticks. The record uses one reversible nearest-neighbour operation and the bipartite structure of the line. Later wave packets overlap in spatial extent while their populated sites stay distinct. A finite detector distinguishes an on-site marker response from a radius-one response even though **every** isolated sharp launch gives identical terminal probabilities under the two laws.

## A one-layer record

Launch at \((0,R)\). Each native tick applies the signed coin \((u,v)\mapsto(u+v,u-v)\), followed by conditional right and left movement. One common factor \(1/\sqrt2\) per native tick gives normalized amplitudes. Immediately after tick one, the two raw ports \((-1,L)\) and \((1,R)\) both carry \(+1\). During a single additional clock slot swap \((1,R)\) with the vacant \((2,R)\). Hold every other port at its absolute location. The swap is a deterministic site permutation on the right rail, applied identically to field and marker, with neither splitting nor normalization change. Then resume native ticks. The control uses a switchable adjacent route and matched hold; an unmodified homogeneous walk alone supplies neither. The blind control idles all ports for the same slot and uses the same read clock. After \(h\) resumed ticks there have been \(t=h+1\) native ticks and \(t+1\) slots including the routing or hold layer, for equal-duration slots. Readout time is an additional device cost. No recorded measurement or postselection occurs during preparation.

Write \(b=1\) for a right first step and \(b=0\) for a left first step. A formal bit that preserves this first direction and then propagates the two sectors separately has raw amplitude \(D_t(b,x,c)\) at logical position \(x\), rail \(c\). For the physically routed field \(A_t(y,c)\) one has exactly

\[
 b=\mathbf1_{\,y\equiv h\pmod2},\qquad x=y-b,\qquad A_t(y,c)=D_t(b,x,c).
\]

The left start stays at \(-1\) and reaches only sites of parity \(h+1\), while the right start moves to \(+2\) and reaches only sites of parity \(h\). A free tick commutes with translation. The transferred right branch evolves as the translation by \(+1\) of its original tagged branch, and the left branch evolves unchanged. The two contributions have disjoint physical site supports at every tick. This proves the formula for all \(h\). Reading final position and rail, together with the active clock, recovers the complete record \((b,x,c)\). The same construction holds on an even periodic ring with the coordinates taken modulo its size and \(N\ge4\) so the three addressed sites are distinct. The line's numerical panels use an unwrapped cone. Starting from a sharp site and taking one compulsory nearest-neighbour move for *all* ports at each clock time leaves every occupied site on a common parity, regardless of the splitter signs. Enabling a one-link displacement of just one branch, with a matched hold elsewhere, changes precisely this restriction.

For a marker whose aggregate right fraction depends only on the two same-site arrivals and is the homogeneously calibrated law \(h_0(u,v)=(u+v)^2/[2(u^2+v^2)]\), the joint terminal mass is \(D_t(b,x,c)^2/2^t\). At the first native tick the two marker masses are \(1/2\), and the swap transports them without change. At a populated site the ordinary untagged marker induction applies because only one first-direction branch can enter that site. On an isolated sharp left-rail input, the first-step and later local law must also hold. This result uses a common on-site rule independent of the route or hidden memory and presumes no influence of the router on later settings. Density of untagged directions from one right launch, with continuity and rescaling, can calibrate that common on-site law. The equality of raw tallies to those of the formal bit holds without those marker premises. It gives no equivalence of the physically distinct marker instruments by itself. ## A radius-one marker that passes every sharp control

Put \(s(y)=u(y)^2+v(y)^2\) for the current physical signed field. A second aggregate marker sends the fraction

\[
 h_1(y)=\frac12+
 \frac{u(y)v(y)}{s(y-1)+s(y)+s(y+1)}
\]

right, with fraction \(1/2\) when the denominator vanishes. It is local to the three sites, homogeneous under a common scaling of their field, and lies in \([0,1]\) by \(2|uv|\le s(y)\). The neutral choice at a completely dark neighborhood gives a total stochastic update even if the rival has routed marker mass to a dark field port. Both laws conserve one marker on the entire line and share the field update, deterministic swap and terminal read. Whenever a sharp, isolated launch undergoes only free ticks, both adjacent sites of each populated site are dark by parity. Then \(h_1=h_0\) at every relevant site. The same reasoning covers any ordinary single-parity input with matching initial marker mass.

For the routed launch the two initial arms remain distinct, yet at logical tick three (\(h=2\)) the physical site \(y=2\) carries \((1,1)\) in the first-right sector. Its left neighbor \(y=1\) carries \((1,0)\) in the first-left sector, and \(y=3\) is dark. Before this split the two markers agree everywhere, since every earlier populated pair has at most one nonzero rail. The parent marker mass is \(2/8=1/4\). Now \(h_0=1\) while \(h_1=1/2+1/3=5/6\). The next left daughter is the physical port \((1,L)\) at native tick four, decoded as \((b=1,x=0,L)\). Its **joint probability among all launches** is zero under the on-site law and \(1/24\) under the radius-one law. The outgoing signed tally is zero in either case. Both markers preserve the total first-direction weights \(1/2,1/2\), since every subsequent move flips the physical parity regardless of the stochastic choice. This detector addresses whether neighboring signed fields affect routing. Input-side calibration alone cannot exclude this rival.

The equal-duration blind hold provides a separate test of the record operation. At native tick three, the ordinary logical left port \((-1,L)\) receives the two words LRL and RLL with signs \(+1,-1\), hence mass zero in the blind control. After the swap these words terminate at physical left ports \(-1\) and \(0\), with squared masses \(1/8\) each. Summing those two decoded logical outcomes gives \(1/4\). The two operations have distinct complete terminal labels. This control verifies what storing and ignoring the bit does to interference. Neither finite comparison is a measurement of an actual apparatus.

## Separation at a specified spatial range

For a finite subsequent horizon \(H\), an alternative moves the two first-tick ports to \(-D,+D\) instead, preserving the rail and delaying both equally. With disjoint, outward nearest-neighbour swaps this takes exactly \(D-1\) parallel layers for \(D\ge1\), the minimum needed to transport a port through that distance at one link per layer. During \(h\le H\) free ticks each arm stays in its radius-\(h\) cone. The distance between the cones is at least \(2D-2H\). Thus \(2D-2H>r\) keeps the other arm outside any radius-\(r\) memoryless update on the occupied support. To compare with an isolated arm, the *full local input* must match, including apparatus settings, field normalization whenever relevant, and local marker population. A matched experiment can retain the same initial split and transport and change only the distant opposite arm at release. The matched arm alone still carries half the launch ensemble.

With a stateful finite-speed controller, equality of the local snapshot of the walker is insufficient. One must also match all prior influences in the backward cone of the tested outcome, including the routing history. For an intervention confined to the opposite release center and its future free cone, if controller effects move at most \(c\) links per free tick, its causal radius after \(h\) ticks is at most \(\max(1,c)h\). Hence \(2D>(1+\max(1,c))H\) is sufficient through \(H\) for point-local readout when initial apparatus states and shared history match elsewhere. An extended read region adds its radius to the right side. A marker carrying a bit set by an earlier router is itself a local source of memory, irrespective of distance to the other arm. Large spatial separation can test a device under a controlled causal intervention. It does not establish the same response when two tags occupy a common splitter. Spin-dependent transport has been used experimentally in a four-step atomic quantum walk; the parity encoding and neighboring-field model here are theoretical protocols.
