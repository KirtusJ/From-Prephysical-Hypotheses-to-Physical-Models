# Classical path records in the switched eight-well walk

The [mixer-visit parity experiment](A_protected_fourth_port_and_balanced_pair_mixers.md) hides the difference between two algebraic embeddings when its memory is unread. This alone does not make the walk classical. Two applications of the same addressed Hadamard return a sharp incident port with probability one in either embedding. Both paths visit two mixers, so the visit-parity bit leaves the return intact. A classical fair routing at each encounter returns only half the launches.

## A record that distinguishes the paths

Keep the same fixed schedule of addressed mixers and a sharp start. At mixer j assign b_j=1 to its second outgoing rail and zero to its first outgoing rail or any bypass. The ordered record b determines the path by induction. Given the current mode and the schedule, a bypass has a unique successor. At an encounter the bit specifies exactly one exit. Two distinct paths therefore differ in at least one recorded bit, including paths that later reach the same final port.

Copying each b_j into a fresh blank orthogonal record and ignoring the records at the end removes every cross term in the port weights. A path with k encounters has squared weight 2^(-k) in either embedding. This is exactly the path law of the classical time-dependent Markov chain that preserves every bypass and divides incident probability equally between the two mixer outputs. It also gives the joint law of any recorded path predicates by summing the path weights. At physical ticks between mixers the flow is deterministic under the stream and exchanges. This is an exact classical stochastic limit for this finite transport model under the stated record and readout assumptions.

## A phase implementation with a test for residual coherence

One can reproduce the endpoint law without storing the full record. Independently choose a fair bit r_j for each scheduled mixer, regardless of whether a path visits it. Apply a minus sign to the second outgoing rail precisely if r_j=1. For path b the sign is (-1) to the power r dot b. The average product of signs for two distinct records is zero by pairing masks differing at their first differing bit. Equal records survive with weight one. Thus averaging the **final probabilities** over masks gives exactly the same classical endpoint law in either embedding. Conditioning on masks retains coherent interference. This construction needs independent mask entropy and coherent phase control. A balanced sharp coin gives equal single-bit probabilities in both embeddings; independence of repeated preparations remains a distinct requirement. Random phases reproduce the distribution of a read; the mask itself does not contain the traversed path. For related coin decoherence on the line see [Brun, Carteret and Ambainis](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0210180).

There is a distinct prediction if a blank bit stores only r dot b by reversible XOR, and is then ignored. Diagonals survive and every distinct pair of paths collides in that bit for exactly half the masks. The averaged endpoint panel is one half the unrecorded coherent panel plus one half the classical panel. Preparing the bit in its antisymmetric state instead gives the sign implementation above, since a toggle on that state has eigenvalue -1. For the two-identical-mixer return test, the rates are 1 with mixer-visit parity, 3/4 with a blank masked path bit, and 1/2 with either full path recording or independent random signs. The three-mixer embedding test has classical panel (3/8,3/8,1/4,0), accidentally identical to its unread visit-parity panel. The two-mixer control resolves this degeneracy. For the sixteen-mixer test the classical return is 10923/32768 versus 513/1024 with unread visit parity.

No rates here were measured. These constructions explain the disappearance of interference conditional on a specified record or phase mechanism and the squared readout of each embedding. They do not derive that readout, autonomous production of records, or continuum classical mechanics.

## An autonomous finite record and its first revival

Repeat the same addressed two-port H block, initially in port zero. After each H, XOR the exit into the first slot of an L-bit tape and rotate the tape by one slot, with L>=1 and every slot initially zero. This is one fixed reversible update per block. In a frame moving with the tape it writes to slot j modulo L at block j. It requires a reusable record and tape shift, but no fresh randomness, clearing, or externally chosen write slot. For at most L blocks the tape distinguishes all histories. Even at L+1 the tape and the current port together determine the history: the current port supplies the most recent bit and hence resolves the only repeated slot. At L+2 there are more nonzero paths than joint port-and-tape basis labels. Record collision by itself does not yet predict the terminal frequencies.

In fact the terminal return probability is exactly one at positive multiples of T = 4L/gcd(L,2), and exactly one half at every other positive clock. To prove it, propagate the port observable Z backwards. For one block with slot j, the update is controlled-X after H. Backwards it sends port Z to port X and port X to port Z times tape X_j; every tape X is fixed in the moving frame. An odd number of blocks leaves a port X, of zero expectation in the initial state. After 2k blocks there is a port Z with a product of k tape X factors on alternate slots, spaced by two. Their slot sequence has period m=L/gcd(L,2). The expectation on the blank tape is one precisely if every slot appears evenly. A partial cycle leaves unequal adjacent visit counts differing by one, so this requires a whole even number of cycles, k divisible by 2m. This proves the formula including L=1. The first revival can occur sooner with an extra bit: three slots give clock 12 and four slots clock 8. This is a property of cyclic slot reuse and this observable.

One permanent unread copy of the **first** exit, made after the first block, removes every subsequent port revival. In the backwards calculation tracing this added record dephases the port after block one. It either preserves a Pauli string or kills it, so a zero expectation cannot become nonzero. At a revival the full backwards observable is the initial port Z alone. Immediately after block one it must therefore be the forward image of this Z, namely port X times first-slot X, which the extra dephasing kills. Consequently the terminal panel stays fair forever for this repeated block with a retained first mark. A fair marginal at every clock is insufficient evidence for independent classical paths. A finite tape plus one bit has bounded record capacity while the number of positive classical paths doubles each block.

The [integer forward calculation](check_cyclic_exit_record.py) checks both preparations for L=1 through 10 through two revival periods each. This is an autonomous record mechanism within the stipulated dynamics, with a specified blank initial condition. It does not establish why physical memories start blank or retain records indefinitely. For a different walk with cyclically reused coins see [Brun, Carteret and Ambainis](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.67.052317).

### A correlation that survives the fair terminal panel

Read and retain the port at block one, then read it again d blocks later. Given the first outcome, both the port and the tape start this interval in computational basis states. The backwards Z calculation works from any starting slot. For positive d divisible by T it returns exactly port Z; otherwise it includes a port X or a nonempty product of tape X factors, with zero expectation in either conditional preparation. Thus the two fair records agree with probability one at multiples of T, and with probability one half at all other positive gaps. In contrast, independent fair routing at every H gives one half at every positive gap.

For the gap T, place one extra unread port copy at an intermediate block 1+s, with 0<s<T. Propagating the terminal Z back to that cut gives port X for odd T-s or port Z for even T-s, possibly multiplied by tape X factors. An unread Z copy kills the first observable and preserves the second. The agreement frequency therefore drops to one half for odd s and stays at one for even s, while both endpoint marginals remain fair. The forward checker verifies the unchecked interval and both cut parities. The comparison can reject an interpretation in which that inserted record only reveals a preexisting value without disturbing any future record. An invasive instrument remains admissible.

### Why port records alone cannot identify the tape

There is an exact rival for every port-only experiment on this apparatus. Draw L independent fair signs once per preparation, reuse them cyclically, and evolve a two-mode amplitude at each block by H followed by a sign on port one from the current slot. In the moving tape frame, a simultaneous X eigenvector of all slots is preserved by every controlled toggle. In that sector the toggle on the tape is precisely the sign on the port. The blank computational tape has equal squared overlap with all sign strings. Any intervention confined to the port and external probes acts identically on the tape indices; tracing the tape annihilates every off-diagonal sector. The resulting *complete record distribution* is therefore the uniform mixture over the same fixed sign strings, even for adaptive intermediate port measurements. One must retain the sign string across interventions; drawing new signs at every step would discard the observed revival. The port itself still evolves coherently within each sector.

This establishes a boundary for inference. The timed records distinguish this process from the fair memoryless path law and expose disturbance by the inserted read. They cannot distinguish the reversible tape from periodic classical phase noise acting on a coherent port. A preparation, intervention or read addressing the tape is needed to discriminate specified models of its internal state. For the broader question of when classical noise reproduces decoherence see [Gu and Franco](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31272169/).

### Addressing one written slot with finite balanced gates

Stop after the first mixer and its exit copy. The port and the written slot are in (|00>+|11>)/sqrt(2), up to an overall sign in the conjugate embedding. Provide independent measurement settings for the two systems. On the port read either Z or X, using the balanced port gate for X. On the slot, allow one extra initially empty local mode 2 and the addressed balanced gates of the [four-port construction](A_protected_fourth_port_and_balanced_pair_mixers.md). This local analyzer is an additional physical requirement on the tape side.

For its first setting assign +1 to projection onto w=(u,1/2,(1-h)/2), with h=1/sqrt(2) and u=(1+h)/2, and assign -1 to both remaining outcomes. To prepare w from slot zero use the ordered mixers 01,12,01 and then exchange modes 1 and 2. Reverse those gates for the read. For the second setting change the sign of component 1 of w using a local sign reversal. On the occupied two-mode subspace the two signed observables are 2 v v-transpose minus identity with v=(u,+/-1/2). The third detector is included among the minus outcomes; no events are discarded.

For either slot setting the Z correlation is u^2-1/4. The two X correlations are respectively +u and -u. This follows directly from the two equal real Bell amplitudes, or by summing the [six joint detector outcomes per setting](check_port_slot_chsh.py). The CHSH combination Z-plus + Z-minus + X-plus - X-minus is therefore **5/4 + sqrt(2)**, about 2.66421. Applying field conjugation consistently to the whole circuit gives **5/4 - sqrt(2)**, about -0.16421. In contrast, if both local outputs are generated from their own settings and shared classical information independent of those settings, the absolute score is at most two: for each pair of predetermined slot signs either their sum or their difference vanishes, and the other has magnitude two. Averaging over local randomness preserves the bound. [Clauser, Horne, Shimony and Holt](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.23.880)

A port-only phase-noise model has no slot observable. Any extension predicting local outcomes on both sides under the shared-classical-information assumptions obeys the bound. An observed violation could exclude that class without identifying a unique internal tape. This is a specified Bell-state preparation and analyzer inside a quantum model, not a derivation of entanglement from prephysical dynamics. Isolation of the two setting/read events, complete outcome accounting, setting independence and coherent access to the written slot have not been demonstrated experimentally.

### A joint witness robust to the two embeddings

Keep the same Bell preparation, port settings and three-outcome slot read. Prepare the slot analyzer vector from zero with the ordered mixer pairs 02,01,12,20,12,10,12 and reverse the sequence to read it. Its entries are a=7/8, b=3/8+sqrt(2)/16, c=1/8-3 sqrt(2)/16. Negating the second component supplies the other slot setting as before. All slot outcomes are counted. The CHSH combination is 2(a^2-b^2+2ab) = 163/64 + sqrt(2)/8. Conjugating the entire circuit changes only the sign of the radical in this score. Thus the two predicted values are 2.72365 and 2.37010, both above the local bound two. The checker enumerates all joint outcomes for both embeddings. Every setting-independent convex mixture of the two joint laws retains a violation. Extending the earlier single-walker detector classification to all bipartite detectors is a separate assumption.

In this family the smaller of the two scores can approach the quantum bound 2 sqrt(2). Indeed the three-mode paired rotation density proved in the [four-port study](A_protected_fourth_port_and_balanced_pair_mixers.md) allows both analyzer vectors of one word to approximate (cos(pi/8),sin(pi/8),0) simultaneously. Substitution in the score formula proves the limit. No finite word with coefficients in Q(sqrt(2)) can attain that positive bound in both embeddings: conjugation would send an exact score of 2 sqrt(2) to its negative. The seven-mixer word is a finite example, with no optimal-length claim. These are predictions under the same independent-setting and isolation premises as the preceding test. [Quantum CHSH bound](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00417500)

### A bound using conditional slot rates alone

The seven-gate test needs less than a single embedding choice for all bipartite counts. Assume only that the port marginal for each port setting is independent of the slot setting. It may be biased and different for Z and X. For each occupied port outcome and setting pair, let the conditional slot-plus probability lie between the two pure-embedding predictions for that branch. The interpolation may change independently in every branch. This interval is a separate operational hypothesis about conditional preparations and can be checked against coincidence counts. A port outcome of zero frequency needs no conditional value.

Write a=7/8 and let b range over the two endpoints 3/8 +/- sqrt(2)/16, with b_min and b_max in increasing order. Conditional on a Z port zero, the slot-plus probability is a^2 at either setting. For port one the upper endpoint is b_max^2. Thus the sum of the two Z correlations is at least twice the smaller of 2a^2-1 and 1-2b_max^2, which is 17/16. For port X, conditioning on the positive port outcome gives slot-plus probabilities with endpoints (a+b)^2/2 and (a-b)^2/2 for the two settings respectively. For the negative outcome those two formulas exchange. Since a>b_max>0, even the smallest bright probability minus the largest dim probability is 2a b_min. The port marginal is the same in both slot settings, so subtracting the X correlations gives at least 4a b_min for any port bias. Adding yields **S >= 19/8 - 7 sqrt(2)/32 = 2.06564078...**. The checker independently minimizes the signed conditional terms at their endpoints for each branch.

This gives a test of calibrated conditional rate intervals in place of a bipartite detector classification. Port no-signalling alone does not imply those intervals. If each interval is widened at both ends by an absolute probability error epsilon, the score lower bound falls by at most 8 epsilon, since one signed binary mean changes by at most 2 epsilon per setting pair. Under exact port marginal invariance, epsilon below (19/8 - 7 sqrt(2)/32 - 2)/8 suffices for a strict violation. This tolerance is a bound on true rates; finite data would also require simultaneous uncertainty bounds.
