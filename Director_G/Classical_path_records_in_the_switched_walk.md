# Classical path records in the switched eight-well walk

The [mixer-visit parity experiment](A_protected_fourth_port_and_balanced_pair_mixers.md) hides the difference between two algebraic embeddings when its memory is unread. This alone does not make the walk classical. Two applications of the same addressed Hadamard return a sharp incident port with probability one in either embedding. Both paths visit two mixers, so the visit-parity bit leaves the return intact. A classical fair routing at each encounter returns only half the launches.

## A record that distinguishes the paths

Keep the same fixed schedule of addressed mixers and a sharp start. At mixer j assign b_j=1 to its second outgoing rail and zero to its first outgoing rail or any bypass. The ordered record b determines the path by induction. Given the current mode and the schedule, a bypass has a unique successor. At an encounter the bit specifies exactly one exit. Two distinct paths therefore differ in at least one recorded bit, including paths that later reach the same final port.

Copying each b_j into a fresh blank orthogonal record and ignoring the records at the end removes every cross term in the port weights. A path with k encounters has squared weight 2^(-k) in either embedding. This is exactly the path law of the classical time-dependent Markov chain that preserves every bypass and divides incident probability equally between the two mixer outputs. It also gives the joint law of any recorded path predicates by summing the path weights. At physical ticks between mixers the flow is deterministic under the stream and exchanges. This is an exact classical stochastic limit for this finite transport model under the stated record and readout assumptions.

## A phase implementation with a test for residual coherence

One can reproduce the endpoint law without storing the full record. Independently choose a fair bit r_j for each scheduled mixer, regardless of whether a path visits it. Apply a minus sign to the second outgoing rail precisely if r_j=1. For path b the sign is (-1) to the power r dot b. The average product of signs for two distinct records is zero by pairing masks differing at their first differing bit. Equal records survive with weight one. Thus averaging the **final probabilities** over masks gives exactly the same classical endpoint law in either embedding. Conditioning on masks retains coherent interference. This construction needs independent mask entropy and coherent phase control. A balanced sharp coin gives equal single-bit probabilities in both embeddings; independence of repeated preparations remains a distinct requirement. Random phases reproduce the distribution of a read; the mask itself does not contain the traversed path. For related coin decoherence on the line see [Brun, Carteret and Ambainis](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0210180).

There is a distinct prediction if a blank bit stores only r dot b by reversible XOR, and is then ignored. Diagonals survive and every distinct pair of paths collides in that bit for exactly half the masks. The averaged endpoint panel is one half the unrecorded coherent panel plus one half the classical panel. Preparing the bit in its antisymmetric state instead gives the sign implementation above, since a toggle on that state has eigenvalue -1. For the two-identical-mixer return test, the rates are 1 with mixer-visit parity, 3/4 with a blank masked path bit, and 1/2 with either full path recording or independent random signs. The three-mixer embedding test has classical panel (3/8,3/8,1/4,0), accidentally identical to its unread visit-parity panel. The two-mixer control resolves this degeneracy. For the sixteen-mixer test the classical return is 10923/32768 versus 513/1024 with unread visit parity.

No rates here were measured. These constructions explain the disappearance of interference conditional on a specified record or phase mechanism and the squared readout of each embedding. They do not derive that readout, autonomous production of records, or continuum classical mechanics.
