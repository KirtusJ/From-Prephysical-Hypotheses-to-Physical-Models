# Moving contact and a phase echo for two distinguishable walkers

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Two labelled walkers moving towards one another can scatter at a common site. Their separated positions measure transmission and reflection. Detecting a relative phase requires preparing two coherent incident alternatives or bringing the outgoing alternatives together again. Both procedures below use local, positive-time updates, signed amplitude addition and squared-coordinate readout. They concern specified preparations and gates. The existence of a physical interaction with these rules remains open.

## When the encounter occurs

Let a coarse cell have length \(\ell\) and a coarse tick last \(\Delta\). A microtick takes \(\Delta/2\), and each particle travels \(\ell/2\), so its speed is \(c=\ell/\Delta\). Place \(A,B\) at fine coordinates \(2a,2b\), with \(a<b\), and launch them with velocities \(+1,-1\). Their free paths \(2a+t,2b-t\) meet precisely at \(t=b-a\), at coordinate \(a+b\). The contact acts on the arriving state before the next stream. At \(j>0\) microticks after contact, the transmitted pair occupies \((a+b+j,a+b-j)\); the reflected pair occupies the reverse. Reflection thus follows the minimum and maximum of the two free trajectories, the equal-mass hard-point paths on this ballistic input. The unordered positions coincide on the two alternatives. A named particle's outgoing direction can distinguish them. [Formal local update and trajectories](lean/CollisionRecords.lean)

An odd coarse gap \(b-a\) puts the meeting at an odd microtick. Reading only even ticks misses the common site. A model that **also applies the interaction only at even ticks** misses the collision itself. In the fine dynamics the reflected labels return to their preceding coarse positions over those two microticks; the transmitted labels exchange positions. If the initial separation is odd even on the chosen fine mesh, equal-time one-step walkers can cross without ever sharing a vertex. These are different physical schedules. In the finite causal control \(A\) starts at 0 and \(B\) starts at either 12 or 18 fine units. The first possible meeting is at tick 6. The local \(A\) position-and-rail distributions coincide through tick 6 and first differ at tick 7, with total variation \(16/25\). ## What a single collision can distinguish

For a fixed-time ideal contact use the ordered incoming channels \(RL,LR\). At coincidence apply \(C=\frac15\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&-4\\4&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and then stream. Elsewhere, or for equal incoming directions, the contact acts as the identity. A single \(RL\) launch gives transmission \(9/25\) and reflection \(16/25\). A classical random collision with those column probabilities reproduces the single-input records. For a coherent input \((RL\pm LR)/\sqrt2\) arriving at the same spacetime point, the probability that \(A\) leaves moving right is \(1/50\) or \(49/50\), respectively. A stochastic update of the two classical incident populations gives \(1/2\) for each input. A persistent reflection marker also gives \(1/2\) if the marker is ignored. Reading it in the balanced basis instead gives, for the plus input and \(A\) moving right, joint probabilities \(1/100\) and \(49/100\) in its two outputs. This discrepancy tests the coherent incident preparation and recombination. The labelled contact itself does not commute with particle exchange. [Scattering update and independent path calculation](check_collision_scattering.py) · [Marker and label controls](results/collision_scattering.json)

A relative phase can also be stored between **different arrival times**. Superpose launches from \((-2,2)\) and \((-4,4)\) with a common or opposite sign. They encounter at ticks 2 and 4. At tick 7 the sharp joint position-and-direction probabilities are identical for both signs because the alternatives then occupy distinct bins. A balanced, coherent mixer at each detector combines its two possible positions on each outgoing direction. The two detector bits agree with probability one for the plus preparation and zero for the minus preparation. Transporting those bins into the same local detector mode takes additional time beyond the seven flight ticks. [Complete time-bin joint records](results/collision_scattering.json)

## Reading an imaginary reflection amplitude

Let \(X\) exchange \(RL\) and \(LR\). At contact let each walker carry a real plane with quarter turn \(J_R^2=-I\), for \(R=A,B\). The scatterer implements \(S_A=(3I+4XJ_A)/5\). Its separated transmission and reflection probabilities for a single incident channel are again \(9/25\) and \(16/25\), for either sign of \(J_A\). Mirrors now return both alternatives coherently to the origin, and a known direction relabelling restores the incident basis. Apply the independently held analyzer \(S_B^\mathsf T=(3I-4XJ_B)/5\). The amplitude at the original incidence is \((9I+16Q)/25\), where \(Q=-J_AJ_B\). Conditional on both implemented gates passing their checks, the named return probability is

\[
 P_{\rm return}=\frac{337+288\langle Q\rangle}{625}.
\]

Aligned planes return with probability one, opposite planes with \(49/625\), and independent real product planes with \(337/625\). Keeping a distinguishable outward path record suppresses the cross term and gives \(337/625\) in every grade. Coherently reversing the marking operation restores the contrast. The echo detects a relative sign between scatterer and analyzer; simultaneous conjugation of both planes preserves it. The finite implementation uses a recurrent region around the origin. Two synchronized Walsh transforms enclose a signed permutation on eight selector labels. At the common pass port the numerator is \(3I+4XJ_R\) over 8, so the pass probability of each gate is \(25/64\) for any normalized data state. The first gate fails with probability \(39/64\); passing the first and failing the second has joint probability \(975/4096\). Both pass with probability \(625/4096\). Thus the unconditional joint mass for a named return and both passes is \(337/4096\) for independent blank planes. If the two independent references are first compared, the negative-grade record, both passes and named return together have probability \(49/8192\) without correction and \(625/8192\) after correction. Both grade records can be used. The reported schedule makes one attempt with each gate and counts both kinds of failure. [Synchronized gates, read order and failure ports](check_collision_phase_reference.py)

During a contact slot the walkers leave the origin, reflect one fine site away and return two microticks later. The computation in the interaction region occupies seven such slots per Walsh gate and one per pass check. Launching three fine sites from the origin and using outer mirrors at radius four gives 47 ticks with a blank-plane anchor, or 53 with a measured grade and equally timed feedforward. The ideal one-tick \(C\) above and this finite processor have different clocks and different scattering matrices. The recurrent processor requires local clean modes, signed permutations, balanced mixers, mirrors and control of the contact sequence. [Duration and apparatus assumptions](results/collision_phase_reference.json)

Ordering matters for loaded data. A deterministic anchor aligns a blank child plane. An occupied child can instead end up in the opposite grade, and correcting a measured grade can conjugate previously encoded child data. In the tested blank launch, alignment before the echo gives return one conditional on both passes; applying the blank anchor after both gates leaves the return at \(337/625\). A grade measurement and child reflection **after the \(A\)-only scatter but before the coherent \(B\) read** still works because those operations commute with the first scatter. No such timing equivalence has been assumed for general loaded interactions. A sheet already calibrated to its plane also needs the corresponding sheet reflection if the calibration is to persist.
