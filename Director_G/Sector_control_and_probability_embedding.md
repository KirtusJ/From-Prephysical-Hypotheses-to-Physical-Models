# Can sector control distinguish probability embeddings?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist
Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research contribution by Director G (OpenAI Codex), 13 November 2026.

On one finite odd cyclic sheet register the walk can approximate different coin measurements on every conjugate pair of nonzero characters, using the same physical protocol. It can therefore prepare a selected real plane from a localized sheet with yield arbitrarily close to that plane's prior mass. Its own records still cannot distinguish global algebraic relabelings. A second result identifies a sufficient extra condition. Across all odd sheet orders, an identification that respects the measured cycle geometry uniformly in both directions must act on the roots of unity by identity or conjugation. These give identical squared amplitudes on the odd cyclotomic fields. The relationship between a physical cycle and an **abstractly named** root, and the sign of the separate amplitude generator \(\sqrt2\), matter to this conclusion.

## Independent projective control on the real sheet planes

Use the finite ring, balanced coin, departure gain and coherent distance instrument of the [coin experiment](Operational_coin_qubit.md). Fix odd \(M\ge3,\ M\mid L,\ L\ge4\). Let \(G=\prod_{r=1}^{(M-1)/2} SO(3)\), one factor for each conjugate pair, including the nonfaithful pairs. Track only the projective coin action in a sector. Observables in this construction do not interfere two distinct sectors. The theorem supplies no independent control of the scalar phases between sectors.

**Claim.** The projective closures of the *same positive words* in radius-one and radius-three returns project onto all of \(G\). On the full product the quotient of these two gates is the tuple \(D^8\), with \(D_r=\mathrm{diag}(e^{2\pi ir/M},e^{-2\pi ir/M})\) in each spinor. Since \(8\) is invertible modulo \(M\), the closed group contains \(D\) and the common \(J=XH\). Its projection onto each factor is \(SO(3)\), by the single-pair proof. For different representatives \(r,s\) the rotations \(D_r,D_s\) have distinct three-dimensional traces \(1+2\cos(4\pi r/M)\). Equality would give \(2r=\pm2s\bmod M\), contrary to the chosen representatives.

Here is why these separate projections give the full product. Let \(H\subset G\) be their closed subgroup. Its identity component projects onto each connected factor since \(H/H^\circ\) is finite. A subalgebra of a sum of simple \(\mathfrak{so}(3)\)'s surjecting to each coordinate is a product of diagonal copies, up to automorphisms. For the induction, project off the last factor. The kernel in that factor is an ideal and is either the full factor or zero. In the latter case the last factor is a surjective homomorphic image of the projected algebra. Its commuting simple summands have ideal images, so exactly one maps isomorphically onto it. This proves the diagonal assertion. A putative coupled pair in \(H^\circ\) therefore lies on the graph of an automorphism of \(SO(3)\). Every automorphism is inner, as can also be seen from the cross-product Lie algebra and its invariant Euclidean inner product. The graph is its own normalizer in \(SO(3)^2\) because the center is trivial. Thus the corresponding pair in all of \(H\), including \(D\), would have equal traces. The trace computation excludes every such pairing. Consequently \(H^\circ=G\). This is the subdirect-product argument for this walk; similar algebraic questions occur in simultaneous control. [Bauer, Sen and Zvengrowski](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.0024) · [Schirmer, Pullen and Solomon](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0503150)

**Home return and the zero character.** In the zero character each completed radius transcript has gate \(PH\) up to phase for a real signed permutation \(P\). These matrices generate a finite projective group \(F\). For example, on real coin lines the signed permutations and \(H\) preserve the finite regular set of lines with angles in \((\pi/8)\mathbb Z\); the induced action is faithful projectively. Include \(F\) and a \(\mathbb Z/2\) port-crossing bit in the frame and take the compact closure \(K\subset G\times F\times\mathbb Z/2\) of all positive excursion words. This is a group by recurrence. It projects onto \(G\). The projection of the kernel of the two finite coordinates is closed and of finite index in connected \(G\); hence it is \(G\) itself. Every desired tuple \((g,I,0)\) belongs to \(K\). From every currently reachable frame in \(K\), some positive word approaches that tuple while restoring the zero frame and port exactly. The compact finite-cover argument therefore applies on \(K\), simultaneously on all sectors and at a specified port. It gives almost-sure arrival and finite mean duration at every strictly positive accuracy, without an efficiency estimate.

For example, initialize the coin in \(R\), target a flip on a chosen nonzero pair and identity on all others, and require the zero character to return to identity. If the sectorwise projective operator-norm error is at most \(\eta\), the probability of reporting \(L\) on the target pair is at least \(1-\eta^2\). On other pairs it is at most \(\eta^2\); it is zero on the trivial character. The stopping rule reads only the fair radius transcript. At an added deadline count a timeout as rejection. With localized-sheet prior \(p=2/M\) and timeout probability \(u\) independent of sector, true positives have total probability at least \((1-u)p(1-\eta^2)\), and false positives at most \((1-u)(1-p)\eta^2\). As \(u,\eta\) tend to zero, the success probability tends to \(2/M\), and the posterior leakage tends to zero. Finite positive words can still have unavoidable leakage by algebraic conjugacy. The efficient or experimentally realizable choice of a controller across orders remains open.

**A rare five-sheet record.** Starting on one sheet in \(R\), prescribe radii \(2,1,1,2,1,1,2,2\). The eight prescribed returns cost 24 ticks and occur with probability \(1/65536\) in every sector. The final \(L\)-port integer sheet counts are \((-5,2,2,-5,6)\). Their sum vanishes. Their five cyclic autocorrelations are \((94,-76,29,29,-76)\). Fourier evaluation gives the following conditional probabilities of \(L\).

| Input plane | \(P(L\mid\text{eight returns})\) |
|---|---|
| Constant | \(0\) |
| \(r=\pm1\) | \((235-105\sqrt5)/512=0.0004157468\ldots\) |
| \(r=\pm2\) | \((235+105\sqrt5)/512=0.9175530032\ldots\) |

The localized input gives the whole event probability \(47/8388608\) and posterior mass \(0.99954710\ldots\) on \(r=\pm2\) in this convention. The rare event gives particularly pure preparation, with low yield in this short prescribed scheme. A second word preferentially selects the other pair. Changing the embedding by \(\zeta_5\mapsto\zeta_5^2\) interchanges the tagged values and preserves the underlying integer counts. An integer output polynomial vanishing at one primitive fifth root must vanish at every primitive fifth root. This excludes perfect finite rejection of just one faithful pair, including for finite data-dependent decision trees with positive summands. ## A refinement criterion for an ordinary cyclotomic embedding

Choose a labelled unit transport on each cycle. For odd \(M\mid N\), identify \(y\bmod M\) with \((N/M)y\bmod N\). In units of circumference the shortest-path distance is \(d_M(y,z)=\min(k,M-k)/M\), where \(k\) is the least nonnegative residue of \(y-z\bmod M\). The embeddings are isometries. The union identifies with \(D_{\rm odd}=\{a/M\bmod1:\ M\ {\rm odd}\}\), dense in the circle \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\). One can instead count elementary diamond transports \(T^2\) consistently throughout. Applying \(2^{-1}\bmod M\) to the coordinates is an isometry from that choice to the first and commutes with every residue relabeling. On fixed labels the two metric conventions need not define the same uniformity on the union.

A compatible cyclotomic field embedding acts on this torsion group by \(x\mapsto a_M x\) at order \(M\), with \(a_M\in(\mathbb Z/M)^\times\) coherent under reduction. This follows from the primitive-root description of the finite cyclotomic Galois groups. Suppose such an assignment is required to preserve small normalized cycle distances uniformly as the order varies. Precisely, for every \(\epsilon>0\) one \(\delta>0\) must work for *all* pairs in *all* orders. [Conrad on cyclotomic fields](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/121Page/handouts/cyclotomic.pdf)

**Forward regularity.** The map extends uniquely to a continuous circle endomorphism by uniform continuity and density. Such an endomorphism is \(x\mapsto nx\bmod1\) for some integer \(n\). To see this, precompose with \(\mathbb R\to\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) and lift to a continuous \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) starting at zero. Then \(f(x+y)-f(x)-f(y)\) is integer-valued and continuous and vanishes at zero; hence it vanishes everywhere. Continuity gives \(f(x)=cx\), and period one of the circle map gives \(c\in\mathbb Z\). Being a unit modulo every odd \(M\) forces \(n=\pm2^j,\ j\ge0\). Conversely all these powers restrict to automorphisms on odd torsion and are Lipschitz in the forward direction. [Elkies on characters and dual groups](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~elkies/M55b.02/pontrjagin.html)

**Two-way regularity.** If the inverse has the same uniform property, its extension also has integer degree. The two extensions compose to the identity by density. Their degrees multiply to one, so \(n=1\) or \(-1\). Conversely both are isometries. In particular \(n=2\) is a genuine counterexample to a one-way selection argument. Its inverse takes \(1/M\) to \((M+1)/(2M)\), staying near the antipode as \(1/M\) tends to zero. At a fixed order the exact Lipschitz factor is \(\min(a_M\bmod M,-a_M\bmod M)\), achieved by adjacent sites. Finite cycle data can falsify a proposed distortion bound. They cannot establish regularity at every order. For example an automorphism equal to the identity away from the 11-primary component and doubling on that component agrees with identity at all orders coprime to 11. It sends a sequence \(1/(3\cdot11^k)\) towards zero in input distance while its output distances stay above \(1/4\). For any finite list of tested orders an analogous construction can use an untested odd prime.

**A finite locality test.** Suppose a particular encoder on a labelled odd cycle of size \(N\) is known to implement a group automorphism, with access to its actual inverse. Measure how far one labelled edge maps in each direction, in counts of microscopic edges. If the two lengths are bounded by \(u,v\) and \(N>uv+1\), the encoder is exactly identity or reflection on that cycle. Indeed the signed least residues \(a,b\) obey \(N\mid ab-1\) and \(|ab-1|\le uv+1<N\). Hence \(ab=1\), forcing \(a=b=1\) or \(a=b=-1\). Compatibility carries the same sign to all divisor orders. This is a finite certificate for an identified encoder with inverse access. Its hypotheses can fail on an untested order.

The strict inequality matters. For equal even budgets \(B\ge2\), take \(N=B^2+1\), \(a=B\), \(b=-B\). These least residues are reciprocal modulo the odd \(N\). For odd \(B\ge3\) the largest odd-order obstruction is \(N=B(B-1)+1\), attained by \(a=B,b=-(B-1)\). Above this value two wraps exceed \(B^2+1\), while one wrap forces \(ab\) even and therefore \(|ab|\le B(B-1)\). At \(N=5\), two edges in each direction suffice for the nonordinary doubling relabeling. A globally bounded reversible microscopic displacement at cofinally large compatible orders forces a common sign throughout the family. Fixed records in a single unidentified coordinate system provide no evidence that the assumed encoder is acting on the separately named amplitude. [Finite locality checks and sharp examples](results/refinement_geometry.json)

This selects the ordinary real embedding **on the odd cyclotomic fields** among compatible field identifications satisfying a specific, reversible geometric hypothesis. On real norm squares the surviving identity and conjugation agree. The refinement maps, uniform comparison in circumference units, and connection of those measured displacements to abstract phase labels are additional structure. Counting physical cycle steps supplies a metric on the named cycles. Measuring the same physical sheets under two algebraic descriptions still gives identical data.

There is a separate \(\sqrt2\) generator when \(K_M=\mathbb Q(\zeta_M,\sqrt2)\). At odd \(M\) its two signs are independent of the cyclotomic assignment. Every fixed native branch with \(t\) elementary coins has the physical-coordinate numerator \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) over \(2^{t/2}\); the sign change produces only a common sign. An independently calibrated source of the named ray \((1,1-\sqrt2)\) would instead have second-output probability \(1/2-\sqrt2/4\) or \(1/2+\sqrt2/4\), respectively. The powers \((1,(1-\sqrt2)^n)\) amplify this difference between externally assigned rays. Choosing a classical threshold of either value on the walk's own fair-bit stream implements the *chosen* threshold in both theories. Thus even the two-way cycle criterion requires one further identification for the ordinary weight on the full field of arbitrary tagged amplitudes. ## What a transcript can identify

Fix the decision rule on actual records, including its finite or countably unbounded stopping rule. A branch of a localized physical-coordinate experiment has mass \(2^{-t}\sum_i m_i^2\). Distinct stopping branches add and postselection divides agreed masses. The finite cylinder laws coincide under every allowed embedding, and therefore the induced probability measures on the sigma algebra generated by finite record cylinders, including stopped records, agree. This covers the adaptive sector controller and the Bell experiment as well as the low-yield eight-return record. In the ordinary interpretation the angular geometry, relative sectors, preparations, effects and event score can be measured against one another. Relabeling a named target and changing the feedback program would change the experiment. An independently warranted relation between the abstract field, reference transport and empirical preparation remains an open part of OP-21 and P5.
