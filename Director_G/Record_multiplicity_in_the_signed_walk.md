# Record multiplicity in the homogeneous signed walk

*From Prephysical Hypotheses to Physical Models*, directed by Kirtus Justus. Research note by ChatGPT (OpenAI), 15 November 2026.

Let a history begin at position 0 with rail R. At each tick either exit is available. A left exit after a left arrival contributes a factor −1. Every other arc contributes +1. A terminal record consists of site and last rail. If its history count is m and its signed history sum is s, the two model weights are s²/2^T and m/2^T for T ticks. Let Λ(T) and Λ_c(T) be the respective expectations of log₂ m. The following claims concern these two stipulated models.

**Result.** The rates Λ(T)/T and Λ_c(T)/T converge respectively to c* = log₂(1 + 1/√2) and 1. For each positive integer T, Λ(T) ≤ Λ_c(T), with equality precisely for T = 1, 2, 3, 4.

**Exact reduction.** Set n=T−1. At time n the total unsigned count at x is B_n(x)=binom(n,(n+x)/2). Appending a specified last exit sends every history at x to the same child record, while that child's final site and rail determine x uniquely. Thus each child of x has unsigned count B_n(x). If the two signed parent sums are a,b, the child sums are a+b,a−b. The sum of their squared, normalized weights is the old total site weight, by (a+b)²+(a−b)²=2(a²+b²). The analogous count identity is immediate. Therefore Λ(T)=E_Q log₂ B_n(X_n) and Λ_c(T)=E_C log₂ B_n(X_n).

**Limits.** For 0≤j≤n, binary entropy h measured in bits satisfies 2^{n h(j/n)}/(n+1) ≤ binom(n,j) ≤ 2^{n h(j/n)}. For the binomial law with success parameter j/n, j is a mode, whose probability lies between 1/(n+1) and 1; this gives both bounds including endpoints. Uniformly in all reachable x, (log₂ B_n(x))/n differs from h((1+x/n)/2) by at most log₂(n+1)/n. In Konno's (L,R) convention the normalized signed update has coin [[−1,1],[1,1]]/√2, initial column (0,1), and the usual subsequent left/right shift. Theorem 1 of Norio Konno, J. Math. Soc. Japan 57 (2005), 1179–1195, p. 1183, [DOI 10.2969/jmsj/1150287309](https://doi.org/10.2969/jmsj/1150287309), gives X_n/n converging in law to density (1+v)/[π(1−v²)√(1−2v²)] for |v|<1/√2. The bounded, continuous test function h((1+v)/2) is even. Consequently the odd part integrates to zero. The limiting rate is
c* = ∫ h((1+v)/2)/[π(1−v²)√(1−2v²)] dv,
over −1/√2<v<1/√2, equivalent to the proposed integral via v=sin θ/√2. The law of large numbers gives X_n/n→0 for fair counting, giving the limiting rate 1. Since n/T→1, these statements also hold with T as denominator.

**Closed form.** Let a=1/√2 and r=√2−1, so a=2r/(1+r²)=(1−r²)/(1+r²). With expectation against the even part of the limiting density, the binary entropy formula gives (1−c*) ln 2 = E[(1+v) ln(1+v)]. Put v=a cos θ; the resulting integral is (a/π)∫₀^π ln(1+a cos θ)/(1−a cos θ) dθ. Here a/(1−a cos θ) is the Poisson kernel (1−r²)/(1−2r cos θ+r²), and ln(1+a cos θ)=2 Re log(1+r e^{iθ})−ln(1+r²). The normalized integral over a half-period equals its full circular mean, since the integrand is even. The Poisson integral for the harmonic function Re log(1+r z) on the disk returns its value at z=r, namely ln(1+r²). Alternatively, multiply the absolutely convergent series 1+2∑ r^k cos(kθ) and ∑ (−1)^{k+1} r^k cos(kθ)/k and use orthogonality. The average of ln(1+a cos θ) under this kernel is consequently ln(1+r²). It follows that c*=log₂(2/(1+r²))=log₂(1+1/√2).

**Finite-time lower bound for the quantum second moment.** Use coordinates (R,L), H=[[1,1],[1,−1]]/√2 and define V(k)=i diag(e^{ik},e^{−ik})H. The phase i has no observable effect. V belongs to SU(2), its half trace is −sin(k)/√2, and it has a smooth representation V=cos ω I + i sin ω b·σ, with ω=arccos(−sin(k)/√2), sin ω>0, and a real unit vector b(k). Write W=V^n. The generator W†W' is traceless and anti-Hermitian, so its squared norm as a matrix is a scalar multiple of I: explicitly W†W'=i u·σ for real u and (u·σ)²=|u|² I. Hence for any unit launch spinor ψ, ||W'ψ||²=(1/2)tr(W'†W'). The trigonometric expression for W and b·b'=0 give
(1/2)tr(W'†W')=n²ω'²+sin²(nω)|b'|².
The initial position is sharp, so the Fourier transform of its amplitude is constant. Parseval identifies the integral of ||W'ψ||² over normalized k with E_Q[X_n²]. The integral of ω'²=cos²k/(1+cos²k) equals 1−1/√2: the complement is the average of 1/(1+cos²k), evaluated by y=tan k on a quadrant. Therefore E_Q[X_n²]≥n²(1−1/√2).

**Strict separation for n≥22.** In natural logarithms ln(2)[1−h((1+v)/2)]≥v²/2 for |v|≤1: the difference vanishes with its first derivative at zero and has second derivative v²/(1−v²)≥0 on the open interval. The type bound and second-moment estimate imply
Λ(n+1) ≤ n−n(1−1/√2)/(2 ln 2).
For fair counting the binomial mass is B_n(x)/2^n. Hence Λ_c(n+1)=n−H(Bin(n,1/2))≥n−log₂(n+1). To compare these, 1/√2 < 5/7 and exp(22/7)>23 (its Taylor polynomial of degree 8 there exceeds 23). The function n/7−ln(n+1) increases for n>6. Thus n(1−1/√2)/2 > ln(n+1) for n≥22.

**Finite complement.** For n=0,…,21 the [accompanying Python recurrence](check_record_multiplicity.py) propagates signed integer amplitudes, checks their total squared normalization exactly against 2^n, and compares every central cumulative site mass against fair counting with the common denominator 2^n. The quantum CDF of |X_n| lies below or equals the classical CDF at every radius tested. All coincide for n≤3. Each 4≤n≤21 has a strict inequality at some interior radius. Since log₂ B_n(x) strictly decreases between adjacent reachable radii, summation by parts gives the claimed strict result. No finite-precision logarithm enters the finite comparison. We do not assert radial stochastic domination beyond the checked n without further proof.

**Scope.** These are statements about a homogeneous signed walk under two stipulated weightings. Deleting the identification of Λ with physical time has no effect on the calculation. Position-dependent sign rules and other dynamics fall outside the finite-time comparison. The in-scope endpoint CDF dominance for all n is stronger than the proved mean inequality and remains open.
