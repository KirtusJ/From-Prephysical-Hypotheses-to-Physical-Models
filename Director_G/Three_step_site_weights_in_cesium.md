# Three-step site weights in two cesium walks

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Robens and colleagues prepared two mirror-image three-step continuations by moving one arm of a cesium walk five lattice sites aside. Of the atoms counted on the undisturbed arm, 107 of 201 arrived at the predicted peak in one setting and 115 of 196 in the other. An ideal fair-coin walk assigns the peak probability $5/8$ by squared amplitudes, $25/28$ by squaring the total site populations again, and $3/8$ by counting paths. Comparing the two settings at the same two wells tests the nonlinear site rule even when well and final-spin sensitivities are unknown. Perfect dynamics and readout deserve scrutiny here. The nominal ordinary prediction with spin-blind gains is itself near the edge of the observed range. [Robens et al., Figs. 2–3 and Appendices A–E](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.5.011003) · [Three-tick site comparison defining the alternative](Two_rail_comparison_for_a_flight_bin.md)

## Which preparation and which denominator

The experiment starts at an imaged site with a sharp internal spin. One fair rotation and conditional translation produce two arms. A spin-selective shift separates them. The untouched arm then starts the last three fair rotations in a single, known spin at its own origin. In the printed coordinates the program's $R$ moves toward decreasing $j$. For example, the untouched $R$ arm in Fig. 3(b) has three-step peak at $j=-1$ and far tail at $j=+1$. In Fig. 3(c) these wells exchange roles. The absolute spin-resolved amplitudes match a Hadamard walk up to reflection and mode phases. The beamsplitter signs are included in the [path-pair enumeration](check_cesium_spatial_weight.py). Disjoint support also makes the conditional distribution of a terminal site-power law within one arm independent of the possibly different overall weight assigned to the other.

## A comparison that cancels well efficiency

Let the two retained settings be $b,c$. At a position $j$, set $w_j=\sum_r|\psi_{jr}|^2$. In the family tested, the event at mode $(j,r)$ has weight proportional to $w_j^{s-1}|\psi_{jr}|^2$ for $s=1$ or 2. The within-site spin split remains ordinary. The ideal site populations out of eight are $(1,5,1,1)$ on the successive wells $-2,-1,0,1$ in $b$ and $(1,1,5,1)$ on $-1,0,1,2$ in $c$. The measured double odds at $j=-1,+1$ are

$$\widehat\Theta=\frac{107\cdot115}{31\cdot27}=14.70. $$

If expected cell counts factor as row brightness times a fixed positive efficiency of each reported well times its model weight, the factors cancel. The predicted odds are 25 for $s=1$, 625 for $s=2$, and 9 for fair path counting.

Allow a separate, fixed gain $e_{jr}\ge0$ for each well and spin, with both small comparison cells detected. The bright peak in $b$ splits into $(4,1)$ in $(R,L)$ at $-1$, while the corresponding tail from $c$ is $(1,0)$. At $+1$ the roles and spins reverse. For $s=1$ the expected double odds become

$$\Theta=\left(4+\frac{e_{-1,L}}{e_{-1,R}}\right)
                 \left(4+\frac{e_{+1,R}}{e_{+1,L}}\right)\ge16.$$

With $s=2$ each peak-to-tail factor gains a further factor of five, so $\Theta\ge400$. The lower bound is attainable by making each peak's minor spin invisible at its own well. The gains refer to displacements from the imaged launch. Applying this model to gains fixed in laboratory coordinates also requires a common distribution of starting sites across settings. Run-dependent brightness can vary freely.

## Statistical comparison and the modest ordinary residual

Treat the atoms as independent observations with stable probabilities in each row. Conditional on the two row totals at the chosen wells and the first column total, the top-left count $X$ has weights $\binom{138}{x}\binom{142}{134-x}\Theta^x$ on its feasible integer support. The inclusive lower tail $P_\Theta(X\le107)$ decreases with $\Theta$. At ordinary spin-blind odds 25 it is **0.0477**. At the smallest spin-sensitive ordinary odds 16 it is 0.422. For the terminal site-squared rule, even its most favorable fixed spin-resolved gains give a lower tail of at most **$4.38\times10^{-21}$**. Under independent fair path counting and spin-blind gains the relevant *upper* tail at 9 is 0.0657. This specific contrast leaves fair path counting viable at 5%; a separate [blank-versus-arm contrast](Cesium_count_fraction.md) tests it more sharply using some of the same atoms.

The peak wells were already visible in this published dataset. A one-sided 95% upper confidence limit for the fixed comparison is 24.828, rounded outward. Covering both tail directions, all three pairs of rows, and all ten pairs of the five central wells by Bonferroni gives an upper limit of 40.014. This family covers choices inside this figure. Dataset selection and dependence between atoms in one sequence enter separately. The authors report an average of 1.2 atoms per sequence. Their bootstrap for the Leggett–Garg statistic cannot be reused for this odds ratio. [Sampling details in Appendix C](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.5.011003) · [Full conditional calculations](results/cesium_spatial_weight.json)

## Sensitivity to decoherence and readout

Let $\lambda\in[0,1]$ multiply each off-diagonal spin block after each tick, preserving diagonal populations. The two histories interfering in the dominant exit spin differ at two intermediate spin labels. Thus its peak numerator is $A=2+2\lambda^2$, with a further unit in the other spin and one unit in the opposite well's tail. Under this channel we test the specified extension that raises the *resulting* site populations to power $s$ before detection. A rule instead applied separately to pure members of a hidden noise ensemble would require that ensemble to be specified.

Without site errors the spin-blind odds become $(A+1)^{2s}$ and the sharp spin-sensitive lower bound becomes $[A(A+1)^{s-1}]^2$. Using $\lambda=0.94$ as a sensitivity point follows the authors' approximate six-percent spin coherence loss per fair tick fitted to measured distributions. It is not an independent calibration for this test. At 0.94 the ordinary spin-blind lower tail increases to 0.087. The nonlinear spin-sensitive lower bound remains 322.5, with a tail no greater than $4.96\times10^{-19}$. With total dephasing, $\lambda=0$, its lower bound falls to 36, inside the selection-adjusted interval. The inference about the nonlinear law therefore needs evidence on the quality of the walk as well as its detector. [Coherence fit and separate Ramsey measurement, Appendix B](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.5.011003)

As a further sensitivity calculation, apply a symmetric error of one neighboring well with total probability $\rho$ after assigning position and spin, then apply the stable gains to the reported modes. For each well, the ratio of two nonnegative gain-weighted mode sums is bounded below by the smallest numerator-to-denominator ratio over its supported denominator modes. Multiplying the two well bounds gives sharp odds bounds again, since the gains can be chosen separately. At $(\lambda,\rho)=(0.94,0.04)$ the ordinary spin-blind odds are 22.03 (lower tail 0.104), and the site-squared lower bound is 320.36 (tail below $5.74\times10^{-19}$). The authors report about 2% adjacent-well misidentification per image, with images at the start and end. This does not establish an effective symmetric error of 4% for displacement. The [checker](check_cesium_spatial_weight.py) retains $\rho=0,0.02,0.04$ for each visibility, including the complete-dephasing boundary.

For a fixed-gain lower bound $L$, positive multiplicative changes in gains from one setting to the other with relative contrast $D=\max_{jr}(e^{b}_{jr}/e^{c}_{jr})/\min_{jr}(e^{b}_{jr}/e^{c}_{jr})$ can reduce the lower bound to $L/D$. This follows by bounding the two ratios of weighted sums in opposite directions. Thus at the fitted visibility and the illustrative 4% site-error channel, allowing the nonlinear law to reach even the wider upper limit 40.014 requires $D\ge320.35/40.014$, about eightfold. This is a required distortion within the specified model. Additive backgrounds, nonlocal assignment errors, uneven spin preparation, setting-dependent transport, and unrecorded detection failures call for their own measurements.
