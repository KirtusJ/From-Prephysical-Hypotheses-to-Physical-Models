## The construction

Retain the signed two-channel walk of G9. At a vertex \(x\), incoming amplitudes are mixed by the real Hadamard matrix \(H=2^{-1/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). The two outgoing channels move right and left by one site. The following candidate tests whether a weak uniform force needs complex numbers on the edges of that walk. It admits a positive construction and an observable countercontrol on exactly the same carrier.

Adjoin a sheet label \(y\in\mathbb Z/M\). On departure from \(x\), shift the sheet by \(x\bmod M\), independently of the outgoing channel, then make the spatial step. Use \(x\in\mathbb Z\), or a ring of length divisible by \(M\). Sheet shifts and channel shifts are permutations. The unnormalized matrix entries on the expanded graph are all \(+1\) or \(-1\), and the normalized step is real orthogonal. The size of the sheet register grows with \(M\).

**Exact sector reduction.** Prepare a sheet wave proportional to \(\exp(2\pi i r y/M)\). At the output sheet \(y\), the input is evaluated at \(y-x\), which multiplies the base amplitude by \(\exp(-ihx)\), \(h=2\pi r/M\). Each subsequent step preserves that sheet sector. Thus the reduced dynamics equals G9's electric walk at every step, with the phase applied **before** the spatial step. For \(r=1\), \(h\to0\) as \(M\to\infty\). For real input vectors the two conjugate sectors together give a real rotation plane. The interpretation of one orientation as a complex amplitude and the admissible preparations and readout still require an account. The character decomposition of lifts has a precedent in the spectral analysis of voltage digraphs. [Dalfó, Fiol and Širáň](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04463)

**Discriminating record.** From \((x,c)=(0,R)\), the three-step record \((1,R)\) has exactly the two histories \(RLR\) and \(LRR\) on the line or on the rings tested below. Each has positive coin amplitude \(1/\sqrt8\). The sums of their departure positions are \(1\) and \(-1\). The probability in sector \(r\) is therefore
\[
p_r(1,R)=\tfrac18|e^{-ih}+e^{ih}|^2=\tfrac14(1+\cos 2h).
\]
At \(M=4\) the zero sector gives \(1/2\), and sector one gives zero. Starting on one localized sheet and summing the final probabilities over sheets gives the uniform sector average \(1/4\) for every \(M>2\). Equivalently, the two histories end on distinct sheets. This is an observable distinction even after ignoring the sheet coordinate. The same graph and edge signs admit all three predictions. In the underlying discrete transport the sheet displacements along the two paths differ by two. Its representation in a chosen sector gives relative phase \(e^{\pm2ih}\).

**What the lift pays for.** If bounded order means the scalar signs on the expanded graph, the construction realizes arbitrarily weak fields with order two. If it means a uniform bound on the order of every internal transport used to implement the field, this construction fails that condition. The sheet generator has order \(M\). Within scalar implementations of the exact one-site translation law \(g(x+1)/g(x)=e^{-ih}\), if all \(g(x)\) are \(q\)-th roots, then \(qh\in2\pi\mathbb Z\). For \(q\le Q\) a nonzero principal field has magnitude at least \(2\pi/Q\). This elementary obstruction concerns the exact law for a scalar phase at every site. More general effective encodings remain open.

## What the elementary holonomy selects

There is a partial answer to the preparation question using only the sheet permutations. Write \(T|y\rangle=|y+1\rangle\). The difference of the two equal-length routes around a spatial diamond is \(T^2\), up to inversion. The real symmetric operator \(L=2I-T^2-T^{-2}\) is consequently independent of which route is named first. A relabelling of sheets conjugates \(L\) and its projectors. This identifies a subspace using the transport relation once the fibre and elementary diamonds have been designated.

For odd \(M\ge3\), the eigenvalues of \(L\) are \(4\sin^2(2\pi r/M)\). Its smallest positive eigenspace is the real two-plane associated with \(2r=\pm1\bmod M\), or \(r=\pm(M-1)/2\bmod M\). The zero eigenspace consists of constants. These conclusions follow by diagonalizing the cyclic permutation, since multiplication by two permutes the residue classes for odd \(M\). For even \(M\), \(T^2\) already splits the fibre into two orbits, so the same identification does not apply as stated.

There is a reason this real plane matters. Its two field angles are \(\pm(\pi-\pi/M)\). For the nearest-neighbour walk the fields \(h\) and \(h+\pi\) give *exactly the same site and channel probabilities* at every integer time for the same initial state on a line or even ring. To see this, set \(C=(-1)^X\). Then \(CU_h=-U_h C\) and \(U_{h+\pi}=U_h C\). Taking the \(n\)-th power leaves \(U_h^n\) multiplied by an overall sign and a diagonal factor \(C^n\) at the output. The two high-frequency sectors are therefore, for this readout, the two opposite weak fields \(\mp\pi/M\). Interferometric readout between different position parities need not respect this equivalence. The real plane also does not single out one member of the conjugate pair. The measured centroid from a general real two-plane preparation must carry its actual pair components and initial base preparation through the reduction.

**Preparation cost.** An ideal projection onto that plane succeeds on a sheet dipole \((|0\rangle-|2\rangle)/\sqrt2\) with probability \(4\sin^2(\pi/M)/M\), asymptotic to \(4\pi^2/M^3\). Each of the two Fourier coefficients has squared magnitude \(|1-e^{-4\pi i r/M}|^2/(2M)\). For odd \(M\ge5\) the next distinct positive holonomy eigenvalue is \(4\sin^2(2\pi/M)\), so the absolute gap above the pair is asymptotic to \(12\pi^2/M^2\). A nonzero overlap gives mathematical access under an ideal filter. It gives neither an available instrument to perform that filter nor a free scalable preparation. The necessity of the extra operation can be tested by conditioning on a filtering outcome and comparing the three-step record with the localized-sheet control above.

The code builds a real holonomy matrix directly from the sheet permutation, diagonalizes it, compares the projector with the Fourier formula, and propagates the high- and low-field walks from a coherent state supported on both spatial parities. All nine time rows at each of seven odd orders, including the smallest degenerate-spectrum case \(M=3\), are retained in the results. The result narrows sector selection to a definite subspace. It leaves the operational selection of that subspace and its physical meaning open.

**A filter made of shifts.** A conditional implementation uses the real contraction \(K=(2I+T^2+T^{-2})/4\). Its eigenvalues are \(a_r=\cos^2(2\pi r/M)\). The constant mode has eigenvalue one, but the dipole has exactly zero overlap with it. Within that complementary space the largest eigenvalue is \(a_1=\cos^2(\pi/M)\) on the selected pair. For odd \(M\ge5\), all other eigenvalues are at most \(a_2=\cos^2(2\pi/M)\). Here the subscripts 1 and 2 order the eigenvalues on the complement. They are not the original sector indices. If \(p=4\sin^2(\pi/M)/M\) and \(s\) consecutive filters succeed, the normalized output has leakage amplitude and joint success probability bounded by
\[
\mathrm{leakage}\le \sqrt{(1-p)/p}\,(a_2/a_1)^s,\qquad
\Pr(\text{all successes})=\|K^s d\|^2\ge p\,a_1^{2s},
\]
where \(d\) is the normalized dipole. Both follow by the orthogonal spectral decomposition. For \(M=3\) the dipole is already entirely in the pair.

One realization prepares two binary path labels in an equal superposition. Conditional on the path, apply \(I,I,T^2,T^{-2}\), respectively. Project the path labels back onto the equal superposition and keep that outcome. The matrix element is exactly \(K\). A single balanced pair of paths with a relative minus sign and shifts \(I,T^2\) similarly prepares the dipole from sheet zero upon a successful recombination. The filter counts below are conditional on the prepared dipole and exclude that initial preparation cost. The construction imports coherent controlled routing, Hadamard mixing, successful postselection and readout. It identifies a concrete sufficiency condition for the instrument. Its availability under the original unaided carrier operations remains open. In particular, incoherently randomizing the four shifts implements a mixture of channels and does not apply \(K\) to amplitudes.

For leakage tolerance \(0.01\), applying the ceiling of the logarithmic bound gave 3 successful filter steps at \(M=5\), 255 at \(M=31\), and 1,202 at \(M=63\). For the last case the observed joint probability was \(3.99\times10^{-7}\) and the observed leakage amplitude was \(2.50\times10^{-4}\). Successful filtering is a rare, conditional preparation. All 1,524 intermediate rows retain the success probability, its lower bound, the leakage and its upper bound. These are exact finite recurrences evaluated numerically, not sampled success counts.

## A position estimate for the forced walk

Work on the line with lattice position \(X\), initial state of finite position second moment and squared-amplitude probabilities. In the Fourier convention of G9, \(X=i\partial_k\) and the unforced fibre is \(U(k)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-ik},e^{ik})H\). Its eigenvalues are \(\lambda_+=e^{-i\omega}\) and \(\lambda_-=-e^{i\omega}\), where \(\omega(k)=\arcsin(\sin k/\sqrt2)\). Their separation is \(2\cos\omega\ge\sqrt2\). The first-band velocity is \(v=\omega'=\cos k/\sqrt{1+\cos^2 k}\). The other band has velocity \(-v\).

Let \(0<h\le1\), \(\tau=nh\), and \(D_n=\langle X\rangle_n-\langle X\rangle_0\). Initially prepare either single band \(b\in\{+1,-1\}\) with normalized momentum weight \(w(dk)\). For a line state \(w\) has a density on the Brillouin circle. Then
\[
\left|hD_n-b\int[\omega(k)-\omega(k-\tau)]\,w(dk)\right|
\le \min\{2\tau,\; (4.5\tau+16\tau^2)h\}. \tag{1}
\]
For bounded \(\tau\), the scaled error tends to zero. A packet concentrated around \(k_0\) incurs at most an additional \(\tau\int d_{\mathbb T}(k,k_0)w(dk)\) when the ensemble of rays is replaced by one central ray. The estimate bounds displacement. A position distribution concentrating on an individual trajectory would require additional control of the spread.

**Proof.** Along a momentum characteristic write \(k_j=k-jh\), \(\psi_j=U(k_j)\psi_{j-1}\), and \(\rho_j=|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|\) for normalized fibre states. The expectation increments are the initial-\(k\) average of \(\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_x\rho_{j-1})\). Indeed the field commutes with position and the conditional translation increases position by \(+1\) or \(-1\). Conjugating that signed channel observable by \(H\) gives \(\sigma_x\). Directly, the plus projector is \(P=(I+\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/2\) with \(\mathbf n=(\cos k,\sin k,\cos k)/\sqrt{1+\cos^2 k}\). Hence \(\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_xP)=v\). For the minus band replace \(P\) by \(I-P\).

We give the constants because following a single eigenphase over a period by numerical diagonalization can hide leakage. Write \(D(A)=U(k)AU(k)^*\) on matrices. \(P'\) is off diagonal relative to \(P\). Define
\[
Q=\frac{P P'(I-P)}{1-\lambda_+\overline{\lambda_-}}+
  \frac{(I-P)P'P}{1-\lambda_-\overline{\lambda_+}},\qquad B=Q-P'.
\]
These are Hermitian. Here \(P\) is the plus projector. For the minus band use \(I-P\), reverse the eigenvalue labels and differentiate that projector. The two conjugate denominators have modulus at least \(\sqrt2\), and \((I-D)Q=P'\). Thus \(D(B+P')=B\).

The unnormalized vector in \(\mathbf n\) is \(a=(\cos k,\sin k,\cos k)\), with \(|a|\ge1\) and \(|a'|,|a''|\le\sqrt2\). Differentiating \(\mathbf n=a/|a|\) gives \(|\mathbf n'|\le\sqrt2\). A second differentiation of \(\mathbf n'=(I-\mathbf n\mathbf n^T)a'/|a|\) gives \(|\mathbf n''|\le\sqrt2+4+2<8\). Hence \(\|P'\|\le1\), \(\|P''\|\le4\). Also \(\|Q\|\le1\) since it has two conjugate off-diagonal blocks. In \(A=P P'(I-P)\), \(\|A'\|\le 1+4+1=6\). Since \(|(\lambda_+\overline{\lambda_-})'|\le2\), differentiating both terms of \(Q\) gives \(\|Q'\|\le2(6/\sqrt2+1)<11\). Consequently \(\|B\|\le2\), \(\|B'\|\le15\).

The trial matrix \(F(k)=P(k)+hB(k)\) has trace one. Taylor expansion at \(k_j\), using \(k_{j-1}=k_j+h\), gives \(\|D_jF(k_{j-1})-F(k_j)\|\le(2+15)h^2\). Unitary conjugation preserves the norm. Telescoping from \(\rho_0=P(k)\) gives \(\|\rho_j-P(k_j)\|\le4h+17jh^2\). For either branch the difference of two rank-one projectors is traceless Hermitian with trace norm twice its operator norm. The current error is therefore at most \(8h+34jh^2\). A tighter term is available here. Differentiating \(B=Q-P'\) directly gives two off-diagonal terms with coefficients \(1/(1-z)-1=z/(1-z)\), \(z=\lambda_+\overline{\lambda_-}\). Their coefficients and their derivatives have the same bounds as those used for \(Q\). It follows that \(\|B\|\le1\), \(\|B'\|\le11\), and the one-step residual is at most \(13h^2\). Thus the current error at \(j\) is at most \(4h+26jh^2\). Summing \(h\) times these errors for \(0\le j<n\) gives \(4\tau h+13\tau^2h\). Replacing the left-endpoint velocity sum by its integral adds at most \(\tau h/2\) because \(|v'|\le1\). This proves the slightly stronger constant 13 in the quadratic term, and hence (1). The trivial \(2\tau\) follows from \(|v|\le1\) and unit maximum displacement per step. The same Lipschitz bound gives the central-ray estimate. All differential bounds are uniform on the momentum circle.

This direct proof specializes the discrete adiabatic argument to a fixed observable and a uniformly gapped two-band fibre. Tanaka's Eq. (3) controls transitions under slow modulation. The observable bound above also uses the bounded single-step current. No inference about an unbounded position operator follows from state-norm proximity alone. [Tanaka, published version and revision history](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.2989)

**Hamiltonian interpretation.** In scaled coordinates \(Y=h x,\ \tau=hn\), \(k(\tau)=k_0-\tau\), the centre ray solves \(dY/d\tau=bv(k)\), \(dk/d\tau=-1\). Its canonical Hamiltonian on the supplied cylinder is \(b\omega(k)+Y\). A fixed smooth weight gives an ensemble of these rays. The finite-\(M\) periodic sheet construction realizes \(h=2\pi/M\) exactly. The analytic estimate is for the base line, equivalently the lifted line in the chosen sector. On a spatial ring the integrated current measures signed transport around the ring. It can differ from both the change of a coordinate across a cut and a circular centroid. Near-perfect revival at the end of one period is a different observable. At unbounded slow times arithmetic of the field can change propagation. [Cedzich et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.111.160601)

## A free Schrödinger envelope and its error

The same walk at zero field has a second limit. Select the plus band around \(k_*=-\pi/2\). Use its normalized eigenvector to encode a scalar envelope isometrically, and write \(q=k-k_*\). The band multiplier after removing the carrier phase is \(e^{-in[\omega(k_*+q)-\omega(k_*)]}\). For Fourier support \(|q|\le\delta\le1\), its operator-norm distance on the scalar envelopes from the free Schrödinger multiplier \(e^{-in q^2/2}\) is at most \(n\delta^4/6\).

Indeed \(d\omega(k_*+q)/dq=\sin q/\sqrt{1+\sin^2 q}\). Its difference from \(q\) is bounded by \(|q|^3/6+|q|^3/2\), using \(|\sin q-q|\le |q|^3/6\) and \(0\le1-(1+u)^{-1/2}\le u/2\). Integration gives the fourth-order phase remainder. The exponential is Lipschitz on real phases and Fourier transformation is unitary. With \(q=\delta p\), \(n\delta^2\to t\), \(|p|\le1\), the demodulated envelope tends to \(i\partial_t f=-\tfrac12\partial_Y^2 f\), \(Y=\delta x\). Comparing at \(t=n\delta^2\) makes the rounding error zero. This is a free scalar evolution in dimensionless units on a selected band. The published quantum-walk envelope derivation already identifies the Schrödinger regime. The estimate here bounds the error for the specific G9 walk. [de Valcárcel, Roldán and Romanelli](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/12/12/123022)

The forced ray and the free envelope are two limits of one stipulated update, with different fields, preparations and scalings. This work does not establish a simultaneous limit with general potentials. At \(k=0\) the two bands instead move with opposite nonzero velocities. Squared probabilities, the Hilbert norm, the initial band, the clock index, and calibration of dimensional mass or action all remain inputs or open obligations.

## Checks and limits of the evidence

- The explicit full real walk was compared at each step with a separately implemented complex sector walk, for every sector at \(M=3,4,5,6,8\). The 182 sector-step comparisons and five localized-sheet controls are retained.
- For the first band, \(k_0=1\) and momentum width \(0.03\), the maximum error in the scaled displacement was 0.1340, 0.06293, 0.03002, 0.01473 and 0.007298 at periods 32, 64, 128, 256 and 512. The last two correspond to 0.6002 and 0.5947 lattice sites. Shrinking scaled error can coexist with a persistent site-scale offset. Both branches, both widths and all 3,968 time rows are retained. The proof covers a continuum of momenta. Numerical quadrature and time rows are deterministic controls, not independent experimental data.
- The bound (1) has a loose coefficient over a full period. At the simulated periods its trivial cap may be the tighter bound. The estimate establishes an asymptotic rate on a bounded interval in scaled time. It does not certify G9's more stringent 0.02-site residual after a connection correction. The earlier adverse residuals 0.075 and 0.038 remain in the Z3 record.
- The largest sampled free-envelope discrepancy was 0.03615 at \(\delta=1/2,\ n=4\), against bound 0.04167. At \(\delta=1/16,\ n=256\) it was 0.0006494 against 0.0006510. Numerical values are double precision. The inequalities above supply analytic bounds.
- G9's free weak-limit theorem, the adiabatic estimate, and an experimental implementation of a field are three different sources of evidence. The atomic experiment explicitly prepares and drives a quantum walk. It demonstrates a physical implementation of an assumed update. It supplies no calibration from a proposed prephysical sheet or signed graph to an atomic preparation. [Grimmett, Janson and Scudo](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0309135), [Genske et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2094)
- The sheet shifts are an exact discrete transport. Ordered products describe their path dependence and the closed-path permutations. A holonomy specifies which sheet transformation is obtained. The observable depends additionally on the prepared representation and on what is recorded. G9's quadrilateral ladder uses the carrier's incidence structure in its rectagraph examples. A geodesic Schild ladder requires midpoint and geodesic operations, together with a convergence analysis for the connection being approximated. No convergence from this sheet walk to such a connection has been established. [Guigui and Pennec](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-021-09515-x)
