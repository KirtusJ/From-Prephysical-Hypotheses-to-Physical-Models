# A fixed transport rule for relative character measurements

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Mathematical development and exact checks by Director G (Codex).

Two localized sheet registers can establish a relative character reference on one time-independent signed graph. Each site has a fixed sheet increment. The difference between parallel transports on two paths, measured at that site, sets the character test. Visiting different sites gives enough tests to prepare an exact relation. The construction also lets us distinguish a probability that follows from a persistent record from an absolute choice of amplitude embedding.

## Which transport the walk can measure

Take the position ring \(\mathbb Z/(2p)\), coin \(c\in\{+1,-1\}\) and sheets \(Y=(y,z)\in\mathbb F_p^2\) for an odd prime \(p\). At each step increment the sheets by \(g(x)\), apply the signed Hadamard coin and move to \(x+d\) in outgoing direction \(d\). The real matrix of signed edges \(A\) satisfies \(A^\mathsf{T}A=2I\). Before normalization the coin is a direct sum of two-by-two sign blocks; both sheet and base shifts are permutations. No gain changes during a run. The position, the choice of \(g\), the localized start and the availability of coordinate records are assumptions. The one-register linear lift in G9 has fewer physical coordinates than this graph. Fixed permutation data as a description of graph covers and their spectra have precedents in voltage graph theory. [Gross and Tucker](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0012365X77901315) · [Dalfó, Fiol and Širáň](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04463)

With a known starting \((x,c)\), observe the site and direction three steps later. The destructive output is \((x-c,-c)\); its two histories differ in the middle departure position \(x+1\) versus \(x-1\). Their other two departures are both at \(x\). Their transports differ by the vector
\[
                     h_x=g(x+1)-g(x-1).
\]
Hence its sheet-count operator is \(T^{h_x}-I\) up to a translation and sign. The constructive output \((x+c,c)\) gives the sum instead. Here \(T^h\) translates by \(h\), and the common normalized three-step factor is \(1/\sqrt8\). At character \(k=(r,s)\), a destructive output has probability \(\frac12\sin^2(\pi k\cdot h_x/p)\). It vanishes exactly when the character is trivial on the relative transport. This is a discrete holonomy test. Compose one path's transport permutation with the mathematical inverse of the other's. The sheet shift is \(h_x\) up to orientation. Sending the walker back along geometrically reversed steps generally applies a different permutation. The test requires neither a continuum connection nor a metric on the sheets.

If all sheet increments lie in the same line \(L\), a localized input stays within its translated copy of \(L\). For example, with \(g(x)=(x,2x)\), each occupied physical cell has \(z-2y\) fixed. Any sheet-unresolved branch in a tagged character depends only on \(r+2s\). Coordinate masks, even on named sheets, cannot extend the support of the localized input beyond this line. This is a genuine limitation of simply duplicating the original linear transport. Conditional dark readings in a single direction are possible, and repeated constructive outcomes can concentrate on its kernel. The available diamond directions remain fixed.

## A fixed graph with all \(p+1\) directions

Define the departure increment at even and odd sites by
\[
        g(2j)=(0,j),\qquad
        g(2j+1)=(j,-j(j+1)/2), \qquad j\in\mathbb F_p .
\]
Direct subtraction gives \(h_{2j}=(1,-j)\) and \(h_{2j+1}=(0,1)\). Thus testers for every projective direction are present on the same ring. Such a prescription is constrained: the \(h_x\)'s on each parity cycle must sum to zero, since their primitives telescope. This example meets both constraints. It needs two sheet coordinates and a spatially varying transport. It removes the time-dependent gain selector from the [two-register experiment](Relative_sheet_transport.md).

Choose a unit \(u\). Begin on two localized sheets. At one odd site retain a destructive output. Then, in any fixed order, retain a destructive output at an even site \(2j\) for each \(j\ne u\). Between tests record single steps until the next centre is reached. Each single-step outcome translates the sheet vector and may reverse its sign, preserving all relative-character tests. Outcomes need not be rejected during these walks to the next centre. To make the pointer record finite in advance one may instead retain a prescribed shortest route at every transfer, or impose a time cap and keep the timeout as a distinct event.

The product of differences kills \(s=0\) and every character with \(r/s\ne u\). On \(r=us,\ s\ne0\), the even-site factors multiply to \(\prod_{j\ne u}(\zeta^{(u-j)s}-1)=p\), up to translation and sign, where \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}\). The odd site contributes \(\zeta^s-1\). Inverting the character transform gives one plus coset and one minus coset of \(u y+z\), separated by one unit, with \(p\) sites in each. Its Schmidt rank is \(p-1\) for a unit \(u\). The preparation has integer squared count \(2p\). On a subsequent test at \(2j\) the difference shifts the coset coordinate by \(d=u-j\). Squaring its counts gives \(0\) for \(d=0\), \(3/8\) for \(d=\pm1\) and \(1/4\) for the remaining settings, conditional on successful preparation. The result identifies a relative character power, even from two localized inputs, using one fixed graph. An externally specified faithful character in one register can also transfer its label along this relation.

For a prescribed route of \(R\) observed single steps the joint probability is \(2p/(8^p 2^R)\). Accepting all transfers gives \(2p/8^p\). Indeed single-step direction readings from a definite incoming coin are fair independently of the sheet state. On a ring of length \(N=2p\), the mean time to hit the next centre from distance \(j\bmod N\) is \(j(N-j)\). This solves the first-step recurrence with zero at both endpoints of the cut ring. Hits occur almost surely because from anywhere there is a uniformly positive chance of hitting the target within \(N\) steps. Each routing stage costs at most \(p^2\) steps in expectation, giving at most \(p^3\) for all \(p\) transfers, plus \(3p\) test steps. The success probability per completed route follows because all route-dependent translations commute with the differences and each successful test fixes its output site. Keeping the actual transfer record and adjusting the recorded offset suffices; forgetting translations can mix different coset profiles but preserves the character support and test probabilities. First-passage identities for walks on finite graphs give an external comparison. [Aldous and Fill](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/Book_Ralph/Ch2.S2.html)

**An incomplete spatial menu.** The simpler polynomial choice \(g(x)=(x,x^2)\) has \(h_x=(2,4x)\). It visits every finite slope and misses the vertical direction. After the prescribed \(p-1\) horizontal differences all nonzero \((r,0)\) survive alongside the desired nonzero line. For any \(s\ne0\) on that line, the product has modulus \(p\). On \(s=0,\ r\ne0\) its modulus is \(|\zeta^{2r}-1|^{p-1}\). Thus the squared count on the desired line is \(p-1\) and the total is
\[
       (p-1)+p^{-1}\binom{2p-2}{p-1}.
\]
For the extra term, apply Parseval in one variable to \((T^2-I)^{p-1}\): its \(p\) distinct residues carry alternating binomial coefficients, whose squared sum is the central binomial coefficient. The initial two-register point gives a factor \(1/p^2\) in character weights. Multiplication of the common horizontal increment by a unit merely permutes the vertical characters in this calculation. At \(p=5,7,13\) the desired weights are \(2/9,1/23,3/52006\). The false-positive modes grow in importance. This excludes this exact word with the incomplete menu. It leaves other coherent operations on the polynomial graph open.

## When the squared probability follows

Director Z's G10 degree-two theorem supplies a way to analyze the same apparatus without taking the Born rule for its intermediate readings as an extra axiom. The premises are substantive. Events are sets of equal-length signed histories. At an endpoint one adds signed counts, then weights the event by summing a common function \(\phi(|n|)\) over endpoints. Require compatibility with continuation by one step, with one common positive factor, for *all* such history events. Set \(\phi(0)=0,\ \phi(1)>0\). On each finite connected component of a degree-two signed graph with \(A^\mathsf{T}A=2I\), G10 forces \(\phi(n)=n^2\phi(1)\) on integers and continuation factor two. Normalization gives \(\mu_t(E)=2^{-t}\sum_v n_E(v)^2\) for a localized input. G10 uses arbitrary history subsets to force the recurrence; most of those subsets need not be physically recorded. [Director Z, G10, Theorem 5.3 and signed lift discussion](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/c1a9a31/edition/PAPERS/G10_Histories_as_Events_the_Order_of_the_Pair_Rule_First_Order_Weights_an/source/body.tex)

**Record registers.** For a finite horizon, append a cyclic clock and a fresh finite slot at each reading. Initialize each slot at zero. The clock advances on every step; on its designated tick an output vertex \(v\) adds the chosen coarse label \(B(v)\) to that slot modulo a size sufficient for its range. Selection of the next finite-horizon read, including a change between single- and three-step reads, can depend on already filled slots. Use distinct slots through the horizon and a period longer than the experiment. For each fixed new base vertex and clock, this pointer transformation is a permutation: the controlling old slots stay fixed, and adding a label on a fresh slot is invertible. Thus the full matrix is a degree-two signed step followed by permutations; its transpose times itself is \(2I\). The clock and pointer are part of a time-independent enlarged graph to which the theorem applies, component by component. They model a specified apparatus; the choice to couple these particular coordinates remains supplied.

For transcript \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\), all histories with that transcript end in the same recorded slots, and different transcripts end in orthogonal coordinate sets. Induction on the reading times shows that their physical signed count vector is the product of the intervening signed steps and the coordinate masks for these labels, applied to the initial point. G10's endpoint sum therefore makes the transcript weight exactly the squared norm of that product divided by \(2^t\). Coarse graining over distinct retained transcripts adds their probabilities. Conditional weights follow by dividing by the positive mass of the prefix. This gives the usual projective instrument statistics for these coordinate records as a consequence of the event and apparatus premises. For a routing experiment stopped at a hitting time, first apply the argument with a finite cap; the elementary hit estimate above makes the timeout probability tend to zero. No extension to a countably additive measure on *unobserved* path subsets is used.

Record persistence matters. In the zero-gain control start at \(0,R\) and look at the destructive three-step output \((-1,L)\). The two histories have opposite signs. A blank pointer gives probability zero. Copying the first direction to a retained bit gives separate endpoint counts \((-1,1)\), hence probability \(1/4\). Reversibly uncomputing the bit before reconvergence restores zero. Reporting a coarse label after recording and forgetting finer alternatives differs from retaining only that label throughout. Pointer correlations as a mechanism for quantum measurement are discussed by Griffiths under different starting assumptions. [Griffiths](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.08725)

**A constraint from recorded statistics alone.** At \(p=5\), start at \(0,R\) on zero sheets. After two steps copy just the predicate “site \(+2\), direction \(R\)” into a persistent bit. In physical coordinates the yes branch has one unit count and the other branch has three. Three more unobserved steps give eight unit counts on yes, and twenty unit counts plus one double count on the other branch. Both histograms follow by literal propagation on the fixed graph. Set the weight of an unoccupied endpoint to zero. Assign a normalized endpoint table proportional to \(\phi(|n|)\) with the same \(\phi\) at both times. Requiring the odds of the old bit to stay fixed yields
\[
         {1\over3}={8\phi(1)\over20\phi(1)+\phi(2)}
             \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \phi(2)=4\phi(1).
\]
For \(\phi(n)=n^\alpha,\ \alpha>0\), this single experiment forces \(\alpha=2\). It compares normalized odds across times, so it makes no assumption about the total unnormalized weight after a free step. For general functions it constrains only the displayed two values. A fixed common response to *integer signed counts* and an undisturbed classical interpretation of the persistent flag are premises. A model with separate time-dependent response functions, or one using additional count-dependent normalization inside the branches, needs separate analysis. The five-step record tests one necessary part of the all-history theorem by a coarse coordinate observation. The following family tests all integer magnitudes.

**All integer responses from a coherent corridor.** Keep the same finite ring of length \(N=2p\ge6\) and any fixed period-\(N\) departure transport in the abelian sheet group. The localized start is \(0,R\). Number steps from one. At step \(j\), copy only whether the output belongs to the following mask, and continue the all-yes word:

- Always admit \((j,R)\) and \((j-2,R)\).
- Also admit \((j-2,L)\) exactly when \(j=1\bmod N\).

Positions are residues modulo \(N\). This uses a periodic clock with \(N\) phases, coherent membership in a three-cell mask, and unresolved sheets. Storing a separate yes/no result is sufficient; revealing which admitted cell was used would destroy the experiment. A branch can stay on the leading path with all steps to the right, or turn left exactly once at a time \(j=1\bmod N\). A second left step would finish at the forbidden offset minus four. The paths have positive signs because no two consecutive steps are left.

For one turn at \(j\) in a history of length \(t\), the accumulated sheet shift is
\[
        H_j(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1} g(i)+\sum_{i=j}^{t-1}g(i-2).
\]
Thus \(H_{j+N}(t)-H_j(t)=\sum_{i=j}^{j+N-1}[g(i)-g(i-2)]=0\) whenever both turns are allowed. All turn histories retained on that branch can interfere at the same sheet without a compensating gate or knowledge of a character. The equality uses commutation of the sheet translations and full periods of the fixed transport.

Stop after \(t=1+nN\), with \(n\ge1\). Copy the trailing-site predicate into a persistent flag. Condition on the corridor transcript. On the trailing site the two incoming directions have integer amplitudes \(n\) and 1 on the same sheet; on the leading site the only amplitude is 1. Take one further unobserved step. The trailing-flag table contains counts of magnitudes \(n+1\) and \(n-1\), the leading-flag table two unit counts. Separate flag values prevent interference even at coincident positions. No other amplitude contributes to those two tables. If the readout on physical endpoints is given by the same nonnegative function \(f(|n|)\) of the signed count, normalized over endpoints with \(f(0)=0, f(1)=1\), stability of the joint previously recorded corridor transcript and flag, comparing a stop now with one further free step, gives
\[
       f(n+1)+f(n-1)=2f(n)+2.
\]
Here all intermediate histories contributing to a given record are added coherently. Intermediate scalar normalizations are avoided by retaining the unnormalized counts for the complete transcript. Equivalently the odds compare the two parts of the retained word in a joint endpoint law. Normalization across all other flag or corridor outcomes cancels from the odds. Stability of the unconditional trailing bit alone would be insufficient if rejected words also carry that bit. A fresh pointer slot for each corridor outcome through the chosen horizon is enough. The same \(f\) across horizons is essential. Its first differences satisfy \(f(n+1)-f(n)=f(n)-f(n-1)+2\), starting from one. Summation gives **\(f(n)=n^2\) for every integer \(n\ge0\)**. This particular implication uses only a family of measured flags, one fixed position-dependent transport, and one repeating mask schedule. The choice of stopping horizon varies with \(n\). It does not require the consistency hypothesis on every unmeasured subset of histories. Conversely the squared response preserves every retained flag marginal by orthogonality of the signed step. The statement selects a response on integer endpoint counts. Rules with preparation-dependent responses, different record semantics, or extra information attached to individual paths fall outside this implication.

The experiment is poorly conditioned at large \(n\). In the derived model the conditioned word has prior probability \((n^2+2)/2^{1+nN}\). If measured odds yield a recurrence residual \(e_j=f(j+1)-2f(j)+f(j-1)-2\), then \(f(n)-n^2=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)e_j\). Even a uniform residual bound \(\epsilon\) up to that horizon permits discrepancy \(\epsilon n(n-1)/2\). An experiment containing finitely many horizons cannot force the untested ordinates. The [integer checker](check_fixed_transport.py) implements the actual time-indexed masks, including sheets, and retains the endpoint counts on 1,440 stops through \(n=96\) across three choices of transport.

The resulting localized-input cylinder probabilities are rational. All candidate embeddings still give the same data, including the dark setting, transfer records and the persistent-flag odds. This imposes a squared response under specified record assumptions while leaving the absolute embedding and the origin of those assumptions open.

## Checks and scope

The [checker](check_fixed_transport.py) retains all 48,276 tagged destructive-port null/non-null classifications over three carriers and five prime orders. It propagates every step of 39 finite preparations and all 373 follow-up settings in physical coordinates. It includes the incomplete-menu residuals, both eraser outcomes, the flag controls, and the integer counts at all 500 endpoints of the five-sheet-pair graph through 24 steps. The analytic statements use finite incidence, Fourier orthogonality and signed-path addition. These are mathematical results conditional on a selected graph and record interpretation. A second PCP participant cross-read the fixed-transport, flag, pointer and all-integer corridor arguments. Separate calculations included the port probabilities, globally normalized flag odds and a fine-reading control. Lean 4.19.0 checked the recurrence for all integer magnitudes, the reduced moving masks, periodic sheet-sum identities and the five-step flag histograms. The empirical standing of a persistent record is a premise of the formal result. Physical realization and empirical identification remain open.
