# What changes when a successful walk preparation is repeated?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

One three-tick position filter sends every accepted history to a single right rail. Although that port is sharp again, a mixed count-and-square law generally predicts a different chance of repeating the success. On the cyclic staircase, two short repeat tests suffice to check whether a stationary law uses a single component. A second construction compares every phase lag at the same final well and time, giving an operational test for the ordinary character relative to a specified physical unit.

All probabilities below use the original launches as denominator. A position mask keeps both rails at each admitted well, with coherent addition among survivors. A sign change occurs only on an L step after L. We use the stationary additive endpoint law on complete product events. Its two-term form on the signed line follows from the single-launch position-mask theorem. On the staircase we use the fixed count/character family and its additional hypotheses concerning the gain alphabet and switching. No independent state reset is included inside a product event. Composition of independently prepared histories is a distinct physical assumption in generalized quantum-measure theory as well. ## A single signed walk

Start at $(h,R)$. For three ticks keep both neighboring wells, then only $h$, then only $h+1$. Call this filter $C$. The only accepted words are RLR and LRR, both positive, both arriving on R. The reference $I$ keeps only $h+1$ on the first tick and has just the RLR word. Translate the next block to the actual new home $h+1$. Starting with $(p,q)$ positive and negative histories on R, $k$ consecutive $C$ blocks return $(2^kp,2^kq)$ on R at $h+k$. This is an identity for the actual masks at every depth. [Standalone Lean proof](lean/RepeatedPositionRecord.lean)

Let $\alpha$ denote the count fraction and $1-\alpha$ the square fraction, with $0\le\alpha\le1$. Each block has eight unrestricted continuations per input word. The success probability for $k$ blocks from one positive launch is

$$S_k=\alpha\,4^{-k}+(1-\alpha)\,2^{-k}.$$

Two fresh, independently prepared copies give $S_1^2$ for two successes. One uninterrupted walk gives $S_2$, so

$$S_2-S_1^2=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{16}.$$

For $\alpha=1/2$, the next block succeeds on $5/12$ of the first successes, against $3/8$ of fresh launches. Conditioning reweights the two contributions by their different success rates. Both pure laws regenerate their own one-block success rate. Exact repeatability excludes an interior mixture in this family. A finite upper bound $d<1/64$ on the gap implies $\min(\alpha,1-\alpha)\le(1-\sqrt{1-64d})/2$. An apparatus that refreshes its latent component between blocks can give zero gap at any mixture weight. It agrees on the fresh one-block runs and uses a different multitime law.

An independent upper bound $A$ with $1/2<A<1$ can resolve this two-branch ambiguity. If both bounds hold on the same preparation and $d<A(1-A)/16$, then $\alpha\le(1-\sqrt{1-64d})/2$. The retrospective cesium comparison gives $A=0.680$ under its instrument and sampling assumptions. In a matched repeat experiment the corresponding gap would need to be below $0.0136$ to eliminate the upper branch. Transferring that numerical limit would require a common response law across the settings and joint uncertainty coverage. The cesium paper contains no such repeat measurement. [Four-step data and limitations](Cesium_count_fraction.md)

There is a positive-port comparison to separate the two pure laws. From $(0,R)$, in setting A keep $\{-1,1\},\{-2\},\{-3,-1\},\{-2\}$ on four successive ticks. Setting B adds the well 0 at tick two. At the final well $-2$, A admits LLLR on R with sign $+$ and LLRL on L with sign $-$. B adds the opposite-sign words RLLL and LRLL on L. Thus the signed output vectors are identical, and every named cell has positive weight in both settings. Relative to the common denominator 16, the R and L probabilities are $(1,1)$ in A and $(1,1+2\alpha)$ in B. The ratio of the L-to-R odds in B to that in A is $1+2\alpha$, equal to one for pure square and three for pure count. Setting brightness and fixed positive gains of the two rail detectors cancel. A source initially on L fails the sharp-R comparison. If an equality of accepted *operators* is needed, prefix both protocols by the singleton positions 1 then 2 and translate their masks by two. Any surviving field at the prefix exit lives entirely on $(2,R)$. Distinguishable microscopic failure records would still distinguish the two apparatuses. ## Two tests for a single component on the staircase

Take the $4M$-site ring with $M\ge3$. Each departure at $x$ increments the sheet by $g(x)=\lfloor x/2\rfloor\bmod M$. A component $s$ evaluates a history sign and sheet residue $j$ as $\mathrm{sign}\,\exp(2\pi i s j/M)$. Its probability is the squared sum at the endpoint divided by $2^t$. Count instead uses the number of words divided by $2^t$. Real word coefficients identify $s$ and $M-s$ in this apparatus. Let the weights of counting and the folded character labels be nonnegative and sum to one. They must be fixed for the preparation, serial comparison and all apertures under discussion. They can physically reflect an unresolved population, even when another description uses a pure law.

For $n=1,2$ keep the union of the positions of the words $R^nL^nR$ and $L^nR^nR$ at each tick. The two branches separate, turn and meet at $h$ at time $2n$. Only an R step is admitted from there. During separation and contraction the only allowed moves follow those words. Their signs are both $(-1)^{n-1}$. Their departure positions are $h\pm r$ with $r=0,1,\ldots,n,\ldots,1,0$. Since $g(h+r)-g(h-r)=r\bmod M$, the relative gain is $n^2\bmod M$, independent of $h$. Using integer lifts gives the same rule across the seam. Thus each component has a per-block probability

$$u_n(*)=2^{-2n},\qquad u_n(s)=2^{-2n}\,[1+\cos(2\pi n^2s/M)].$$

Both words end on the same R rail at $h+1$. After translating the next filter, word counts multiply and the character amplitudes multiply up to common phases. For a random component label with the fixed weights just specified, the one-block and two-block probabilities are $\mathbb E u_n$ and $\mathbb E u_n^2$. Their difference from independent repetition is $\operatorname{Var}(u_n)$. This uses unconditional joint success even when an entire component is dark.

At $n=1$ cosine distinguishes all folded labels. Counting ties only with the quarter-turn label when $4\mid M$. At $n=2$ the latter has cosine one and counting has zero interference. Consequently **both gaps vanish if and only if the distribution is concentrated on one folded label or on counting**. For $M=4$, equal weight on counting and the quarter-turn label gives first gap zero and second gap exactly $1/1024$. For finite tolerances, let $d_M$ be the minimum Euclidean distance between distinct prediction vectors $(u_1,u_2)$, counting included. If the two variance bounds are $v_1,v_2$, the largest component weight satisfies $1-w_{\max}\le 2(v_1+v_2)/d_M^2$. Indeed, the sum of variances is half the mean squared distance between two independent draws, while the probability of different labels is $1-\sum_j w_j^2\ge1-w_{\max}$. These are estimates of weights for a fixed model and calibrated probabilities. ## Comparing the ordinary character at a common well and time

The same walk can present a two-word interference experiment for any relative residue $r$ with exactly the same clock time, start and terminal detector across the settings. Work with lifted positions beginning at zero. Split twice, onto RR and LL paths, leaving separation four sites. At separation four, a shared R or L move in both arms has no cross-edge to the other arm's next position. A move that separates them to six sites and back to four also has no such edge. For $M\ge3$ these separations fit the ring: at maximum separation six on the smallest ring of size 12, the opposite way around the ring still has length six.

For even integer lag $\ell=4+2k$, use a top word $RR\,R^k\,LLR$ and a bottom word $LL\,R^k\,RRR$ ($k\ge0$). For odd $\ell=9+2k$ with $k\ge2$, put $W=R^{k-1}L$. Use $RR\,W\,RL\,LLR$ above and $LL\,W\,LR\,RRR$ below. Retain their position union at each tick. In the even construction each branch has one L-after-L. In the odd construction the top word has two and the bottom word has two. At departure the gain difference is half the separation: changing the common center by an odd number of sites preserves this equality. The basic split and contraction contribute four, the $k$ shared moves contribute $2k$, and the added excursion in the odd case contributes $2+3$. Both branches end on a common right rail. For each residue choose any representative $\ell\ge13$ with $\ell\equiv r\bmod M$. This covers zero as well as the nonzero lags.

To equalize the clocks and detector, choose an even $T$ sufficiently large for every constructed endpoint $(x_i,R)$ at time $t_i$ to reach $(-1)$ in $T-t_i-1$ steps and finally take R to $(0,R)$. Parity holds since $x_i\equiv t_i\bmod 2$. Retain one position per tick along one such suffix. All histories take the same suffix and preserve their relative sign and gain. A reference that retains the upper path alone has one word, at the same detector and time, with success probability $2^{-T}$ in every component. The original two-word setting has

$$P_r=2^{1-T}\left(1+\sum_s b_s\cos(2\pi sr/M)\right),$$

where each $b_s$ is the total weight of a folded character and the remaining weight is on count. With equal exposure or calibrated ratios to the one-word references, the sequence $2^{T-1}P_r-1$ gives those cosine coefficients by the discrete Fourier transform. The count fraction is $2-2^{T-1}P_0$. The information in a lag curve was already known from native edge ports at varying horizons. These masks put all entries at one horizon on one detector, and provide an experimentally separate check of the purity assumption. The common denominator can be exponentially costly.

For a pure character with a nonflat scan, inspect the nonzero lag maxima. The ordinary pair $s=\pm1$ gives precisely the maximizers $r=\pm1$ (modulo $M$). Conversely, a nonfaithful nonconstant character has a nonzero lag with cosine one while its value at $r=1$ is smaller. For a faithful character the maximizers solve $sr=\pm1\bmod M$. Hence $r=1$ is among them exactly for $s=\pm1$. Counting and $s=0$ each give a flat scan at a different absolute level. For $M\ge4$ the ordinary response exceeds the largest response at a lag distinct from $0,\pm1$ by $2^{1-T}[\cos(2\pi/M)-\cos(4\pi/M)]$. A simultaneous reassignment of both the detector's character and the unit defining the physical staircase changes the interpretation of the labels. Identifying that unit and obtaining the comparison are necessary to give this mathematical test physical force.
