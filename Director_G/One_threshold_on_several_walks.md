# Reading one count threshold across several walks

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Three forward walks give a finite comparison of two ways to record the same position event. On one eight-well walk a detector that squares the two port masses predicts a hit rate of $25/34$. On three copies, a uniformly assigned count threshold gives at most $2/3$ for **any** common monotone two-port response after random port interchange. The ordinary law predicts $5/8$ in both experiments. A measured disagreement would distinguish the procedures. A measured agreement, by itself, would permit other probability laws.

With more banks, the count reader can also supply a reference rate for the one-walk mass. The construction uses reversible Boolean control and a single terminal read. It needs no inverse walk, selective phases, or intermediate detection. Its cost lies in preparing multiple banks and controlling their joint records. A related single-register test uses a long sequence of matched echoes. [Single-register reference](Testing_a_binary_probability_with_echo_thresholds.md)

## One flag, one detector read

On bank $i$, let $P_i$ project onto a chosen terminal event. The bank flags commute. Their joint word projectors $P_b$ for $b\in\{0,1\}^m$ give kinematic weights $\|P_b\psi\|^2$ summing to one. A density operator gives the corresponding traces. Write $K=\sum b_i$ and $p_m=\mathbb E_{\rm kin}(K/m)$. This is the average of the single-bank squared masses even for entangled banks. The notation $\mathbb E_{\rm kin}$ means summation with those geometrical weights. It makes no assertion about a frequency before the detector is specified.

Choose $J$ uniformly from $1,\ldots,m$ independently of the state and detector. Reversibly compute the bit $[K\ge J]$ into a blank flag, retaining the word as unmeasured memory. For example, increment an integer register conditional on each bank event, XOR its threshold comparison into the flag, and reverse the increments. This takes $m$ forward and $m$ reverse controlled increments on $\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil$ count bits. The original walks need no reversal. The two final flag sectors must be pooled before applying the readout. Threshold choices can also be assigned in balanced groups if they sample the same population at each setting.

Put $s_j=\Pr_{\rm kin}(K\ge j)$. A fixed type with binary response $r(s)$ reports the flag at rate $A_m(r)=m^{-1}\sum_j r(s_j)$. The finite identity $m^{-1}\sum_j s_j=p_m$ follows by counting each weight-$k$ word exactly $k$ times. The ordinary response $r(s)=s$ therefore agrees with the single-bank average at every $m$ and for every state. An actual detector can depend on the residual word, the threshold choice or the number of banks. Using one curve on the pooled flag across the stated settings is an apparatus premise.

## When the record approaches the ordinary mass

Allow every fixed continuous binary response $r:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ with $r(0)=0$, $r(1)=1$. Then
$$ A_m(r)-p_m\longrightarrow0\ \text{for every such }r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Var}_{\rm kin}(K/m)\longrightarrow0. $$
No convergence of $p_m$ is required. For a proof introduce an independent *mathematical* draw $K'$ from the same kinematic count table and put $X=K/m$, $X'=K'/m$. The detector $r(s)=s^2$ has discrepancy
$$D_m=p_m-A_m(r)=\frac1m\sum_j s_j(1-s_j)=\tfrac12\mathbb E_{\rm kin}|X-X'|.$$
The last equality follows by counting thresholds that separate $K,K'$ and including both orders. Since $|X-X'|\le1$ and $\mathbb E(X-X')^2=2\operatorname{Var}X$,
$$\operatorname{Var}X\le D_m\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}X/2}.$$
The sharper upper bound $D_m\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}X/3}$ follows by writing $X=Q(U)$ for a uniform $U$ and increasing quantile $Q$. Integration over two ordered uniforms gives $D_m=\int_0^1(2u-1)(Q(u)-p_m)\,du$. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz and $\int_0^1(2u-1)^2\,du=1/3$. Uniform count grids approach equality. Thus the single allowed curve $s^2$ proves necessity. This is an asymmetric two-port response assigning success probability $s^2$ and failure probability $1-s^2$. It differs from squaring and renormalizing both cell masses. [Binh's bound on the Gini mean difference](https://math.ac.vn/uploads/files/11_V36N3Acta_10_27_B3.pdf)

Conversely the fraction of threshold masses in $[\eta,1-\eta]$ is at most $D_m/[\eta(1-\eta)]$ for $0<\eta<1/2$. If calibration bounds $|r(s)-s|$ by $e$ in the two endpoint neighborhoods, the total absolute bias is at most $e+(1-e)\min(1,D_m/[\eta(1-\eta)])$ for $0\le e\le1$. Continuity just at the two endpoints suffices for the limit; a finite error estimate needs their neighborhoods. This convergence holds for each fixed detector. At each finite size all tails of a nondegenerate binomial law lie strictly below one, so a size-dependent continuous detector can stay dark at every tested tail and rise to one beyond the largest. In mixtures, the argument can be applied at each type and averaged by bounded convergence if the assigned settings sample the same ensemble.

The variance equals $m^{-2}$ times the sum of the $m$ individual variances and twice the sum of the distinct pair covariances in the kinematic table. Pairwise independent flag coordinates give variance at most $1/(4m)$ even when the full word does not factor. At fixed type identical product banks of mass $p$ give $p_m=p$. Correlated classical banks supported only at the all-zero and all-one words give $s_j=p$ for every threshold and $A_m(r)=r(p)$ at every size. The coherent superposition of these words gives the same count table. Register count and spatial separation alone do not establish concentration. More generally, expectation values of frequency operators need an operational link to records. Here that link is the specified endpoint response and the physical collective flag measurement. [Caves and Schack on the frequency operator](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0409144)

## What a random port interchange can hide

With a fair independent interchange and decoding of the two physical ports, the observed response has the form $G(s)=[r(s)+1-r(1-s)]/2$. Hence $G(1-s)=1-G(s)$. The asymmetric witness $s^2$ becomes the identity under this operation. For endpoint-sharp continuous complementary responses, concentration is sufficient and there is also an exact wider criterion. Give each $s_j$ weight $1/m$. Every such $G$ has $A_m(G)-p_m\to0$ precisely when every weak accumulation measure of these threshold-mass distributions **on the full interval** has reflection-symmetric restriction to $(0,1)$. Endpoint atoms may have different weights.

Indeed $G(s)-s$ is continuous, zero at the endpoints, and odd under $s\mapsto1-s$. Symmetry of the interior cancels it on every subsequential limit. Conversely, if the interior restriction differs from its reflection, a smooth antisymmetric test function $\phi$ of compact interior support separates them. The curves $s+c\phi(s)$ for sufficiently small $|c|$ stay in $[0,1]$ and are strictly increasing. One such curve has a nonzero discrepancy along the convergent subsequence. Compactness on $[0,1]$ gives the equivalence. For a finite table the criterion for equality for every such detector is equality of multiplicities at every interior pair $s,1-s$ apart from the unrestricted midpoint.

Equivalently, the finite count histogram must be symmetric about its midrange. If $a,b$ are its least and greatest supported counts, its interior tail values occur at $j=a+1,\ldots,b$. Descending order forces complementary tails to pair as $s_j=1-s_{a+b+1-j}$. This equates the survival functions of $K$ and $a+b-K$. The converse follows by the same identity, including deterministic counts and gaps.

For example take eight banks with equal kinematic weights at $K=2,8$ and distribute weight uniformly within each layer. Each bank has mass $5/8$. Two threshold sectors have mass one and six have mass one half. Every sharp complementary detector reports exactly $5/8$ on average, although the count-fraction variance is $9/64$ and the asymmetric $s^2$ detector gives a shortfall of $3/16$. Encoding each flag into an equal number of perfectly correlated coordinate bits keeps these values at arbitrarily large sizes. A port swap can remove fixed gains and also erase evidence of correlated preparation. Measurements that will be used to diagnose such correlation should retain the physical port assignments.

## A three-copy comparison after three ticks

Launch a sharp right-going walker at zero on eight wells. At each of three steps apply the signed Hadamard coin with negative $L\to L$ entry, then shift with the outgoing rail. At site $+1$ the two rail amplitudes have numerators $2,1$ in units of $1/\sqrt8$. Flag that site as a whole, pooling both rails in one sector and all other sites in the other sector. Its mass is $p=5/8$. With three conditionally identical product banks, the three threshold masses in descending order are
$$ (s_1,s_2,s_3)=(485,350,125)/512. $$
For a common nondecreasing complementary curve, $s_3<1-s_2$ gives $G(s_3)+G(s_2)\le1$. Thus $A_3(G)\le2/3$, regardless of the shape of the response. The midpoint step attains that bound, as does a continuous sharp response with a sufficiently narrow transition. The bound persists if absolute shifts of the second and third sector masses sum to at most $37/512$. Source factorization supplies the quoted masses; the sum condition suffices for the upper bound alone.

Under the binary power-two detector, the one-bank hit rate is $p^2/[p^2+(1-p)^2]=25/34$. If that native hit rate is observed and the above premises hold for the reference, the threshold average must fall short by at least $7/102$. Squaring separately at all eight sites would test a different detector. Across heterogeneous source types a population mean of $5/8$ alone is insufficient for the three-copy bound. For example mix a proportion $25/32$ of product sources with $p=4/5$ and the rest at $p=0$. For the first type all three threshold masses exceed $0.51$. One continuous complementary response that rises from zero at $0.49$ to one at $0.51$ gives the population reference rate $25/32$. Each setting must retain every original block and account for unresolved reads.

For a prospective two-arm experiment use $n$ independent single-bank launches and $n$ independent triple-bank blocks. Assign each triple threshold uniformly and independently. Let $\widehat\Delta$ be the single-bank rate minus the triple-bank rate. To handle erasures of otherwise valid reports, score each unresolved single-bank read as zero and each unresolved triple-bank read as one. This makes the recorded difference conservative for the complete binary reports. Reject equal complete rates when $\widehat\Delta\ge7/204$. The one-sided Hoeffding bound gives size at most $\exp[-n(7/204)^2]$. When the mean difference **after this loss coding** is at least $7/102$, it gives the same upper bound on a missed rejection. With $n=2545$ both bounds are below 5%, using 5,090 binary observations and 10,180 bank launches. This is a sufficient exposure, conditional on independent blocks and the stated gap. Common noise across blocks or enough erasures can remove the guarantee of power. No launches have been made. [Hoeffding's bound for independent bounded variables](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830)
