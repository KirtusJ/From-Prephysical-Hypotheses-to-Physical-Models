# A probability test using only the cubic rail phase

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

The [four-phase comparison](Four_phase_rail_probability.md) uses a quarter-turn. What can a walk test if its only phase increment is a third of a turn? Two-well ticks switch between $H$ and $DH$, where $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{2\pi i/3})$ and $H$ is the real balanced Hadamard coin. The prepared Bloch point and its rotation under each word are coherent kinematic labels. For an event at the initial well after an even number of ticks its probability $F(s)$ is initially arbitrary between zero and one. Assume the preparation point suffices for that detector, including coherent programs of different lengths when comparing across clocks. The outcome probability of a classical mixture is the weighted mean of its component frequencies. No probability formula for a spinor or mixed state has been assumed. ## What a phase triangle misses

The equal mixture of $s,Ds,D^2s$ makes a triangle about the rail axis. For a triangle with axis $n$ and latitude $t$, its mean point is $tn$ and its second moment matrix is $t^2 nn^T+(1-t^2)(I-nn^T)/2$. Both quantities are independent of azimuth. Thus even comparison with every differently oriented triangle **at the same axis and latitude** misses every quadratic perturbation of an affine detector. A calibrated counterexample is $F_2(s)=(1+s_z)/2+(1-s_z^2)/8$. It is increasing in $s_z$, normalized as a binary event together with its complement, and takes zero and one at the two sharp poles. A hypothetical requirement $F(-s)=1-F(s)$ on the same detector would remove even perturbations but has not been imposed. The quadratic blindness follows from cancellation of the first two angular Fourier harmonics. The third survives: for the equatorial triangle of angles $0,2\pi/3,4\pi/3$, averaging $(s_x)^3$ gives $1/4$; advancing the initial angle by $\pi/3$ gives $-1/4$. This comparison illustrates the possible geometry. The cubic phase knob cycles its three vertices; turning that knob again does not itself realize a new rotated triangle. Related exact integration results for finite point sets appear in spherical design theory. [Delsarte, Goethals and Seidel](https://www.math.unipd.it/~alvise/PHD_2024/LEZIONI/SPHERE/PDF_SPHERE/DELSARTE%20Spherical%20codes%20and%20designs.pdf)

If two triangles offset by a **sixth-turn** can be compared at every axis and latitude, degree two is the exact ceiling for bounded Borel responses satisfying all those equalities. For a sphere rotation $R_n$ through $\pi/3$ about $n$, equality reads $\sum_{j=0}^5(-1)^j F(R_n^j s)=0$ for all $n,s$. In $L^2(S^2)$ each degree-$l$ spherical harmonic space is irreducible under rotations. Orthogonal projection to that degree commutes with each rotation and hence with every alternating sum. Within each degree the common nullspace of all the sums is rotation-invariant. For $n=z$ the alternating sum on an azimuthal mode $m$ is six times that mode when $m\equiv3\pmod6$, and zero otherwise. Every $l\ge3$ contains $m=3$. Thus its common nullspace is proper and, by irreducibility, zero. Degrees zero through two survive. Completeness gives a degree-at-most-two function $P$ equal to $F$ almost everywhere. [Spherical harmonics and rotation representations](https://people.ucsc.edu/~rmont/classes/LieGpsW2017/lectures/SphericalHarmonics.pdf)

The pointwise conclusion uses the full pointwise comparison premise. Fix $s$ and choose an axis $n$ uniformly on the sphere. For each $j=1,\dots,5$, the latitude $s\cdot R_n^j s=c+(1-c)(n\cdot s)^2$, $c=\cos(j\pi/3)<1$, has an absolutely continuous distribution since $n\cdot s$ is uniform on $[-1,1]$. The output law is invariant under axial rotations around $s$, so it is absolutely continuous with respect to sphere area. Almost surely all five other vertices avoid the exceptional area-zero set where $F\ne P$. Applying the alternating identity to $F-P$ at the fixed first vertex now gives $F(s)=P(s)$. An independently measured complement identity $F(s)+F(-s)=1$ for every $s$ then forces the even part of $P$ to be the constant $1/2$ and leaves just the affine odd part. The degree ceiling applies to this ideal comparison family. On the stipulated switched cubic hardware a sixth-turn about the rail axis is **not** an exact word as a transformation on every input. Indeed its action on the residues $(X,Z)$ modulo three would be $(-X,Z)$, whereas, as shown below, a native word can only preserve or exchange these two residues. Inputs $x$ and $z$ distinguish these maps. This restriction on a gate allows accidental agreement on individual states.

## Four fixed reference preparations

Write a Bloch vector in coordinates $(x,y,z)=(X,\sqrt3 Y,Z)$. The transformations on $(X,Y,Z)$ are
$$H:(Z,-Y,X),\qquad D:((-X-3Y)/2,(X-Y)/2,Z).$$
In particular every word from the sharp poles has dyadic rational $X,Y,Z$. The metric in these coordinates is $X^2+3Y^2+Z^2=1$. With $0=H$, $1=DH$, read a bit word from **left to right in time**. The word $01$ is $D$, $0000$ is the identity and $1010$ prepares $v_1$ from $v_0$ as listed below. Append $01$ once or twice to prepare $v_2,v_3$; pad with $00$ to eight ticks. All reference preparations start from R at the original well.
$$v_0=(0,0,1),\quad v_1=(0,1/2,-1/2),\quad v_2=(-3/4,-1/4,-1/2),\quad v_3=(3/4,-1/4,-1/2).$$
They are affinely independent. The barycentric coordinates of a queried point $s=(X,Y,Z)$, in that order, are
$$\lambda_0=(1+2Z)/3,\quad \lambda_1=(2-2Z+12Y)/9,\quad \lambda_2=(2-2Z-6X-6Y)/9,\quad \lambda_3=(2-2Z+6X-6Y)/9.$$
They sum to one and reconstruct $s$. They can be signed. Set $N=\sum_i(-\lambda_i)_+$ and $L=1+N=\sum_i(\lambda_i)_+$. Compare a mixture with weights $1/L$ on $s$ and $(-\lambda_i)_+/L$ on the negative reference vertices to one with weights $(\lambda_i)_+/L$ on the positive vertices. Both sides contain only positive classical exposures and have exactly the same coherent-coordinate barycenter. That identity specifies the comparison. Equality of measured click means is an **additional operational premise** whose failure has observable consequences.

For an even word of length $d$, implement each prescription at the common clock $\max(d,8)$ with identity padding. A common denominator for the $\lambda_i$ is $M=9\cdot2^d$. Put $m_i=M\lambda_i$ and $K=\sum_i(m_i)_+$. A block of $K$ trials on each side suffices to realize the designed setting frequencies exactly, with $M$ trials of the query on the first side. Reduce all counts by their common divisor if desired. For independent randomized allocation use uniform labels $0,\dots,K-1$, assigned to each setting in its prescribed count. Draw $\lceil\log_2 K\rceil$ independent fair bits; reject labels $\ge K$ and redraw **before launching the walker**, with fewer than two rounds in expectation for $K>1$. Program-independent input and stable original-launch denominators matter. Stratification at the stated counts avoids a fair-bit source if drift and setting order are controlled separately.

To use one interpolant across clocks, the four reference responses must remain the same when padded to each query clock. Without that premise each tested clock has its own fitted reference values. The difference of the two mean event probabilities is exactly $[F(s)-\sum_i\lambda_i F(v_i)]/L$. Thus if it vanishes for every finite even native R word, the **same affine interpolant** $A$ through the four fixed measured reference probabilities agrees with $F$ on every one of those prepared points. No continuity or measurability of $F$ is needed. The same tests with a per-query discrepancy bounded by $e_s$ give $|F(s)-A(s)|\le L e_s$. The sharp uniform coefficient over the sphere is
$$L_*=(7+2\sqrt{13})/9,\qquad \sup_s\sum_i|\lambda_i|=2L_*-1.$$
Indeed $N=\max_{J\subseteq\{0,1,2,3\}}(-\sum_{i\in J}\lambda_i)$. For each affine subset sum $c+w\cdot s$, the maximum of its negative on the sphere is $|w|-c$ in ordinary Euclidean coordinates. For the bottom singleton it is $(2\sqrt{13}-2)/9$. The other nontrivial types (top singleton, bottom pair, top and one bottom, three bottoms, top and two bottoms) give respectively $1/3,4/9,1/3,0,(2\sqrt{13}-7)/9$. At least the bottom-singleton maximizer is approached by the even words, as follows.

Both $D$ and $HDH$ are exact even-tick words and rotate about perpendicular axes by $2\pi/3$. Their product has determinant-one spinor representative of trace $2\cos^2(\pi/3)=1/2$. A finite-order projective rotation would have a spinor trace that is a sum of roots of unity and thus an algebraic integer. The generated group is infinite. Any infinite proper closed subgroup of $SO(3)$ preserves an unoriented line: its Lie algebra must be one-dimensional, since two independent cross-product generators span all three with their bracket. Its identity component is a circle and the full subgroup normalizes its axis. The two third-turns about distinct axes have no common invariant line. Their inverses are squares, so finite positive even words reach a **dense** subset of the sphere. This argument supplies no tick bound for approximating a requested direction. [Radin and Sadun, *Subgroups of SO(3) associated with tilings*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9601202)

The opposite sharp pole cannot be prepared exactly from R at an even clock using these switches. Reduce dyadic $X,Z$ modulo three (two is invertible there). Under $D$ the new $X=(-X-3Y)/2$ equals $X$ modulo three and $Z$ is unchanged. Each tick $H$ or $DH$ therefore swaps the two residues. After even time they are $(0,1)$, whereas at $-z$ they are $(0,-1)$. The nonattainment concerns exact words and is compatible with density. The worst barycentric multiplier above is also a supremum: its bottom-singleton maximizers have $Z=1/\sqrt{13}$, outside the dyadic orbit.

Since $A$ is affine, its values in $[0,1]$ on this dense set imply the same bounds everywhere, independently of how $F$ behaves off the prepared set. Supply and test an additional sharp L launch **at the same starting well**, with idle $00$ pairs so that it uses the same final well and detector as the R reference. This supplied source must realize the $-z$ preparation label for the same event, with the same response to equivalent preparations. Its comparison to the same four R references imposes $F(-z)=A(-z)$. The lower-pole comparison uses only twelve equal-exposure slots per side: nine L launches and three R launches on one side, four launches of each bottom reference on the other. Twelve is the least equal-slot count for these particular weights, whose reduced denominators are four and three. If the two sharp calibrations give one on R and zero on L, then $A(s)=1/2+z\cdot s/2$ in physical Euclidean coordinates: its linear part has axial component $1/2$ and norm at most $1/2$. Every compared even-word probability therefore equals the squared R amplitude. This does not assign any probability to untested points outside the orbit. An independently available exact rail exchange followed by the same detector could supply the L preparation. Dense approach to $-z$ alone does not calibrate an independently supplied L source whose probability law is unrestricted. If the L preparation is unavailable, exact R certainty and positivity still permit $A(s)=1-k+k Z$ for $0\le k\le1/2$.

## An eight-tick test against the quadratic law

From a sharp R input take word $1110$ and pad to eight ticks. Its point is $(3/4,1/8,5/8)$ with $\lambda=(3/4,1/4,-1/2,1/2)$. Use six equally weighted trial slots per side. One side uses the query four times and $v_2$ twice. The other uses $v_0$ three times, $v_1$ once and $v_3$ twice. The ordinary R probability gives the same rate on the two sides. The calibrated quadratic above gives a discrepancy of $9/256$ per launch, although it survives every azimuth change at a fixed triangle axis and latitude. For the covariant cubic law $F_3=(1+Z^3)/2$, the signed discrepancy is $-81/512$ per launch. The checker retains its separate prediction and every even word through length eight. ## A six-tick, two-slot comparison

Director Z's independent review found that two native equal-weight triangles with different axes can have the same zero barycenter and distinguish the quadratic law. Their predicted means are $9/16$ and $153/256$, a gap of $9/256$. This prompted a search over pairs of preparations at a common clock. [Director Z’s cross-review and exact search](../Director_Z/cross_review/subagent_xr81_three_phase_rail_probability/REPORT.md)

Use a sharp R source. On one side choose between the six-tick words `000000` and `001011` with equal weights. On the other choose between `001111` and `101111` with equal weights. Both mixtures have scaled mean coordinates $(X,Y,Z)=(-3/8,-1/8,1/4)$. The two endpoint $Z$ values are $(1,-1/2)$ on the first side and $(5/8,-1/8)$ on the second. The ordinary means are both $5/8$. The quadratic means are $43/64$ and $371/512$, giving a second-minus-first contrast of $27/512$. The cubic law gives $23/32$ and $287/512$, a contrast of $-81/512$. These are setting allocations within each side. Repeated original launches are needed to estimate a contrast.

The [exact checker](check_cubic_rail_law.py) compares all unordered pairs with repetition on the native R states at even depths zero, two, four and six. These clocks have 1, 3, 8 and 18 distinct states. The four-tick clock already has different pairs with an equal midpoint, but their quadratic contrast vanishes. Six is the first common even clock with an equal-weight pair comparison that separates these two candidate laws. This search concerns the stated R source, two switch choices, observable and class of comparisons. A single preparation on each side with equal kinematic coordinates has identical predictions for both laws. Agreement on these two-slot panels still leaves untested detector laws and source histories open.

A finite set of tested protocols can constrain only the queried probabilities under these assumptions. A history-dependent detector, altered gain under the switch, a permanent unswitchable cubic phase, or losses conditioned away before recording change the experiment. Ordinary classical mixing and externally verified control kinematics are premises. Deriving them from the primitive walk and extending the probability law to its many-body experiments remain open.
