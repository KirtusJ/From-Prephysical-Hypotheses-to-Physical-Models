# What the force walk can tell from its own records

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist  
Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada  
Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A three-step return test compares the force walk's sheet displacement with a separately implemented step on the same sheet. It gives probability $1/2$ or zero according to whether their strides agree up to orientation. A second experiment uses closed path differences to test the speed of transport during the force step. Both have finite preparations and registered terminal events. The first determines a relation between two physical operations. Identifying either operation with a particular abstract cyclotomic generator requires a further premise. ## Comparing two strides by returning to a marked cell

Use a line for the base and a cycle $\mathbb Z/M$ for the sheet, $M\ge3$. One tick from base $(x,c)$ multiplies amplitudes by the real signed Hadamard coin, moves the base in the selected direction and adds $kx$ to the sheet at departure. The only negative coin entry is $L\to L$. Fix the observable before launch. Start at $(0,R,y_0)$, with a detector marking the same physical sheet address $y_0$ throughout. Take two force ticks. Keep the final base position and coin as orthogonal modes. Couple a fresh balanced coin to an independent sheet step of stride $\ell$, then read whether the sheet returns to $y_0$. Both $k$ and $\ell$ are units modulo $M$.

Write $a,b,d\in\{\pm1\}$ for the first two directions and the fresh probe direction. The base endpoints $(a+b,b)$ distinguish all four force histories. The sheet offset at the detector is $ka+\ell d$. The eight elementary records therefore have weight $1/8$ apiece under the squared-coordinate law. For $k=\ell$ exactly the four choices $a=-d$ return, and for $k=-\ell$ the four choices $a=d$ return. These cases are disjoint since $M\ge3$ and $\ell$ is a unit. All other $k$ give zero.

$$P(\text{marked cell after two force ticks and one ruler tick})=
\tfrac12\,{\bf1}\{k=\pm\ell\bmod M\}.$$

This is an unconditional single-launch test. It uses only equality of final and marked addresses and preserves the unresolved base modes. There is no input Fourier-state preparation, address metric, intermediate measurement, or postselection. The base origin is physical too: at a launch from $x_0$ the displacement acquires $2kx_0$. Replacing the fiducial with an independently sampled uniform address gives return probability $1/M$ at every $k$. A common shift of both the detector and initial sheet leaves the test unchanged. [Signed-coordinate checker and displaced controls](check_holonomy_embedding.py) · [Finite record tables](results/holonomy_embedding.json)

The same apparatus measures the entire unoriented ratio of the two strides. After the two force ticks take $t$ ordinary balanced ruler steps. Prepare a fresh force and probe state for each trial and choose its single terminal read time in advance. In units of the ruler let $d$ be the shortest cyclic distance of $k\ell^{-1}$ from zero. No path from either lobe can reach the marker for $t<d$. At $t=d$ there is a unique shortest route from each lobe, since a unit at order $M\ge3$ cannot be antipodal. Every probe direction along that route is the same. Its squared amplitude is $2^{-d}$ for a fresh localized direction state and the four orthogonal force modes have total mass one. The earliest horizon with positive terminal probability is therefore exactly $d$, with unconditional mass $2^{-d}$. It can be expensive to observe a distant ratio. At the correct horizon $N$ independent trials all miss it with probability $(1-2^{-d})^N$. A finite sample of misses does not establish a dark port. Monitoring the probe continuously is a different instrument. [Terminal return census](results/holonomy_embedding.json)

An independently implemented primitive step of stride $\ell=1$ thus rejects every other unoriented *physical* unit except $\pm1$ at the one-step horizon. For example, at $M=8$ it separates actual force strides 1 and 5. If instead one conjugates the whole apparatus by $y\mapsto uy$ for a unit $u$, that map transports the marker, force coupling and ruler together. The event has probability $1/2$ for $(k,\ell)=(u,u)$ just as for $(1,1)$. Likewise, changing only the embedding used to describe an unchanged integer circuit changes no address or port. All the finite masses above are rational and the normalized Hadamard's choice of sign of $\sqrt2$ gives a common depth-dependent sign. The test can ground the ordinary real cyclotomic assignment **conditional on identifying the named force phase with the independently held ruler unit**. The walk has supplied a comparison that can fail if a device changes. An external or primitive identification of the named phase, and a reason for the squared-coordinate readout, are still required. ## A comparison without a sheet marker across time

To compare two repeatable runs using their terminal records alone, start two independent copies from $(0,R,0)$ and take three force ticks. At base port $(1,R)$ the signed sheet numerators at $+k,-k$ are $+1,+1$. At $(-1,L)$ they are $-1,+1$, respectively. Each noncoincident cell has squared probability $1/8$. All other base ports have at most one history. Record whether both runs reach the same one of these two ports and their cyclic sheet distance is two. The probability at $M\ge4$ is $1/16$ if $2k=\pm2\bmod M$, zero otherwise. Each comparison cancels an unknown translation assigned to its terminal base port, provided both runs use the same translation.

The detector can also avoid numeric distances. For a pair ending at the same chosen port with distinct sheet addresses, launch a fresh two-step balanced sheet walk from the first recorded address, with stride $\ell$. Score equality with the second address. From a localized source the probe puts $1/2$ back at its start and $1/4$ at each of $\pm2\ell$ before any coincident sites are merged. The unconditional event has probability $1/64$ when $2k=\pm2\ell$ and the two probe destinations differ, zero when the relation fails. At $M=4$ the two destinations coincide and the probability is $1/32$. At $M=3$ the equality protocol still gives $1/64$ although every nonzero distance on that cycle is one. A destructive first read requires a way to relaunch at the recorded address. The signed circuit also retains an interference control in which making an intermediate route record changes a probe probability from $5/8$ to $3/8$. [Full port checks for orders 3 through 64](results/holonomy_embedding.json) · [Exact three-tick and alias arithmetic](lean/HolonomyRead.lean)

For odd $M$ the relation $2k=\pm2\ell$ fixes $k=\pm\ell$. For $M=2m$, replacing $k$ by $k+m$ adds the common shift $m[n x_0+n(n-1)/2]\bmod M$ after $n$ ticks from $x_0$. Indeed the departure position at tick $j$ has parity $x_0+j$. The shift is therefore independent of the whole direction history. When $4\mid M$ both choices can be units, sometimes distinct even up to reflection. Any comparison of sites invariant under a shared within-tick translation misses this ambiguity. The persistent marker and independent final step of the first protocol make that translation measurable. [Physical controls and simultaneous relabelings](results/holonomy_embedding.json)

## Equal-sized history events with the same summed phase

Director Z's Z16 question asks whether the multiset of gains at one end vertex can distinguish two events with the same size and the same summed gain. At order five a scalar gain is uniquely $s\zeta_5^j$, with $s=\pm1$. Launch as above at $(0,R,0)$, take seven force steps at $k=1$, and fix the base end vertex $(3,R)$. Its fifteen direction words give exactly two positive and one negative history at **each** residue. Let $A$ contain all ten positive histories. Let $B$ contain one positive and one negative history at each residue. Both have ten histories. The scalar sum on $A$ is $2(1+\zeta_5+\cdots+\zeta_5^4)=0$, also zero in every conjugate primitive character. On $B$ it vanishes by pairwise cancellation. The physical sheet amplitude rows, before the common normalization, are $(2,2,2,2,2)$ and $(0,0,0,0,0)$. Their squared sheet-event weights are $20/128$ and zero. The unfiltered port in the ordinary walk has mass $5/128$. The two formal subsets require separate coherent selectors if they are to be measured as experimental settings. [All signed words and event selections](results/multiset_fibres.json)

The mathematical distinction is present even if a rule is defined directly on gain multisets. Write $D_j$ for the number of positive entries minus the number of negative entries at residue $j$. Then $F=\sum_jD_j^2$ is invariant under switching by any element of the ten-element gain group. On a signed Hadamard block its two output vectors are $U+V$ and $U-V$, up to signed cyclic permutations of the input and output coordinates. The parallelogram identity gives exact factor-two consistency for all multisets. Every norm-preserving degree-two block of roots has this form up to row and column switches, because its two cross terms cancel. In the finite Fourier decomposition of $F$, only the zero *sheet* frequency distinguishes $A$ from $B$ here: it retains the sign of each gain. The sign character alone gives squared sums 100 and zero and is multiplicative on products. These are explicit instances of G10's existing pair-kernel classification. An unconstrained multiset rule that must satisfy consistency only on these blocks cannot be forced to factor through size and the primitive scalar sum. Requiring that factorization or changing the admitted record algebra adds a hypothesis. The [whole-multiset block theorem](Multiset_block_law.md) gives the complete classification for this signed degree-two block under common-depth abundance. ## Path area bounds the speed of exact sheet transport

Set $k=1$ and start at base 0. Let $A(w)$ be the sum of the departure positions along direction word $w$. Interchanging adjacent $LR$ with $RL$ changes $A$ by two and preserves the endpoint after those two steps. For a word with $p$ right and $q$ left steps and $I$ left-before-right pairs,

$$ A(w)=\binom p2-\binom q2+pq-2I.$$

The extreme rearrangements are unique. Take $R^aL^aR$ and $L^aR^aR$. Both end at $(1,R)$ after $2a+1$ ticks. Their gains are $a^2$ and $-a^2$, the maximal possible difference at that port. Keeping the final shared step closes the comparison even when the sheet origin depends on the incoming rail. If an arbitrary phase-coordinate change adds $f(t+1,x',c')-f(t,x,c)$ to each link, it telescopes between the shared endpoints. The difference of the parallel transports, or holonomy of the closed comparison, survives. No experimental reversal of the walk is required. [Formal path and gauge calculation](lean/MovingPhaseClock.lean) · [Lattice translation systems and gauge fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10850)

Suppose each realized sheet edge has a signed integer lift of magnitude at most $v$, and its error relative to the gauge-transformed native edge has an integer lift of magnitude at most $E$. The errors may correlate across both paths. For $4a^2\le M$, the cyclic distance of the relative transport from zero is $2a^2$. Summing both paths' displacement and error budgets gives

$$a^2\le (2a+1)(v+E).$$

Thus uniformly bounded $v,E$ cannot implement this exact gain on arbitrarily large scales at one microscopic tick per base step. Additional transport time, changing the sheet metric, auxiliary apparatus that changes the readout, or agreement solely on a restricted family of input packets each defines a different implementation question. The bound compares the physical sheet metric and gauge translations specified above. ## A terminal record that sees the speed bound

Start with a localized sheet and assume the ordinary squared-coordinate read. Write $P_b(y)$ for the joint mass of final base position and direction $b$ and final sheet $y$. A speed-$v$ register can occupy only an arc of radius $vn$ at tick $n$; allow an arbitrary origin shift chosen separately for each final $b$. Minimize over all probability laws supported on those arcs, with even the base marginal allowed to vary. The exact minimum total variation distance is

$$D_n=\sum_b\left[\sum_y P_b(y)-
 \max_{s}\sum_{y\in[s,s+2vn]}P_b(y)\right].$$

Any probability outside a candidate support contributes at least that mass to the distance. For the converse, move all excluded mass at each port inside its best arc. This attains the bound and preserves the base marginal. A nonzero distance tests realized terminal distributions, including potential ancillary dynamics subject to the same localized support and read. Vanishing distance leaves open whether one compatible dynamics realizes those marginals at successive ticks.

For $v=1$ the first positive $D_n$ is $D_7=1/16$. At $(1,R)$ the signed counts at $y=-9,-7,\ldots,9$ are $(1,-1,-2,1,-1,-1,1,-2,-1,1)$. Squaring and dividing by 128 gives port mass $1/8$. A fifteen-cell arc at this port misses at least $2/128$. Across all ports the excluded numerator is 8. The independently optimized mean sheet distance moved, constrained to preserve each base port, is $1/8$ of a cell. At $n=8,32,80$ the corresponding TV distances are $3/32$, approximately $0.334730$, and approximately $0.388233$. Both launch directions were propagated through all horizons on a 256-site base with 65,536 sheets; there is no winding in these comparisons. An exact control with gain $\operatorname{sign}(x)$ has zero excluded mass through every tested control horizon. [All per-port optima and controls](results/holonomy_cone.json)

Enumerating all direction words for each initial rail and scanning every possible arc placement at each occupied base port reproduces all four values. There is also a decision rule whose event can be fixed **before** knowing any of the port origins. Make two independent localized launches with the same settings. Read the full terminal triples, then score equal base ports and a cyclic sheet distance greater than $2vn$. Every candidate with a frame common to the two launches gives zero, even if the frame was drawn randomly once. For the native walk at tick 7 the six contributing port numerators are $2,6,2,2,6,2$ over $4^7$. The unconditional event probability is $5/4096$, or $5/526$ conditional on matching base ports. At tick 80 its unconditional probability is approximately $0.0257322$. Independently redrawing the reference between launches can itself produce positive events. A detector that sums all sheet positions cannot perform this test. [Ordered-pair distributions and word oracle](check_holonomy_cone.py)

For every $n\ge7$ take $p=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, $q=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$, followed by one common right step. The maximal and minimal rearrangements of that prefix end at the same full port with sheet separation $2pq>2n$. Each extreme is unique and has squared mass $2^{-n}$. On sufficiently large rings the marginal distance is therefore at least $2^{-n}$, and the unconditional two-launch event at least $2\cdot4^{-n}$. These inequalities prove positivity at every finite horizon in the stated regime. They give no positive asymptotic lower bound.
