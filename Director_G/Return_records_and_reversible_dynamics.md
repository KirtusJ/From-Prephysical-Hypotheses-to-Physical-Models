On the fixed staircase walk, two independent requirements constrain probability. Forgetting a terminal sheet label ties the weights at different characters to one another. Recurrence of a finite closed reversible system then eliminates the remaining counting contribution. Together they yield the squared signed-coordinate probabilities for the localized launch. Neither requirement relies on selecting an algebraic name for a complex root of unity. The event law, physical meaning of the sheet, and status of the complete system as finite and reversible are explicit hypotheses. ## Allowing the detectors to have different endpoint laws

Take the position ring of length \(N=4M\), \(M\ge3\), two incoming rails \(d=\pm1\), physical sheets \(j\bmod M\), and launch at \((0,R,0)\). At departure from \(x\) the sheet advances by \(g(x)=\lfloor x/2\rfloor\bmod M\). A balanced splitter contributes a minus on \(L\to L\). The walker streams to \(x+d'\) in its newly chosen rail. The base gain group is \(C=\mathbb Z_M\times\mathbb Z_2\); the sign element \(J\) and sheet advance \(T\) are separate labels even when they give the same scalar in a representation.

Allow an endpoint \(v=(x,d)\) its own time-independent function \(F_v\) of the multiset of gains arriving there, with \(F_v(0)=0\). A weight of a selected set of histories is the sum of these endpoint values. Assume such weights are nonnegative, the launch has unit weight, and extending every history in any one equal-depth subset by both outgoing choices multiplies its weight by one common positive factor \(\lambda\). These are assumptions about a weight on histories; an arbitrary interfering history subset need not be a measurable detector outcome. A terminal-port probability is the normalized weight for that specified instrument. [G10, Z20](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/481a04f/edition/PAPERS/G10_Histories_as_Events_the_Order_of_the_Pair_Rule_First_Order_Weights_an/source/body.tex) · [Sorkin on quantum measures](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9401003)

From this launch every position at tick \(t\) has parity \(t\bmod2\). In particular there is no single time at which both parities can receive every gain. For either fixed parity \(p\), however, every port of that parity receives every signed gain along at least \(2^k\) distinct histories at the same tick \(12k+10M+10+p\). A word can first encode each bit by exchanging the order of two closed loops with equal total gain, then select an arbitrary gain at site two and append a common-length route to the requested port. The physical steps, exit gains, injection, parity and common time are formalized. This is an existence bound. [Staircase proof](lean/StaircaseAbundance.lean) · [Numerical word check](results/staircase_abundance.json)

Consequently one can realize any finite array of gain multisets on one parity as a subset at a single time. Distinct endpoint/gain classes use disjoint histories. Apply continuation separately to arrays on even and odd inputs. Their output parities are disjoint, so adding the two functional equations yields the global equation on arbitrary arrays. Time independence lets the same functions enter both equations. This supplies the hypothesis of the global-law part of Z20 directly; a one-time all-port abundance premise on this bipartite graph would be false.

The unknown factor is fixed even before classifying the functions. On singleton inputs write \(h(v,g)=F_v(\{g\})\). The two outgoing endpoints differ, so \(\lambda h(v,g)\) is the sum of the singleton weights at the successors with transported gains. Summing over all lifted ports \((v,g)\) gives \(\lambda\sum h=2\sum h\) because each lifted port has exactly two predecessors. The normalized launch and positivity give \(\lambda=2\). Closed paths at the launch have gain ratios \(T\) and \(JT\); on the finite group the closed-path gains form a subgroup containing both ratios. It is all of \(C\). The base is strongly connected by nearest-neighbor words ending in a prescribed last direction. Arbitrary closed gains then make its gain lift strongly connected too. The singleton harmonic equation has only constant solutions by the maximum principle. A depth-dependent family \(F_{v,t}(m)=(\lambda/2)^t|m|\) would give the same normalized counting probabilities at any positive chosen \(\lambda\).

Apply Z20 Theorem 5.22 and the strongly connected global-law conclusion of Corollary 5.23. Each allowed character obeys \(A_\psi^*A_\psi=2I\). Stream and sheet phases are permutations or unit-modulus multipliers; the overlap of the two columns at a splitter vanishes exactly when \(\psi(J)=-1\). Thus the same count coefficient \(c\) and signed-character squares occur at every endpoint. A cancelling pair isolates \(c\ge0\). Large integer multiples of arbitrary signed residue vectors force the Fourier quadratic form to be positive semidefinite, giving nonnegative folded character coefficients. The singleton normalizes their sum with \(c\) to one. This removes a common-detector-function assumption from the [earlier staircase reconstruction](Counting_and_character_records.md).

Director Z subsequently proved the classwise statement for general finite strongly connected gain lifts in edition Z21. For this particular staircase the subgroup of quotients of gains reaching the same endpoint at the same time is the whole signed group: the two equal-length closed-word comparisons give \(T\) and \(JT\). On the resolved graph the same-sheet quotient subgroup is \(\{+,-\}\), since the simultaneous abundance supplies both signs at the same sheet. Thus Z21 Theorem 5.26 gives the same classification directly on attainable events. Other periodic carriers can have smaller quotient subgroups and admit measurements of fewer character combinations. [Z21 general periodic-carrier result](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/93fbbd4/Director_Z/addenda/ADDENDUM_EDITION_Z21_SESSION_4.md)

## Forgetting the sheet of a terminal record

Now treat \(j\) as a physical endpoint coordinate with only the sign as gain. The previous abundance result gives all sheets and signs at each reachable rail/position. In this resolved graph the closed signed gains at a fixed sheet contain a negative element: closed base gains generate \(C\), including \((-,0)\). The same parity argument and global theorem give a resolved weight \(c' n+(1-c')D^2\) at each \((v,j)\), with \(D=n_+-n_-\). The sign character gives a norm-preserving step since the two columns sharing successors cancel in their overlap. The trivial character gives a nonzero overlap.

Require that forgetting the final sheet on the **same event** and the **same launch** sums its resolved weights to the base value. There is no earlier sheet read in this comparison. One positive and one negative arrival at the same residue give \(2c=2c'\). Two positive arrivals at distinct residues then require the off-diagonal Fourier kernel to vanish at each nonzero lag. Its value at zero is \(1-c\). Discrete Fourier inversion fixes the symmetric weight of each character to \((1-c)/M\), or twice this for a two-member conjugate pair when folded. Abundance supplies all these two-history inputs. Conversely these weights satisfy the coarsening requirement by Parseval. Equality only of observed unfiltered histograms is a weaker premise. For the complete base-port event at time \(t\) the resulting prediction is

\[
 P_t(v)=2^{-t}\left[c\,n_v+(1-c)\sum_j D_{v,j}^{\,2}\right].
\]

Here \(n_v\) counts all arriving histories. Exactly two opposite-sign histories reach \((1,R,j_0-1)\) after five ticks from sheet \(j_0\). Their words are \(LRRLR\) and \(RLLRR\), so the probability there is \(c/16\) for every \(M\ge3\). Reading position at tick one distinguishes them and changes the same final probability to \(1/16\) when that earlier outcome is forgotten. A position-only read at tick two leaves them together and preserves \(c/16\). Sheet-blind at the five-tick base port, the count numerator is six and the square numerator is zero for \(M=3\), six for \(M=4\), and four for \(M\ge5\), all over 32. Order four separates at tick six at \((2,L)\), with numerators five and three over 64. These are prospective probabilities. [Complete subset, sheet and intermediate-read controls](results/sheet_coarsening.json)

The distinction between a history weight and a detector probability is already visible in these five ticks. Take the full 32-history event and remove just \(LRRLR\). In the signed resolved law the dark port changes from zero to \(1/32\), with all other endpoint sums unchanged. The normalized weight of the formal subset is now **33/32**. Counting instead assigns 31/32. Applying a physical path filter would introduce apparatus and records, whose dynamics would have to be computed separately. ## Forward dynamics as a test of counting

Make a fresh localized launch and leave the walk undisturbed until its one terminal read. Let \(P_t\) denote the probability of its original **position and right rail**, ignoring the sheet. Counting treats each choice of direction as an independent fair sign. At any positive time, reflection about the initial position pairs the two possible last rails in any path returning to that position. The count return on odd ticks vanishes. On even positive ticks it is

\[
 q_t=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\cos(2\pi j/N)^t .
\]

The eigenvalues come from Fourier transforming the nearest-neighbor position transition. At two ticks \(q_2=1/4\), and all subsequent even terms decrease toward \(1/N\). Zero time is exceptional because its rail was prepared sharply. All allowed coherent signed characters act by finite unitary matrices for this walk. Their finite direct sum has simultaneous arbitrarily late even returns as close to the identity as desired. Thus for the count fraction \(c\), even **before** fixing the character weights by sheet coarsening,

\[
 \limsup_{t\to\infty} P_t=1-c+c/N .
\]

For the upper bound use the count limit and the bound one on each coherent return. For the lower bound take the simultaneous recurrence sequence. At any positive tick \(P_t\le1-3c/4\), so a high return with a certified lower bound \(P_t\ge1-\eta\) gives \(c\le4\eta/3\). This is an inequality for the actual probability; one successful trial establishes no confidence interval. [Exact finite forward reads and count spectral checks](results/ring_recurrence.json)

There is an operational premise under which recurrence is compulsory without already assuming a Hilbert state. Suppose the complete predictive state of this closed, resettable instrument belongs to a compact convex set \(K\) of finite affine dimension. Randomizing preparations acts by convex combination, the same autonomous time step is an affine automorphism of \(K\), and the launch-port effect is continuous with value one at the prepared state. The symmetric convex body \(K-K\) has interior in its linear span. Its Minkowski functional defines a norm in which the linear part of this automorphism is an isometry. For every integer \(L>0\) and every precision the even subsequence of states sampled at multiples of \(2L\) has two terms within that precision by total boundedness. Applying inverse steps gives a return at a positive multiple of \(2L\). Continuity of the effect gives \(\limsup P_t=1\). The counting formula then forces **\(c=0\)**. Combined with terminal-sheet coarsening this specifies \(P_t(v)=2^{-t}\sum_j D_{v,j}^2\) at every depth. The same resolved law gives \(2^{-t}D_{v,j}^2\) at each sheet. [Wallace, recurrence for bounded dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.3925)

This is a conditional derivation of a readout law for this carrier and launch. It uses stationarity, additivity over disjoint endpoints, multiset dependence, history continuation, terminal coarsening and a recurrent complete operational description. Position and rail have finite alphabets, which place no finite-dimensional bound on invisible records. Neither the time of a useful return nor a measured return probability has been supplied. The five-tick resolved port offers a finite complementary test.

For an affine probability effect with values in \([0,1]\), its difference is bounded by the \(K-K\) norm, since its range on \(K\) has width at most one. If the mixed law with \(c>0\) holds through tick \(H\), the \(H+1\) orbit states are pairwise at least \(3c/4\) apart: translate any pair by inverse evolution until the first member is the launch and apply the return gap. In affine dimension \(d\) the states fit in a translated norm unit ball. Comparing volumes of the disjoint open balls of radius \(3c/8\) therefore gives \(H+1\le(1+8/(3c))^d\). This weak bound needs physical control of the dimension and of the record probabilities.

An exact reversible countermodel explains the finite-prefix limitation. Start with \(H\) fair independent tape bits and a rotating pointer. Swap the current direction with the addressed bit, stream in the new direction, apply the same sheet increment and sign update, and advance the pointer. The inverse reads the previous pointer, reverses the displacement and sheet/sign changes, and swaps back. Through \(H\) ticks each direction sequence occurs once among the \(2^H\) tape inputs. A finite permutation of the enlarged state eventually recurs. On a two-sided infinite fair tape the same shift and swap give all forward counting records indefinitely. The induced action on probability measures is reversible and affine, and the cylinder effects are continuous in the weak topology. The complete predictive state space of measures on that tape is infinite-dimensional. Compactness in that topology alone does not make the action an isometry in a totally bounded operational norm. For order three, the verified one- through five-bit circuits had simultaneous returns of all sharp launch states at 12, 36, 24, 60 and 180 ticks. ## A controlled reversal at an assigned time

An echo can put the return at a specified time if the apparatus supplies another operation. Put \(U=TDH\) for the stream, departure shift and normalized symmetric signed splitter in right-to-left order (here \(T\) denotes spatial stream). The permutation \(S(x,d,j)=(1-x+d,d,j)\) is an involution and \(SUS=U^*\). For an input rail \(d\) its intermediate departure is \(1-(x-d)\), where the staircase gain is \(-g(x-d)\bmod M\). The last reflection places either output rail at \(x-d\); splitter symmetry supplies the correct transpose. Thus \(S U^t S U^t\) restores every signed physical amplitude. Implementing this reflection requires routing coherent data across the position ring. A second construction uses the local exchange of neighboring directed lanes, a signed rail phase, and physical sheet reflection; the latter may require transport on the sheet. It contributes a known overall sign on certain block lengths that matters if the echo is bypassed coherently. [Physical-column tests and control costs](results/staircase_echo.json)

For an echo starting at one sharp base port, the counting return to that port is \(r_t=\sum_v p_t(v)^2\), where \(p_t(v)\) is the ordinary counting distribution after the first leg. Reversing the adjacency transposes its real stochastic matrix, so this is the squared column norm. The deterministic reflection adds no new branching. With a common count fraction and perfectly coherent delivery, the return is \(1-c(1-r_t)\). Summing over the final rail changes the test: at one forward and one reverse tick the **position alone** returns certainly even under counting. Requiring also the original sheet instead can reduce the count return. At order six and five forward ticks the base position-and-rail count return is \(35/256\). Archiving each forward history and retaining a coherent backward leg gives \(31/256,33/256,37/256,39/256\) in sectors 0, 1, 2, 3. An unread forward tag can therefore imitate or distort the counting signal. The checks include partial tag visibility, both legs recorded, and a coherent erasure. [Tag schedules and finite-visibility controls](check_staircase_echo.py)

There is a separate finite-time premise that suffices without waiting for recurrence. Start at one site with either of two sharp input rails, chosen with equal prior probability. Both give **exactly the same complete classical counting distribution** after the first balanced step: the sheet advance is common, and either outgoing rail is fair. For a preparation-independent fraction \(c\) of *memoryless* counting, any later decoder that accesses only the resulting state and fresh resources receives the same counting input in both cases. Consequently the total variation distance between its two output distributions is at most \(1-c\). The inverse that is coherent on the signed sectors attains this distance. The correct rail can be recovered with average probability at most \(1-c/2\). Perfect recovery therefore forces \(c=0\), and certified average recovery of \(1-\eta\) bounds \(c\le 2\eta\). This uses a common fraction and independent decoder; the source train or a pointer retaining the original rail would invalidate the information-loss premise. The finite tape above stores precisely the missing direction and can restore it if the decoder has access. [Two-input recovery matrix](results/ring_recurrence.json)

## Testing reversibility on the interacting walk

Consider a finite open version of the same [two-particle electric-string circuit](Electric_string_quantum_and_classical_motion.md) used for quantum binding and classical rebound. A sharp occupation pair \((i,j)\), \(i<j\), uniquely fixes the vacuum-sector link bits. Different hop histories reaching that pair leave the same string. Compare this with an apparatus that supplies a separate, initially blank pointer on each active bond of each layer. Store whether a hop occurred. A localized input and the full pointer transcript fix every intermediate pair configuration. Forgetting these orthogonal pointers turns each single-occupation rotation into its stochastic matrix of squared entries. The electric phase on energized links then drops out of the position read.

For \(n\) scalar sites the resulting transition on \(D=\binom n2\) pairs is doubly stochastic. Both staggered layers admit holding with positive probability, and any elementary legal adjacent move can be made in one full cycle by holding on the other bonds. The ordered-pair graph is connected, since each pair can be moved to \((0,1)\) and the moves can be reversed. The positive holding probability makes the Markov chain aperiodic. Hence each marked return tends to \(1/D\). For \(n\ge3\) this differs from a coherent return; the one-state case \(n=2\) carries no test. The permanent path-pointer model requires new storage, a reset, or an environment to sustain this law indefinitely.

In the narrower class of preparations that mix this permanently recorded flight with the coherent unitary flight, with common fraction \(c\) on the marked flight, the late upper envelope of any localized pair return is \(1-c+c/D\). Recurrence of the coherent finite matrix supplies the attaining sequence. Recurrence of the **complete** closed finite-dimensional operational state, if physically warranted, therefore excludes positive \(c\). This competing-model test on the interacting apparatus uses a specified two-component family; no Z20 classification of every possible law for its unequal hopping angles has been proved.

For four sites and a quarter-turn \(-i\) per energized link after both layers, the return of \((0,2)\) after three periods was exactly \(2360363562576/3814697265625\) in the coherent model and \(596352247344/3814697265625\) with path pointers. With a rational link phase \((3-4i)/5\), the predicted return at \((0,3)\) after 2,973 forward periods was 0.99957652, compared with 0.16666667 with pointers. A measured lower probability bound of 0.99 there would give \(c\le0.01150\) under this calibrated model. The reported horizon was selected in a scan of ticks 1 through 32,768; all six launches and four phase settings are retained. Global conjugation of the link phases in the real matter gauge preserves every tested localized position probability. The finite-device recurrence test precedes an infinite-line or semiclassical limit; no bound uniform in the increasing number of sites follows.
