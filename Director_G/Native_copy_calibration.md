# Calibrating the long direction word with the field left on

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

One extra pair of ticks on calibration launches can test whether an early diffraction read and a late accelerated arrival are trustworthy on the other launches. Both preparations can start at the same sharp site, rail and sheet address and use the same staircase field throughout. The comparison is with the probability of the entire direction word per original launch. Its sensitivity improves with word length because a localized sheet loses interference when an unreported copy separates paths that would have met at the same final well and sheet address.

## A one-setting bound on the permanent staircase

Take the signed Hadamard walk on $4M$ sites with departure gain $\lfloor x/2\rfloor\bmod M$. After two ticks read whether the rail changed, coherently over its sites. Repeat for $t$ consecutive opposite-rail passes. The ideal probability from a localized sheet is
$$
 I_t=\frac1M\sum_{s=0}^{M-1}a_t(h_s),\qquad
 a_t(h)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\prod_{j=1}^t\sin^2(q+(2j-1)h)\frac{dq}{2\pi},\quad h_s=\pi s/M .
$$
Use $2t<4M$. A secret persistent record of the precise site after each pass in $K\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t-1\}$ instead gives $W_t(K)=M^{-1}\sum_s\prod_i a_{\ell_i}(h_s)$ for the segments separated by $K$. Conditional on the sector, each new segment starts at a sharp well on the known rail and its survival is independent of the well. All other readouts run as specified. [Native word and finite-ring convention](Same_flight_sheet_selection.md)

Suppose the original source has a persistent fair-count fraction $c$. The remaining mass is a sharp localized sheet measured with the squared law. Among those launches the detector has an exogenous latent set $X$ of copy decisions. Its law can have arbitrary correlations across time but is common across sheet labels and independent of the realized path. Selecting which launches receive an extra switch leaves this common prefix law unchanged. For the result applied to flight, the apparatus after release implements the same continuation on all copies, with no further hidden memory changing its actions. These conditions specify the rival class for calibration.

Take even $m\sim\lambda M^{2/3}$, $\lambda>0$. Send calibration launches through $t=m+1$ switches and record their unforced survival $S_t$. This includes a sensitivity test for the otherwise invisible copy at the end of pass $m$. Put
$$
 r_{M,t}=\frac{\max\{2^{-t},\ \max_{1\le j<t}W_t(\{j\})\}}{I_t}.
$$
When $r_{M,t}<1$, the fraction $B=c+(1-c)\Pr(X\cap\{1,\ldots,m\}\ne\varnothing)$ obeys
$$
 B\le \frac{1-S_t/I_t}{1-r_{M,t}},\qquad
 r_{M,t}\longrightarrow\tfrac12 .
$$
For finite samples a lower confidence bound for $S_t$ yields an upper bound for $B$. An observed frequency above the ideal can arise from sampling; a true rate above the ideal contradicts the stipulated class. The gain in using the unforced word is that a whole, possibly correlated copy pattern can be tested at the field strength used for flight.

**Why more copies can only decrease the localized survival.** During a passing two-tick block the intermediate rail is free and the final rail is fixed opposite the incident rail. The two successive coin signs multiply to $+1$ if the intermediate rail is right and $-1$ if it is left, for either incoming rail. The final displacement fixes the sum of all intermediate directions because the other directions are the prescribed word. Thus all histories ending at a given well have the same sign. On the ring a change of lift by $4M$ changes the inferred number of intermediate left steps by $2M$, preserving its parity. At a given final site and sheet address, and at any already retained copy word, refining the word splits a same-sign path sum. The sum of squares over the new pointer values can only decrease. In particular every nonempty pattern is bounded by its single cuts. Individual Fourier sectors can increase under a copy; summing the full localized input before making this comparison is essential.

**Why the worst single cut tends to one half.** Write $j$ for the shorter segment, $1\le j\le t/2$. Hölder bounds $a_j(h_s)\le a_j(0)=\binom{2j}{j}4^{-j}$. It follows that $W_t(\{j\})/I_t\le a_j(0) I_{t-j}/I_t$. At lengths $u/M^{2/3}\to\mu>0$, the localized survival has order $1/(M\sqrt u)$. More precisely, $M I_u/a_u(0)$ approaches the sum of two positive Gaussian series: the weak integer ladder and the ladder about a half turn, indexed by integers on even orders and half-integers on odd orders. Each term at offset $r$ is $e^{-\pi^2\mu^3r^2/3}$. The sine-product bound by $C\exp[-c\min(u,r^2)]$ after division by $a_u(0)$ controls the tails uniformly for $\mu$ in compact subsets of $(0,\infty)$. This argument also applies to odd $u$ because only the modulus of the source enters. The two parity subsequences can have different constants. [Bounded offsets, uniform tail and parity](Same_flight_sheet_selection.md)

Consequently $I_{t-j}/I_t\to1$ for fixed $j$ along either parity. The ratio is bounded uniformly for $1\le j\le t/2$ by subsequence compactness. If $j\to\infty$, $a_j(0)\to0$; if $j$ is bounded, its maximum is $a_1(0)=1/2$. The single first cut attains the limiting upper bound since $a_1(h)=1/2$ for every sector. The fair-count ratio tends to zero. Averaging the clear class with the remaining classes proves the bound. A mixture of clear and endpoint-copy launches shows why the limiting factor two is sharp. Finite ratios must be calculated at the orders used.

## What the calibration buys on the science launches

On launches ending the direction word at $m$, let $E$ be any event requiring successful preparation and a specified continuation on that packet. Under an arbitrary fixed copy word the original-launch probability of $E$ lies in $[0,I_m]$ by the same-sign inequality, including a final copy which leaves the word probability unchanged. The ideal probability lies in the same interval. Fair counting contributes at most $2^{-m}\le I_m$; resolving every intermediate well in the localized word gives that lower bound on $I_m$. Mixing over the common exogenous pattern and including the clear cases therefore gives
$$
 |\Pr(E)-\Pr_{\rm ideal}(E)|\le B I_m .
$$
The same bound applies with a terminal diagnostic of the winding included: its event is a subset of success, and the original-launch bound sums over the incident sheet. The fair-count component requires a separate count-class label in that joint comparison. This bound holds for one attempt; retries can change the detector's law conditional on all past failures.

In particular, the ideal joint event of a fixed positive-width quantum aperture and a later accelerated side has order $1/(M\sqrt m)$ with strictly positive leading coefficient. So does $I_m$. A relative survival deficit tending to zero gives a relative error tending to zero in those two original-launch probabilities. Their posterior unit-winding fraction still tends to one since the ideal wrong-sector joint is $o(I_m)$. No rate of the form $o(1/M)$ for the deficit is required. Further conditioning on an event negligible relative to $I_m$ needs its own denominator. Estimating a relative deficit $\epsilon$ near $I_t$ to fixed confidence on independent launches costs order $1/(I_t\epsilon^2)$ by binomial variance. Rare releases and the known apparatus model are material costs of this comparison. [Joint two-time law and finite discrepancies](Two_scales_on_one_flight.md)

The scope is operational. If all sectors have a common defect law, observing $S_t=I_t$ at an order with strict gap forces both $c=0$ and a clear prefix in this class. Other hidden instruments, phase kicks, an alternative score law or an apparatus which recognizes calibration launches are further possibilities. In particular, a *zero-field* calibration averaged over sectors can miss a detector copying only on $s=\pm1$: its deficit is at most $2/M$ even when field-on unit survival becomes $2^{-m}$. A site copy solely after the last prepared block has no effect on the measured $m$-word and can change the next read. A common absolute relabelling of all sheet characters and their instruments still preserves observable probabilities.

## Exact all-clear inference and its blind depths

At fixed $M$ and $m\ge2$, the ratio of the measured alternating contrast to the positive ideal contrast gives the **original-launch clear-square mass** $(1-c)\Pr(X\cap\{1,\ldots,m-1\}=\varnothing\mid\mathrm{square})$. The count contribution is constant over forced settings and cancels. This comparison requires the forced probes to add precisely the designated site dephasings and preserve the latent law across settings. The $m$th copy remains invisible until a later read. Its magnitude on the permanent-field localized source has a simple area interpretation. Expand the successful block word as ordered pairs of histories with the same terminal well and sheet. Each contributing pair has weight $4^{-m}$ because its two path signs agree. Inclusion-exclusion over all internal copy coordinates cancels exactly the pairs whose wells agree at *any* internal boundary. [Boolean contrast proof](Forced_copy_calibration.md)

For two paths write $d_j$ for half their well difference after block $j$ on the lift. Each increment is $-1,0$ or $1$, $d_0=d_m=0$, and an uncancelled pair has all $d_j$ strictly positive or all strictly negative internally. The two departure gains in block $j$ differ by $d_{j-1}$ and $d_j$ because the compared sites have the same parity. Hence equal terminal sheets require $2\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}d_j=0\bmod M$. The unit increments have one paired intermediate-rail choice each; a flat increment has two. For positive paths every integer area from $m-1$ through $\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor$ occurs: start with the maximal triangle or flat-topped triangle, lower any maximal height at least two by one, and repeat to the height-one path. Neighbor differences remain at most one at each lowering. The negative orientation contributes equally.

For $m\ge2$, in the unwrapped range the denominator is therefore positive exactly when that integer interval contains a multiple of $K=M/\gcd(2,M)$. The first such depth is $\lceil2\sqrt K\rceil$: before that depth the maximum is below $K$, and at the first depth the minimum is at most $K$. At later depths through $K+1$ the interval still contains $K$; from $K+2$ on its width $\lfloor(m-2)^2/4\rfloor$ is at least $K-1$. Thus the contrast eventually works at the flight depth. At $K=q^2$ with $q\ge2$, $m=2q$, only the extremal triangle in either orientation survives, and the denominator is exactly $2/4^m$. These onsets can be expensive to resolve. At $M=8$, $m=4$, the ideal rates over forced masks in increasing binary order are $(20,16,18,16,16,16,16,16)/256$, giving contrast $1/128$. Here low-order bit one designates the first boundary. The two deterministic hidden masks $101$ and $111$ have identical complete forced tables here. An exact all-clear estimate does not identify every pattern on the permanent staircase. The unforced gap can be strictly positive even when the full alternating contrast is zero. For every $m\ge5$, two paths can have differences $(0,1,0,-1,0)$ on four successive blocks. Their integrated area is zero, hence they meet on the same final sheet at every order. Padding with identical intermediate rails before and after the motif realizes a nonzero difference at any chosen internal cut, since the first or third internal site of the motif can serve. The orientation can also be reversed. At least $2\cdot2^{m-4}$ ordered pairs are lost to each such cut, giving a finite gap $W_m(\varnothing)-W_m(\{j\})\ge2^{-m-3}$. At four blocks the middle cut can be blind, as at $M=101$. This finite witness gives a weaker quantitative bound than the large-word result.

The independent finite census compared 9,616 ordered cut patterns at 17 orders and 2,872 refinements, with 56 ties in the refinement checks. At $M=129$, the survival of sector $s=60$ increased by a factor of about 560 under its best copy pattern, although every localized-sheet refinement obeyed monotonicity. At $M=8$ and three blocks one cut keeps the entire survival; at four blocks the worst single cut keeps 90 percent. The asymptotic half bound cannot replace these finite controls.
