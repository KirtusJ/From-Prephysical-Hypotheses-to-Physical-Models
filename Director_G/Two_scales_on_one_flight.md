# An early quantum position read and a late classical arrival on one flight

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A prepared walker passes through a position aperture while its packet is still spreading. It continues under the same field. A second read at a later time separates two accelerated branches. The first aperture biases which side registers at the second read. Both probabilities follow from one joint law. Recording the precise well inside the early aperture changes that law even if the apparatus displays the same early bit.

## Two records on one preparation

Start with a sharp site, rail and cyclic sheet address in the $4M$-site signed Hadamard staircase. The field acts on every tick. Assume the squared-coordinate realization and a passing direction read that preserves coherence across sites. From the latest sharp home record $m$ consecutive opposite-rail outcomes, one every two ticks, with $m$ even and $m/M^{2/3}\to\lambda>0$. Either use a single attempt or read the failure site and rail and retry on the same sheet until a declared deadline. Index flight ticks from release and positions from the sharp home of the successful attempt. The ring light cone of both reads fits on the lift for the clocks below. [Single-attempt source](Same_flight_sheet_selection.md) · [Same-sheet retry law](Keeping_the_sheet_after_a_failed_attempt.md)

Fix $F,\tau,T>0$, let $\delta_q=(\pi/(MF))^{1/3}$ and $\delta_c=(\pi/(MF))^{1/4}$. On flight tick $n_1$ with $n_1\delta_q^2\to\tau$, read membership in a fixed bounded interval $A$ of positive length in $Y_q=\delta_q x$. Apply the position projection coherently on all admitted wells and both rails. Run a further $n_2$ ticks with $n_2\delta_c^3\to T$. On the same lift, read the two disjoint tubes in $Y_c=\delta_c^2 x$ centered on $vFT^2/2$, $v=\pm1$, of fixed radius $0<R<FT^2/4$. The alternative outcome comprises all other sites. The total time from release on the late clock also tends to $T$. Centers and radii converging to the stated values give the same limits.

These coordinates describe how an observer samples one discrete evolution. Put $\ell=\delta_c^{1/3}$. For any tick and well, $Y_c=\ell^2Y_q$ and $t_c=\ell t_q$ exactly. Relative momentum at a band carrier scales as $p_c=\ell p_q$, since $p_i=(q-q_*)/\delta_i$. Write $L=\lambda(\pi/F)^{2/3}=\lim m\delta_q^2$. For either unit winding $s=\pm1$, conditional on release from that sector, let $J_v(A)$ denote the limiting probability of early membership followed by late side $v$. With $\mathcal N(y;\mu,V)$ the normal density of mean $\mu$ and variance $V$, the prediction is
$$
 J_v(A)=\frac12\int_A\mathcal N\!\left(y;\,
       vF(L\tau+\tau^2/2),\ \frac{L+\tau^2/L}{2}\right)\,dy .
$$
For $A\subset(0,\infty)$ the positive-side joint probability strictly exceeds the negative-side one. Moving the aperture across the origin reverses the inequality. The two signed-sheet orientations give identical side laws. A test of this prediction uses the two scheduled observations.

## Propagation between resolutions

For each bounded integer winding $s$, fold the parity replicas at $q_*=\pm\pi/2$ and choose the two curvature branches $b=\pm1$. The even switch word on either sharp rail gives each branch limiting squared norm $1/2$. Its normalized scalar in the fixed-action chart tends in norm to $(L/\pi)^{1/4}\exp[-L(p+sFL)^2/2]$. This follows by expanding the centered product of $m$ cosines and controlling its tails. On bounded times the two Fourier envelopes evolve by $bp^2/2+sFY_q$. Their mean momentum on release is $-sFL$, the position mean is zero, and the centered initial position and momentum variances are $L/2$ and $1/(2L)$ with zero symmetrized covariance. Hence at the first read each branch has the Gaussian above with mean $-bsF(L\tau+\tau^2/2)$. Its unnormalized squared norm after passing is one half of its interval mass. [Source, shear and error control](Field_on_source_and_release.md)

To continue the projected state, approximate the aperture on each limiting Gaussian by smooth compact multipliers. The Gaussian boundary mass tends to zero as the smoothing width shrinks. Approximate the resulting envelopes in norm by smooth functions with compact momentum support; perform each approximation at fixed accuracy before taking the lattice limit. The early norm convergence, lattice Riemann sums and contractivity of the reads transfer these approximations to the discrete source. On the compact supports the smooth spectral projectors tend to their carrier projectors. The common position mask acts scalarly on the two rail components, so each approximating packet stays on its original curvature branch up to a vanishing error. The parity replicas are folded copies, with complementary microscopic band labels at the second carrier. A sharp aperture can have long momentum tails, which is why this argument takes the norm approximation first. [Folded chart and band cutoffs](Central_window_chirps.md)

For a fixed smooth approximation the initial late-clock position and momentum deviations from zero vanish, by the exact factors $\ell^2$ and $\ell$ above. The forced band estimate on a compact $p_c$ support applies over $n_2$ ticks. In the comparison evolution, the position deviation about $-bsFT^2/2$ is bounded in norm by the initial position deviation plus $T$ times the initial momentum deviation. Removing the approximations by unitarity and projection contraction establishes concentration for the original aperture branch with its actual unnormalized weight. For $s=\pm1$, just the curvature satisfying $v=-bs$ reaches side $v$. For $s=0$ both arrive at zero, and for $|s|\ge2$ fixed both miss the tubes. This proves the conditional joint formula for either launch rail. [Shrinking-action flight estimate](Same_flight_sheet_selection.md)

For labels at bounded distance from a half turn, write $h=s\pi/M=h_0+e$ with $h_0=\pm\pi/2$ and $e=O(M^{-1})$. The passing source is a shifted cosine product, now at $q=0,\pi$. On each replica its normalized shape in $z=\sqrt m(q-q_*+me)$ converges in norm to a Gaussian. The $x/n_1$ characteristic function shifts $z$ by $\xi\sqrt m/n_1\to0$, hence shifts the initial component by $o(1)$ in norm. This uses its actual width, since $m/n_1$ stays finite and positive. On these arcs the exact two-tick phase has $2\cos\theta=\cos(2q+h)+\cos h$. The branches are gapped, their phase derivatives tend to opposite numbers of magnitude one, and their successive eigenframe error is $O(n_1|e|)=o(1)$. The characteristic-function argument therefore puts every limiting $x/n_1$ mass on $\{-1/2,1/2\}$. A bounded $A$ requires $|x|/n_1=O(\delta_q)$, so these sectors already miss the early aperture. This includes half-integer offsets on odd orders. [Two-tick eigenphases and tail bound](Same_flight_sheet_selection.md)

## Rates from the original launch

A sharp sheet has mass $1/M$ per winding. On a single attempt the probability of the switch word in the unit sector is asymptotic to $a_M=e^{-\pi^2\lambda^3/3}/\sqrt{\pi m}$. For each side the joint probability of the word, early pass and late side equals $(2/M)a_M[J_v(A)+o(1)]$. Given this joint event, the unit fraction tends to one. To sum over all windings, the single-attempt envelope divided by the zero-sector success is at most $C\exp[-c\min(m,r^2)]$ with $r$ the distance from a half-order multiple. The subsequent projections only decrease the squared norm. Sum first over bounded weak and half-turn offsets, then remove the cutoff. [Uniform survival bound](Same_flight_sheet_selection.md)

With retries, let $C_1(H)$ be the probability of releasing a unit by the total block deadline $H=\lfloor Bm\rfloor$. For every fixed $B>1$, the corresponding probability from a localized original is $(2/M)[C_1(H)J_v(A)+o(1)]$, again with unit fraction tending to one. The retry envelope grows by at most $C(B+1)(1+r)^{1/3}$ relative to the preceding exponential bound. Translation to the home of each success is exact, and the limiting law agrees for both start rails. At finite order an asymmetric early aperture can depend on the start rail, so a finite retry prediction must keep its distribution. Increasing $B$ sufficiently slowly by a diagonal choice gives $(2/M)[J_v(A)+o(1)]$ with vanishing preparation duration on the late clock. On the early clock the successful word itself takes time tending to $2L$. The squared law, force scale, clock and instrument are premises. Absolute cyclotomic identification and the physical origin of the probability rule remain open. [Deadlines and original denominators](Keeping_the_sheet_after_a_failed_attempt.md)

## A control that copies every admitted well

Suppose the early read also stores each admitted site in a distinct, persistent record. Tracing out that record leaves a mixture of sharp wells with local rail spinors. For every fixed bounded weak $s$, the later probability of either tube for this mixture is $O(\delta_c)$. Indeed, from any such early well the late tube has $J=O(\delta_c^{-2})$ sites of microscopic speed $O(\delta_c)$ and the late field has $|h|=O(\delta_c^4)$. Stationary phase on the exact transported branches bounds their amplitude by $C n_2^{-1/2}$ per such site uniformly in the initial spinor. Transport between smooth frames adds a total norm error $O(n_2|h|)$. Squaring after projection bounds the probability by $O(J/n_2+(n_2 h)^2)=O(\delta_c)$. The bound is uniform over early wells since their displacements from home are $O(\delta_q^{-1})$. Recording the well changes the late arrivals even though it leaves the early pass probability exactly intact. [Central stationary-phase bound](Rested_window_cost.md)

For $M=29,30$ the checker has $m=2$, four early flight ticks and six more before the late read. The early aperture admits sites 2 and 4; the late tubes contain sites $-2$ and $+2$. An exact signed-history enumeration from the original localized launch gives early-pass and late-minus, plus, other counts of $(104,468,900)/16384$. With a hidden persistent site copy on pass the counts are $(94,420,958)/16384$. In both protocols the preparation failure probabilities are $1/2$ and $1/4$. At $M=128,129$ both late tubes are empty on the occupied parity. At $M=32768$ the unit-sector early pass given the switch word is about $0.33565$, against $0.33256$ in the limit. On the same switch-word denominator the late positive-side joint is still $0.00741$, against $0.30358$. The finite archive gives no demonstrated order at which the latter limit is practically accurate. It includes failures, misses, reflected launches, a site-copy comparison and a fair-count control.
