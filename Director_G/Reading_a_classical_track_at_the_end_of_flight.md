# Reading a classical track at the end of flight

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Mathematical development and checks by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Can a nonstandard detector record the Newtonian motion of a single forced walk? In the prepared two-ray experiment, **one binary test of the stored path suffices** if a nearly certain outcome in squared coordinate norm has a nearly certain physical reading. The ordinary law at intermediate weights is unnecessary. Timing matters. Reading each label as it is written can amplify small changes in the detector law, even though reading a single terminal flag suppresses the chance of a bad word. The conclusion describes the recorded path after successful preparation. [Walk, preparation, reversal schedule and bin estimates](Sequential_position_records.md)

## Store the labels, then read the path

Use the same prepared walk, externally timed force, observation times and whole-line bins as in the position-record proof. Let $a>0$ be the first read time and choose it so the two Newtonian rays $y_+(a)$ and $y_-(a)$ are separated. Given the offset of the bins, compute both entire words $w_+,w_-$ containing the respective ray centres at the attained times. At each time, copy the **actual** bin into a separate blank orthogonal register by the stipulated reversible coordinate permutation. Keep these registers intact. The position labels can coincide at a later crossing. Two complete words still differ at their first label for all sufficiently fine widths. A string switching between rays at separated times fails the membership test unless its entire string coincides with one of the two nominated words.

For fixed settings the terminal state has the form $\sum_w K_w\psi\otimes|w\rangle$, where $K_w$ is the ordered product of the free flight steps and bin projectors. This is an identity about the vector amplitudes. A subsequent unitary that never acts on earlier registers cannot recombine two different labels in them. A reversible equality circuit writes membership in $\{w_+,w_-\}$ to one blank bit. The squared norm of its pass sector is

$$ B=\|K_{w_+}\psi\|^2+\|K_{w_-}\psi\|^2. $$

The prior projection estimate proves $B\to1$ uniformly on good offsets with the original growing read schedule. Here it gives a property of the stored state. No actual probability of an intermediate word has been substituted. In particular the measurements leave coherence within each copied bin and entangle different bins with different labels. A full position copy followed by erasing digits changes the state. The uniform offset in the proof may be supplied by an independent classical design. There is no need to infer fair control bits from an uncalibrated walker. Alternatively enumerate the offset grid on separate runs with the same prepared input and detector bound, retaining every offset with its designed weight. [Projection calculation, steps 1–5](Sequential_position_records.md) · [Gao’s sequential projection bounds](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.92.052331)

**Physical read assumption.** For any binary probe used here, when the sector of the tested adverse outcome has squared mass $e$, its conditional probability is at most $h(e)$ for small $e$, with $h(0)=0$ and $h(e)\to0$ as $e\downarrow0$. Use a common upper envelope over the admitted apparatus histories, settings and prepared states, or a justified average bound for the actual population. It need only control outcomes with small mass. On receipt of an outcome use the normalized projection into its sector, or independently establish the equivalent conditional record-support and readback property. These are assumptions on the instrument. They can be challenged with known inputs near either endpoint and coherent repeat or reversal controls. A finite menu alone does not establish a uniform modulus. The grouping circuit is programmed with both nominated words, and its response must apply to this composite flag. An offline sum of individually measured word rates has no such guarantee.

If bad offsets have probability $\gamma$ and on the good ones $1-B\le b$, the unconditional probability of a bad physical record after preparation is at most $\gamma+\sup_{0\le e\le b}h(e)$. On a pass, read an additional binary label separating the two first bins in the protected registers. Each nonzero reported sector then contains **one actual stored word**. Read the bits of those same registers. Every bit is sharp and $h(0)=0$ makes its readout deterministic. For an arbitrary device with a measured probability $\eta$ of disagreement between the pass indication and that protected readback on the incident ensemble, add $\eta$ to the error bound. A device that fabricates a passing display supplies no such readback. Losses require separate outcomes in the incident denominator. The branch weights may be biased, fluctuate, or suppress a ray entirely. Convergence for a branch conditioned on a rare observed label requires a bound on the error divided by that label’s probability.

On either nominated word the interpolated bin centres differ uniformly from its ray by at most half a bin plus the modulus of the ray at the largest read spacing and rounding error. Both tend to zero. Thus the distance of the **interpolated record** from the two-ray set tends to zero in physical probability, including with the field reversal. The read operations leave the positions between sample times unresolved. For example, a detector can use ordinary probabilities outside the interval $[0.4,0.6]$ and always choose the first labeled alternative inside it. This device meets the endpoint premise while returning only one branch on that middle-weight test. The original half-and-half law additionally follows if the second binary selector, on successful states with sector weights tending to $1/2$, converges to a fair split. It is not needed to check path shape. A nearby offset chosen in response to an unobserved source type, a changed force, or a phase kick caused by a pointer can invalidate the kinematic estimate. ## What changes if every outcome must arrive during the flight?

Start with a coherent binary side-of-origin read at $a$. Its two reference sector norms tend to $1/\sqrt 2$ and projecting on either side removes the opposite ray up to $o(1)$ in norm. Thus for either reported side the normalized projected state follows its corresponding ray conditional on successful tracking, regardless of the detector’s odds at this first split. Include a bin read at $a$ for positional resolution. For its nominated continuation, let $e_j$ be the squared mass outside the next prescribed bin on the normalized state given **all earlier prescribed outcomes**, with the corresponding projected update. These $e_j$ are computed from the matrices. Put $b_*=\prod_{j=1}^K(1-e_j)$. It is the reference squared norm of the continuation, conditional on the side read. On good offsets $b_*\to1$ for both sides. Indeed the side projection alters the designated unnormalized ray by $o(1)$, suppresses the opposite component, and the original word of projections preserves the designated ray in norm up to $o(1)$. The denominator of this normalization stays bounded away from zero.

For a **multibin** real-time reader, assume its total probability of reporting anything other than bin $j$’s nominated label is at most $h(e_j)$ on that actual prefix. This is an extra instrument contract. A binary test of one predesignated bin against all other bins is another implementation when the first side and target path are supplied to the apparatus. It does not, on rejection, report a full off-path position. With complete readouts and the stated update, the chain rule and $-\log(1-e)\ge e$ give

$$
 P(\text{any wrong subsequent label}\mid\text{side, good offset})
 \le \sum_j h(e_j),\qquad
 \sum_j e_j\le-\log b_* .
$$

When $h(e)\le C e$ near zero the original refining schedule works. For $h(e)\le C e^\alpha$, $0<\alpha\le1$, the corresponding sufficient bound is $C K^{1-\alpha}(-\log b_*)^\alpha$, capped at one. With a uniform envelope tending to zero at zero but no specified rate, a slower sequence of precisions and observation counts exists. First hold a bin width, finite grid of times and margin fixed, choosing an offset away from the finitely many boundary positions for both rays. Then all $e_j\to0$ by the same position tail and projection estimates. For stage $m$ take width and maximal spacing tending to zero and use sufficiently small flight parameter that the finite sum of errors is $<1/m$. This diagonal construction supplies no usable rate without quantitative detector and preparation bounds.

For a sharp obstruction to a universal real-time inference, take $K\ge3$ binary reads, conditional correct-prefix error mass $e_K=1/[1+(K-1)^2]$ at each step. This sequence is realizable with $K$ separate identical two-level probes having coordinate ratio $1:(K-1)$. Under the reference rule $1-(1-e_K)^K\le K e_K\to0$. A single fixed, continuous, complemented detector with wrong-outcome chance

$$g(e)=\frac{\sqrt e}{\sqrt e+\sqrt{1-e}}$$

has $g(e_K)=1/K$. Reading each probe gives success $(1-1/K)^K\to e^{-1}$ under the specified history-conditional response. Copy the same labels and read one all-correct flag instead. Its rejection probability is $g(1-(1-e_K)^K)\to0$. This exhibits a failure in a concrete reference instrument, not an assertion that the Newtonian walk realizes these particular leakages. More generally, for a fixed binary $g(e)\to0$ near zero, $g(e)=O(e)$ is necessary and sufficient to pass **every** such array with vanishing total reference error. If $g(e_n)/e_n\to\infty$, choose $K_n=\lceil1/g(e_n)\rceil$. Then $K_n e_n\to0$ while the correct-word probability is at most $\exp[-K_n g(e_n)]\le e^{-1}$. The proof concerns what follows from the aggregate leakage alone. [Exact forward-walk and repeated-probe checks](check_endpoint_ray_records.py) · [All outputs](results/endpoint_ray_records.json)

## Preparation and independent checks

The prepared input and its purity are physical premises common to the two protocols. A kinematic calculation for an accepted fixed preparation word still specifies its normalized state under the assumed projection update. The actual chance of obtaining that word, the weights of a mixture of accepted words and their costs require the corresponding detector laws. An unconditional release guarantee does not follow from endpoint calibration or from the earlier Born-likelihood yield. One can instead impose the same uniform packet error for every accepted word and report the measured acceptance yield. A positive preparation rate is needed to observe the conditional experiment. No path test here determines a probability embedding or derives the projector update.

The finite checker evolves a sharp forward Hadamard walk with signed integer amplitudes and reads the side at ticks 2, 4 and 6, assigning the origin to the right. The two constant-side words have squared mass $59/64$. A detector with binary response $p^2/[p^2+(1-p)^2]$ gives $3481/3506$ for the terminal pooled read and $2187/2210$ for live reads of the same labels. The difference tests the order of physical readout on one specified walk. It checks a nonordinary normalized-square detector and the common square-root counterexample; these finite examples are instrument checks, independent of the asymptotic packet proof.
