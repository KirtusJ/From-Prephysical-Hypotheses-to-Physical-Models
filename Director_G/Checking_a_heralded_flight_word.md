# Calibrating a probability law on heralded flight records

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A rare preparation can preferentially select a detector response that scarcely figures in an unconditioned calibration. When the apparatus can preserve that response and reset only the walker, a remote-position test *after* recorded acceptance probes the mixture that actually flies. A separate test of the complete output word can certify agreement between a reported history and a protected reference history using one random parity bit per sampled flight. Both statements have operational premises. Neither follows from accurate one-time positions. ## Which exponents reach a rare flight

Let $H$ mean acceptance by a specified stored herald. In the site-power family an exponent $q>0$ assigns a distribution $T_q p$ to labels with kinematic weights $p_i$, where $(T_q p)_i=p_i^q/\sum_j p_j^q$. If $a(q)$ is the probability of the actual herald at exponent $q$, then the retained exponent measure is $d\mu_H=a(q)d\mu/\int a\,d\mu$. For example, choose $Y=1/(k^2+1)$ and split ordinary accepted mass $Y$ uniformly among $k^6$ fine labels. Keep the remaining mass in one rejected label. An exponent $1/2$ changes the acceptance probability to $k^2/(k^2+1)$. Giving this exponent launch weight $Y$ and exponent 1 the rest makes exactly half of all accepted runs come from exponent $1/2$. The initial contamination tends to zero. Exact remote equalities on a common persistent family can exclude it, but finite prior accuracy must be compared to the yield.

Once the herald is committed, randomly route a representative subset of the accepted launches into each of the existing five- and seven-tick signed remote tests. Supply the same sharp probe initialization, retaining the latent response and the herald; also retain no-click outcomes. The true body of the flight is run on another randomly assigned subset. If the response parameter and conditional detector law are shared by these continuations, the positive witnesses $W_5,W_7$ now measure integrals with respect to $\mu_H$. For $0<\delta<1$ the existing separation constant $c_\delta=\inf_{|q-1|\ge\delta}(W_5+W_7)>0$ gives

$$ \mu_H(|q-1|\ge\delta)\le B_H/c_\delta, $$

where $B_H$ is a simultaneous upper bound on the accepted-cohort witness expectation after accounting for efficiencies, backgrounds and drift. If there is also a retained fair-count component with fraction $f_H$, a separate calibrated dark-well test bounds $f_H$. The same inequality bounds the *unnormalized* remaining exponent-tail fraction, so their bounds may be added. Conditioning on acceptance before randomization uses accepted attempts as the probe denominator. Retain the number of original starts and the herald frequency separately. A random split does not create extra accepted launches. At accepted yield $Y_H$, an expected $m$ accepted probes in a randomly allocated fraction $s$ of trials costs $m/(sY_H)$ original starts under stable sampling.

A common persistent parameter across reset and flight is an additional hypothesis. If the probe reset makes $q=1$ regardless of its previous value, every calibration read can be ordinary while the flying subset retains $q=2$. If probe and flight both draw a fresh exponent from the same post-herald law, the probe can calibrate that new common law. Identifying it with the Bayesian posterior of the pre-herald exponent requires persistence or a measured transfer rule. Even when $q=1$, a phase scramble can change the later kinematic word law while two perfectly correlated registers still agree. Similarly, an apparatus can apply the ordinary map to each site total yet read its internal record bits with a different rule. The remote equality by itself places no constraint on such a pointer. The pointer labels must be mapped to the same calibrated readout law, including the treatment of unresolved coordinates. A recognized loss or timeout needs its own output code or a separately bounded error. ## How a small exponent error affects complete words

Fix one unitary recorded-flight preparation and its known normalized kinematic word distribution $p$ within $H$. Assume that conditional on a retained good $q$, the protected reference word has law $T_q p$. This holds, for example, when complete joint labels have a common ordinary kinematic distribution, receive a single power at terminal readout, and $H$ is a union of labels. Conditioning cancels the common scale of their weights. It need not hold if an intervening measurement changes the field differently in different sectors, if different accepted launch ports give different ordinary flights, or if a fresh nonlinear rule acts at every tick. Such preparations must be matched, stratified or modeled.

Let $n$ be the number of positive $p_i$; zero cells stay zero at $q>0$. For $1\le q\le1+\delta$, Jensen's inequality gives $\sum p_i^q\ge n^{1-q}$, hence $(T_qp)_i\le n^\delta p_i$. Sum positive excesses to bound total variation by $n^\delta-1$. For $1-\delta\le q\le1$, $\sum p_i^q\le n^{1-q}$ and $p_i^q\ge p_i$, giving $(T_qp)_i\ge n^{-\delta}p_i$. Summing deficits gives the tighter $1-n^{-\delta}$. Thus if a fraction $\beta$ of the accepted responses is uncontrolled, the remaining exponents obey $|q-1|\le\delta$, and $Z$ is the protected word,

$$d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(Z\mid H),p)\le\beta+(1-\beta)\min(1,n^\delta-1).$$

This controls every event of the same word simultaneously, including an entire path tube. When the alphabet grows, $\delta\log n\to0$ suffices alongside $\beta\to0$. The dependence on refinement is real. Give one Newtonian tube total mass $1/2$ in one word and an equally likely tube $n=2^k$ equally likely words. With $q=1+1/k$, the latter tube has measured probability $1/3$ for every $k$, even though $q\to1$. All these words may describe paths inside arbitrarily narrow tubes. If a device first *groups* their kinematic weights into two categories and then applies the same power, both probabilities are $1/2$. Grouping plotted outcomes after fine-label detection gives $1/3$. Equivalence between the two physical readings requires measurement.

For the two-ray flight an efficient target uses three disjoint categories on accepted runs. They indicate that the *entire* interpolated record follows the first ray, follows the second, or lies outside both specified tubes. Use tubes separated by at least one sampled time, and include all exceptional codes in the third category. The position checks, clocks and mesh must be those of the sequential flight theorem. A physical coarse detector with the power law on these three total masses only has $n\le3$. To pass that calibration to a reported fine word, also verify the coarse category computed from that word against the persistent coarse register. Otherwise the refinement example survives. ## Transferring a ten-tick calibration to a mechanical position read

Suppose an accepted cohort can be split between the [ten-tick addressed calibration](Three_signed_ticks_and_the_probability_power.md) and the flight of interest. Each arm must have the same population law for the terminal probability rule, including its exponent or its fair-path-count type. Conditional on a power type, the calibration and flight can partition their rails differently. The calibration permits pooling the two rails at a site or reading them separately. The flight can use any partition into terminal cells each assigned to a reported position bin. For type \(A\), let \(p_A\) be the ordinary squared masses on its actual flight cells, normalized on the same accepted cohort. The reference distribution is the mixture of these ordinary laws coarsened to the reported bins with the same type weights. To identify this reference with a particular mechanical prediction, its flight preparation and dynamics must yield that prediction. The same marginal type law suffices for the bound. A sharp reference reset that changes that law can invalidate it.

Let \(D\) be the **complete population mean** of the addressed calibration score on this cohort. Let \(N\ge1\) bound the number of positive terminal cells for every powered flight in the comparison. For a fair-count type the reference is its specified counterfactual ordinary flight law on the same reported positions. Zero cells stay dark even at \(q=0\), and the \(q=\infty\) limit may put mass on maximizers. A fair-count type can have any flight law. Write \(K_N=\max(1,\log N/\log9)\). Then

$$ d_{\rm TV}(P_{\rm flight},P_{\rm ordinary})\le \min\{1,K_N\sqrt{17D}\}. $$

**Proof.** For \(q>1\) let \(Z_q=\sum_i p_i^q\). Both \(p_i\) and \(p_i^q/Z_q\) dominate \(p_i^q\), whose total \(Z_q\) is at least \(N^{1-q}\) by convexity. For \(0\le q<1\) both dominate \(p_i/Z_q\) on the positive support, whose total is at least \(N^{q-1}\) by concavity. At \(q=0\), \(Z_0\) counts the positive cells. Since total variation is one minus the sum of the componentwise minima, the discrepancy for a finite power is at most \(1-N^{-|q-1|}\). This tightens the earlier total variation bound for each retained type with \(|q-1|\le\delta\) to \(1-N^{-\delta}\). [Normalized power distributions](https://perso.telecom-paristech.fr/rioul/publis/201808rioul.pdf)

Put \(g(q)=1-9^{-|q-1|}\), and \(g=1\) for infinite power or a fair count. For \(0\le u\le1\), \(1-u^a\le\max(1,a)(1-u)\) when \(a>0\): use \(u^a\ge u\) for \(a\le1\) and the tangent inequality at one for \(a\ge1\). At \(N=1\) finite powers have zero discrepancy directly. Thus every type, including the two assigned \(g=1\), has discrepancy at most \(K_Ng\). The ten-tick calibration gives \(g^2\le17D_A\) on every type: its three score cells are single-rail, the largest cell has normalized powered weight at least \(1/17\), and \(|1-9^{1-q}|\ge g(q)\). For the limiting maximum rule \(D_A=1\), and for fair count \(D_A=6297/512\). Coarsening to position contracts total variation. Convexity for the common mixing law and Cauchy–Schwarz give the stated bound. No pairing of individual calibration shots with individual flights is needed.

The population result requires no independence between repeated launches. Converting observed frequencies into an upper bound \(D\le U\) requires a justified sampling model or assignment design. A missing fraction \(\ell\) with zero logged score adds up to \(81\ell\) to the complete mean before sampling uncertainty. Score-dependent losses and conditioning after the random split can change either population. A score of zero gives exact equality within the assumed response family. It does not establish which phase, force or prepared band produced \(p_A\).

**Growing numbers of cells.** Consider here the earlier example as a terminal probability vector. Give one cell ordinary mass \(1/2\) and \(2^k\) other cells equal shares of the remaining half. With \(q_k=1+1/k\) the latter set has powered mass \(1/3\), giving a fixed total variation \(1/6\). For the all-sites-pooled ten-tick calibrator, write \(Z(q)=\sum_x m_x^q\) for the positive site masses in its published integer table. Its score on the same type is \(D_k=324^{q_k}(1-9^{-1/k})^2/Z(q_k)\). As \(k\to\infty\), \(Z(q_k)\to1024\), hence
$$ D_k\log^2(2^k+1)\ \longrightarrow\
   \frac{324}{1024}(\log9)^2(\log2)^2>0. $$
Thus \(D_k\to0\) alone cannot control terminal probabilities for arbitrary finely resolved vectors. This example establishes the scale for unrestricted alphabets. Realizing these masses with a specified walk and detector is a separate problem. An ordinary-small tail alone also cannot justify truncation for \(q<1\). For example a normalized infinite tail proportional to \(j^{-2}\) can have arbitrarily small total ordinary mass, while its square roots have divergent sum. With demonstrably finite power normalizers and a uniform bound on the variance of log mass under nearby powers, the [log-weight variance comparison](Calibration_and_the_log_weights_of_a_flight.md) gives a separate transfer criterion. Finite cutoffs of the divergent example can carry almost all the powered probability at \(q=1/2\).

**What transfers to the force calculation.** Suppose \(D_n K_{N_n}^2\to0\) for matched preparations and reads, with physical partition errors also vanishing. At a fixed flight time the bound transfers every terminal bin probability up to a common error tending to zero. Any ordinary weak position limit transfers through the same reported coordinate map, since expectations of bounded test functions differ by at most \(2\|f\|_\infty d_{\rm TV}\). In the [constant-force calculation](Forced_envelope_and_classical_motion.md), the ideal terminal scaled position in the specified band and shrinking-action regime concentrates at \(Y_0+p_0T-FT^2/2\). Its initial momentum support is compact and its position support is generally infinite. The number of ticks does not bound the occupied positions of that prepared packet.

One sufficient readout for this application is a finite position grid covering a bounded interval around the limiting ray point, with mesh tending to zero there, and a dedicated overflow cell **formed before** the power response. For example, windows growing to the line with mesh tending to zero and ordinary overflow probability tending to zero suffice. Mapping each finite bin to a representative changes the ordinary weak limit by vanishing error locally. The powered overflow probability differs from the ordinary one by at most the same total variation bound. One may report an overflow symbol or give it a conventional position for this limit. Software that groups fine outcomes after applying their powers realizes a different law. With just the published force dynamics, these are proposed detector and calibration conditions. The conclusion concerns separate terminal position measurements: the kinematic Schrödinger spinor, the band and field preparation, mass and force scale, momentum readout, and a joint path law require their own arguments.

## Does the reported word agree with its stored reference?

Let $R$ be the committed report and $Z$ an independently accessible protected reference for the same flight. Encode both injectively as equal-length bit strings, including every timing or failure code needed to distinguish outcomes. For the three categories above, $R$ can instead be an encoded category computed from the committed fine report and $Z$ the corresponding protected category. After both values are fixed, choose a fresh uniformly random mask $s$. Compare the computed parity $s\cdot R$ with $s\cdot Z$ obtained using the reference. On an actual persistent binary register a clean ancilla can accumulate the latter by controlled XORs from the addressed bits. This tests the device's ability to retain and read that same register. Reusing the reporting software to compute both parities of $R$ would always pass.

If $R\ne Z$, select any differing coordinate. Toggling just that coordinate in the mask bijects agreeing parities to disagreeing parities. Therefore $P(s\cdot R\ne s\cdot Z\mid H)=P(R\ne Z\mid H)/2$. With $h$ independent masks on unchanged records the coefficient becomes $1-2^{-h}$. Reading many bits to compute the reference parity still costs physical operations; the one-bit figure counts the *reported check result*. If the observed any-failure rate is at most $r$ and the chance its verdict differs from the ideal parity verdict is at most $a$ on the same audited cohort, then

$$d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(R\mid H),\mathcal L(Z\mid H))\le P(R\ne Z\mid H)\le (r+a)/(1-2^{-h}).$$

Random audit selection makes the bound apply to the unaudited cohort only when assignment preserves the record and the comparisons include misses. The parity construction is a standard universal hashing idea. A [Lean proof](lean/StoredWordParity.lean) verifies the half-count recurrence, and the [enumerator](check_heralded_record_transfer.py) tests every mask and difference word through four bits. [Carter and Wegman](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(79)90044-8)

The measured word error adds to the previous bound by the triangle inequality. No per-tick independence or sum of per-tick rates is needed. For reports taking values in the recorded path space, total variation also bounds the change in probability of every path event after interpolation. For the three-category implementation, use tube diameters tending to zero, ideal category masses tending to $(1/2,1/2,0)$, and vanishing total calibration error; these imply convergence in law to the same two Newtonian tracks. This conclusion refers to the specified monitored flight and the stored herald that actually selected it.

Three controls distinguish the assumptions. (1) At a force reversal, a reporter can flip its late side label with every earlier position recorded correctly. The late marginal remains fair, but the crossing history is always incorrect. A late independent parity on committed words detects this. (2) If a mask is known before the report is fixed, the reporter can change the word by a nonzero vector in that mask's kernel whenever at least two bits are available. (3) Two perfectly agreeing registers can share a false write at the source. No downstream comparison establishes their coupling to physical position, or to the intended *true* herald, without separate evidence. Tracing over a coherent flag also does not give a pre-existing joint pair of classical strings for this audit. The parity inference applies at a verified persistent classical read interface or to an independently validated nondemolition instrument with the same operational joint records.
