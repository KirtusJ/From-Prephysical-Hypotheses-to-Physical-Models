# Calibrating a probability law on heralded flight records

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A rare preparation can preferentially select a detector response that scarcely figures in an unconditioned calibration. When the apparatus can preserve that response and reset only the walker, a remote-position test *after* recorded acceptance probes the mixture that actually flies. A separate test of the complete output word can certify agreement between a reported history and a protected reference history using one random parity bit per sampled flight. Both statements have operational premises. Neither follows from accurate one-time positions. ## Which exponents reach a rare flight

Let $H$ mean acceptance by a specified stored herald. In the site-power family an exponent $q>0$ assigns a distribution $T_q p$ to labels with kinematic weights $p_i$, where $(T_q p)_i=p_i^q/\sum_j p_j^q$. If $a(q)$ is the probability of the actual herald at exponent $q$, then the retained exponent measure is $d\mu_H=a(q)d\mu/\int a\,d\mu$. For example, choose $Y=1/(k^2+1)$ and split ordinary accepted mass $Y$ uniformly among $k^6$ fine labels. Keep the remaining mass in one rejected label. An exponent $1/2$ changes the acceptance probability to $k^2/(k^2+1)$. Giving this exponent launch weight $Y$ and exponent 1 the rest makes exactly half of all accepted runs come from exponent $1/2$. The initial contamination tends to zero. Exact remote equalities on a common persistent family can exclude it, but finite prior accuracy must be compared to the yield.

Once the herald is committed, randomly route a representative subset of the accepted launches into each of the existing five- and seven-tick signed remote tests. Supply the same sharp probe initialization, retaining the latent response and the herald; also retain no-click outcomes. The true body of the flight is run on another randomly assigned subset. If the response parameter and conditional detector law are shared by these continuations, the positive witnesses $W_5,W_7$ now measure integrals with respect to $\mu_H$. For $0<\delta<1$ the existing separation constant $c_\delta=\inf_{|q-1|\ge\delta}(W_5+W_7)>0$ gives

$$ \mu_H(|q-1|\ge\delta)\le B_H/c_\delta, $$

where $B_H$ is a simultaneous upper bound on the accepted-cohort witness expectation after accounting for efficiencies, backgrounds and drift. If there is also a retained fair-count component with fraction $f_H$, a separate calibrated dark-well test bounds $f_H$. The same inequality bounds the *unnormalized* remaining exponent-tail fraction, so their bounds may be added. Conditioning on acceptance before randomization uses accepted attempts as the probe denominator. Retain the number of original starts and the herald frequency separately. A random split does not create extra accepted launches. At accepted yield $Y_H$, an expected $m$ accepted probes in a randomly allocated fraction $s$ of trials costs $m/(sY_H)$ original starts under stable sampling.

A common persistent parameter across reset and flight is an additional hypothesis. If the probe reset makes $q=1$ regardless of its previous value, every calibration read can be ordinary while the flying subset retains $q=2$. Similarly, an apparatus can apply the ordinary map to each site total yet read its internal record bits with a different rule. The remote equality by itself places no constraint on such a pointer. The pointer labels must be mapped to the same calibrated readout law, including the treatment of unresolved coordinates. A recognized loss or timeout needs its own output code or a separately bounded error. ## How a small exponent error affects complete words

Fix one unitary recorded-flight preparation and its known normalized kinematic word distribution $p$ within $H$. Assume that conditional on a retained good $q$, the protected reference word has law $T_q p$. This holds, for example, when complete joint labels have a common ordinary kinematic distribution, receive a single power at terminal readout, and $H$ is a union of labels. Conditioning cancels the common scale of their weights. It need not hold if an intervening measurement changes the field differently in different sectors, if different accepted launch ports give different ordinary flights, or if a fresh nonlinear rule acts at every tick. Such preparations must be matched, stratified or modeled.

Let $n$ be the number of positive $p_i$; zero cells stay zero at $q>0$. For $1\le q\le1+\delta$, Jensen's inequality gives $\sum p_i^q\ge n^{1-q}$, hence $(T_qp)_i\le n^\delta p_i$. Sum positive excesses to bound total variation by $n^\delta-1$. For $1-\delta\le q\le1$, $\sum p_i^q\le n^{1-q}$ and $p_i^q\ge p_i$, giving $(T_qp)_i\ge n^{-\delta}p_i$. Summing deficits gives the tighter $1-n^{-\delta}$. Thus if a fraction $\beta$ of the accepted responses is uncontrolled, the remaining exponents obey $|q-1|\le\delta$, and $Z$ is the protected word,

$$d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(Z\mid H),p)\le\beta+(1-\beta)\min(1,n^\delta-1).$$

This controls every event of the same word simultaneously, including an entire path tube. When the alphabet grows, $\delta\log n\to0$ suffices alongside $\beta\to0$. The dependence on refinement is real. Give one Newtonian tube total mass $1/2$ in one word and an equally likely tube $n=2^k$ equally likely words. With $q=1+1/k$, the latter tube has measured probability $1/3$ for every $k$, even though $q\to1$. All these words may describe paths inside arbitrarily narrow tubes. If a device first *groups* their kinematic weights into two categories and then applies the same power, both probabilities are $1/2$. Grouping plotted outcomes after fine-label detection gives $1/3$. Equivalence between the two physical readings requires measurement.

For the two-ray flight an efficient target uses three disjoint categories on accepted runs. They indicate that the *entire* interpolated record follows the first ray, follows the second, or lies outside both specified tubes. Use tubes separated by at least one sampled time, and include all exceptional codes in the third category. The position checks, clocks and mesh must be those of the sequential flight theorem. A physical coarse detector with the power law on these three total masses only has $n\le3$. To pass that calibration to a reported fine word, also verify the coarse category computed from that word against the persistent coarse register. Otherwise the refinement example survives. ## Does the reported word agree with its stored reference?

Let $R$ be the committed report and $Z$ an independently accessible protected reference for the same flight. Encode both injectively as equal-length bit strings, including every timing or failure code needed to distinguish outcomes. For the three categories above, $R$ can instead be an encoded category computed from the committed fine report and $Z$ the corresponding protected category. After both values are fixed, choose a fresh uniformly random mask $s$. Compare the computed parity $s\cdot R$ with $s\cdot Z$ obtained using the reference. On an actual persistent binary register a clean ancilla can accumulate the latter by controlled XORs from the addressed bits. This tests the device's ability to retain and read that same register. Reusing the reporting software to compute both parities of $R$ would always pass.

If $R\ne Z$, select any differing coordinate. Toggling just that coordinate in the mask bijects agreeing parities to disagreeing parities. Therefore $P(s\cdot R\ne s\cdot Z\mid H)=P(R\ne Z\mid H)/2$. With $h$ independent masks on unchanged records the coefficient becomes $1-2^{-h}$. Reading many bits to compute the reference parity still costs physical operations; the one-bit figure counts the *reported check result*. If the observed any-failure rate is at most $r$ and the chance its verdict differs from the ideal parity verdict is at most $a$ on the same audited cohort, then

$$d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(R\mid H),\mathcal L(Z\mid H))\le P(R\ne Z\mid H)\le (r+a)/(1-2^{-h}).$$

Random audit selection makes the bound apply to the unaudited cohort only when assignment preserves the record and the comparisons include misses. The parity construction is a standard universal hashing idea. A [Lean proof](lean/StoredWordParity.lean) verifies the half-count recurrence, and the [enumerator](check_heralded_record_transfer.py) tests every mask and difference word through four bits. [Carter and Wegman](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(79)90044-8)

The measured word error adds to the previous bound by the triangle inequality. No per-tick independence or sum of per-tick rates is needed. For reports taking values in the recorded path space, total variation also bounds the change in probability of every path event after interpolation. For the three-category implementation, use tube diameters tending to zero, ideal category masses tending to $(1/2,1/2,0)$, and vanishing total calibration error; these imply convergence in law to the same two Newtonian tracks. This conclusion refers to the specified monitored flight and the stored herald that actually selected it.

Three controls distinguish the assumptions. (1) At a force reversal, a reporter can flip its late side label with every earlier position recorded correctly. The late marginal remains fair, but the crossing history is always incorrect. A late independent parity on committed words detects this. (2) If a mask is known before the report is fixed, the reporter can change the word by a nonzero vector in that mask's kernel whenever at least two bits are available. (3) Two perfectly agreeing registers can share a false write at the source. No downstream comparison establishes their coupling to physical position, or to the intended *true* herald, without separate evidence. Tracing over a coherent flag also does not give a pre-existing joint pair of classical strings for this audit. The parity inference applies at a verified persistent classical read interface or to an independently validated nondemolition instrument with the same operational joint records.
