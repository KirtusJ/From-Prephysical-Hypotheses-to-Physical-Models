# Temporal noise and the spatial pattern of a photonic walk

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Asapanna and colleagues injected a laser pulse at one site of a two-loop optical lattice. They applied a random perturbation to the splitting angle at each step. In one setting they redrew the perturbation every step. In another they held it for the two steps of a Floquet period before drawing again. A third run used zero imposed noise. This compares two ways to time a disturbance with the same specified single-step noise distribution. The program's monitored walk also distinguishes schedules with matching single-time marginals. Its instrument writes position to a persistent record. The optical experiment changes a coupling and reads out the propagating field. [Experiment, Fig. 2 and Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16204v2) · [Monitored walk protocol](Diffusion_from_three_position_records.md)

## Sum the signal and compare the whole spatial pattern

For each of 40 periods the Fig. 2 deposit gives a 64-bin spatial intensity profile for the $\beta$ loop and the site-summed intensities for both $\alpha$ and $\beta$. The pulse preparation was in the $\alpha$ loop. The two noisy settings used nominal angles $0,\pi/4$, phase zero and a uniform step perturbation in $[-\sigma/2,\sigma/2]$ with $\sigma=0.4\pi$. The authors averaged the complex fields across 100 realizations before taking the squared magnitude. The figures supply processed intensity, with no incident-photon counts or realization-level uncertainty. All comparisons below use the numerical deposit, version 2.0. [Experiment and methods](https://doi.org/10.1103/dxjw-bmng) · [Data and file-level descriptions](https://doi.org/10.57745/7MIDRA)

First use the authors' two-loop total $C(M)=\sum_n (|\bar\alpha_n^M|^2+|\bar\beta_n^M|^2)$ at each recorded period $M$. The held-noise setting has more coherent signal than the redrawn setting at **37 of 40 periods**. The order reverses at periods 1, 34 and 40. The ratio of the sums over all 40 periods, $\sum_M C_{\rm held}(M)/\sum_M C_{\rm redraw}(M)$, is **1.290915**. This ratio counts a field at multiple times. It has no interpretation as a fraction of detected launches.

Next compare shape at the same time, separately normalizing each deposited $\beta$ profile by its own sum over all 64 bins. If $u$ is the normalized noisy profile and $v$ the normalized noiseless profile at that period, report $\sum_n |u_n-v_n|/2$. The held-noise profile is closer to noiseless at **15 of 40** periods, and the redrawn profile at **25 of 40**. There are no ties at the published precision. For example, at period 10 the held signal has two-loop total 0.122470 versus 0.061442 for the redrawn signal, while their respective profile distances are 0.467037 and 0.267857. The comparison of shape uses only the $\beta$ loop because the deposit gives the $\alpha$ loop as a site sum. The counts of periods describe these complete traces. Observations at consecutive times arise from the same evolutions. [All 40 rows, including reversals](results/coherent_field_noise.csv)

## Which observable could measure diffusion?

Writing $b_r(n,M)$ for the field in realization $r$, the deposited profile is proportional to $|\bar b|^2$. The intensity that would enter an ensemble position distribution is $\overline{|b|^2}$. Their difference is exactly $\overline{|b-\bar b|^2}$. To see why this matters, give each realization of the same spatial wave a global phase of 0 or $\pi$ in equal proportions. Every realization has the same position intensities, while the coherently averaged field vanishes. Normalizing the deposited coherent pattern at each time cannot reconstruct the missing term. Even for the noiseless setting the reported two-loop coherent total falls from 0.8894 at the first period to 0.04994 at the last. The authors discuss phase and amplitude extraction errors and show a single-realization comparison. [Fig. 2 and experimental discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16204v2)

The deposited signals show a dependence on the timing of an imposed disturbance. Inferring a walker displacement variance would require realization-averaged spatial intensities for both loops with a known normalization. Testing the program's timing covariance through joint return events would also require ordered outcomes on the same walk. Those observables are absent from this deposit.
