# A midpoint sign tests coin balance with a terminal rail read

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

For a repeated two-mode walk, compare the opposite-rail frequency after two bare ticks with the frequency after reversing the sign of the left rail between ticks. The shortfall of their sum from one measures the spread of the underlying coin's flip probability about one half. This uses a terminal rail read in both settings. By contrast, any adaptive sequence of sharp rail measurements and otherwise free ticks can miss a whole population of unbalanced coins if the measurement refreshes the coin. [Monitored double-flip test and free-clock obstruction](Testing_an_unknown_two_mode_coin.md)

## Two coherent paths to one output

Use the two-well ring, launch rail $R$, and basis $(R,L)$. On a coherent launch draw a unitary $U$ from a stable subprobability $\nu$ of mass $1-c$. Use that same $U$ on both sides of a lossless rail-diagonal plate $P_\lambda=\operatorname{diag}(1,\lambda)$, $|\lambda|=1$. A fraction $c$ makes independent fair direction choices; it is insensitive to a diagonal phase. Let $C_\lambda$ denote the opposite-rail rate per **original** launch after $U P_\lambda U$. The bare two-tick rate is $C_1=L_2$. The plate setting must preserve the source ensemble and the repeated coin. Terminal rail rates must be known or separately calibrated. In particular, calibrating a detector by presupposing the first tick is balanced would assume part of the assertion being tested. A switch on the arriving left rail at the *first* tick implements the midpoint sign when that control is available. [Sign operation and terminal-detector limitations](Calibrating_the_two_well_detector.md) · [Two-path interferometer using a phase shifter between splitters](https://blogs.dickinson.edu/pearsonb/files/2012/02/AJPv78p471.pdf)

Put $q=|U_{LR}|^2$, $A=U_{RR}$, $B=U_{LL}$. Unitarity gives $|A|^2=|B|^2=1-q$. Adding the two physical paths gives $(UP_\lambda U)_{LR}=U_{LR}(A+\lambda B)$. The parallelogram identity yields
$$|U_{LR}|^2\big(|A+B|^2+|A-B|^2\big)=4q(1-q).$$
The two fair-count contributions sum to $c$. Consequently
$$D=\frac{1-L_2-C_{-1}}{4}=\int(q-1/2)^2\,d\nu.$$
This quantity vanishes exactly when the coherent components are balanced almost surely. Pure fair counting gives zero as well. Combining $D$ with the independent free-clock witness $W=L_3+2(1-a)L_2+(a^2-1)L_1$ from the monitored test excludes fair counting and identifies $(q,|\operatorname{tr}U|^2)=(1/2,a)$ almost surely at their joint zero. For the cubic target $a=3/2$, the balanced prediction is $L_2=3/4$, $C_{-1}=1/4$. The cubic unbalanced pair with $q=1/2\pm1/16$ gives $L_2=3/4$, $C_{-1}=15/64$. Its sign-setting deficit of $1/64$ per launch is four times the double-flip excess of $1/256$ in the monitored test. These two margins alone do not compare statistical power across instruments.

## One oriented phase and an exact defect identity

For $q<1$ define $\eta=B/A$. Its modulus is one. At $q=1$ choose $\eta$ arbitrarily since its weight below vanishes. The full identity for one plate is
$$1-C_\lambda=\frac c2+\int\!\left[4(q-1/2)^2+q(1-q)|1-\lambda\eta|^2\right]d\nu.$$
Indeed $|1+\lambda\eta|^2+|1-\lambda\eta|^2=4$. The first two contributions bound the count mass and coin imbalance even if the unknown diagonal phase depends on the hidden label, with the relevant phase inside the integral. If $C_\lambda\ge1-e$, then $c\le2e$ and $\nu(|q-1/2|\ge d)\le e/(4d^2)$ for $d>0$. For a known common phase the last term also bounds phase error. On $\delta\le q\le1-\delta$, $0<\delta\le1/2$, the mass with $|1-\lambda\eta|\ge h>0$ is at most $e/[\delta(1-\delta)h^2]$.

For the balanced target $U_\beta=\operatorname{diag}(1,\beta)H$ the diagonal ratio is $\eta=-\beta$. At $\lambda=-\bar\beta$ the rate is one. Conversely saturation forces $c=0$, $q=1/2$ and $\eta=-\beta$ almost surely. Remove each coin's overall phase to set $A=1/\sqrt2$. Its diagonal is then the target diagonal. Conjugating by a diagonal unitary sets the upper-right entry to $1/\sqrt2$, and column orthogonality forces the remaining entry to $\beta/\sqrt2$. Thus only the overall phase and the phase convention for the two rails remain free. For $\beta=1$, the single midpoint sign $C_{-1}=1$ suffices; for $\beta=-1$, $L_2=1$ suffices. The conjugate of a nonreal $\beta$ gives $C_{-\bar\beta}=(\operatorname{Re}\beta)^2$, or $1/4$ for a cubic unit. This orientation test presupposes the plate's physical phase relative to the walk. Scanning an uncalibrated phase can test balance through the upper contrast; it cannot name that orientation by itself.

Coin retention is material even at saturation. Let
$$H(q)=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{1-q}&\sqrt q\\ \sqrt q&-\sqrt{1-q}\end{pmatrix}.$$
An equally weighted $q=1/2\pm d$ pair, $0<d\le1/2$, at every bare launch agrees with $H(1/2)$ on all free clocks since $H(q)^2=I$. If firing the midpoint sign changes the second coin to $H(1-q)$, then $H(1-q)P_{-1}H(q)|R\rangle=|L\rangle$ for either member. The all-launch sign rate is one for this unbalanced and setting-dependent implementation. Misses, rail confusion and an ensemble that changes with the plate setting also require measurement or explicit bounds before attribution.

## Every adaptive rail history can survive a hidden redraw

Let $0\le a<2$. Use the earlier pair at fixed $r=|\operatorname{tr}U|^2=a$ with $q_\pm=1/2\pm(2-a)/8$. Unitaries with these parameters exist by $r\le4(1-q)$. Hold the coin fixed through any unmeasured interval, then independently redraw it from the same pair **after each sharp rail read**, regardless of the outcome. The pure comparison model keeps $q=1/2,r=a$ at all times. Allow either sharp rail preparation, arbitrarily many free ticks between reads, and all future spacings and stopping decisions to depend on the recorded outcomes and independent classical randomness.

Conditional on a segment length $t\ge1$ and its incoming rail, its flip probability for a fixed coin is $q h_t(a)$ with $h_t(a)=U_{t-1}(\sqrt a/2)^2$, where $U_j$ denotes the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind. This follows from the two-dimensional characteristic recurrence and holds in either direction. The next coin is independent of the entire past conditional on a new segment, so the flip probability is $\mathbb E(q) h_t(a)=h_t(a)/2$ at **every** history. These conditional kernels coincide with the pure target. Multiplying them along each transcript proves equality for every finite adaptive protocol. It persists under postprocessing, stopping and forgetting outcomes. Infinite records on the usual product space agree on all finite cylinders and hence in law. This argument covers protocols built from free gaps and sharp rail reads. A coherent midpoint operation, coherent re-preparation or an independent source of coin information introduces an additional instrument.

Conversely, with a persistent hidden coin and repeated readings after each tick, the flip indicators are conditionally independent Bernoulli($q$). Two distinct flip indicators have covariance $\operatorname{Var}(q)$ for a coherent population. A nonzero value distinguishes that persistent population from the independent redraw construction. Zero leaves the balanced target and the redrawing unbalanced population observationally identical. A measurement-induced disturbance is therefore an empirical premise wherever repeated projected records are used to identify a generator.

The checker propagates exact Gaussian-rational unitary amplitudes, computes both midpoint outputs and their phase defects, and retains positive and negative controls including an adaptive tree with early stopping. All conclusions assume the coherent squared-amplitude rule or the specified counting alternative. Establishing a prephysical probability law and a common physical implementation for these controls remains open.
