# Testing the coin as well as its phase

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

A joint first-flip and return record can test whether the two-mode walk's coin is balanced. Combined with three ordinary terminal reads, it selects a designated balanced coin up to the rail phase conventions that these instruments cannot resolve. The competing coherent operations may be **arbitrary fixed $2\times2$ unitaries** drawn from a common distribution. Unfiltered records alone admit unbalanced coins whose predictions agree at *every clock* for every target except the negative real phase. The added record must preserve the hidden coin between its two steps. [Two-clock test under balanced gains](Two_well_phase_witness.md) · [Director Z’s challenge on unbalanced coins](../Director_Z/cross_review/subagent_xr67_two_well_witness/NOTES.txt)

## Three free outputs and one monitored history

On the two-well ring the position at each tick is deterministic; keep the arrival rail. Prepare R. Let a stable subprobability $\nu$ of mass $1-c$ range over coherent coins $U\in U(2)$. For one launch its selected coin is applied at each tick; terminal probabilities use its squared amplitudes. The remaining mass $c$ uses independent fair directions at each tick. Write
$$q=|U_{LR}|^2,\qquad r=|\operatorname{tr} U|^2.$$
The indices give outgoing, incoming rail. Unitarity implies $|U_{RL}|^2=q$. Let $L_t$ be the unconditional probability of final L at tick $t$ on independent preparations with no earlier read. Separately record $F=P(L\ {\rm at\ tick}\ 1,\ R\ {\rm at\ tick}\ 2)$ on a monitored launch; use **all original launches** as its denominator. In that setting a nondemolition projective rail read at tick one retains the selected coin and the L rail before the second step. A coherent, perfectly distinguishable first-rail memory read jointly with the final rail gives the same probabilities when the subsequent coin acts independently of the memory. The selected distribution and detector calibration must transfer across settings. A projective or equivalently calibrated record is an extra physical instrument. Repetitive nondemolition qubit interrogation has been demonstrated in a different ion apparatus; that experiment does not measure $F$ for this walk. [Hume, Rosenband and Wineland, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.99.120502)

Choose a square root of $\det U$ and divide it out to obtain $S\in SU(2)$. Then $S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&v\\-\bar v&\bar u\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $|u|^2+|v|^2=1$, and $s=\operatorname{tr}S=2\operatorname{Re}u$ satisfies $s^2=r$. The characteristic equation $S^2=sS-I$ gives the off-diagonal entries of $S,S^2,S^3$ as $S_{LR}$ times $1,s,s^2-1$, respectively. The overall phase from $\det U$ cancels in every rail probability. Thus
$$L_1=\int q\,d\nu+c/2,\quad L_2=\int qr\,d\nu+c/2,\quad L_3=\int q(r-1)^2d\nu+c/2,\quad F=\int q^2d\nu+c/4.$$
The last identity uses both directed flips. Measuring the first rail and allowing it to evolve coherently without keeping the first outcome would define a different observable.

## The two nonnegative quantities

For the desired port convention let the balanced target be $Z_\beta H$ with $Z_\beta=\operatorname{diag}(1,\beta)$ and $H$ the real Hadamard matrix. For $|\beta|=1$, its parameters are $q_*=1/2$ and $a=r_*=1-\operatorname{Re}\beta\in[0,2]$. The four rates give
$$W=L_3+2(1-a)L_2+(a^2-1)L_1=\int q(r-a)^2d\nu+\frac{1+(a-1)^2}{2}c,$$
$$D=F-L_1+1/4=\int(q-1/2)^2d\nu.$$
Consequently $W=D=0$ holds exactly when $c=0$ and almost all selected coins have $(q,r)=(1/2,a)$. Indeed $W=0$ excludes counting while allowing any diagonal coin $q=0$; $D=0$ excludes those diagonal coins and every other nonbalanced value. Conversely each coin with the target parameters gives both zeros.

For balanced $U$, these two real parameters determine its class up to overall phase, a diagonal change of rail phases and complex conjugation. To see this for any two coins with $q=1/2$ and the same $r$, the $SU(2)$ representation fixes $|v|$ and $|u|$. The sign of the square root of $\det U$ selects the sign of $\operatorname{Re}u$. Complex conjugation then selects the sign of $\operatorname{Im}u$ if necessary, so the two diagonal entries can be matched exactly. Conjugating by a diagonal unitary supplies any remaining phase difference in $v$. All three operations preserve the specified sharp launch, free terminal-rail reads and projected rail histories. A mixture may still vary arbitrarily among these equivalent descriptions. No such operation supplies a physically named orientation for the phase or establishes the microscopic gain carrier.

For the cubic unit, $a=3/2$. The target left probabilities are $(L_1,L_2,L_3)=(1/2,3/4,1/8)$ and the predicted monitored rate is $F=1/4$. Thus the proposed measurement specifies an absolute, unpostselected probability that the narrow balanced-coin test did not require.

A measured upper bound on $W$ gives $c\le 2W/[1+(a-1)^2]$. For $\epsilon>0$, a bound on $D$ gives $\nu(|q-1/2|\ge\epsilon)\le D/\epsilon^2$. For any $\delta>0$, splitting at $q=1/4$ gives $\nu(|r-a|\ge\delta)\le16D+4W/\delta^2$. Experimental frequency errors $e_1,e_2,e_3,e_F$ in absolute probability propagate into $W$ by at most $e_3+2|1-a|e_2+|a^2-1|e_1$ and into $D$ by $e_F+e_1$. Negative finite-sample estimates are possible and should retain their uncertainty. Calibration of a terminal detector alone does not validate the intervening instrument.

## An ambiguity at all free clocks

For every $t\ge1$ the off-diagonal amplitude of $S^t$ is $S_{LR}C_{t-1}(s/2)$, where $C_n$ denotes the Chebyshev polynomial of the **second** kind. This follows from the same characteristic recurrence with initial factors $0,1$. The square of the factor depends only on $r$ by parity. Thus the free left probability at fixed $r$ is proportional to $q$ at every tick, even if arbitrarily many ticks can be tested.

The feasible parameter region is $0\le q\le1$ and $0\le r\le4(1-q)$. Necessity follows from $r=4(\operatorname{Re}u)^2$. To realize any point, choose $u=\sqrt r/2+i\sqrt{1-q-r/4}$ and $v=\sqrt q$. For every target $0\le a<2$, mix two coins at $r=a$ with equal weights and $q_\pm=1/2\pm(2-a)/8$. Both are feasible. Their mean $q=1/2$ gives exactly the target's unfiltered distribution for every $t$. Their monitored $F$ is $1/4+[(2-a)/8]^2$. For the cubic target this excess is $1/256$ per original launch. At the endpoint $a=2$, two free conditions already suffice within this family: $W=0$ confines each non-diagonal coin to $r=2$, hence $q\le1/2$; $L_1=1/2$ then forces $q=1/2$ almost surely with no diagonal mass. The necessity of an additional measurement for the other targets concerns the specified free terminal observations; other initial states, gates or measurements can reveal other information.

Now allow the coin to be redrawn independently *only after* the intermediate record. The same two-component ensemble produces all the same free probabilities, but its monitored $F=(\mathbb E q)^2=1/4$. The apparent $W,D$ both vanish despite the two unbalanced coins. The same false result occurs if the measurement physically resets the second-step coin to a fresh draw from this law. A double-flip frequency therefore tests balance only together with the premise that the intervention preserves the relevant coin setting. Also, a detector that returns both possible rails with the wrong gain or a process outside the unitary-plus-count family can evade the conclusion.

The [trapped-ion count tables](Trapped_ion_phase_witness.md) contain free output frequencies. Their nominal swap and preparation controls do not contain this intermediate projected outcome, and no measured $F$ for those pulse words is inferred here. The criterion gives an experiment for relaxing the balance premise. It still assumes unitary coherent components, the counting alternative, a persistent instrument and calibrated record probabilities.
