# Testing the probability rule with a sign and a return

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Send one walker from a known well. At three ticks, the two outer wells jointly carry exactly one quarter of the signed walk's squared norm, for any initial rail state. Change the sign on both wells and reverse the flight. The outgoing walker then misses the starting well with mass three quarters. A direct binary read and the return experiment thus give two records on the same carrier. There is a measurable relation between their frequencies which forces a probability of one quarter in this example. On a sufficiently rich menu, the same relation determines the entire ordinary binary probability curve, including among competing curves with jumps. The signed walk, its controls and the physical hypotheses on the detector enter explicitly below.

## A site record that a later sign can test

Let $V$ embed both input rails at the launch well, $U$ denote the forward flight, and $Q$ be the projector on all rails of a set of selected wells. Use a union of complete mirror shells in the real Hadamard walk on the line, or on a ring with no aliases over the forward light cone. Reflection gives $V^*U^*QUV=sI$ for every input coin, mixed or pure. The scalar $s$ is a norm computed from the signed propagation. The physical experiment is to determine how reported probabilities depend on that norm. [Shell identity and exact masks](Position_masks_for_an_unknown_input_coin.md)

Apply $D_Q=I-2Q$, then the matched inverse $U^*$. Accept **both** rails on return to the launch well. The two-by-two amplitude block there is $(1-2s)I$. The squared mass of the return is $(1-2s)^2$, and that of escape is
$$T(s)=1-(1-2s)^2=4s(1-s).$$
These are conditional kinematic identities, valid also with an untouched spectator. A coherent realization writes the membership bit into a clean flag, applies a sign to its marked value and unwrites it. As a control, replace the sign by the identity, keeping the rest of the sequence. The control returns every input to the launch well. A matched inverse, reliable site-selective sign and a real two-port exit read are apparatus requirements. Zähringer and colleagues reversed five trapped-ion steps by changing the displacement and coin pulses. Their motional packets had finite overlap. The discrete site-register identity here assumes orthogonal well labels. [Zähringer et al., reversibility experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.1876)

At two ticks choose only the central well. Its transfer block in $(R,L)$ convention is $\frac12\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, so its effect is $I/2$. An ideal sign switches return from certain to impossible. A device that has already turned position into persistent classical alternatives at the sign plane has zero sign contrast. Within each site the pulse acts by a scalar and leaves its conditional state unchanged, even if the device has reset the local coin or retained a record elsewhere. The branch frequencies can be arbitrary. This conclusion requires that the alternatives stay separated at the sign intervention, that all subsequent operations agree across settings except for the specified pulse, and that the readout cannot learn the setting through another route. Setting assignment must leave the incident populations comparable. A retained quantum record that can later be coherently erased, or a device that reprepares a superposition after its lookup and before the pulse, needs a different model. A destructive final detector requires a separate coherent routing control. A success in the reversible arm transfers to the destructive arm only with evidence about their common routing. A complementary six-tick intervention distinguishes the two kinds of record using only forward motion. Endpoint calibration is enough for this exact two-tick contrast. Its magnitude alone need not measure how much coherence survived. Attach normalized inaccessible records to the central and outer groups with real overlap $\gamma>0$, using the same record on both outer wells. The two return masses are $(1+\gamma)/2$ and $(1-\gamma)/2$. A complementary threshold read may turn those two masses into one and zero for arbitrarily small positive $\gamma$. Conversely a contrast observed on a common incident population bounds the fraction of perfectly sign-insensitive launches by one minus the absolute true rate contrast. ## A measurement relation that selects the curve

Let $p=G(s)$ be the rate when the mask feeds one binary port directly. Let $q=G(T(s))$ be the rate for escape after the sign and return. This uses a **common population response** in both arms. The masks and read conventions must be stable across the terminal configurations, elapsed times and source populations used for a comparison. Buffer a direct flag coherently to the common read time if available and check the buffer's action. A fair independent interchange of the physical ports followed by decoding gives $G(1-s)=1-G(s)$ for a mass-only, stable binary response. It cannot remove an apparatus dependence on the mask or the arm. Nondecreasing $G$ with this symmetry has $G(1/2)=1/2$ and preserves the two halves. Sharp endpoint responses may be calibrated separately.

The proposed test compares the measured $q$ with $T(p)$. One can estimate $p$ from complete original launches and transform its confidence interval. Alternatively twice the rate at which two independent direct binary readings at the same setting disagree equals $T(p)$. This is a scaled statistic. If two draws have the same marginal $p$ and covariance $c$, the doubled disagreement is $T(p)-4c$. Sharing a detector type between conditionally independent draws gives $c=\operatorname{Var}(g_A(s))$. Mask choice, physical pulse mode and population matching require controls at the level at which that pair is made. A comparison of the two marginal rates avoids a pair-independence premise. Use simultaneous confidence bounds if multiple settings are examined. For unresolved readings, the certain and possible hits both use the original incident denominator. Transform a $p$ interval through the full parabola, including its maximum when the interval crosses $1/2$.

One population can satisfy every identity below even though its types differ. For example, mix $g_±(s)=s±s(1-s)(2s-1)$ equally. Both curves are nondecreasing and complementary, with ordinary population mean at every common mass. At one quarter, their respective direct rates are $5/32$ and $11/32$. Twice the paired disagreement is $3/4$ under independent redraws of type and $183/256$ under a shared type with independent within-type trials. The quarter and full-menu conclusions apply to the specified population. A subsequent target whose mass varies with detector type needs another comparison.

## Why a small discrepancy bounds the ordinary law

Suppose $G:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ is nondecreasing and complementary. If the measured population rates support
$$|G(T(s))-T(G(s))|\le\delta$$
at every $s$, with $0\le\delta\le1$ (cap any looser bound at one), then $\sup_s|G(s)-s|\le\sqrt{\delta}$. In particular, exact agreement fixes the ordinary response pointwise. The test compares two observed rates through a known return transformation. It does not presume that either rate already equals its squared input mass.

To prove the bound, put $a=\arcsin\sqrt{s}$, $k(a)=\arcsin\sqrt{G(\sin^2a)}$ on $[0,\pi/2]$, and let $L(a)=2\min(a,\pi/2-a)$. Squaring the sine transforms $L$ into $T$. For $u,v\in[0,\pi/2]$ one has $\sin^2|u-v|\le|\sin^2u-\sin^2v|\le\sin|u-v|$, by factoring the middle term into $|\sin(u-v)|\sin(u+v)$. Thus the angular discrepancy $|k(L(a))-L(k(a))|$ is at most $e=\arcsin\sqrt{\delta}$. Since $a,k(a)$ lie on the same closed branch of $L$, $|L(k(a))-L(a)|=2|k(a)-a|$. With $D=\sup |k(a)-a|$, the triangle inequality gives $2D\le e+D$. Returning to $s$ gives the result. Suprema suffice throughout, including for discontinuous $G$. The classical conjugacy and earlier results on *continuous* functions commuting with the tent map provide mathematical context. [Plakhotnyk, self-semiconjugation of the tent map](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.09753)

For a finite measured menu including both endpoints, let $h$ be the largest gap in the ordered angular coordinates $\arcsin\sqrt{s_i}$. At the nodes the angular displacement is at most $(D+e)/2$. Between neighbors, monotonicity bounds displacement by the larger node displacement plus $h$. Hence
$$\sup_s |G(s)-s|\ \le\ \sin\!\left(\min\left\{\frac\pi2,\arcsin\sqrt{\delta}+2h\right\}\right).$$
An ordinary largest gap $d$ gives $h\le\arcsin\sqrt d$. This argument uses only node discrepancies and the mesh, without treating interpolation as observed data. It applies separately at each read clock with matched direct and echo populations. Across increasing clocks convergence follows if both the largest angular gap and the largest certified residual at each individual clock tend to zero. A response change across clocks prevents a backward transfer to an earlier experiment.

A forward-only version can bound the same discrepancies with one family of terminal shell reads. For each tested $s_i$, choose actual mask masses $l_i\le T(s_i)\le u_i$ at the same clock. The two endpoint readings bound $G(T(s_i))$. An upper bound on the maximum of their two distances from $T(G(s_i))$ suffices. Simple overlap of intervals can leave a large discrepancy unexcluded. At tick three the complement mask has mass $3/4$ exactly and can supply the quarter comparison. Forward bracketing relies on the calculated shell masses and monotone read. The sign intervention separately tests whether a persistent site lookup could explain the observed interference. If the two arms have independently programmable response curves, the equality can be engineered on those settings. The finite and uniform conclusions concern the measured binary population response on these preparations. ## A finite menu and its classical scope

At tick 256 the [menu checker](check_echo_probability_map.py) constructs 33 lower-half masks by requesting angles with rational tangent and rounding down using whole mirror shells. Complementation extends the menu. The midpoint is a virtual node fixed by the port symmetry and calibrated bright endpoint. Its discrepancy follows from $G(1/2)=1/2$ and $G(1)=1$. It was not routed at this clock. On the resulting 67 nodes the integer enclosures give $\sin h<0.038421637$. If the routed node discrepancies vanished exactly, the uniform curve error would be below **0.076844**, by $\sin(2h)\le2\sin h$. Actual data need their simultaneous error bounds. A discontinuous admissible curve in the [retained controls](results/echo_probability_map.json) matches every tested direct and echo value yet differs from the identity by more than 0.0108 at an untested mass. The [independent propagation](check_shell_sign_return.py) follows both incident columns through each forward step, sign and reverse step, including every escaped well. A classical trajectory generally requires multiple successive records. Suppose separately that each conditional branch, given any common record prefix through a finite sequence of $K$ binary reads, obeys the same calibrated error bound $b_j$ relative to the proposed squared-rule transition at that prefix. Take $0\le b_j\le1$, and require the bound at every prefix with positive probability under both laws. Couple the next two Bernoulli results using one common uniform number while their histories coincide. Their probability of differing at that read is at most $b_j$. The total variation distance of the two complete record laws is therefore bounded by $1-\prod_j(1-b_j)\le\sum_j b_j$. A sequence with a growing number of reads requires the accumulated bound to vanish for this argument. An isolated terminal calibration does not give that branchwise premise. Two fair records that are always equal have the same single-time marginals as two independent fair records and their joint laws differ by total variation $1/2$. The proposed experimental bridge to classical paths still needs those conditional measurements, preparation and disturbance control.
