# A fixed reciprocal sheet connection selects the terminal probability power on a ring

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Six wells, a two-state sheet and one fixed link suffice for a conditional probability test. A traveler crossing the link switches sheets in either direction. Launch a right-going walker on sheet zero, apply eleven uniform real Hadamard steps and read the well and sheet together. Two statistics of that single histogram distinguish ordinary probabilities from a mixture that redraws its power and its choice of rail resolution on each launch. The ordinary model sits at a positive distance from populations with a prescribed positive fraction of types a fixed distance from ordinary. The histogram has not been measured. [Earlier construction with two timed sheet swaps](Five_ticks_from_a_localized_sheet.md) · [Coined walks on cycles, Aharonov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0012090)

## The connection and the recorded coordinates

Label wells \(x=0,\ldots,5\) and sheets \(s=0,1\). Every tick applies \(H(r,l)=(r+l,r-l)/\sqrt2\), then shifts the two outputs one well right and left. On the undirected edge \(\{5,0\}\) replace \(s\) by \(1-s\) on **each** crossing. All other transports are the identity. Traversing an edge and immediately retracing it restores the sheet. Parallel transport once around the base exchanges the two sheets, so its monodromy is the nonidentity permutation of two elements. In the joint coordinate \(j=x+6s\bmod12\), the conditional shifts are exactly \(j\mapsto j\pm1\). This identifies the lift with a twelve-cycle and makes the shift reversible. The inverse tick undoes the shift and then applies \(H\).

A local relabeling of the sheet at one or more wells moves the apparent seam. On an open chain all edge swaps can be removed that way. On a cycle the product of the transports around the loop is invariant up to relabeling at its base, and here is a swap. The device must maintain coherent transport along the ring and identify which joint output its terminal labels denote relative to the preparation. A permanently recorded crossing would retain extra path information and change interference. This construction stipulates a carrier with this connection. Its double-cover geometry alone fixes no probability rule. [Earlier fixed-ring winding measurements](Fixed_ring_winding_weights.md)

Starting at \((0,0,R)\), the cover amplitudes are signed integers over \(\sqrt{2048}\) at tick eleven. All six even coordinates have zero amplitudes and zero words by parity. The entries below include all other coordinates.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | (1,0) | 12, 3 | 153 | 463 |

Removing the sheet swap gives a disconnected pair of six-cycles. Starting on sheet zero then leaves sheet one unoccupied, and the three occupied rail pairs become \((36,3),(3,3),(-7,26)\) at base sites 1, 3, 5. Measuring just the base site in the connected run instead pools the two sheets. For example the reported site 5 receives both the 680 row and the 81 row. These interventions change which histogram is available.

## Two statistics from one histogram

On each launch permit a finite \(q\ge0\) common to all its positive cells. At *each joint coordinate* a detector may pool its two rails before applying \(m\mapsto m^q\), or power each occupied rail separately and report their summed frequency. Normalize all resulting weights on that launch. At zero power every positive cell receives unit weight and dark cells remain dark. Also allow selection of a maximal cell (the infinite-power limit) or fair counting of the 2,048 direction words. Different detector types may be mixed in any proportions. All six reachable outcomes, including the unscored ones, enter each normalization.

Put \(a=81,b=225,c=576\), \(\lambda=\log(c/b)/\log(c/a)\), so \(a^\lambda c^{1-\lambda}=b\). From the probabilities \(P_j\) of the terminal joint outcomes compute
\[
D=\lambda P_{11}/a-P_3/b+(1-\lambda)P_7/c,\qquad H_5=P_5.
\]
Every scored coordinate has just one occupied rail. At finite power the numerator of \(D\), after multiplication by the positive normalizer, is
\[
\lambda a^{q-1}+(1-\lambda)c^{q-1}-b^{q-1}.
\]
Weighted arithmetic–geometric mean gives nonnegativity, with equality exactly at \(q=1\). For fair counting \(2048D=\lambda(463/81)+(1-\lambda)(220/576)-341/225>0\). Indeed \(\lambda>1/3\) follows from \(c^2a>b^3\), and \(463/(3\cdot81)>341/225\).

The unique largest cell is at \(j=5\) in every one of the eight distinct partitions of occupied rails. Its mass is either \(680\) or \(676\), and every other location has cells of mass at most \(576\). Consequently at infinite power \(D=0\) and \(H_5=1\). At the ordinary power \(D=0\) and \(H_5=680/2048=85/256\). If the population mean of \(D\) is zero, positivity confines its types almost surely to ordinary or infinite power. The second observed probability equals \(85/256\) only when the infinite-power fraction also vanishes. Each equality is needed for this argument. For instance, a population consisting entirely of maximal-cell reads passes the first equality.

## Which terminal bit must the detector report?

The six-outcome instrument can report the base well and a single bit computed from the terminal rail and sheet. Let the four mode labels be \(0R,0L,1R,1L\). A fixed Boolean mask marks a subset of these modes at **every** well. Within each reported (well, bit) bin permit any partition of its occupied modes before applying the common power, followed by one global normalization. Squared masses add inside a group. Keep every incident outcome in the histogram. Under these conventions four of the seven nonconstant masks, up to bit complement, distinguish the ordinary power even in the presence of fair direction counting.

| Modes marked by the bit | Mass of each pure reported bin, in well order 1, 3, 5 | Counts in those same bins |
|---|---|---|
| \(0R\) | 144, 225, 4 | 252, 211, 130 |
| \(0R,0L\) (sheet) | 576, 225, 81 | 220, 341, 463 |
| \(1R\) | 576, 324, 81 | 90, 130, 211 |
| \(0L,1R\) (exclusive or) | 144, 324, 4 | 382, 260, 382 |

Each pure bin has one positive squared mode. Its unsigned count includes **all** paths ending in the bin, even those ending in a mode of zero signed amplitude. For any row, sort the three masses as \(a<b<c\) and use the preceding weighted arithmetic�geometric mean statistic with their reported bin probabilities. It is strictly positive at every finite \(q\ne1\). For the first row, \(\lambda=\log(225/144)/\log(225/4)>1/16\) because \((225/144)^{16}>225/4\). Its count score times 2,048 therefore exceeds \(130/(4\cdot16)-252/144>0\). The [checker](check_reciprocal_ring_score.py) uses rational lower bounds on \(\lambda\) and verifies positivity in all four rows. At infinite power the middle bin cannot maximize because the larger pure mass is present. The score is nonnegative and the smallest bin has probability zero under every maximal-cell tie rule. Once the mean score vanishes, the ordinary probability \(a/2048\) in this smallest bin excludes every remaining infinite-power contribution.

The other masks, represented by \(0L\), \(0R,1R\) (outgoing rail), and \(0R,0L,1R\), produce at most two distinct pure masses. The same obstruction applies when a terminal bit is read at only one or two of the three occupied wells, even if each addressed well can choose its own local Boolean mask. Each occupied well has three live modes, so its division into two bins gives at most one pure bin. The resulting ordinary histogram can be reproduced exactly by a mixture of nearby nonordinary powers and different hidden partitions. To see the obstruction, center any supporting linear statistic at its ordinary expectation. Its derivative at \(q=1\) must vanish for every partition since exponents on both sides are allowed. Splitting two positive modes inside one bin changes this derivative by a nonzero entropy term times that bin�s coefficient. Thus every mixed bin has coefficient zero. The sums of coefficients at each distinct pure mass \(m\) obey both the zero-mean and zero-derivative equations, weighted respectively by \(m\) and \(m\log m\). With at most two masses both sums must vanish separately, so every such supporting statistic vanishes identically on the power family. The ordinary histogram is consequently in the relative interior of the convex hull of the nearby nonordinary laws. This argument allows arbitrary partitions inside each reported bin on each launch.

Computing the bit once at the terminal read is sufficient. For instance a clean flag \(b\) can be updated reversibly by \(b\mapsto b\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}[s=0\ \mathrm{and}\ r=R]\) while retaining the walk modes. This requires a calibrated terminal rail-and-sheet predicate and coincidence with the base-site record. An existing direct sheet read has the second row and needs no rail-controlled predicate. A flag-only histogram discards the coincidence. In the single-mode example the ordinary marked probability is \(373/2048\); allowing unrestricted hidden partitions in the *two flag bins* leaves both bins mixed, so this scalar does not identify the power. Copying and retaining a rail flag at each intermediate tick instead would distinguish complete direction words. The ordinary terminal masses for the single-mode flag in the three marked bins would then be the unsigned counts \((252,211,130)/2048\) in place of \((144,225,4)/2048\). The resource in this calculation is a nondisturbing terminal label of the stipulated coherent walk. Physical efficiencies, register gates and operating cost have not been measured.

## Finite deviations and scope

Fix \(0<\delta<\infty\). Give the powers the compact topology of \([0,\infty]\) under \(q\mapsto q/(1+q)\), include the eight possible partitions, and add fair counting as an isolated type. Both \(D\) and \(H_5\) are continuous on this compact space, including at infinity by the unique maximum. Call the closed set \(\{|q-1|\ge\delta\}\), including infinity, together with fair counting, \(B_\delta\). For each \(0<\eta\le1\), the probability measures on types with mass at least \(\eta\) on \(B_\delta\) form a compact set: under weak convergence the mass assigned to a closed set can increase at the limit. The image under the two expectations is compact. It excludes \((0,85/256)\) by the preceding argument. Its Euclidean distance from that pair is therefore positive. For a sequence of matched populations whose two statistics approach their ordinary values, the fraction of fair counts and powers outside each fixed \(\delta\)-neighborhood tends to zero. This compactness argument supplies no numerical sample budget. Arbitrarily small deviations from \(q=1\) still require high precision.

For an explicit diagnostic of the high-power end, there are at most seven occupied cells outside \(j=5\). Hence \(H_5(q)\ge[1+7(576/676)^q]^{-1}\), which is above 0.649 for \(q\ge16\). A population can mix high and low values of \(H_5\). The strict lower bounds on the intermediate powers come from the first statistic. To interpret either statistic in measured data requires a calibrated joint-site-and-sheet instrument, matched preparation, outcome efficiencies or independently bounded losses, and sampling uncertainty on the incident population. Applying an arbitrary per-coordinate exponent, keeping a which-crossing record, or changing the normalization after a loss exceeds the tested family. The finite ring provides a relative transport and a terminal weight test. It supplies no absolute cyclotomic embedding, continuum force, or emergence of the stipulated Hadamard dynamics.

## What a base-site instrument can see on an odd ring

For odd circumference \(L\), a sharp start and a fixed tick, every word reaching a given base site \(x\) ends on the same sheet. This follows from the [odd-ring parity correspondence](Fixed_ring_winding_weights.md#finite-identification). In the reciprocal lift \(j=x+Ls\bmod 2L\), a step changes the parity \(p\) of \(j\). At a fixed parity the only possible sheet over \(x\) is \(s_p(x)=p\mathbin{\oplus}(x\bmod2)\). Write \(V_p|x,c\rangle=|x,s_p(x),c\rangle\), with \(c\) the rail. Projection onto \(x\) identifies either parity sector with the entire bare \(L\)-ring. Its ordinary tick \(U\) and the connected tick \(\widetilde U\) obey \(\widetilde U V_p=V_{1-p}U\). For a sharp site and sheet this gives the same signed amplitudes in the decoded ports and the same word counts as the bare ring, at every tick. Pooling the sheet at an occupied site leaves even the sitewise split-or-pooled rail powers in this paper unchanged, provided the same partition and exponent are used in the comparison.

**Time-stamped records.** Under ordinary probabilities, insert any sheet-blind position or rail projector between ticks, including a coarse position mask or any local rail instrument with the same Kraus operators on the two sheets. Such an operator \(K\) obeys \(\widetilde K V_p=V_p K\). For a specified sequence of outcomes, each compatible product of these operators and ticks obeys the corresponding identity with the parity of its tick count. Tracing the squared branch norm therefore gives the same joint record probabilities on the two rings. This also holds for classically chosen local coins and future settings based on earlier records. A common hold on all ports preserves parity. A classical stopping time has the same law by equality on every finite record prefix; the probability of never stopping also agrees. A classical uncertainty about the start site or the number of elapsed ticks preserves equality when the preparations and clock law match, although it can prevent reconstruction of a unique sheet from the final \(x\). A sheet-sensitive controller, a coherent sum of different stopping times, or a control whose branches execute different lift parities does not follow this argument.

For an arbitrary initial density operator \(\rho\), the connected ring with base-only site/rail records acts like the bare ring started from \(\rho_{\rm conn}=\sum_p V_p^\dagger\rho V_p\). Cross-sector final amplitudes occupy opposite sheets at a given time and contribute zero to every declared record probability. The identity seam with the **same physical input** instead acts like the bare ring started from \(\rho_{\rm id}=\sum_s\langle s|\rho|s\rangle\). Equality of these two effective inputs is sufficient for indistinguishability by this instrument family. In particular any sharp base site qualifies, even with a mixed or coherently correlated sheet and rail. For an input on one fixed sheet the connected reduction removes the coherence between opposite base-site parities and preserves same-parity coherence. When the input uses several sheets, it is the two reductions that must be compared.

**An interference test.** On three wells prepare \((|0,0,R\rangle+|1,0,R\rangle)/\sqrt2\) in coordinates \((x,s,c)\), transport for two ordinary ticks and read only \(x\). In site order \(0,1,2\), the identity seam gives \((5,1,2)/8\), and the reciprocal seam gives \((3,3,2)/8\). Reversing the source sign changes the identity result to \((1,5,2)/8\) and leaves the reciprocal result unchanged. Preparing the second term on sheet one instead exchanges the two histograms. Thus the base detector measures an interference change without reading the crossing or the final sheet. Preparing the coherent two-site input and its relative phase is an additional control.

A sharp launch can test the same obstruction with a controlled hold. Prepare a fair coherent clock flag in product with \(|0,0,R\rangle\). Apply three ticks in one branch and two ticks plus a phase-matched hold in the other, read the flag in the plus/minus basis and retain **both** outcomes jointly with \(x\). At flag \(+\), the three joint probabilities have numerators \((6+4\sqrt2,7-2\sqrt2,3-2\sqrt2)\) over 32 for the identity seam. For the reciprocal seam they are \((6,7,3)/32\). At flag \(−\) the terms in \(\sqrt2\) reverse sign. Ignoring the flag gives identical site distributions in this example. The comparison requires phase coherence across the two flight durations. On the even four-ring no such control is needed: a sharp \(R\) launch and three compulsory ticks already give site masses \((0,8,0,0)/8\) for the identity seam and \((0,6,0,2)/8\) for the reciprocal seam.

The [control replay](check_odd_ring_visibility.py) compares signed words with physical-seam propagation for each interference example. It also tests 1,008 comparisons of sharp launches and record schedules on four odd circumferences, including coarse rail projectors and persistent site records. The [complete tables](results/odd_ring_visibility.json) retain the reversed phase, exchanged source sheet, both clock outcomes and even-ring control. These are instrument predictions under ordinary probabilities. They identify circumstances in which a base-only record can distinguish the two stipulated transports.

## Reproduction

The [checker](check_reciprocal_ring_score.py) enumerates all 2,048 signed and unsigned words with the reciprocal link. It independently propagates on the cover, checks all eight partitions including the large-power limit, and runs the identity-connection control. Among \(3\le L\le t\le11\) only \((L,t)=(6,11)\) gives three distinct positive masses at coordinates with exactly one occupied rail. This finite scan concerns that particular sufficient construction. A smaller four-well example at tick 9 already gives a zero-amplitude coordinate reached by 136 unsigned words out of 512, which by itself distinguishes path counting from all positive-cell power rules. The [Lean 4.19.0 replay](lean/ReciprocalRingScore.lean) checks all twelve joint rail pairs directly on the physical seam and the integer inequalities for counting. Every trial, including the negative ones, is in the [record](results/reciprocal_ring_score.json). Run `python -B Director_G/check_reciprocal_ring_score.py fresh.json`.
