# An occupation walk with a hard-point quantum and classical limit

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist

Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada

Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Consider a line of binary occupation modes. A rule on adjacent modes determines a scalar two-particle Schrödinger equation with a hard wall at coincidence. Under narrower preparation on the same walk the ordered positions converge to the elastic collision of equal-mass classical hard points. We derive both limits for the determinant choice of the phase applied when two neighboring modes are occupied. The continuum behavior of a different choice remains open. Two finite interference experiments distinguish that phase from another reversible choice with the same one-particle dynamics, exclusion and causal neighborhood. This gives a model for a short-range interaction observable with no round trip to a distant source. The earlier periodic-force construction used a different carrier and a signal tour. ## The microscopic choice

Cell \(j\in\mathbb Z\) contains scalar modes \(2j,2j+1\). First operate on all the within-cell pairs, then on the disjoint pairs \((2j+1,2j+2)\). On the one-occupied subspace the gates are \(s_\alpha=\cos\alpha\,I-i\sin\alpha\,X\) and \(s_{-\beta}=\cos\beta\,I+i\sin\beta\,X\), with \(X\) the swap matrix. Fix the vacuum amplitude at one. When both modes of an addressed pair are occupied, the local rule applies a scalar \(g\). The model below takes \(g=\det s=1\). This specifies a reversible local gate of dimension four, conserves the number of occupied modes and permits one particle at each of two different sites of the same cell. The complex matrices are a convenient representation. For this occupation apparatus both bond gates have a common real form, as shown below. The pair gates, their timing and squared occupation readout are supplied. A separate collision circuit can build a phase reference by contact, with an explicit timing cost. Compiling and timing a whole array of such gates from the original signed walk is an open implementation problem. [Meeting, phase comparison and readout](Moving_contact_and_phase_echo.md)

Let \(U\) be the one-cycle walk on the single-mode amplitudes. For two orbitals \(f,h\) define \(w(x,y)=f(x)h(y)-h(x)f(y)\) at scalar sites \(x<y\). For a neighboring two-mode gate the transformation of these minors is precisely its occupation action. A spectator on either side keeps its ordering. When both sites lie on the addressed bond the minor picks up the determinant. Unaffected minors retain their amplitudes. Composing the local identities gives the two-particle evolution \(\bigwedge^2 U\) by linearity on the finite-support basis and density on the line. The determinant formula extends to larger fixed finite particle numbers by the same adjacent-row argument. On a ring, sorting a hop through the cut moves it past \(N_p-1\) other particles, so periodic occupation gates use a one-body boundary twist \((-1)^{N_p-1}\) in the determinant representation. Omitting the twist already changes a two-particle position record on a small ring. **An identifiable contact phase.** Choose \((\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)=(3/5,4/5)\) and \((\cos\beta,\sin\beta)=(4/5,3/5)\). The alternative \(g=-1\) at each addressed doubly occupied bond preserves all one-body records. Start three open modes in the occupied pattern \(\{0,2\}\), operate on bonds \(01,12,01\) and read the same pattern. Two intermediate histories contribute \(36/125\) and \(-80/125\) for \(g=1\). Changing \(g\) changes the latter sign. Return probabilities are \(1936/15625\) and \(13456/15625\). An intermediate full occupation read gives \(7696/15625\) for both. No localized launch distinguishes them in fewer than three layers by a terminal occupation read: changing a first-layer phase on a doubly occupied source bond multiplies its whole branch by a common sign, and the last-layer sign is diagonal on the measured output. The same reasoning for the last layer alone bounds arbitrary coherent launches; \((|01\rangle+|12\rangle)/\sqrt2\) distinguishes the signs after two layers. Here \(|ij\rangle\) lists the two occupied sites.

On four modes the Plücker expression \(w_{01}w_{23}-w_{02}w_{13}+w_{03}w_{12}\) vanishes for a single antisymmetrized product. Two layers from \(|02\rangle\) give zero for the determinant lift and \(288/625\) for the alternate rule. This certifies a genuine departure from single-product evolution before a sharp position port from a localized source can distinguish the rules. ## A scalar quantum limit on the ordered line

Let \(a\) be the pitch of a *two-mode cell*, \(\tau\) the duration of both layers, and \(\varepsilon\) the action scale. With Fourier angle \(k=ap/\varepsilon\), the one-body matrix and its positive quasienergy branch obey

\[
 U(k)=(\cos\beta I+i\sin\beta(\cos k\,X+\sin k\,Y))s_\alpha,\qquad
 \cos\omega(k)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta\cos k .
\]

Choose the eigenvalue \(e^{-i\omega(k)}\) near \(k=0\). With the rational settings, \(\gamma=\alpha-\beta>0\), \(\cos\gamma=24/25,\ \sin\gamma=7/25\). The isolated branch has \(\omega(k)=\gamma+\kappa k^2/2+O(k^4)\), \(\kappa=12/7\). Put \(\tau=m\kappa a^2/\varepsilon\) with \(m>0\). Use the Fourier isometry sending a scalar band-limited wave to its Bloch eigenvector in this branch. At \(t_n=n\tau\le T\), after multiplying by \(e^{in\gamma}\), the one-body norm error against the free Schrödinger propagator is bounded by \(C T a^2P^4/(m\varepsilon^3)\) on \(|p|\le P\), for \(aP/\varepsilon\) sufficiently small. This follows from \(|e^{iu}-e^{iv}|\le|u-v|\) and the uniform fourth-order Taylor remainder on that compact chart. Replacing the varying eigenvector by the fixed \((1,1)/\sqrt2\) costs \(O(aP/\varepsilon)\) in norm. Finite-energy tails can instead be cut in norm before the estimate. Comparing to a time between ticks costs an additional one-body phase error at most \(\tau P^2/(2m\varepsilon)\). [Dispersion derivations for extended walk packets](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/12/12/123022)

The two-body propagator inherits at most twice the one-body norm error on a tensor momentum cutoff, followed by projection to the antisymmetric space, and its rest phase is \(e^{2in\gamma}\). Restrict the limiting antisymmetric scalar wave to \(x<y\) and multiply by \(\sqrt2\). Odd extension across \(x=y\) is the inverse isometry. Reflection symmetry of the free Laplacian intertwines its evolution with the Dirichlet Laplacian on the wedge, by odd reflection on the relative coordinate. Thus the quantum Hamiltonian in the ordered coordinates is

\[
 -\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2m}(\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2),\qquad x<y,\quad \psi(x,x)=0 .
\]

The Dirichlet condition is the zero trace on the operator or form domain, with odd extension defining the propagator on all square-integrable data. It refers to the scalar continuum envelope in this band. At finite lattice spacing both sublattices of a cell may be occupied and its measured double-occupancy probability can be positive. Free spinless fermions and impenetrable point bosons have the same ordered-position probabilities in the continuum correspondence. Interpreting these data as a measurement of particle statistics would need additional observables. [Girardeau's one-dimensional correspondence](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1703687)

## Collision of two classical rays

Prepare two normalized scalar Gaussians of position and momentum widths \(O(\sqrt\varepsilon)\), initially centered at distinct \(q_1<q_2\) and momenta \(p_1>p_2\). Encode their normalized antisymmetric product in the positive band. The one-body overlap tends to zero exponentially for distinct fixed phase-space centers. Free Schrödinger propagation on \(0\le t\le T\) gives centers \(Q_j(t)=q_j+p_jt/m\) and position widths \(O_T(\sqrt\varepsilon)\). For the ordered pair measure, put \(r=\operatorname{sort}(Q_1,Q_2)\). Squared distance from \(\operatorname{sort}(x,y)\) to \(r\) is bounded by the labelled cost for either matching of \(x,y\) to the two centers. Bounding the squared determinant by twice the sum of its two product intensities then bounds the ordered mean squared distance by \(O_T(\varepsilon)/(1-|\langle f,h\rangle|^2)\). This estimate includes the time the rays coincide. A vanishing trace on the diagonal for every smooth Dirichlet state is compatible with weak convergence to a point on the boundary at collision time.

For fixed \(P>\max |p_j|\), the Gaussian cutoff has vanishing tail; choose \(a=o(\varepsilon^{3/2})\) in fixed units. The quantum comparison transfers the weak sorted position law for bounded continuous position reads. To compare position reads, identify a cell sample with its piecewise constant interpolation. On the cutoff the lattice Fourier isometry samples the continuous scalar wave on the Nyquist grid with factor \(\sqrt a\). Integrating the fundamental-theorem estimate on each cell bounds the interpolation error by \(a\,\|\partial_x\psi\|_2\le aP/\varepsilon\); on a tensor cutoff one applies it to each coordinate. Freezing the spinor as above and moving a sublattice site to the cell origin then transfers bounded continuous reads, using tightness for observables continuous only on compact sets. The finite ring data check the formula with their boundary twist; the analytic argument is on the line. The continuum rays in the ordered chamber exchange velocities at collision, the elastic equal-mass hard-point solution. Their unordered positions also agree with those of two freely passing point particles. The microscopic phase test above measures information that this classical trajectory does not retain. The scalar Dirichlet calculation uses the corresponding two-orbital source. The real preparation below suffices for the classical positions. ## Real gates and independently prepared sources

Put \(D_j=1\) on even scalar modes, \(D_j=i\) on odd modes. On an occupied configuration let \(D\) multiply by the product of its occupied mode phases. In real coordinates \(D^\dagger s_\alpha D\) on an even–odd bond is \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\alpha&\sin\alpha\\-\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). On an odd–even bond \(D^\dagger s_{-\beta}D\) has the same real form at angle \(\beta\). The vacuum and both choices of the neighboring double-occupation sign stay real. This change of coordinates is fixed for all times. It commutes with every occupation projector. A localized occupied configuration changes by a global phase, so the three-layer return discriminator requires only real local gates and local source/readout coordinates. The same argument applies with a time-dependent real hopping angle.

For the fixed rational settings a local signed implementation has an explicit price. Write the one-occupied rotation as (Z(aZ+bX)/5), with ((a,b)=(3,4)) or ((4,3)). A Walsh selector of size eight applies (|a|) copies of signed (Z), (|b|) copies of signed (X), and moves the unused label into orthogonal junk. On the empty and doubly occupied subspaces use five signed identities, with the specified contact sign; the final (Z) acts only on the single-occupation block. Undo the Walsh preparation and read the common success flag. The success operator on the entire four-dimensional bond space is (5/8) times the desired gate. It acts correctly on entangled inputs. Coarse failure, the actual inverse and a forward retry give per-gate timeout (f_l=(1-p)[1-2p(1-p)]^l) at (1+2l) calls, (p=25/64). For a finite schedule of (G) gates with clean flags, the probability that every gate succeeds within its deadline is ((1-f_l)^G), since each success effect is scalar even conditioned on earlier successes. Failed schedules retain their timeout records. One can choose (l=O(log(G/eta))) to keep their total weight below (eta). The schedule pads early successes, needs spatially local ancillas for each addressed bond, and adds physical substeps. Treating its effective gates as instantaneous when taking a fixed-speed continuum limit would omit that cost. The band-limited simulation and the clock bound below refer to the stipulated local-rotation model. More generally, write the transformed complex preparation as \(v=x+i y\), \(x,y\) real. Each specified transcript of real gates and occupation reads has a real branch operator \(K_b\). Its probability is \(\|K_bx\|^2+\|K_by\|^2\). A source choosing the normalized real \(x\) or \(y\) with the corresponding squared weights reproduces the complete law, including feedback based on the record. This also holds for the two-particle state. Preparing real and imaginary parts of a *global* determinant need not amount to preparing two orbitals independently, and a new analyzer with complex entries in this real gauge would require separate analysis.

There is a cheaper source in its phase requirements for the sorted **classical** law. In the real frame let \(v\) be an orbital supported in the positive quasienergy band near \(k=0\). Its coordinate conjugate occupies the opposite band at \(-k\); the gap makes their inner product zero. The real orbital \(r=\sqrt2\,\operatorname{Re}v\) is normalized and propagates by the same real circuit. At every set of microscopic sites its intensity is bounded by twice that of \(v\). Consequently it follows the same limiting position ray on every fixed finite time interval. This argument includes the rest phase at each tick; taking the real part after an arbitrary time-dependent rephasing would change the microscopic preparation. Either real quadrature works. For two initially separated packets of this kind, separately prepare \(r_1,r_2\) and use their normalized exterior product. Their overlap tends to zero by initial position concentration and stays constant. The preceding sorted-distance or neighborhood bound therefore applies to this source too. In the ideal model disjointly supported real orbital approximations can be prepared starting on disjoint intervals: a sequence of adjacent real two-mode rotations builds each prescribed normalized vector, splitting off one desired component at each step. Truncating localized tails incurs their norm error. These are addressable source rotations with a cost proportional to interval length on each chain. Uniform circuit timing and synthesis from the original fixed controls are further physical assumptions.

The exact quantum records can distinguish the global source from independent real quadratures. For example, in a frozen band spinor use two cells, scalar orbitals \(f=(1,1)/\sqrt2,\ h=(i,-i)/\sqrt2\), and the fixed spinor \(D^\dagger(1,1)/\sqrt2=(1,-i)/\sqrt2\). The complex Slater state has one occupation in each cell with certainty. The global real quadrature has the same cell law in this example. With the two independently real orbitals \(r(f)=(\operatorname{Re}f,\operatorname{Im}f)\), \(r(h)=(\operatorname{Re}h,\operatorname{Im}h)\) per cell, the probability of different cells is \(1/2\); each cell has double occupancy with probability \(1/4\). All three experiments prohibit double occupation of a single microscopic mode. In the moving-packet example at \(\varepsilon=1/8, a=1/128\), the near-collision total variation between independent and globally coherent real sources was 0.08014 on the 64-cell central read with two tails, compared with 0.01546 in width-1/2 bins. For \(\varepsilon=1/32, a=\varepsilon^2/2\), both sources put about 0.99072 in the central unit-width collision window. These contrast different quantum records with the same limiting classical location. This finite source test and the packet data use cell positions; sharper sublattice reads can distinguish individual global quadratures. ## Keeping a finite propagation speed

At fixed angles the preceding Schrödinger scaling has \(a/\tau=\varepsilon/(m\kappa a)\), which diverges in the simultaneous limit. The same *local occupation rule* also has a finite-speed refinement. This requires retuning the intercell angle. Fix \(\sin\alpha=s=4/5,\ \cos\alpha=d=3/5\), speed \(c>0\), and rest energy \(\mu=mc^2>0\). Put \(\tau=s a/c,\ u=\mu\tau/\varepsilon,\ \beta=\alpha-\gamma\), with \(|\gamma-u|=O(u^2)\). Rational local rotations suffice: round \(\tan(u/2)\) to \(t\) on a grid of pitch at most \(u^2\) and use \((\cos\gamma,\sin\gamma)=((1-t^2)/(1+t^2),2t/(1+t^2))\). The new rational angles can require growing gate resources. This physical clock assumes that each stipulated local rotation fits in one half-cycle. For the two bond layers, single-hop range is \(a/2\) per \(\tau/2\), giving a strict microscopic speed bound \(a/\tau=c/s\).

Set \(A=sZ-dY\). Multiplying out the two gates at \(k=\gamma=0\) gives \(U(0)=e^{-i\gamma X}\) and \(\partial_k U|_{0,0}=-isA\). Here \(A^2=X^2=I,\ AX+XA=0\). Taylor's theorem in \((k,\gamma)\), with \(k=ap/\varepsilon\), therefore bounds the per-cycle norm difference from

\[
 \exp[-i(\tau/\varepsilon)(\mu X+cpA)]
\]

by \(C_P(\tau/\varepsilon)^2\) on \(|p|\le P\) at fixed \(m,c\). Unitarity gives accumulated error \(C_P T\tau/\varepsilon^2\), and at most twice that on the two-particle wedge. At fixed action this gives a spinor quantum limit. Taking \(\tau=o(\varepsilon^2)\) also permits the semiclassical limit for fixed \(P\). The spinor at positive energy \(E(p)=\sqrt{\mu^2+c^2p^2}\) depends on \(p\). In that band the center of a narrow packet follows \(q+t c^2p/E(p)\): in momentum representation the Heisenberg position on a phase-multiplied packet gains \(tE'(p)\); bounded \(E''\) and a smooth eigenvector control its width and the \(O(\varepsilon)\) connection term. Repeating the sorted-distance argument gives two ordered relativistic rays. Their velocities approach \(p/m\) with error bounded by \(|p|^3/(2m^3c^2)\). At finite \(c\), the Dirac spinor can be antisymmetric in its internal components at equal positions. The scalar Dirichlet conclusion above applies to the scalar envelope limit.

A scalar nonrelativistic quantum approximation from this spinor branch is also controlled after removing the rest phase: on \(|p|\le P\) the energy error from \(p^2/(2m)\) is at most \(P^4/(8m^3c^2)\). The associated one-body phase error is at most \(T P^4/(8m^3c^2\varepsilon)\), and replacing its eigenvector by the rest spinor costs \(O(P/(mc))\). At fixed \(c\) these estimates describe a momentum and duration regime. A uniform scalar limit over fixed momenta as \(\varepsilon\) shrinks needs its own hierarchy. In units \(c=\mu=1\), at \(p=0.75,\ \varepsilon=0.25\), five fixed-action refinements gave full-spinor errors decreasing from \(0.06014\) to \(0.00343\); the band velocities approached \(0.6\). Reversing the mass tuning leaves a comparable classical speed while changing the full quantum operator.
