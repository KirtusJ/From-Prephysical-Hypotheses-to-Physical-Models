# Testing a spatial bin with two cubic phase switches

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Two switchable phase increments suffice for a finite test of one position probability after a walk. One switch addresses both rails at the chosen position. The other addresses a single starting rail at its launch well. On an eight-well ring, every subsequent move uses the original forward balanced tick. A comparison at tick 27 distinguishes the usual probability from an alternative that passes every isolated rail calibration. The comparison concerns one prepared flight, and its predicted frequencies have yet to be measured. ## An exact mixture comparison

Let $\psi=U|0\rangle$ be a normalized flight, $P$ its chosen bin and $p=\|P\psi\|^2\in(0,1)$. In the ordinary complex-coordinate kinematics write $g=P\psi/\sqrt p$ and $b=(I-P)\psi/\sqrt{1-p}$. Each can have many nonzero coordinates. Supply a cubic phase switch $D=I+(\omega-1)P$ and a switch $C=I+(\omega-1)|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$, with $\omega^3=1$, $\omega\ne1$. A complete flight inverse and a selective phase at the sharp source implement $C$ by conjugation. The ring construction below implements the same operator by forward evolution. A source mask that also phases orthogonal states generally changes $C$.

Words in the switches preserve the two-dimensional packet space. For a prepared word $v=a g+c b$, let $q=|a|^2$. The uniform ensemble of $v,Dv,D^2v$ has matrix $qgg^\dagger+(1-q)bb^\dagger$, since $1+\omega+\omega^2=0$. Prepare two words of populations $l<p<u$. Define $\lambda=(p-l)/(u-l)$. The twirled query and the mixture of twirled high and low words with weights $\lambda,1-\lambda$ have exactly equal kinematic matrices, even when neither endpoint packet is reachable. Comparison weights use only the specified path amplitudes. A detector can distinguish two ensembles with the same matrix until operational equality is checked.

At one matched clock let $F_0$ be the bare query's bin-event rate and $\bar F,\bar F_h,\bar F_l$ be the three twirled rates. Every launched walker contributes to the denominator, including no-report events. Check $|F_0-\bar F|\le e_q$, $|\bar F-\lambda\bar F_h-(1-\lambda)\bar F_l|\le e_m$, and endpoint rates $\bar F_h\ge1-\eta_h$, $\bar F_l\le\eta_l$. Binary probabilities and $p=\lambda u+(1-\lambda)l$ give
$$
\lambda(1-\eta_h)-p-e_m-e_q\ \le\ F_0-p\ \le\
\lambda+(1-\lambda)\eta_l-p+e_m+e_q.
$$
Equivalently, direct simultaneous calibrated bounds $|\bar F_h-u|\le d_h$, $|\bar F_l-l|\le d_l$ give $|F_0-p|\le e_q+e_m+\lambda d_h+(1-\lambda)d_l$. Small kinematic leakage alone supplies neither measured endpoint inequality for an arbitrary detector. The query-to-twirl term is needed even when the mixture equality holds. For example, changing just the three query rates by $t,-t/2,-t/2$ preserves their mean, whenever all rates stay in $[0,1]$ and those three settings are distinct from the references. Measured rate comparisons need simultaneous uncertainty and a stable random assignment or controlled stratification. A weight error of magnitude $\epsilon$ changes a mixture of two binary rates by at most $\epsilon$.

The [Lean check](lean/CubicFlightMixture.lean) verifies the two-sided bound in common integer rate units and the exact short-panel exposure identity. The kinematic and apparatus requirements are separate.

## Which populations can two cubic switches reach?

In the packet space each switch acts projectively as a rotation by $2\pi/3$. The rotation axes of $C$ and $D$ have dot product $2p-1$ and are distinct as unoriented lines for $0<p<1$. The determinant-one matrix $CD^{-1}$ has trace $3p-1$. This follows by expanding the product of the two rank-one phase changes in the packet space and using $\operatorname{tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|P)=p$. At finite projective order its eigenvalues are roots of unity (a power is $\pm I$), so their sum is an algebraic integer. A rational algebraic integer is an integer. For rational $p$ strictly between zero and one, the only interior exceptions are therefore $1/3,2/3$.

For every other rational $p$ the generated rotation group is infinite. Its closure is compact. By the closed subgroup theorem, an infinite closed subgroup of $SO(3)$ has positive-dimensional identity component. A proper connected subgroup fixes an axis: its Lie algebra has dimension one since the cross product prevents any two-plane from being a Lie subalgebra. The entire closed subgroup then preserves that unoriented axis by normality. Every element reversing the axis has order two, as its restriction to the orthogonal plane is a reflection. Both cubic generators would have to rotate about the same axis, contradicting their distinct axes. Thus the closure is all of $SO(3)$. In particular a finite word search eventually reaches arbitrarily small and arbitrarily large bin populations, simultaneously for the requested pair of tolerances. Inverses of the two switches are their squares. This is an existence result with no uniform word-length bound. All nontrivial dyadic bin populations of finite unforced signed Hadamard flights qualify. [Compact subgroup arguments for gate universality, Bouland and Aaronson](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2013/147/revision/3/download/)

At $p=1/3$ the two axes are tetrahedron vertex directions (dot product $-1/3$). Their rotations preserve the four vertices, starting from the source vertex. Rotation around the bin axis reaches the three non-bin vertices. Rotation around the source axis from one of the other two non-bin vertices reaches the bin vertex. Hence the only reached bin populations are $1/3,1$. At $p=2/3$ take the opposite bin axis as the second vertex, giving populations $0,2/3$. The two switches alone cannot strictly bracket the query in either exception. At $p=0,1$ the query already has definite kinematic bin membership. A compulsory repeated pair also fails in general. With the **same** cubic phase on the bin and the source ray of the current preparation, one pair leaves unwanted squared norm $(1-p)(1-3p)^2$. It leaves this norm unchanged at $p=2/3$ and increases it at $9/10$. Grover's cubing of the unwanted probability uses a **sixth-root** phase $\pi/3$. [Grover's fixed-point search](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.95.150501)

## Forward ticks and original-launch counts

Use the eight-well ring with two rails. The unforced tick $W$ applies the signed Hadamard, then streams R right and L left. Assume independently addressed sheet increments $0,1,2\bmod3$ on selected incoming arcs, without a permanent background gain, on a fixed nontrivial cubic character. The two conjugate characters also work for a sheet-blind read that treats their conjugate preparations alike. This is a choice of eight base wells and a three-sheet knob. An apparatus tying base size to four times the sheet order needs a separate construction.

In Fourier mode $q=2\pi j/8$ the eigenvalues of the balanced tick can be written $e^{-i\theta},-e^{i\theta}$ with $\sin\theta=\sin(q)/\sqrt2$. Hence $\theta$ can be chosen from $0,\pm\pi/6,\pm\pi/4$ and $W^{24}=I$ on all sixteen oriented ports. The 24-tick period of this walk is known. A positive power of this particular streaming step can therefore supply the exact inverse. This argument uses the full cyclic state and a closed coherent flight. It supplies no inverse for irreversible records or general forced schedules. [Konno, Shimizu and Takei](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06396)

Launch sharply at $(0,R)$, take $U=W^3$, and choose $P$ at $x=1$, including **both** rails. The signed R,L numerators at $x=-3,-1,1,3$ are $(0,1),(-1,0),(2,1),(1,0)$, over $\sqrt8$. Thus $p=5/8$. Write $ij$ for the pulse pair $D^i$ then $C^j$, read in time order. Phase the two arrivals at $x=1$, run 21 plain ticks and apply $j$ only on arrival at $(0,R)$, then run three forward ticks. This is $W^3D_0^jW^{21}D^i=C^jD^i$. A terminal or initial bin increment can be composed onto the incoming arcs of the preceding existing tick. No extra walking time is inserted. Pad any missing pair with 24 free ticks. Origin, addressed arc identities, unchanged amplitudes and coherence across the extra ticks are explicit operational premises. To infer a rate for the unpadded tick-three flight also compare that bare read across the two clocks.

**Neither normalized reference packet is exactly reachable in this apparatus from the sharp launch.** After $t$ forward ticks and any number of cubic masks, every native port amplitude has the form $(a+b\omega)/\sqrt{2^t}$ for integers $a,b$. A mask multiplies a numerator by a unit of $\mathbb Z[\omega]$. A tick adds or subtracts two numerators, divides by $\sqrt2$ and permutes ports. Thus each port has squared norm $(a^2-ab+b^2)/2^t$, a dyadic rational even if pulses leave the two-packet plane. In the normalized in-bin packet $g$ the rail with numerator 1 has squared norm $1/5$. In the normalized complementary packet $b$ each of its three occupied ports has squared norm $1/3$. Neither is dyadic, and a global phase preserves those norms. Within the plane spanned by $g,b$, populations 1 and 0 would require exactly those two rays. There the dense orbit approaches both endpoint populations without ever attaining them. Other states outside this plane can have definite position-bin membership. The obstruction is to preparing these particular reference packets with the stated gates.

At common tick 27 use query with a blank pair, high word $11$, low word $12$. For each setting append the uniform bin increment to the final tick. The populations are respectively $5/8,365/512,5/512$, giving $\lambda=7/8$. One complete mixture comparison allocates 24 original launches per side: eight per query phase on the left, seven per high phase and one per low phase on the right. The phase-zero query group also measures $F_0$. Repeated blocks provide precision. For a law that squares the weights of the occupied **sites** before normalizing and then reads each site's rails in their ordinary conditional proportions, the in-bin response at packet population $q$ is $f(q)=3q^2/[3q^2+(1-q)^2]$. The three exterior sites share the remaining mass equally for these words. This law passes every isolated one-site rail test and is invariant under the terminal phase. Yet its two mixture rates differ by exactly $47166975/752237689$ (about $0.06270$ per launch); the ordinary rule gives zero.

For a sharper finite calibration, use high word $(12)^3(22)^3(21)^3$ and low word $11\,22$, padding to nine pairs at tick 219. The high population is $142657359321985565/144115188075855872>0.989$, the low population $5/32768$. The exact high mixture weight is $2001111162552320/3169674868654001$. Randomized settings or measured bounded weight error can replace that large exact quota. The same competing law has a discrepancy about $0.26155$. These populations and all nine phase settings in each panel were propagated in integer Eisenstein coordinates on the original oriented ports. The checker also tests all basis inputs under 24 forward ticks and retains both 12- and 16-well failures. [Finite programs](check_cubic_flight_bin.py) · [Exact output](results/cubic_flight_bin.json)

The limited number of addresses is the tradeoff with independent phase randomization at every coordinate and calibration on separately prepared coordinate inputs. For a different flight the two-switch argument remains valid if its selective source operator is available. Rare heralds still require separate tests of joint and marginal rates per original launch with error small compared with the herald yield. Connecting these finite preparations, stored records and detector behavior to physical hardware remains open.
