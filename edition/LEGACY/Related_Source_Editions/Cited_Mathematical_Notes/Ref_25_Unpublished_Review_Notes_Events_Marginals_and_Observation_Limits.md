## Generalized event kernels are not automatically quantum channels

The specified four-to-two Hermitian aggregation has real nullity 12. Normalization by $\mathbf1^\dagger D\mathbf1=1$ is not density-matrix trace normalization. A designated input pair with trace distance $1/4$ and target distance $1/2$ excludes exact deterministic CPTP realization of those targets; the $1/8$ approximation **lower bound** and heralded success-$1/2$ construction remain separately scoped. An attained minimax optimum is not inferred merely from that lower bound.

Two-copy nonproduct Boolean events can identify imaginary antisymmetric entries up to conjugation, while every identical-copy Boolean experiment still agrees for $D$ and $\overline D$. A three-copy generalized event value $9/8$ demonstrates why positive normalized generalized event weights cannot silently be read as ordinary event probabilities. Calibrated quantum reference effects can change the observation menu but do not emerge from the generalized kernel axioms. Failures remain part of every heralded information account. ## Marginals, exchangeability, and contexts

For finite alphabets of sizes $q_i$, the space invisible to all proper marginals has dimension $\prod_i(q_i-1)$. Higher-order parity controls show why pairwise independence or agreement of every smaller subsystem need not imply global independent preparation. Finite and infinite exchangeability have different extension obligations. The ternary example retains its positivity interval $[-1/6,1/3]$ and four-extendibility interval $[-1/6,2/15]$; distances $2/5$ and $1/3$ refer to different competitor classes. (Source limitation: the ternary family and the two competitor classes are not defined in these selected passages, so the reported intervals and distances are not self-contained proof claims.)

A cofiltered diagram of finite nonempty sets with coherent total restrictions has a global inverse limit under its stated assumptions. For a finite diagram with greatest context $K$, that limit is canonically $C(K)$, not a singleton unless $C(K)$ is one. Nondirected contextual covers can have no global completion. Relabeling-invariant uniform probabilities cannot be natural under every finite surjection with unequal fibers. (The general inverse-limit assertion is reported here without its full proof.)

Classical set notation does not establish Bell-locality. The local response factorization and setting assumptions must be stated. A single simultaneous classical joint assignment has its CHSH bound; quantum and no-signalling comparisons use different supplied state/effect rules. None of these distinctions alone reconstructs quantum theory.

## Spectra, geometry, balance, and observation limits

The occurrence of $\pi$ in a path-Laplacian asymptotic assumes a chosen graph and normalization. Permutation averaging is not an orbit quotient. Positive semidefinite, even strictly positive-definite, correlations need not produce a metric under $-\log R_{ij}$; a Euclidean Gram distance is a different selected transform. No Lorentzian metric follows. (The graph choices, correlation examples, and full arguments are not reproduced here.)

All-degree positivity of the stipulated unoriented matching Gram matrices restricts the loop parameter to nonnegative integers, but does not choose one integer or a physical probability rule. Finite-degree positivity can conceal failure at larger degree. (The defining matching Gram matrices and all-degree argument are not included in this passage.)

Zero signed sums, zero Fock-parity trace, and the zero integral of a Ricker profile can coexist with nonzero structure and many distinguishable alternatives. None chooses a preparation law, positive entropy, physical persistence, or a frequency. A formal profile is not a fundamentally assigned waveform. Symmetry covariance of an open-system map is not conservation of every chosen observable. (These are reported comparisons; their full model definitions and witnesses are outside this excerpt.)

For a fixed projector and positive effect, the reported sharp sector discrepancy is

$$
F(\alpha,\beta)=
\frac{|\alpha-\beta|+
\sqrt{(\alpha-\beta)^2+4\min(\alpha,\beta)}}2.
$$

At symmetric leakage $\alpha=\beta=\varepsilon$, it is $\sqrt\varepsilon$, not $\varepsilon$; at one-way leakage $\beta=0$, it is $\alpha$.

Fixed compact effects, norm-precompact families, moving projectors, a countably resolved PVM, and a fixed noncompact binary discriminator are different contracts. Escape from a fixed restricted observation is not annihilation or loss of all distinguishability. Repetition depends on an additional copy and resource law. The effect definitions and the proof of the displayed leakage bound are given in *Observation Contracts, Repetition Resources, and Sharp Sector-Leakage Bounds*, sections 2–6.
