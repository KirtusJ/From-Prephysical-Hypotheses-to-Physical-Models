## 2. Unlimited repetition of insufficient finite-order observations

**Proposition 1 (adaptive fixed-cutoff nonidentification).** Let $P_A^{(k)}$ and $P_B^{(k)}$ denote complete equality-pattern distributions of $k$ independently sampled formal labels. Suppose

$$P_A^{(k)}=P_B^{(k)}\quad\text{for every}\quad 1\le k\le K.$$

On each experimental attempt, an observer may choose any $k\le K$, predictably from the prior history and private randomness. Assume the attempt draws *fresh independent samples* from the designated candidate preparation law and returns only the within-attempt equality pattern. Then, for every finite number $N$ of attempts,

$$\boxed{\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal L_A(H_N),\mathcal L_B(H_N))=0.}$$

*Proof.* Condition on each shared history. The policy has the same conditional assignment law under both models, and the specified next conditional observation law agrees by hypothesis. Induction establishes equality of the complete joint histories. Any randomized decision has identical rejection probability under both models. This includes adaptive selection of lower arities; increasing the number of independent uses cannot recover information that is exactly absent from every admitted outcome law. $\square$

**Transfer guard.** Cross-record equality measurements are different observations. They require a common persistent label-reference convention and are not computable from the separately recorded within-attempt partitions. With such additional comparisons the effective jointly observed arity can increase beyond $K$; Proposition 1 no longer applies. This is not a claim of fundamental indistinguishability.

## 3. Growing joint arity restores asymptotic identification

For a deterministic paintbox vector $p=(p_i)$ put $s_m(p)=\sum_i p_i^m$. Consider two laws whose first collision-moment difference occurs at $m=K+1$ (the Prouhet construction is one source of such laws):

$$\Delta=s_m(p_A)-s_m(p_B)\ne0.$$

For $n\ge m$ independent raw label samples, their complete equality partition supports the bounded $U$-statistic

$$U_{n,m}=\binom nm^{-1}\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[n]\\ |S|=m}}\mathbf 1\{\text{all labels in $S$ agree}\}.$$

It has expectation $s_m(p)$. Changing one sampled label alters at most $\binom{n-1}{m-1}$ of the summands and hence changes $U_{n,m}$ by at most $m/n$. The standard bounded-differences inequality gives each one-sided deviation bound

$$\Pr\!\left(U_{n,m}-\mathbb E U_{n,m}\ge t\right)\le e^{-2nt^2/m^2},$$

and the corresponding lower-tail bound. The midpoint test between the two expectations, with $t=|\Delta|/2$, therefore yields

$$\boxed{\operatorname{TV}(P_A^{(n)},P_B^{(n)})\ge\max\left(0,1-2e^{-n\Delta^2/(2m^2)}\right).}$$

In particular $\operatorname{TV}(P_A^{(n)},P_B^{(n)})\to1$. The concentration estimate is quite conservative; it gives a rigorous sufficiency statement for one growing-arity comparison, not a sample-optimal protocol. It assumes *joint* availability of all within-$n$ equality relations and independence of the sampled labels, neither of which is generated by the proposed prephysical hypotheses.

This agrees with Kingman's correspondence: the asymptotic ranked block frequencies of the infinite observed partition recover the deterministic paintbox masses, and distinct moment sequences give mutually singular complete infinite partition laws.

### Exact finite controls for the first Prouhet witness

For the $K=2$ construction use the finite core integers $A=(1,4,6,7)$ and $B=(2,3,5,8)$ with weights $a/36$ or $b/36$, together with a common geometric tail $2^{-j-1}$ for $j\ge1$. Both are normalized and produce complete equality-pattern distributions with strictly positive probabilities at every finite order. Their pair-collision probabilities agree at $35/216$, while the three-way all-equal probabilities are $425/13608$ and $439/13608$.

The source reports obtaining these values by regrouping all labelled patterns according to integer block-size shapes, computing each shape probability by partition-lattice inclusion-exclusion from *exact* collision power sums (including the geometric tail), and checking normalizations. They are small but strictly positive and increasing over this tested range. The asymptotic conclusion follows from the preceding proof, not extrapolation from the table.

The first-separation formula is as follows. For $m=K+1$, if $\Delta_m=s_m(p_A)-s_m(p_B)$, then a particular labelled pattern with $b$ blocks has difference $(-1)^{b-1}(b-1)!\Delta_m$. Thus the complete order-$m$ total variation is

$$\frac{|\Delta_m|}{2}\sum_{b=1}^m\left\{\!\!\begin{matrix}m\\b\end{matrix}\!\!\right\}(b-1)!.$$

The source reports simple-hypothesis KL lower bounds of 81,288 and 81,670 independent full three-position records for 5% size and 95% power. They are **necessary, not sufficient**, and apply to separately repeated three-position records, rather than the growing-arity experiment above.

## 4. Composition has to be mathematically typed

The following two compositional examples are not direct replacements for one another.

1. **Disjoint-position amalgamation** takes partitions of separate sets and returns a *set* of possible partitions on the union:

$$\mathcal A_{k,\ell}:\mathcal P_k\times\mathcal P_\ell\to\mathcal P(\mathcal P_{k+\ell}).$$

It preserves all compatible cross-identifications but does not select one. Associativity holds after extending the operation to sets of possibilities. Uniform probability on each binary output is nonassociative: the three-singleton left/right parenthesizations differ in total variation by $1/12$.

2. **Common refinement** overlays two partitions of the *same* set:

$$\wedge_k:\mathcal P_k\times\mathcal P_k\to\mathcal P_k.$$

For independent paintbox laws, refinement corresponds to tensoring the underlying probability mass vectors $p\otimes q$ and is associative and restriction-consistent within the supplied probability class.

The first operation **adds arities** $k+\ell$ and has set-valued output. The second **keeps arity fixed** and operates on probabilistic partition laws after independently stipulating preparations. A categorical or operational theory cannot substitute one for the other without an explicit interface-identification and typing rule. Their simultaneous mathematical existence does not select a unified physical composition mechanism.


For real weights, $(xy)z$ and $x(yz)$ are equal by associativity of multiplication.

The following Ewens refinement and Pitman--Yor alias is reported: independent Ewens$(1)$ refinement agrees through order three with Pitman--Yor$(\alpha=2/7,\theta=13/7)$ but has full order-four total variation $59/4896$. This refutes one proposed family closure, not the validity of the Pitman--Yor family itself.

## 5. Feasible coherent entropy lower witness

The submitted *unrestricted* mean-order-one entropy optimum over all integer-partition types, with the empty partition admitted, is

$$H_*=2.420267156348295\ldots\ \text{bits}.$$

The additionally required one-family restriction consistency imposes a positive gap. The provided one-dimensional KL relaxation yields a conservative upper value

$$H_{\mathrm{coh}}\le 2.41988092049132\ldots\ \text{bits},$$

**not** the exact optimum. The displayed relaxation is a source-reported numerical bound. To improve the positive lower witness, fix a single all-size Ewens family with $\theta=4/3$. For each order $k$ let $Q_k$ be the induced law on integer-partition *types* (not uniform labelled set partitions). For a type $\lambda\vdash k$ with $m_j$ blocks of size $j$, its exact probability is

$$Q_k(\lambda)=\frac{k!\,\theta^{\sum_j m_j}}{(\theta)_{k\uparrow}}\prod_{j\ge1}\frac1{j^{m_j}m_j!}.$$

Define $h_k=-\sum_{\lambda\vdash k}Q_k(\lambda)\ln Q_k(\lambda)$, and an independent distribution of attempted arity

$$\pi_k\propto e^{h_k-1.047262 k}\quad (0\le k\le22),\qquad \pi_k=0\quad(k>22).$$

The Ewens family itself is still defined and projectively consistent at every order; the support truncation applies only to the chosen arity-mixing distribution. For $\theta=4/3$, all $Q_k$ for $0\le k\le22$ were normalized in *exact rational arithmetic*. High-precision evaluation of the above explicit probability distribution gives

$$\mathbb E_\pi[k]=0.99999623944300555\ldots<1,$$

$$H(\pi,Q)=2.39533583382905207\ldots\ \text{bits}.$$

Therefore the constructive comparison gives a bounded interval

$$\boxed{2.3953<H_{\mathrm{coh}}\le2.419881\quad\text{bits}.}$$

The lower witness is an explicitly feasible probability construction, not a numerical optimization claim. The displayed high-precision decimals are not formal interval enclosures. Neither endpoint selects the artificial cost $r(\lambda)=|\lambda|$ or proves a physical entropy/capacity principle.
