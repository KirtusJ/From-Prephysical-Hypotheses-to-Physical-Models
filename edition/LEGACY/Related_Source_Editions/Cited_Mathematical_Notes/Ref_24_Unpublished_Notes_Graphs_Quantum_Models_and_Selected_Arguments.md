## 4. Selected mathematical arguments

### 4.1 Rank-one and rank-r operational normalization

For a positive functional `W`, the unrestricted normalized cone is `K_W={D>=0:Tr(WD)=1}`. If `ker W` is nontrivial, positive states can have arbitrarily large null-sector contributions without changing normalization. Every universally bounded affine functional depends only on the compression to the support of `W`; otherwise testing rank-one rays `|x+zv><x+zv|` with `v in ker W` would produce unbounded linear or quadratic terms in `z`. After conjugation by `W_R^(1/2)` on its rank-r support, all universally bounded positive affine measurements are ordinary POVMs on a rank-r density-matrix state space. For the proposed `W=J=11†`, `r=1`, these effects are constant. This mathematical failure depends on an **unrestricted** state cone and **globally bounded affine** effects; it does not prove that the null sector does not exist ontologically.

For `D_g=B+gP`, `P>=0`, `P1=0`, any *fixed* positive linear normalization-preserving map takes `P` into a positive matrix of zero total-event weight and hence into another balanced sector. If an exact strongly decoherent output cell `A` exists for two compared values of `g`, its probability is the row sum of B, independent of `g`. The set of *eligible cells* might nonetheless vary with `g`; this is not an absolute hidden-variable prohibition.

### 4.2 Generalized scores and stochastic transformations

Positive generalized decoherence kernels need not assign probability `<=1` to every subset. Only an independently justified decoherent or operational measurement context converts a family of scores into a normalized probability law. A column-stochastic matrix `T` sends classical probabilities `p` to `Tp`; it does **not** generally send the classical diagonal kernel `diag(p)` to `T diag(p) T^T`. The latter can create off-diagonal terms from a previously classical kernel. Coherent congruence therefore cannot be identified with classical stochastic diffusion without an additional justified map. A restricted entrywise-nonnegative PSD cone avoids some normalization failures, but has an ordinary classical joint-probability representation.

### 4.3 Exact finite exchangeability boundary

For the anti-balanced four-slot count law `P_4=(1/162,16/81,16/27,16/81,1/162)`, all five-/six-slot four-coordinate marginals determine a unique extension even without imposing exchangeability. Nonnegative degree-four count polynomials force forbidden total-count classes to have zero probability; the remaining four-marginal linear evaluation map is injective. For seven slots, the probability of a balanced four-sample is at most `18/35`, while the target gives `16/27`, yielding the sharp distance `74/945`. Infinite exchangeability gives the tighter upper bound `3/8` for that event and distance `47/216`. These are **distributional distances**, not experimental uncertainties.

For the separate five-slot finite Gibbs approximation problem with action-range `Delta` and `R=e^Delta`, the sharp minimax four-marginal TV is:

```
d_*(R) = 16/27 - 3R/(3+5R),    1 <= R <= 78/5;
         32/[27(5R+2)],       R >= 78/5.
```

Symmetrization and two bounded event-function witnesses give the lower bounds, and explicit positive weights `a,b,c` saturate them. Exact zero error requires hard zeros; a finite Gibbs law can still reproduce **one** designated four-subset marginal by appending an independent fifth variable. A finite-positive-support obstruction must not be turned into a universal prohibition on enlarging a statistical system.

### 4.4 Time-neutral action does not entail a physical clock

The double well `S_0(t)=lambda(t²-r²)²` evaluates to zero at the *only* two values carried by its independently selected prior. Neither the statistical predictions nor their quantum chart depend on `lambda`. The auxiliary commutator penalty with `Q=mZ,R=X` satisfies `[Q,R]†[Q,R]=4m² I`, hence is also state-independent. These actions are mathematically well-defined without a temporal parameter but **do not select the state or physical activity**. A Gibbs-weighted distribution on an added latent interval can make action matter, but only after explicitly adding that interval, a reference measure and a probability rule.

### 4.5 Spin-Casimir and universal singlet budget

The stipulated qubit operator `H_N=J_N²` obeys

```
H_N = (3N/4)I + (1/2)sum_{i<j} (X_i X_j+Y_i Y_j+Z_i Z_j),
P_s^(ij) = (I - X_i X_j-Y_i Y_j-Z_i Z_j)/4,
sum_{i<j} P_s^(ij) = [N(N+2)/8]I - H_N/2.
```

Since the smallest eigenvalue is `0` for even `N` and `3/4` for odd `N`, the average singlet fraction cannot exceed `(N+2)/[4(N-1)]` for even `N` or `(N+3)/(4N)` for odd `N`. Every minimum-sector state saturates this *average* bound, including selected dimers and fully symmetric ground-sector mixtures. The action does not select how singlet weight is distributed among pairs. At four spins, two orthogonal zero-action states reduce to pure singlet vs uniform triplet on the same chosen pair; any Werner singlet fraction from zero to one is possible at the same global action. Arbitrarily weak perturbations can therefore select quantum-incompatible pair structures.

For all sizes, a fixed ordered matching provides compatible minimizing states `Gamma_{2k}=P_s^⊗k`, `Gamma_{2k+1}=P_s^⊗k ⊗ I/2`. Last-factor partial traces work exactly. But the matching is an additional input. The fully invariant ground projector family has even-to-odd compatibility and odd-to-even failure; do not infer failure of all possible minimizing projective families.

### 4.6 SU(2) plus permutations forbid a unique pure selector at N>=3

If a Hamiltonian commutes with diagonal `SU(2)` and all permutations, a unique ground **ray** must be invariant under SU(2) (which has no nontrivial one-dimensional continuous character) and transform as a one-dimensional `S_N` character (symmetric or antisymmetric). The symmetric N-qubit subspace has spin `N/2`, not spin zero, while the fully antisymmetric wedge of `N>2` two-dimensional factors vanishes. Thus no such pure ray exists for `N>=3`. A unique **mixed** invariant selector, a different internal algebra, or a symmetry-broken state is not excluded. This is a group-representation theorem inside an imported SU(2) qubit model.

### 4.7 Exact distance from all infinitely exchangeable quantum families

For `P_min,N` of rank `d_N` and canonical `rho_N^*=P_min,N/d_N`, quantum de Finetti gives every indefinitely exchangeable state as `omega_N=int tau^⊗N dmu(tau)`. By SU(2) invariance, put one-site `tau` in a basis with eigenvalues `p,1-p`. For `N=2k` the min-spin sector overlap is `d_N[p(1-p)]^k`; for `N=2k+1` it is `(d_N/2)[p(1-p)]^k`. Both are at most `d_N/2^N`. Measuring `{P_min,N,I-P_min,N}` proves `D_tr(rho_N^*,omega_N)>=1-d_N/2^N`. The independent tracial product `I/2^N` attains equality because `rho_N^*` is uniform on the projector support. Therefore

```
inf_{infinitely quantum-exchangeable omega_N} D_tr(rho_N^*,omega_N)
  = 1 - d_N/2^N.
```

The same lower bound holds for any state wholly supported on min spin, but sharp equality is only asserted for the **canonical uniform** state. This theorem does not cover arbitrary *finite* exchangeability, a different tensor-product algebra, or nonexchangeable singlet pair hierarchies.

### 4.8 Highest-spin projective countermodel

`Sigma_N=P_sym,N/(N+1)` equals the Haar average `int (|psi><psi|)^⊗N dpsi` over pure qubits. This immediately proves full permutation invariance, SU(2) invariance, separability and exact arbitrary-partial-trace compatibility. Its number of positive `Z` outcomes is uniform on `0,...,N`; even distinct-slot sign moments equal `1/(2m+1)` and odd ones vanish. Its two-factor state is the uniform triplet projector, different from independent `I/4` by trace distance `1/4`. Exchangeability does **not** entail statistical independence or a physical spacetime.

### 4.9 Permutation twirl changes physical claims

For `Gamma_N` the matched singlet state, averaging over all permutations at each N gives `rho_N^*` by Schur–Weyl irreducibility on the minimum-spin sector. For `N=2k-1` and `2k`, the exact full-state trace distance is `1-1/C_k` and complete labeled signed-`Z` TV is `1-2^k/binom(2k,k)`. If permutations are gauge transformations, only gauge-invariant observations count and named-pair distinctions may be unphysical. If permutations represent a physical symmetry, choosing a matching amounts to extra state preparation or symmetry breaking. **Neither interpretation is supplied by the formal action.** Averaging separately at each size breaks the pair family's prefix projectivity.

### 4.10 Strong Bell monogamy and the Bell-excess selector

For conventional qubits, CHSH violations on overlapping reduced pairs cannot both occur, *even when each pair optimizes measurement settings independently*. Cheng and Hall (PRL 118, 010401, 2017) strengthen the original common-settings Toner–Verstraete relation for this qubit case. Therefore the undirected graph of CHSH-violating pairs is a matching. If the action is **defined** as `A_N=-sum_{i<j}max(0, B_ij-2)`, where `B_ij` is the optimized CHSH capability, then Tsirelson's bound gives `A_N >= -floor(N/2)(2sqrt(2)-2)`, attained by Bell-state dimers. A deficit less than `2sqrt(2)-2` forces a maximum-cardinality violation matching. This is a valid **quantum resource-counting construction** but imports exactly the local quantum operations, tensor structures and Bell criterion whose physical origin the ontology has not derived. Its symmetry does not choose one labeled matching.

### 4.11 Distinct action functionals select different states

The shared-source pure-product branches are entirely in the maximal-spin sector, `Tr(Omega_N H_N)=N(N+2)/4`. The phase-paired branch at `N=2` is also triplet-supported and satisfies `Tr(Xi_2 H_2)=2` (maximum), not zero (singlet minimum). At larger sizes its exact expectation is

```
Tr(Xi_N H_N) = 3N/4 + k/2
             + [binom(N,2)-k] r²(2-r²)/2,
k=floor(N/2).
```

Hence neither projective branch is derived by unchanged **minimum** Casimir selection. The zero-dimensional signed law, the phase gate, the Casimir, and the matching were each separately supplied. A new map explicitly connecting them is required.

### 4.12 Local/global and signed/quantum distinctions

For canonical min-spin even-N states, the complete signed outcome law is fair independent signs *conditioned on exact balance*, although fixed-size marginals tend toward independent fair signs. Their global Z TV from unconditioned iid is `1-binomial(N,N/2)/2^N->1`; their full quantum trace distance from `I/2^N` is `1-d_N/2^N->1` even faster. The canonical Z-basis relative-entropy coherence is `log(k+1)` for `N=2k` and `log((k+2)/2)` for `N=2k+1`. A different nonprojective antialignment Gibbs model has locally vanishing correlations and globally nonzero limiting TV/KL. These are **different** mathematical regimes. Neither local asymptotic independence nor a vanishing action divided by an arbitrarily chosen growing normalizer proves convergence of complete states.
