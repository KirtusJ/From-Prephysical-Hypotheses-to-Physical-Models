\textbf{Abstract}\par
On a gain digraph, a family of set functions on walks of each length is one function on sets of unending walks exactly when the measure of a set of walks equals, up to a fixed factor, the measure of the set of all its continuations. Under that condition an additive rule that is invariant under switching, on a digraph without loops and of constant out-degree, is a multiple of the number of walks, so the weighting of returning walks by their holonomy that invariance under switching allows is excluded. On the signed arc digraph of a line, a rule that adds over end vertices a function of the modulus of the summed gains satisfies the condition only if the function is the square, and the same holds on every finite connected signed digraph that is norm-preserving of degree two; on any norm-preserving gain digraph in which two walks merge, the only power of the modulus that satisfies it is the second. In degree two, for gains in the $q$-th roots of unity with $q$ even that reach the vertices of a block in abundance, the rules of this form that satisfy it are exactly the combinations of the squared moduli in the conjugate characters, a space of dimension $\varphi(q)/2$, of dimension one for $q=2$, so that the rule is unique up to a factor exactly for the orders $2$, $4$ and $6$: the condition forces the order of the rule and leaves the embedding of the amplitudes undetermined. Multiplicativity on products of carriers reduces the freedom to the choice of one conjugate character. If the gain of a walk is itself a record, the carrier is a signed lift, on which the rule is unique for every order and is the mean of the squared moduli over the characters in which $-1$ acts as $-1$; the choice of an embedding belongs to the reduction by a character. Among the switching-invariant pair rules, with no choice of records and for any finite abelian group of gains and any degree, the consistent ones are, under a hypothesis on the quotients of gains, exactly the real combinations over the characters for which the step is norm-preserving; in degree two these are the characters in which $-1$ acts as $-1$. On carriers with a full block, on which the step is norm-preserving, and with abundant gains neither the modulus nor the sum need be assumed: a consistent rule that adds over end vertices any function of the summed gains, vanishing at zero, is a quadratic form, a combination of the conjugate squared moduli if it is unchanged by switching; and a consistent rule that adds any function, vanishing on the empty multiset, of the multiset of gains of the walks ending at a vertex is, when the gains of the block generate the group, a combination of the count of walks and of squared moduli of gains summed through characters. Nothing selects between the count and the squares. The second order of the pair rule of paper G8 thus follows from the condition, the form of the rule and the carrier. A position-dependent gain translates the quasi-momentum exactly only if it is linear in position, so a translation by $\Phi$ per step needs gains of order at least $2\pi/\Phi$. For gains of order three or more no quasi-energy lies in the spectrum of the free step multiplied by every gain value, apart from four band edges for order four; in computed instances a staircase of gains of order three, four or six confines a walk to a block, leaks slowly or is a strong field; a packet, computed for order four, does not follow the ray of the mean field. For a linear gain the centroid of a packet follows the ray averaged over the packet, displaced by the connection of the branch eigenvector, with a remainder that falls about as the first power of the field in the instances computed. The statements are conditional on the event structure, the form of the rule and the carrier. Neither quantum nor classical mechanics is derived, and no physical correspondence is asserted.\par
