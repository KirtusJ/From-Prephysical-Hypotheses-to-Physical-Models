\begin{abstract}
For finite channels, a Bayesian reverse kernel converts mutual-information
loss under a specified full-support probe into explicit weighted relative-entropy
and total-variation recovery bounds.  Zero loss is equivalent to a common
stochastic recovery of every input row.  Equal channel capacities therefore
imply recovery when the coarse channel has a full-support optimizer, but
existence of a full-support optimizer for the original channel alone is
insufficient, even for strictly positive channels.  For a specified three-input,
four-output example with equal capacities before and after output merging,
we prove that the sharp worst-case per-input recovery error is one quarter.
The same fixed example has arbitrarily small information loss under a sequence
of full-support probes, showing why a quantitative probe-mass condition matters.
All conclusions concern explicit population probability models; finite-sample
coverage and physical correspondence require separate premises.
\end{abstract}
