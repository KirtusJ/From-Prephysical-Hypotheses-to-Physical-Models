\subsection{2. Independent result: exact detector partial-identification interval}\label{independent-result-exact-detector-partial-identification-interval}

Let trusted density matrices rho\_i (i=1,\ldots,m) have exact measured response rates p\_i for an otherwise unknown but stable binary measurement effect 0 \textless= E \textless= I on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. For a target state rho\_T define

\begin{verbatim}
L(p) = min Tr(E rho_T),   U(p) = max Tr(E rho_T),
subject to Tr(E rho_i)=p_i for every i and 0 <= E <= I.
\end{verbatim}

These are finite semidefinite programs, and by definition are the sharp possible target-response bounds for the entire admissible effect class. Both extrema are attained when the calibration vector is feasible because the effect set is compact. The image is a closed interval by convexity. \textbf{They are data dependent}: a target outside the real reference span does not imply ambiguity for every possible calibration dataset.

For any real vector lambda, put A=lambda-weighted subtraction \texttt{A=rho\_T-sum\_i\ lambda\_i\ rho\_i}. Then

\begin{verbatim}
Tr(E rho_T) = sum_i lambda_i p_i + Tr(E A)
            <= sum_i lambda_i p_i + Tr(A_+),
\end{verbatim}

where \texttt{A\_+} is the positive part of A and the inequality is sharp when E is its positive spectral projector if no other constraints are imposed. Consequently,

\begin{verbatim}
U(p) <= inf_lambda [sum_i lambda_i p_i + Tr((rho_T-sum_i lambda_i rho_i)_+)].
\end{verbatim}

The inequality is equality under the usual finite-dimensional Slater condition: at least one compatible effect satisfies 0 \textless{} E \textless{} I. At boundary response vectors, rely on the primal SDP or limiting dual, not an unqualified interior-strong-duality assertion. For normalized densities, \texttt{L(p)=1-U(1-p)} follows by exchanging E and I-E.

\subsubsection{Elementary complete proof for a qubit}\label{elementary-complete-proof-for-a-qubit}

Use two trusted reference states \textbar0\textgreater\textless0\textbar{} and \textbar1\textgreater\textless1\textbar. Suppose their calibrated click probabilities are p0,p1. A possible effect has the form

\begin{verbatim}
E = [[p0, z], [conjugate(z), p1]].
\end{verbatim}

Positivity of E and I-E is equivalent to

\begin{verbatim}
|z| <= R := min(sqrt(p0*p1), sqrt((1-p0)*(1-p1))).
\end{verbatim}

For the held-out target \textbar+\textgreater=(\textbar0\textgreater+\textbar1\textgreater)/sqrt(2), \texttt{q=(p0+p1)/2+Re(z)}. Choosing z=+/-R proves the \emph{exact} interval

\begin{verbatim}
(p0+p1)/2-R <= q <= (p0+p1)/2+R.
\end{verbatim}

In particular, if p0=p1=p, the range is \texttt{{[}max(0,2p-1),min(1,2p){]}}. At p=0 the calibration forces E=0 and q=0, despite \textbar+\textgreater\textless+\textbar{} lying outside the real reference span. At p=1/2 the same two reference probabilities are compatible with every target rate from zero to one. These are extreme physical effects, not a numerical fitting artifact.

This is an instance of established quantum partial tomography and set-identification SDPs (e.g.~\emph{Physical Review A} 98, 042318, 2018), not a claim of mathematical originality. Its application to the present measurement contract is useful because it avoids inferring universal ignorance from mere failure of reference informational completeness.

\subsubsection{Uncertain reference data}\label{uncertain-reference-data}

When independently justified intervals \texttt{{[}l\_i,u\_i{]}} replace exact p\_i, use the same primal with inequalities. Its Lagrangian bound reads

\begin{verbatim}
U <= inf_{beta_i,gamma_i>=0} [sum_i (beta_i u_i-gamma_i l_i)
      + Tr((rho_T - sum_i (beta_i-gamma_i)rho_i)_+)].
\end{verbatim}

Equality follows under appropriate strict feasibility. This is how uncertainty and physicality should jointly enter, rather than treating a noisy point estimate as exact.

\subsection{3. Cross-check: tetrahedral source-response bound}\label{cross-check-tetrahedral-source-response-bound}

For four trusted tetrahedral qubit states rho\_i=(I+r\_i dot sigma)/2, define D\_i=(3rho\_i-I)/2. They form a dual frame and satisfy sum D\_i\^{}2=(5/2)I. The sixteen probabilities \texttt{p\_ij=Tr(E\ rho\_i\ tensor\ rho\_j)} determine E exactly. Under entrywise errors \textbar delta p\_ij\textbar{} \textless= delta, the \emph{maximum response over separable states}, b\_sep(E), changes by at most 4 delta, which is sharp. The proof uses the dual weights

\begin{verbatim}
w_i(x) = Tr(D_i rho_x)=(1+3r_i dot x)/4.
\end{verbatim}

The exact maximum of sum\_i \textbar w\_i(x)\textbar{} is two for Bloch vectors \textbar x\textbar\textless=1, attained at a tetrahedral antipode, with weights (-1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2). Product response weights therefore have absolute sum at most four. Direct construction of a sign-perturbation near E=I/2 attains the bound without leaving the effect set for sufficiently small delta.

Using alpha=.05, Delta=13/1280, and seventeen independent settings of n trials each, the sufficient requirement n \textgreater{} 50 log(34/alpha)/Delta\^{}2 gives n=3,161,479, total 53,745,143. This is a \emph{conservative sufficient} shot budget with trusted preparations, stable effect, and independent Bernoulli trials, not a necessary hardware cost.
