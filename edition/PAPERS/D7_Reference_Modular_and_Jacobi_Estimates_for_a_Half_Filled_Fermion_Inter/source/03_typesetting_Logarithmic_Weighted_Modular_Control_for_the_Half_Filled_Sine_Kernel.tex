\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}

For the finite interval of a half-filled infinite discrete sine-kernel projection, we establish an explicit logarithmic upper bound on the occupation-weighted quadratic discrepancy between its exact modular generator and the established commuting-Jacobi scaling function. The proof uses an elementary trace-class estimate for the half-line sine-kernel Hankel blocks and a scalar logarithmic inequality. Combined with the independently established Wilson--Dirac positive-majorant pairing estimate, this shows that the \emph{transverse} contribution to the fixed-scaled-mass relative-entropy modular correction is sublogarithmic. An elementary fractional spectral-moment bound yields a considerably weaker sufficient condition for the leading logarithmic law than uniform boundedness of the modular-error operator. The separate argument in \emph{Uniform Bulk Modular Asymptotics and the Leading Wilson Entropy Law} supplies the longitudinal bound and leading entropy law. Neither the present estimate nor that leading law determines the finite part or its proposed inverse-logarithmic remainder rate.

\subsection{1. Reference operators and established inputs}\label{reference-operators-and-established-inputs}

Let \(N=2L\ge2\). On \(\mathbb C^N\) define the half-filled sine-kernel covariance

\[
(B_N)_{jk}=\begin{cases}\tfrac12,&j=k,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\sin(\pi(j-k)/2)}{\pi(j-k)},&j\ne k,
\end{cases}\qquad 0\le j,k<N.
\]

Let \(J_N\) be the real-symmetric tridiagonal matrix with zero diagonal and \((J_N)_{j-1,j}=j(N-j)/N\) for \(1\le j<N\). These matrices commute, \(0<B_N<I\), and \(\|J_N/N\|\le1/2\). The strict occupation bounds follow from the fact that a nonzero finitely supported sequence has Fourier transform that cannot vanish identically on either of the two nonempty half-circle frequency arcs.

Set

\[
W_N=B_N(I-B_N),\quad K_N=\log(I-B_N)-\log B_N,
\] \[
\eta(t)=\pi t\,{}_3F_2\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac34;1,\tfrac32;4t^2\right),\quad E_N=K_N+N\eta(J_N/N).
\]

The generalized hypergeometric series has nonnegative coefficients and converges at its argument 1. Therefore

\[
A_\eta:=\pi\,{}_3F_2\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac34;1,\tfrac32;1\right)<\infty,
\qquad |N\eta(\lambda/N)|\le A_\eta|\lambda|
\]

for every \(|\lambda|\le N/2\). We use the separately proved reference Jacobi moment estimate

\[
M_{2,N}=\operatorname{Tr}(J_N^2W_N)\le\frac{32}{\pi^2}(H_N+1),\quad H_N=\sum_{k=1}^N\frac1k.
\tag{1}
\]

The sharper identity \(M_{2,N}=(3\pi^2)^{-1}\log N+O(1)\) is available, but unnecessary below.

\subsection{2. Explicit trace-class estimate for the reference crossing}\label{explicit-trace-class-estimate-for-the-reference-crossing}

Let \(P\) be the orthogonal projection on \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\) onto the Fourier arc \((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\), and let \(\chi_N\) project onto \(N\) consecutive sites. Then \(B_N=\chi_NP\chi_N\), as an operator on the interval. Define \(X_N=\chi_NP(I-\chi_N)\). The full-line projection identity yields

\[
X_NX_N^*=B_N(I-B_N)=W_N,
\qquad \operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{W_N}=\|X_N\|_1.
\tag{2}
\]

For either exterior half-line, after reversing the interior index, the cross-boundary matrix has entries

\[
\frac{\sin\{\pi(i+j+1)/2\}}{\pi(i+j+1)},
\qquad 0\le i<N,\quad j\ge0.
\]

Write \(H^{(0)}_{ij}=1/(i+j+1)\). The sine factor is a difference of two complex exponentials; each gives diagonal unitary modulation of \(H^{(0)}\). Therefore the nuclear norm of each half-line block is at most \(\pi^{-1}\|H^{(0)}\|_1\).

The elementary Laplace representation

\[
H^{(0)}_{ij}=\int_0^\infty e^{-t(i+j+1)}\,dt
\]

is a rank-one Bochner integral. Its integrand has nuclear norm

\[
\mathcal W_N(t)=e^{-t}\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{-2Nt}}}{1-e^{-2t}}.
\]

Consequently, for the union of both exterior half-lines,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{W_N}=\|X_N\|_1\le\frac2\pi I_N,
\qquad I_N=\int_0^\infty\mathcal W_N(t)\,dt.
\tag{3}
\]

For \(0<t\le1\), the concavity inequality \(1-e^{-2t}\ge(1-e^{-2})t\) and \(1-e^{-2Nt}\le\min\{2Nt,1\}\) give

\[
\mathcal W_N(t)\le\frac{1}{1-e^{-2}}\min\left\{\sqrt{\frac{2N}{t}},\frac1t\right\}.
\]

For \(t\ge1\), \(\mathcal W_N(t)\le e^{-t}/(1-e^{-2})\). Splitting the integral at \(t=1/N\) and \(t=1\) gives

\[
\boxed{I_N\le\frac{\log N+2\sqrt2+e^{-1}}{1-e^{-2}}.}\tag{4}
\]

All constants are independent of \(N\). This is a deliberately nonoptimal estimate.

\subsection{3. Uniform quadratic modular estimate}\label{uniform-quadratic-modular-estimate}

\textbf{Theorem 1.} For every even \(N\ge2\),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(W_NE_N^2)
\le \frac{16}{\pi} I_N+2A_\eta^2 M_{2,N}
\le\frac{16(\log N+2\sqrt2+e^{-1})}{\pi(1-e^{-2})}
+\frac{64A_\eta^2}{\pi^2}(H_N+1).}
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, \(\operatorname{Tr}(W_NE_N^2)=O(\log N)\).

\emph{Proof.} For \(b\in(0,1)\) let \(k=\log((1-b)/b)\) and \(s=\sqrt{b(1-b)}=1/(2\cosh(k/2))\). Since \(\cosh(k/2)\ge k^2/8\),

\[
b(1-b)k^2=s^2k^2\le 4s.
\tag{6}
\]

Functional calculus gives \(\operatorname{Tr}(W_NK_N^2)\le4\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{W_N}\). Because \(B_N\), \(J_N\), \(K_N\), \(E_N\) and \(W_N\) commute,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(W_NE_N^2)
\le2\operatorname{Tr}(W_NK_N^2)
+2\operatorname{Tr}\{W_N[N\eta(J_N/N)]^2\}
\le8\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{W_N}+2A_\eta^2M_{2,N}.
\]

Substitute (1), (3), and (4). \(\square\)

\textbf{Qualification.} The estimate (5) is far weaker than the numerically observed decay of the weighted variance and does not itself prove a decaying variance bound. The stronger estimate \(O(\log N/N^2)\) is established in \emph{Uniform Bulk Modular Asymptotics and the Leading Wilson Entropy Law}. Its value is that it is independent of any unproved pointwise modular-eigenvalue comparison.

\subsection{4. Consequence for the Wilson--Dirac modular correction}\label{consequence-for-the-wilsondirac-modular-correction}

For the mass-scaled Wilson--Dirac covariance perturbation \(\Delta_N\), use its established full-line-projector decomposition compressed to the interval, \(\Delta_N=S_N+D_N\), with positive operator \(G_N\). In the common eigenbasis \(v_j\) of \(B_N,J_N\), put \(b_j=\langle v_j,B_Nv_j\rangle\), \(\lambda_j=\langle v_j,J_Nv_j\rangle\), \(q_j=\langle v_j,G_Nv_j\rangle\), \(e_j=\langle v_j,E_Nv_j\rangle\). Established inputs are

\[
\operatorname{Tr}G_N\le M_\mu:=\frac\mu\pi,
\quad |\langle v_j,S_Nv_j\rangle|\le q_j,
\quad |\langle v_j,D_Nv_j\rangle|\le2\sqrt{q_jb_j(1-b_j)}.
\]

\textbf{Corollary 2 (transverse modular correction).} For each fixed \(\mu>0\),

\[
\boxed{\left|\operatorname{Tr}(D_NE_N)\right|
\le2\sqrt{\frac\mu\pi\operatorname{Tr}(W_NE_N^2)}
=O_\mu(\sqrt{\log N})=o(\log N).}
\tag{7}
\]

\emph{Proof.} Apply the pairing bound, sum over common eigenvectors and use Cauchy--Schwarz. Then apply Theorem 1. \(\square\)

Combining (7) with the exact two-bond asymptotic established in the companion Wilson reference-geometry result, bounded nonlinear correction via the Jacobi fourth moment, and bounded Wilson cross-boundary entropy difference yields the leading-order reduction

\[
\boxed{\mathcal D_L(\mu)=\frac{\mu^2}{6}\log L
+\operatorname{Tr}(S_NE_N)+O_\mu(\sqrt{\log L}),\qquad N=2L.}
\tag{8}
\]

The leading coefficient follows if \(\operatorname{Tr}(S_NE_N)=o(\log L)\). Neither (7) nor (8) proves this condition alone; \emph{Uniform Bulk Modular Asymptotics and the Leading Wilson Entropy Law} supplies the stronger estimate \(O_\mu(1)\).

\subsection{5. Fractional spectral moments and weaker sufficient hypotheses}\label{fractional-spectral-moments-and-weaker-sufficient-hypotheses}

The established positive-majorant spectral-tail estimate is

\[
\nu_N((t,\infty)):=\sum_{|\lambda_j|>t}q_j
\le\min\left\{M_\mu,\frac{a_\mu}{t}+\frac{b_\mu}{t^2}\right\},
\quad a_\mu=\sqrt{\frac2{15}}\frac{\mu^2}{\pi},\quad b_\mu=\frac\mu{3\pi}.
\tag{9}
\]

\textbf{Lemma 3 (uniform fractional majorant moment).} For every \(0<\alpha<1\) and every even \(N\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_jq_j|\lambda_j|^\alpha
\le M_\mu+\frac{\alpha a_\mu}{1-\alpha}
+\frac{\alpha b_\mu}{2-\alpha}.}
\tag{10}
\]

\emph{Proof.} Use layer-cake: \(\sum q_j|\lambda_j|^\alpha=\alpha\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}\nu_N((t,\infty))dt\). Integrate the total-mass bound on \((0,1)\) and the inverse-power bound on \((1,\infty)\). \(\square\)

\textbf{Corollary 4 (sufficient sublinear spectral envelope).} If there exist \(0<\alpha<1\) and \(C_\alpha<\infty\), independent of even \(N\), such that

\[
|e_j|\le C_\alpha(1+|\lambda_j|^\alpha)\quad\text{for every reference eigenpair},
\tag{11}
\]

then \(|\operatorname{Tr}(S_NE_N)|=O_{\mu,\alpha}(1)\), and

\[
\mathcal D_L(\mu)=\frac{\mu^2}{6}\log L+O_\mu(\sqrt{\log L}).
\tag{12}
\]

The envelope (11) is only a \textbf{sufficient, unresolved hypothesis}, and is weaker at large \(|\lambda|\) than a logarithmic eigenpair envelope.

\textbf{Alternative sufficient condition (uniform sublinearity).} One can weaken (11) further: suppose that for each fixed \(R<\infty\), the modular errors are uniformly bounded on \(|\lambda_j|\le R\), and

\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{N\text{ even},j:\,|\lambda_j|>R}
\frac{|e_j|}{|\lambda_j|}=0.
\tag{13}
\]

Then \(\operatorname{Tr}(S_NE_N)=o(\log N)\) and the leading logarithmic law follows. Indeed, for any \(\varepsilon>0\), (13) bounds \(|e_j|\le\varepsilon|\lambda_j|\) outside a fixed window while the window contributes \(O(1)\). By layer cake and (9), \(\sum_jq_j|\lambda_j|\le M_\mu+a_\mu\log N+b_\mu\), up to harmless fixed-size adjustments. Dividing the resulting estimate by \(\log N\), taking the limsup, then letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), proves the claim. Condition (13) is \textbf{not currently established}.

\subsection{6. Scope and sources}\label{scope-and-sources}

Theorem 1, Corollary 2, and Lemma 3 are analytic consequences of the stated finite sine-kernel model and the referenced, separately established Jacobi/majorant results. They use no numerically inferred uniform limit. The longitudinal modular estimate is supplied separately in \emph{Uniform Bulk Modular Asymptotics and the Leading Wilson Entropy Law}. None of these statements proves the proposed full finite part or the \(O_\mu((\log L)^{-1/103})\) remainder.
