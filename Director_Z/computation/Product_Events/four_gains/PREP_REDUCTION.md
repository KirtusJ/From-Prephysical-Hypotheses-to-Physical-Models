# PREP for four gains reduces to the one-gain preparation (Director Z, 14 November 2026)

**Status.** Proof by the director, written before any check. To be challenged and formalized. Nothing here is in the paper yet.

## Notation

The notation is that of `FINDINGS.md`.

- **Arcs and gains.** The four arcs carry gains a: (x,R)→(x+1,R), b: (x,R)→(x−1,L), c: (x,L)→(x+1,R) and d: (x,L)→(x−1,L), all in a finite abelian group A. Put δ = ad(bc)⁻¹ and H = ⟨a, d, bc⟩ = ⟨a, bc, δ⟩.
- **The comparison carrier D_δ.** This is the same digraph with gains (1, 1, 1, δ): every arc has gain 1 except (x,L)→(x−1,L), which has gain δ. It is the carrier of G10 Lemma 5.34 with h = δ, of order r = ord δ.
- **Events.** Events are sets of walks from (0,R). A position product event of length T is fixed by sets of sites A_1, …, A_T. For an event S and a vertex y, m_S(y) and m^δ_S(y) are the multisets of gains, in the four-gain carrier and in D_δ respectively, of the walks of S that end at y.

## Lemma R (recoding)

Let w be a walk on the line of length T ≥ 0 from (0,R) to site x. Put λ = (T−x)/2 and κ_{T,x} = a^x (bc)^λ. Then:

- if w ends on R, g(w) = κ_{T,x} · g_δ(w);
- if w ends on L, g(w) = κ_{T,x} · bδd⁻¹ · g_δ(w).

Here g is the gain in the four-gain carrier and g_δ the gain in D_δ. Hence, for every event S of walks of length T:

- m_S(x,R) = T_{κ_{T,x}} m^δ_S(x,R);
- m_S(x,L) = T_{κ_{T,x} b δ d⁻¹} m^δ_S(x,L).

**Proof.**
1. Let ρ be the number of right steps, λ the number of left steps and n the number of LR arcs. By Lemma 0 of `FINDINGS.md`:
   - a walk ending on R has g = a^ρ d^λ δ^(−n);
   - a walk ending on L has g = a^ρ d^(λ−1) b δ^(−n).
2. The same lemma applied to D_δ, where a = b = c = 1 and d = δ (so its δ is δ), gives g_δ = δ^(λ−n) on R and δ^(λ−1−n) on L.
3. Divide, using d/δ = bc/a:
   - on R the ratio is a^ρ (d/δ)^λ = a^(ρ−λ) (bc)^λ = a^x (bc)^λ;
   - on L the ratio is a^ρ (d/δ)^(λ−1) b = κ_{T,x} · (δ/d) · b.

Both ratios depend only on T, x and the rail. ∎

## Proposition P (PREP on the line)

Assume G10 Lemma 5.34 for D_δ on the line. Then PREP(⟨δ⟩) holds on the line for all four gains.

Precisely: for every κ ∈ H, every U ∈ M(κ⟨δ⟩) and every V ∈ M(κ b d⁻¹⟨δ⟩), there are a site x and a position product event S with m_S(x,R) = U and m_S(x,L) = V.

**Proof.**
1. **Choose the target coset.** H is finite and generated by a, bc and δ, so the cosets of ⟨δ⟩ in H are exactly the classes of a^x (bc)^μ with x, μ ≥ 0. Choose x, μ₀ ≥ 0 with κ₀ := a^x (bc)^(μ₀) ∈ κ⟨δ⟩.
2. **Fix the D_δ targets.** Put U' = T_{κ₀⁻¹} U and V' = T_{(κ₀ b δ d⁻¹)⁻¹} V. Both are multisets in ⟨δ⟩: κ b d⁻¹⟨δ⟩ = κ b δ d⁻¹⟨δ⟩, and κ₀ lies in κ⟨δ⟩.
3. **Apply Lemma 5.34.** It gives a position product event S' in D_δ, of some length T₀, with m^δ_{S'}(x,R) = U' and m^δ_{S'}(x,L) = V'. Then T₀ ≡ x (mod 2). Put λ₁ = (T₀ − x)/2.
4. **Padding.** Let S'' be the event "first a step to the left (A_1 = {−1}), then a step to the right (A_2 = {0}), then S' shifted by two ticks". In D_δ the prefix is the single walk (0,R) → (−1,L) → (0,R), of gain 1, so m^δ_{S''} = m^δ_{S'} at the same site. Its length is T₀ + 2, so λ grows by one. Repeating this gives the same D_δ multisets at every length T₀ + 2k.
5. **Choose the length.** Let e = ord(bc). Choose k ≥ 0 with λ₁ + k ≡ μ₀ (mod e). For the padded event S of length T = T₀ + 2k, (bc)^((T−x)/2) = (bc)^(μ₀), so κ_{T,x} = κ₀.
6. **Conclude.** By Lemma R, m_S(x,R) = T_{κ₀} U' = U and m_S(x,L) = T_{κ₀ b δ d⁻¹} V' = V. ∎

## Corollary (four-gain line theorem with one cited lemma in place of the hypothesis)

On the line, for every finite abelian A and all four gains, assume the rule is a sum over end vertices of site-independent functions F_R, F_L of the multiset of gains, consistent with factor 2 on position product events. Then on multisets that occur:

- F_R = F_L = α|m| if r is odd;
- F_R = F_L = α|m| + γ|σ_χ(m)|² if r is even, where χ(δ) = −1.

Conversely, these rules are consistent on all events (Theorem 6). This is Corollary 4 of `FINDINGS.md` with PREP(⟨δ⟩) replaced by Director G's Lemma 5.34. For a = b = c = 1 it is G10 Theorem 5.36 on the line.

## Rings: where the reduction stops

On ℤ_N, ρ is fixed only modulo M = N/gcd(N,2). Walks that differ by winding j have gains in the ratio (a/d)^(Mj) in the four-gain carrier and δ^(−Mj) in D_δ.

So the recoding ratio carries the factor (aδ/d)^(Mj) = (a²/(bc))^(Mj) = B^(−Mj), with B = bc/a².

- **When B^M = 1.** The ratio is fixed. Then (a/d)^M = (Bδ)^(−M) = δ^(−M), so Δ_N = ⟨δ⟩, and Proposition P holds on rings with M ≥ 6 by the ring case of Lemma 5.34.
- **When B^M ≠ 1.** The reduction fails, and PREP(Δ_N) stays a hypothesis.

The condition B^M = 1 is the group form of ω = χ(B)^M = 1 for every χ: exactly the quantity that position masks read (G10 Corollary 5.40).

## What would break this

- Lemma 0 being wrong. The challenger confirmed it in 112 of 112 line cases and 896 of 896 ring cases.
- Lemma 5.34 being wrong (Director G's; replayed but not reproved).
- The padding changing the D_δ multiset (it does not: the prefix walk is unique and has gain 1).
- A coset of ⟨δ⟩ in H not reachable as a^x (bc)^μ with x, μ ≥ 0. Every element of a finite group is a non-negative power of itself, so all are reachable.
