**Label: STIPULATED CANDIDATE FOR DISPROOF.** Not a claim about the world, not an ontology; no gate of the program is passed by it. ## Name

**Odd Lap.**

## One line

A closed world whose every lap flips the sign: of the two squared rules that a ring with two classes of winding admits, this world uses the one in which a history that has gone once more round is added with a minus.

## What it commits to

- One rule, with no free constant: at each end vertex the weight is (ε₀ − ε₁)², the square of the difference of the signed sums of the two winding classes.
- A world that can tell whether its own size is even or odd: one experiment with two histories (the fork) is dark at one port when M is odd and dark at the other when M is even.
- Completeness under its stipulations: no other rule satisfies them (G10 Theorem 5.61 and the composition argument in FORM.md).

## What it is not

- Not a derivation of the squared rule. The count is removed by a stipulation (O5), and the form of the rule (a sum over end vertices of a function of the gains there) is stipulated (O4).
- Not Tally Ring Z2 under a new name. Z2 offered Re(u^M) as an open quantity and was killed on its own criterion: that quantity is classified by phase symmetries. Odd Lap fixes the value (−1), adds composition, and rests on a completeness theorem that did not exist when Z2 was compiled. It inherits Z2's surviving statements (the fork and the earliest separating clock) and says so.
- Not a statement about rotating molecules, loops of wire or any laboratory ring. Its ring is the whole of its space.
- Not physics. It is a toy universe written to be broken.

## Why it is "out there"

It says that a universe closed in one direction comes back to itself with a sign, that this sign is the only thing left to choose once consistency, composition and darkness are demanded, and that the choice is odd. Each of those four clauses is a place to hit it.
