# Candidate Z8, "Two Books": plain-English overview

**Label: STIPULATED CANDIDATE FOR DISPROOF.** Not a claim about the world, not an ontology; no gate of the program is passed by it. Kirtus Justus, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada; compiled with the assistance of Director Z (a Claude session). Governed by [the candidate process](../CANDIDATE_PROCESS.md). Final candidate of the sprint of 15 November 2026.

Picture a tiny universe: a row or a ring of rooms, and something that walks through it one room per tick, either way, turning or not. A "history" is one complete walk. Many different histories can end in the same room at the same tick. Call "ended in this room, at this tick" a record.

The candidate says that every record can be looked up in two books.

**The book of chance.** Each history carries a sign, plus or minus. To find the chance of a record, add up the signs of all the histories behind it and square the result. Histories with opposite signs cancel; a record whose histories cancel completely never happens. This is the rule this research program uses for interference. Here it is simply stipulated.

**The book of heat.** Ignore the signs and count the histories behind the record. The more histories lead to a record, the more ways there were to get there. The book of heat writes down the logarithm of that number and calls it the record's entropy.

Now take the average entropy, weighting each record by its chance. The candidate proves one thing about this number: **if the universe is left alone, it never goes down.** The reason is simple. At each tick, all the histories in a room go on to the same two neighbouring places, so each of those places has at least as many histories behind it as either half of the room had. Chance is passed on room by room, so the average can only rise. It stands still only when every occupied room has all its histories arriving from one side.

Interfering with the universe is different. Blocking some rooms throws histories away, and the average entropy can fall.

The far-fetched part is what the candidate then says, without proof: that this count of histories is what heat-entropy really is; that the direction in which the average rises is the direction of time; and that the universe began with low entropy because it began with a single history.

What it does not do: it does not explain why chances are squares, it defines no energy or temperature, and its second law would hold just as well if chances were plain counts, which the candidate admits. It is a toy, built to be attacked. If any stipulation is shown false, empty or in conflict with another, it is abandoned at once.
