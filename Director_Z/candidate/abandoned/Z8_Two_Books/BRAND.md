## Name

**Two Books.**

## One line

One set of histories is read in two books: the book of chance weighs a record by the square of the signed sum of the histories behind it, the book of heat gives the same record the logarithm of their number, and the second law is a theorem that joins the two.

## What it commits to

1. **A second law that is proved, not assumed.** Averaged over the book of chance, the entropy of the book of heat never falls when the world is left to run, and it rises strictly unless every occupied site has all its histories on one rail (FORM, T-1).
2. **Entropy adds over independent parts** (T-2).
3. **Selection can lower it.** A mask, which keeps some histories and discards others, can make the mean entropy fall (T-3). The law is about a world left alone.
4. **The identifications, which are the far-fetched part and are only stipulated:** the logarithm of the number of histories behind a record is that record's thermodynamic entropy (S4, S5), and the direction in which a world is left to run is the direction of time (S6).

## What it is not

- Not a derivation of the squared rule: S3 stipulates it.
- Not special to the squared rule. The proof of T-1 uses only that weight is conserved site by site, which the plain count also satisfies. The candidate says so here, and FORM names it as the first place to hit.
- Not thermodynamics of any real system. No energy, no temperature, no heat bath is defined.
- Not Count Veil (Z3), which made the count the chance and died on the cesium data. Here the count is never a chance.
- Not physics. It is a toy universe written to be broken.

## Why it is "out there"

It says that the quantum description and the thermal description of a world are two readings of the same pile of histories, that the growth of entropy is forced by the way histories merge and has nothing to do with ignorance or coarse-graining, and that a low-entropy beginning is simply a beginning with one history.
