# Candidate Z6, "Odd Lap": plain-English overview

**Label: STIPULATED CANDIDATE FOR DISPROOF.** Not a claim about the world, not an ontology; no gate of the program is passed by it. Kirtus Justus, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada; compiled with the assistance of Director Z (a Claude session). Governed by [the candidate process](../CANDIDATE_PROCESS.md).

Picture a tiny universe that is a ring of rooms. Something walks through it, one room per tick, either clockwise or anticlockwise, and at each tick it may keep going or turn. A "history" is one such walk. Many histories can end in the same room at the same tick.

Each history carries a tally that changes as it walks. The simplest part of the tally is a sign, plus or minus. When several histories end in the same room, the universe does not count them; it adds their signs, and the chance of finding the walker there goes with the square of that sum. Two histories with opposite signs cancel, and the room is dark. This much is ordinary: it is how this research program models interference, and it is stipulated here, not derived.

On a ring there is one more thing a history can do: go all the way round. Two histories can end in the same room having gone round a different number of times. The mathematics of this program shows that, under the candidate's stipulations, there are then exactly two possible universes. In one, a history that has gone round once more is added as it is. In the other it is added with its sign flipped.

**Odd Lap says: we are in the second one.** Every lap flips the sign. Nothing forces this; it is the one free choice left, and the candidate makes it and stands by it.

What follows is definite. Send out just two histories, one clockwise and one anticlockwise, and let them meet on the far side of the ring. One step later there are two rooms where they can be found. In the odd-lap universe one of those rooms is completely dark. Which one depends on whether the ring's size (halved, if even) is an odd or an even number. So the inhabitants can learn the parity of their universe's size from a single experiment, and they can tell their universe from the even-lap one.

What the candidate does not do: it does not explain why chances go with squares, it does not say what the rooms or the walker are, and it says nothing about laboratory rings. It is a toy, built to be attacked. If any one of its seven stipulations is shown false, empty or in conflict with another, it is abandoned at once.
