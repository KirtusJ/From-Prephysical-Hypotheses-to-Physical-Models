# Candidate Z10, "Slow Clock": brand

**Label: STIPULATED CANDIDATE FOR DISPROOF.** Not a claim about the world, not an ontology; no gate of the program is passed by it. Kirtus Justus, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada; compiled with the assistance of Director Z (a Claude session). Governed by [the candidate process](../CANDIDATE_PROCESS.md). Final candidate of the sprint of 15 November 2026.

## Name

**Slow Clock.**

## One line

On the signed line, the record of an interfering world ages at a fixed fraction of a tick per tick in the long run, c* = 0.771553…, while the record of a counting world on the same histories ages at one tick per tick.

## What it commits to

1. **A number.** The age per tick of the interfering world tends to c* = (1/(π√2)) ∫ h((1 + sin θ/√2)/2) / (1 − sin²θ/2) dθ over (−π/2, π/2), with h the binary entropy; c* = 0.7715533031636 to thirteen places. The counting world's tends to 1.
2. **A forward prediction not computed by its author.** With gap(T) = c* − (age at T)/T: gap(2400) lies strictly between 0.0020 and 0.0035. The author computed the gap only up to T = 1200 (0.016472, 0.009068, 0.004950 at 300, 600, 1200) and has not looked beyond.
3. **An inequality.** At every length the interfering age is at most the counting age, with equality exactly at lengths 1 to 4 (computed to length 1200; not proved).
4. **The far-fetched part, stipulated:** age, not the number of ticks, is the time the world's own records keep; so interference slows a world's clock, by the factor c* on this carrier.

## What it is not

- Not a statement about the state of a world. Age belongs to the record of what was done: two worlds in the same state can have different ages, and the candidate says so (this is what killed Two Books, which called the same quantity an entropy of the world).
- Not universal. c* is for this carrier, the signed two-rail line from a sharp start, running free. No claim is made for rings, other signs, other starts or masked events.
- Not a claim that the counting rule is anyone's chance. The counting world is a comparator; as a rule of chance it was killed in this sprint (Count Veil) by the cesium data.
- Not proved. The limit c* and the inequality are computed, not derived. The limiting distribution behind the formula is recalled from the literature and was not inspected.
- Not thermodynamics, not relativity, not physics. "Clock" and "age" are names for a defined quantity. It is a toy written to be broken.

## Why it is "out there"

It says that time, as a world's records keep it, is the mean number of bits of history behind what happens; that cancelling histories make a world younger than its ticks; and that the price has a definite value that anyone can check.
