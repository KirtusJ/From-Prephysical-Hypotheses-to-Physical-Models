# Candidate Z10, "Slow Clock": plain-English overview

**Label: STIPULATED CANDIDATE FOR DISPROOF.** Not a claim about the world, not an ontology; no gate of the program is passed by it. Kirtus Justus, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada; compiled with the assistance of Director Z (a Claude session). Governed by [the candidate process](../CANDIDATE_PROCESS.md). Final candidate of the sprint of 15 November 2026.

Picture a tiny universe: an endless row of rooms and something that walks through it, one room per tick, either way, turning or not. A "history" is one complete walk. After a given number of ticks, many different histories can end in the same room, arriving from the same side. Call that a record.

For every record, count the histories behind it and take the logarithm in base two. That is how many yes-or-no choices, on average, were needed to pick out one of those histories: a number of bits. The candidate calls the average of this number, over the records that actually happen, the **age** of the world. Age is not about where the walker is now. It is about how much history lies behind what happened, like the number of entries in a ledger.

Which records "actually happen" depends on the rule for chances, and here there are two worlds on the same row of rooms.

In the **counting world**, a record's chance is simply the share of histories behind it. There the age grows by very nearly one bit per tick.

In the **interfering world**, each history carries a plus or a minus, the signs behind a record are added, and the chance goes with the square of the sum, so histories can cancel. The chances then favour records far from the middle, which have fewer histories behind them. The candidate says the age of this world grows more slowly, and in the long run by a fixed fraction of a bit per tick: **0.771553…** It gives a formula for that number, and the number was written down before any long run was computed. Up to 1,200 ticks the computed rate climbs towards it from below: 0.7551, 0.7625, 0.7666 at 300, 600 and 1,200 ticks.

The far-fetched part, which is only stipulated: that age is the time a world's own records keep, so that interference makes a world's clock run slow.

What it does not say: nothing about the present state of the world, nothing about rings or other rules of walking, and nothing about real clocks. It is a toy, built to be attacked. The simplest attack is to compute further: the candidate predicts that at 2,400 ticks the rate is short of 0.771553 by more than 0.0020 and less than 0.0035, and its author has not looked.
