Proof. A gadget for u is a product event all of whose walks end at one vertex, with pair u there. P is the set of pairs that have a gadget.
Step 0. Appending one R step keeps u at an R vertex; appending L gives u (from R) or swap u (from L). So P is the same for both directions and closed under swap.
Step 1 (joint lemma). Take gadgets H1 (length t1, pair u) and H2 (length t2 >= 0, pair v; empty means v = (1,0)) and a > t1. Upper branch: R, then H1 translated by one in time and position, then R^k with k = a + t2 - t1. Lower branch: L^a, R, then H2 translated by (a+1, 1-a). The two branches share no site after time 0 (the upper sites have x >= 2 - s up to time t1 + 1 and x >= s - 2 t1 after; the lower sites have x = -s up to time a and x <= s - 2a after). At time a + 1 + t2 their distance is 2D with D >= a - t1 >= 1; the upper continues with L^D and the lower with R^D, meeting at one position X. No walk changes branch; translated walks keep their gains and each suffix contributes one common sign. The event has exactly u' in {u, swap u} at (X,L) and v' in {v, swap v} at (X,R).
Step 2. An event with the single pair u at one vertex and nothing at its partner gives F_L(u) = F_R(u) =: F(u), and F(swap u) = F(u).
Step 3. (1,0) is in P. Step 1 with H2 empty, continued one step, puts u' + (1,0) and swap u' + (1,0) in P; with closure under swap, u + (1,0) and u + (0,1) are in P. Induction on p + q gives (1).
Step 4. Choose a, c so that G = F - a n - c s^2 (n = p + q, s = p - q) vanishes at (1,0) and (2,0). G is symmetric under swap. The continuation of a Step 1 event has u' + v' at (X+1,R) and swap u' + v' at (X-1,L), so G(u+v) + G(u + swap v) = 2G(u) + 2G(v) in all four sign cases (by symmetry of G). u = v = (1,0) gives G(1,1) = 0. Suppose G = 0 on totals at most N, N >= 2, and put g(p) = G(p, N+1-p). With v = (1,0): g(p+1) = -g(p) for p = 0..N. With v = (2,0): g(p+2) = -g(p) for p = 0..N-1. Hence g(p) = g(p+2) = -g(p) = 0 for p <= N-1, then g(N) = g(N+1) = 0. So G = 0 and (2) holds; (3) is direct.
