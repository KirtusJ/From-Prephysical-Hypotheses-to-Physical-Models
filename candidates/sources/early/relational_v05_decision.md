## 2. Additional conditional correspondence theorem

A new elementary but consequential transfer obstruction is proved in the revised technical specification. Suppose a model sends each homomorphic-equivalence class `[A]` to a *nonzero finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space* `H_A` and identifies the quotient categorical product with the **ordinary** Hilbert tensor product, up to isomorphism. Idempotence gives `[A]∧[A]=[A]`, hence `H_A⊗H_A ≅ H_A`, so `d_A²=d_A`. Nonzero finite dimension forces `d_A=1`. This excludes a nontrivial finite-dimensional quantum-system realization **under that strong monoidal contract**.

It is neither a no-go theorem for physical quantum mechanics nor a derivation from a prephysical foundation. Infinite-dimensional, differently composed, graded, weak/lax monoidal, or richer operational realizations do not satisfy the same assumptions and require separate analysis. Importing an ordinary physical tensor-product interpretation to get this negative theorem does not make the tensor product fundamental.

## 3. Why a repair is not free

Enlarging the observation class from Boolean existence tests to all homomorphism counts can recover copy multiplicity. In finite graph theory, the full homomorphism-count profile identifies a graph up to isomorphism (Lovász; see Reggio 2022). Such enlargement changes the equivalence relation; it does not preserve the original compression theorem's meaning. Alternatively adding a formal copy-number grading to the quotient is possible but introduces extra independent data. Neither route has acquired physical authority.

The current model remains a test harness, not an approved foundation. Non-relational, continuous, contextual and process-based candidate formalisms remain legitimate competitors; following established literature does not privilege one as ontology, nor does unfamiliarity rule one out.
