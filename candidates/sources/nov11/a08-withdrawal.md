## 2. Replacement principle: less structure, more competitors

Choose an abstract finite **incidence/exclusion hypergraph** `H=(E,T)` where `E` contains primitive labels and each `T` is a finite compatible/exclusive event family. At this base layer, no physical time, ordering, frequency, wavelength, state, probability, measurement, Hilbert space, metric, physical carrier, tensor factorization, or apparatus is assigned. The use of the term *event* is combinatorial, not an assertion that a temporal occurrence happened.

A later **operational stipulation** may interpret certain `T` as outcome tests, and assign numbers `p_e∈[0,1]` summing to one on each test. This step is not supplied by `H` alone and should not be called a derivation of the Born rule or of experimental observation. One may consider the maximal mathematically consistent probability assignment set or a smaller admissible state subset, but the latter must be selected by a separately stated principle.

Transformations, sequential composition, composites, reversible dynamics, continuous parameters, physical preparations, and instruments form further *independent* bridge obligations. None is a consequence of mere event normalization.

## 3. New proof: finite linear normalization yields a polytope, not the exact qubit ball

**Hypotheses:** `E` and `T` finite, and either (a) every event belongs to at least one normalized test, or (b) every event explicitly satisfies `0≤p_e≤1`.

Let `A` be the test-event incidence matrix. The maximal state set is

`Omega_H = {p in R^{|E|} : p_e ≥ 0, A p = 1}`.

It is the intersection of a finite family of closed half-spaces with an affine subspace. The coverage or explicit upper-bound hypothesis makes it bounded. Thus it is a polytope and has only finitely many extreme points. The normalized complex-qubit state space is affinely isomorphic to the three-dimensional Euclidean unit ball, whose boundary sphere contains uncountably many extreme points. There is no affine isomorphism between them. The same obstruction applies to the real-qubit disk and the higher-dimensional spin-factor balls.

**Self-correction:** Without the coverage or explicit upper-bound condition, an isolated unused event can have arbitrarily large `p_e`, so the raw set is an *unbounded polyhedron*, not necessarily a polytope. Earlier shorthand treating every finite hypergraph assignment set as bounded was overbroad. This corrected theorem must be used in all future statements.

This does not exclude quantum operational reconstructions using infinitely many tests, nonlinear admissibility restrictions, other primitive axioms, or nonfinite structures. It only falsifies the simplistic route through *finite linear test normalization alone*.

## 4. Exact finite competitors on a single test skeleton

Two independent binary tests `T_x={x+,x-}` and `T_z={z+,z-}` with no additional relations admit all expectation pairs `(r_x,r_z)∈[-1,1]^2`, with `P(x+)= (1+r_x)/2` and `P(z+)= (1+r_z)/2`. This is a square, not a disk. The point `(1,1)` obeys both test normalization contracts but violates the qubit-like disk condition `r_x²+r_z²≤1`.

Every square point has a **classical four-ontic-state completion**: choose deterministic hidden outcomes `(s,t)∈{±1}²` and probabilities

`w_{s,t}=[(1+s r_x)(1+t r_z)]/4`.

These weights are nonnegative, normalized, and reproduce both test expectations exactly. The same test names can therefore support the maximal square model, a disk-restricted model, or a classical hidden-variable completion when only the specified binary tests are accessible. Distinct models can be operationally equivalent on this restricted menu.

This observation is *not* a claim that every fully operational quantum experiment admits a classical explanation. Additional experiments, composites and contextual constraints can discriminate the alternatives; those resources are not present in the primitive skeleton.

For an even `K≥4` of evenly spaced supporting measurement directions in a planar representation, test-wise bounds give a regular `K`-gon enclosing the unit disk. Its farthest vertices have radius `sec(pi/K)`; the exact radial excess is `sec(pi/K)-1`. The gap is positive at every finite `K`. These are mathematical resolution limits of the selected finite direction menu, not measured instrument errors.

## 5. Classical reversible dynamics obstruction

A finite classical `n`-state simplex `Delta_n` has exactly `n` extreme vertices. Every affine bijection of the simplex maps extreme vertices to extreme vertices, and is therefore exactly a permutation of the vertices. Its reversible affine automorphism group is the discrete finite group `S_n`.

Any continuous one-parameter homomorphism from connected `(R,+)` into that discrete group has constant image, and must be the identity because the parameter-zero element acts as the identity. Thus no nontrivial **continuous reversible one-parameter flow** is obtained from a fixed finite classical simplex with only such automorphisms.

This does not prevent discrete Markov chains, finite-time permutations, irreversible stochastic semigroups, continuous reversible dynamics on a continuous phase space, or dynamics under different physical primitives. It prevents claiming Hamiltonian flow from a fixed finite classical simplex without further structure. Physical time is not assumed at the incidence layer.

## 6. Nonquantum comparator at the composite interface

Once *an additional operational composite and nonsignalling constraint* is expressly introduced, classical and quantum theories still need not be selected. Define the PR distribution, for inputs/outputs in `{0,1}`,

`P_PR(a,b|x,y)=1/2 if a XOR b = x*y, else 0`.

It is normalized and all local output marginals are `1/2`, independent of the other setting. Its correlators are `E_xy=(-1)^{xy}`, giving `CHSH=4`. Every local deterministic assignment has `CHSH=±2`, hence all local convex mixtures satisfy `|CHSH|≤2`. Standard two-qubit quantum mechanics has the Tsirelson maximum `2sqrt(2)` under its different operator assumptions. No-signalling alone therefore does not select quantum probabilities.

These tests require an operational account of separate inputs, composite outputs and nonsignalling. They must **not** be back-projected as properties of the afrequent, time-free prephysical layer.

## 7. Additional alternative: continuous reversible symmetry does not select complex qubits

For each `d≥2` consider the state ball `Omega_d={r∈R^d:||r||≤1}` and the effects `e_u(r)=(1+u·r)/2` for `||u||≤1`. These models support continuous reversible orthogonal rotations and normalized binary tests. Their dimensions differ, as do their composite and information-processing possibilities. The ordinary complex-qubit Bloch ball corresponds to `d=3`, but continuity and convex positivity alone do not select `d=3` or the complex scalar field. The examples are **alternative stipulated models**, not claims of new physical theories.
