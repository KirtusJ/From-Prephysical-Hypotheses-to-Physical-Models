# A force walk built from two fixed composition contacts

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research extension by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

Can the contact that writes the candidate's record change how a walker moves? Let its two travel directions call two different contacts on a shared memory. Both contacts are fixed throughout space and time. Reversing their order writes a central label; on interfering paths this label records a signed area. In a coherent signed walk the area has exactly the effect of a uniform electric field in each nontrivial Fourier component of the memory. A sharp launch with a blank memory gives a ten-tick return probability of **49/64**, compared with **9/128** when the two contacts commute. The candidate supplies the contact algebra. This extension supplies rails, coherent superposition, the walk clock and ordinary squared-amplitude readout. ## Fixed local operations

Use the signed Hadamard coin followed by a one-site shift on the line. The right output appends contact \(g=e_1\) and the left output appends \(h=e_2\) to the shared register by right multiplication. Use the first two source coordinates \((r,l)\) and the component \(z\) of the central covector along \(e_3^*\). All other components stay zero. The group law on this invariant subspace is
\[
 (r,l,z)(a,b,c)=(r+a,l+b,z+c+(rb-la)/2).
\]
For the submitted candidate the field is \(\mathbb F_5\), where \(1/2=3\). The chronological difference between appending \(g,h\) and \(h,g\) is one central unit. The compensated commutator also writes one unit. These controls act as permutations on orthogonal register labels. We stipulate their coherent linear extensions and isolate the register during flight. A fresh external observer who learns the direction every tick implements a different walk.

From a point launch at zero with a blank register, the source coordinates at tick \(j\) and displacement \(x_j\) are \(r_j=(j+x_j)/2,\ l_j=(j-x_j)/2\). Writing \(d_j=x_{j+1}-x_j\), the group law gives \(4(z_{j+1}-z_j)=x_j-jd_j\). Summation by parts yields
\[
 z_n=\tfrac12\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_j-\tfrac14(n-1)x_n .
\]
Thus every path arriving at the same site and time has the same source coordinates, and its central label differs from half its accumulated departure position by a common endpoint correction. These identities hold in any odd prime field. A single contact appended only on right steps would also leave an ordinary record, the number of right proposals modulo the prime. It is fixed by \((n,x_n)\). Such a tally preserves every interference term within that site, and gives the free position probabilities for a point launch. The central component above can distinguish paths ending at the very same site and rail.

Take a central Fourier label \(k\), with transform convention \(\widehat a(k)=\sum_z a(z)e^{-2\pi i k z/p}\). Translating the register multiplies this component by the corresponding phase. Removing the known endpoint phase gives the departure factor \(e^{-i\Phi_k x_j}\) with \(\Phi_k=2\pi(k\,2^{-1}\bmod p)/p\). Division uses the field inverse. The denominator of the nontrivial field angle for \(p=5\) is five. A trace over a blank central register averages the five sector probabilities with equal weights. More generally a central state independent of the source supplies its Fourier populations to this marginal. Its coherences can matter for a later measurement of a named central label.

## One finite record and one large-flight record

Start on the right rail. At tick ten the two rails at the origin have the following integer amplitudes in central-label order \(0,1,2,3,4\), in units of \(1/32\):
\[
 R=(26,-5,-5,-5,-5),\qquad L=(2,1,1,1,1).
\]
Squaring each and summing gives \(784/1024=49/64\). In the trivial component the two numerators are 6 and 6. In each nontrivial component they are 31 and 1 because the four nontrivial roots sum to minus one. Thus the conditional return rates are \(p_0=9/128\) and \(p_*=481/512\). If the trivial Fourier population is \(w_0\), the marginal return rate is \(w_0p_0+(1-w_0)p_*\). This one terminal read determines \(w_0\) in the model. Redrawing a memory and discarding the written direction every step gives a binomial endpoint law with ten-tick return \(63/256\). No measured frequencies enter these calculations.

For a nontrivial fifth-root field the evolution over five ticks is translation invariant. In momentum \(k'\), use \(D(k')=\mathrm{diag}(e^{ik'},e^{-ik'})\) and the real Hadamard \(H\). The five-step matrix is an ordered product of \(D(k'+j\Phi)H\) for the five consecutive shifted momenta, up to Fourier orientation and choice of period origin. The trace is invariant under \(k'\mapsto k'+\Phi\) by cyclicity. Its Laurent polynomial has only the odd harmonics through order five, leaving \(\pm5\). The all-right and all-left paths give \(2i\,2^{-5/2}\sin(5k')\), up to the Fourier sign. The determinant is minus one. Hence the absolute band velocity per tick is
\[
 2^{-5/2}\frac{|\cos(5k')|}{\sqrt{1-2^{-5}\sin^2(5k')}}\ \le\ 2^{-5/2}.
\]
The two bands are separated. The spectral weak-limit calculation for a localized launch therefore confines the limiting velocity to this interval. Up to four residual ticks cannot alter the weak speed limit: the displacement during those steps is bounded in the Heisenberg position difference, or one can compare their action on smooth functions of position divided by time. This is an asymptotic edge; finite flights have nonzero probabilities beyond it. The rational electric-walk dispersion and velocity calculation also appear in the primary literature. [Cedzich et al., Appendix, equation 25](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.2081) · [Abdul-Rahman et al., Lemma 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15664) · [Grimmett, Janson and Scudo, weak limits](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0309135)

The free component instead has speed edge \(1/\sqrt2\). The cut \(|x_n|/n>1/4\) lies strictly between these two edges. The free ordinary speed density gives a tail \(A=1-2\arctan[(1/4)/\sqrt{1-2(1/4)^2}]/\pi\approx0.833742\). Thus the asymptotic fraction beyond the cut is **exactly \(w_0 A\)**. With a blank it is about 0.166748. In a common launch population the ten-tick return and the far-field tail must give the same \(w_0\). A fixed but unobserved classical choice of electric phases with the stipulated five weights reproduces these walker-only predictions. For position and rail operations preserving the sector this extends to adaptive walker transcripts by expanding their branch operators in the same central Fourier basis. These observations test an effective force and a memory population. To test coherent memory itself requires access to another memory observable.

## What it takes to transport a wave packet

For a superposition over initial sites \(x_0\), keeping the same blank source coordinates at every site is insufficient. After two free ticks, start with \((|-1,R\rangle+|1,R\rangle)/\sqrt2\), a constant ordinary-label blank and a trivial central character. The two routes arriving from different launch sites at \(x=1\) have different accumulated direction counts. Tracing the ordinary labels gives \(3/8\) there. Synchronously encoding the labels yields \(5/8\). This boundary case matters when extending the single-source argument to a quantum envelope.

At each initial site encode the ordinary coordinates \((x_0/2,-x_0/2)\) and translate the central input by \(x_0/4\). This is an isometry on an arbitrary packet and an arbitrary input central state. Now \(r_j=(j+x_j)/2, l_j=(j-x_j)/2\) still hold. With the initial offset the same endpoint correction follows, since summation by parts contributes \(-x_0/4\) to the change in \(z\). At the output erase the two known ordinary labels reversibly using site and time, and translate the center by \((n-1)x_n/4\). On the encoded subspace this gives the *full* walk with central gain \(2^{-1}x_j\) per departure, not only its position marginal. Complex envelope phases require this known decoding. Ordinary position probabilities are insensitive to terminal coordinate-dependent phases and to these redundant ordinary labels.

The field-five candidate has fixed angle spacing and finite memory. To obtain the program's weak-field limit by this mechanism, explicitly **extend the candidate** to odd primes \(p>3\) growing without bound. An algebraically consistent choice is the normalized associator \(6^{-1}\det\) in each field, whose six-order comparison is \(\det\). At five this is the original representative. The group law and contact identity then hold with the same two fixed input labels. In character \(k=2\) the effective angle is \(2\pi/p\). Choose \(F>0\) and \(F\varepsilon\delta^3=2\pi/p\), with site pitch \(\varepsilon\delta\) and tick duration \(\varepsilon\delta^2\). Fixed action \(\varepsilon=1\) supplies the forced Schrödinger envelope. For example \(\varepsilon=\delta=(2\pi/(Fp))^{1/4}\) has \(\delta^2/\varepsilon\to0\). With the previously specified shrinking, single-band input the resulting lattice positions concentrate at \(Y_0+P_0T-FT^2/2\) at each fixed scaled time. The packet preparation, the coherent central character, the growing label space, the scale conversion and ordinary probability readout all enter this deduction. It supplies a return route from the finite algebra to the two conditional mechanics results. Deriving those premises from prephysical records remains open.
