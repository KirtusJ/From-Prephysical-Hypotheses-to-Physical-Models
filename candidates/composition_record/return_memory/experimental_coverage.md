# Which archived measurements constrain the translated Hadamard walk?

One published cesium experiment supplies a quantitative three-step site comparison. A photonic processor supplies both the three- and five-step clocks with a sharp input rail. Neither record contains all the physical source translations of the ten-setting experiment. The distinction matters because the proposed contrast uses a **fixed detector** and a **different incident ensemble at each assigned launch setting**. [Ten settings and detector model](three_grains.md)

## A measured three-step contrast

Robens, Alt, Meschede, Emary and Alberti moved one arm of a cesium walk aside after its initial split. That left a sharp-spin source for three subsequent steps on the undisturbed arm. Figure 3(b,c) reports both mirror preparations. At the same relative wells \(j=-1,+1\), the published atom-count bars give \((107,31)\) in (b) and \((27,115)\) in (c). Each panel reports 404 detected atoms, including 203 and 208 in the displaced regions. The peak-to-tail comparison conditions on the two target wells. Its effective row denominators are 138 and 142. The empirical double odds are \(107(115)/(31(27))=14.70\). Retained, displaced and anomalous counts remain available in the original three-panel extraction. [Original experiment, Fig. 3 and Appendices A–E](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.5.011003) · [Integer bars and all 51 recorded cells](../../../Director_G/Cesium_negative_read.md)

For independent atoms with stable probabilities within each setting, conditioning on the row and column totals gives table weights proportional to \(\binom{134}{x}\binom{146}{138-x}\Theta^x\), where \(x=107\) was observed and \(\Theta\) is the population double odds. Inverting the inclusive lower tail gives a one-sided 95% upper limit of 24.828 for this fixed comparison. A Bonferroni envelope covering either tail, all three row pairs and all ten pairs of the five central wells gives an upper limit of **40.014**. For the ideal walk, squaring each site population a second time and retaining ordinary within-site spin weights gives \(\Theta\ge400\), even with unknown fixed well-by-spin efficiencies. At 400 the inclusive lower-tail probability is \(4.372\times10^{-21}\) or less. Ordinary spin-blind detection predicts 25, close to the unadjusted limit. Dynamics and detection matter here: with complete spin dephasing the site-squared lower bound falls to 36, inside the wider interval. Several atoms can share a sequence, and the per-sequence shot records and initially lost atoms cannot be recovered from the bars. [Conditional likelihood, gain assumptions and dephasing sensitivity](../../../Director_G/Three_step_site_weights_in_cesium.md)

The fluorescence images were registered to each atom's imaged starting site. The public four-step figure gives relative sites under three arm-displacement protocols. It supplies neither acquisition rows grouped by absolute input site nor a five-step acquisition with the same mask. The two observed rows constrain a fixed site-power rival under the stated model. They cannot determine the ten-setting contrast for arbitrary mixtures of powers and resolutions.

## Both clocks in a sharp-source photonic circuit

Federico Pegoraro's *Photons, Space and Time: The many Ways of Quantum Walks*, §5.1.2, Table 3 and Fig. 37, reports genuine single-photon Hadamard walks at steps one through six. The two inputs are \(|0,\uparrow\rangle\) and \(|0,\downarrow\rangle\). Every position–coin output is separately routed to the same superconducting detector. Twelve routing acquisitions produce each row of the heatmap. The thesis reports source parameters, integration and settling times, an expected photon rate and normalized position probabilities. The inspected report supplies no integer numerator and incident-shot denominator for a translated fixed-mask arm. An expected count calculated from the source rate cannot supply either. [Author's thesis, printed pp. 86–88](https://digital.ub.uni-paderborn.de/hs/download/pdf/8260695)

Mathematically, if propagation **and the entire detector law** are translation covariant, one can sum the relative-position histogram over \(A-s\) to predict what source \(s\) would give in an absolute target \(A\). Doing this with measured images assumes the gains, routing losses and reporting law transfer between preparations. More importantly, adding separately scored outputs produces a post-detection sum. A physical apparatus that first pools \(A\) may instead score \(g_q(\sum_{i\in A}p_i)\). For a nonlinear response these are different operations. Where several masks reuse a histogram their estimates also share counts and sampling errors. The experimental profiles can test an explicitly resolved, calibrated output model; they cannot supply ten independently assigned binary-mask rates.

## What the public downloads contain

- Wang, Dong and colleagues' *Efficient learning of mixed-state tomography for photonic quantum walk* reports a five-step Hadamard experiment. The authors identify their public notebook as a **synthetic-data generator**. At revision `fac932f`, its example input contains floating-point probabilities and tomography basis matrices. Neither that input nor the reported supplementary figures provide the translated incident-shot table. [Paper and supplementary inventory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12697567/) · [Authors' pinned repository](https://github.com/Shaojun-Dong/NDO/tree/fac932fb47cf5b047896bf9ce496435a5ffbbabd)
- Camacho, Meinecke and Wolters deposited a 157,607-byte archive for *Quantum walk on a square lattice with identical particles*. Its published MD5, `5ae08a0043dbc6f088a08fb3f09aa6d3`, matches the download. Inspecting the members shows two-particle coincidence arrays and floating-point state tables for the authors' square-lattice calculations. The archive filename `raw_data.zip` does not identify hardware shots. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.111.052416) · [Authors' data deposit](https://zenodo.org/records/15260434)
- Liu, Tornow, Kessler and Barkai released integer multiplicities of complete 20-bit hardware records. The single-excitation preparation has 40,000 incident shots at each of 99 angles and permits a three-tick first-return contrast with a sampling interval. Those jobs used three monitored spins, a site-0 source and varying link angles. The times and translations of the nine-site experiment are different. [Original data deposit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18429670) · [Counts and simultaneous confidence intervals](angle_pairs/README.md)

The same three-spin archive contains a one-bit site-occupation read at each measurement clock. The proposed nine-tick experiment uses **two position bits at each of three clocks**, with independently assigned quarter-turn signs between measurements. Neither the unmeasured position bits nor the response to those interventions can be recovered from the archived strings.
