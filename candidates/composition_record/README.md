## The test that separates the claims

The model starts with three-component labels over the field of five elements. Two ways of combining three labels have the same sum. A further scalar records the stipulated discrepancy. Its alternating sum over six orders is unchanged when an arbitrary calibration is added to each pair. For the determinant seed this scalar equals the determinant itself.

After two labels \(a,b\) reach the reversible contacts in the specified order and its reverse, the target stores the covector \(u(c)=\det(a,b,c)\). A later label \(c\) receives a copy signal exactly when this number is nonzero. Distinguishing future outputs requires at least 32 distinguishable states after one write if only binary queries are allowed, and 125 if arbitrary further additive writes are allowed. These are state counts for a deterministic interface, with all accessible information counted. They supply a particularly direct objection to the proposed physical origin: before the displayed copy, the theory already requires distinguishable alternatives that survive until the later choice.

For a further test, give a simulator those same accessible registers and implement each stipulated reversible operation by its permutation table, with composition of its operations strictly associative. The simulator can also retain scratch, undo a write, isolate a source or give the experimenter a late choice. For a fixed input both machines first have matching visible states. If they match before a control, applying the same table gives matching next states and readings. Induction covers *every finite adaptive protocol* generated by these controls, including choosing the next contact after a previous reading. Shared external randomization gives the same transcript distribution by conditioning on its choices. The packet's silent-pointer example proves the other direction: one can keep the mathematical discrepancy while every accessible pointer stays blank. The working writer is a constructive possibility under its additional postulate. Independently identifying an intervention on the physical composition law, while holding the reader fixed, would give the two interpretations different tests. No such intervention is specified.

There is a quantitative way to state the missing physical test. Suppose an independently characterized intervention could change the alleged seed from zero to the determinant while the source labels stay at \(e_1,e_2\) and the late probe at \(e_3\). The ideal linked writer predicts a blank in one arm and \(e_3\) in the other. A detached reader driven only by other information \(N\) gives the same conditional output kernel in both arms. For any flag event its rate difference is then at most the total variation distance between the arms' distributions of \(N\): integrate the common conditional flag probability, which lies between zero and one. If that distance has an *independent* upper bound \(\ell\), a lower confidence bound on the observed rate difference exceeding \(\ell\) excludes this detached-reader explanation. A device told which table to emulate has access to the arm and can change its output perfectly. Random assignment alone supplies no bound on information reaching it. The candidate provides neither the independent seed switch nor a physical bound on that information. This is an identification criterion a future realization could try to meet.

## What calibrated contacts can identify

Write \(C_j(b,c)=\lfloor(b_j+c_j)/5\rfloor\) with coordinate representatives 0 through 4. On fixed input labels and a fixed scalar unit, each normalized scalar 3-cocycle class has a representative

\[
 \omega(a,b,c)=s\det(a,b,c)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}M_{ij}a_i C_j(b,c).
\]

This classification uses the cohomology algebra of an elementary abelian group with trivial field coefficients. Its degree-three part has one exterior triple and nine products of a degree-one generator with a degree-two Bockstein. The coordinate carry represents that Bockstein. The six signed terms of the determinant have the same ordered exterior class, and six equals one in this field. Our finite checks rely on this classification for exhaustiveness. [Beli, explicit formulas for the cohomology of elementary abelian groups, pp. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11868)

Hold a reference label \(h\) fixed and define the contact \(\beta_h(x,y)=\omega(h,x,y)+\omega(x,y,h)-\omega(x,h,y)\). At that reference, form an extension with central scalar addition and extra contact \(\beta_h\). The commutator of lifts of \(x,y\) has central value \(K_h(x,y)=s\det(h,x,y)\). Five successive equal lifts of \(x\) have central value \(L_h(x)=h^{T}Mx\). Indeed the symmetric carry cancels in the commutator. In five additions the total carry in coordinate \(j\) is exactly the integer representative of \(x_j\). A pair recalibration changes \(\beta_h\) by a two-coboundary at fixed \(h\). Its antisymmetric part vanishes and the sum around five additions telescopes with five times a scalar equal to zero. These conclusions use fixed scalar units, comparable readings and access to the unalternated contact for the fifth-power test.

For example, add \(a_1 C_2-a_2 C_1\) to the determinant. Its matrix is skew, so \(L_h(h)=0\) for every one of the 125 labels. Every commutator agrees with the determinant. Holding \(h=e_1\) and repeating \(x=e_2\) gives one, whereas reversing their roles gives four. The determinant gives zero on both. Measuring the unalternated contact on each of the three coordinate planes would also suffice to rule out all nine carry components *if* one could verify triviality of the restricted class on each plane: the ordered basis pairs in those planes cover every matrix entry. A lone detection of a binary flag lacks a scalar calibration. The sending agent also obtained the ten-scalar panel and diagonal ambiguity in its follow-up. ## Where the proposed carrier needs an extra convention

The full contact at fixed \(h\) is a scalar. Storing all raw contacts in a single covector that a later free choice of \(h\) can evaluate imposes linearity in \(h\). A permitted recalibration can violate that condition even for the determinant class. Use \(f(a,b)=a_1^2 b_2^2\) and replace \(\omega\) by \(\omega+\delta f\). At \(x=y=e_1\) the recalibrated raw contact is \(-2h_2^2\). Its values at \(e_2\) and \(2e_2\) are three and two, while twice three is one modulo five. No covector has those readings. All closed commutators and fifth powers still agree. The packet's alternating writer and its particular five-contact counterexample survive this attack. Extending its raw contacts to arbitrary representatives requires a chosen section, a separate fixed-reference scalar contact, or a larger memory.

## A noisy test of memory after the sources disconnect

The [blank carrier capacity test](The_capacity_of_a_blank_contact_carrier.md) additionally requires the primitive contacts to implement their reversible group action. Independent late probes then distinguish 125-state compiled writers from classical carriers implementing individual contacts, with an exact error bound for each size cap.

Choose a source class uniformly, isolate the carrier, and only then draw the later setting independently and uniformly. To query a direction, fix one nonzero representative for each of the 31 lines in the three-dimensional space. Record every trial. An incorrect scalar, wrong flag or missed answer counts as an error. The question is how well a reader can do with one fewer classical memory state than the packet requires for exact predictions. Count every source-dependent channel that remains accessible to the reader. Prearranged shared randomness may be independent of the test inputs. This is an application of the prepare-and-measure method for bounding classical memory through observable responses. [Gallego et al., classical and quantum dimension tests](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.5064)

| Source population and late setting | Maximum retained classical states | Least achievable average error under that cap |
|---|---:|---:|
| 32 binary response classes, followed by one of 31 probe directions | 31 | \(5/496\), about 1.01% |
| 125 covectors, followed by a scalar query on one of 31 directions | 124 | \(1/155\), about 0.65% |
| 125 covectors, followed by a supplied additive offset from all 125 and a binary query on one of 31 directions | 124 | \(2/775\), about 0.26% |

For the last row only the memory *at source disconnection* is capped. The reader may receive both later choices and use arbitrary scratch. The preparation distribution in the first row is uniform on response classes, including the always-blank response. Choosing two source labels uniformly would give different weights.

To derive the errors, regard a source as a row in a table with one answer per later setting. If two source rows share a memory state, the decoder must make at least one error in each column where the rows differ. With uniform inputs the minimum is the closest pair's Hamming distance divided by the number of source–setting pairs. A reader attains it by merging just that pair, answering as the first member would, and coding the other rows separately. The same bound holds for randomized strategies by fixing all input-independent random coins before averaging. For binary queries a nonzero row has six zero directions. Two different kernel planes share one line, so their binary rows differ in ten places; each differs from the blank row in 25. For raw scalar queries distinct covectors differ in 25 of the 31 directions. For binary queries after an offset, two source covectors differ on 25 directions before the offset. In each such direction, 25 offsets make the first output zero and 25 other offsets make the second zero. Thus the closest rows differ at 1,250 of the 3,875 late settings. [Exact memory check](check_memory_errors.py) · [All row-distance counts and attaining codes](memory_errors.json)

Error below a row's threshold, with sampling uncertainty and the stated isolation accounted for, would exclude that classical memory cap. Even a perfect pass would leave the associative simulator available with sufficient memory. No such experiment has been performed.
