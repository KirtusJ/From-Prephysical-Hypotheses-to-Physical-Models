# Can a late query distinguish a coherent write from a random phase?

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Research supplement by Director G (OpenAI Codex).

The submitted finite writer adds a covector \(u\) to memory. A later choice of \(h\) asks whether its value on \(h\) is zero. Suppose two coherent routes can control whether the writer acts. **Every measurement on the routes alone, including an adaptive sequence of further controlled translations and erasures, admits an exact classical random-phase simulation.** A joint query does more. After a calibrated conditional undo, count bright route **and** blank flag on the same trial. An initially blank coherent write gives this event with probability one. Every separable route–memory state at source disconnection gives at most one half when \(u(h)\ne0\). This is a conditional test of a quantum implementation of the proposed memory. It assumes coherent control of the submitted permutations, a calibrated recombiner and the ordinary probability rule. ## Why arbitrary path-only controls can miss the memory

Let \(G=(\mathbb F_5^3)^*\), with 125 possible memory values. On the stipulated Hilbert-space extension take basis \(|m\rangle\), \(m\in G\), and translations \(T_u|m\rangle=|m+u\rangle\). All \(T_u\) are diagonal in the same Fourier basis \(|k\rangle\), with eigenvalues the characters \(\chi_k(u)\) (choose the Fourier sign accordingly). A route-controlled translation is block diagonal in \(k\); its \(k\)-block applies the corresponding phase on that route. Operations on the routes and their external apparatus also preserve these blocks. An entire path-only measurement branch, with later choices dependent on earlier outcomes, has the same property by composition of its Kraus operators. The final probability uses only diagonal memory blocks, since the memory is traced out. Therefore one simulator draws a **single** character for the entire launch, carries it across all later controls, and applies the associated phases. For a product preparation its draw has probabilities \(\langle k|\rho_M|k\rangle\); for initially correlated inputs it uses the corresponding conditional route state. Zero-weight blocks are absent. Giving each translation fresh independent phases would generally destroy the return echo.

This statement isolates a concrete limitation of inference from a route echo. Distinct dilations of a dephasing channel, including classical feedback-assisted corrections, have long been studied. The common translation eigenbasis here supplies the stronger multistep simulation for the stated operations. A measurement on the memory or a control that mixes its character blocks changes the hypothesis. [Buscemi, Chiribella and D’Ariano, *Inverting quantum decoherence by classical feedback from the environment*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.95.090501)

## A test with one of the existing binary flags

Prepare a blank memory and balanced routes. Apply \(T_u\) on route 1 only, leaving the joint state \((|0\rangle|0\rangle+|1\rangle|u\rangle)/\sqrt2\) at disconnection. The tester subsequently applies \(T_{-u}\) on route 1, recombines the routes at relative phase zero, and queries the submitted binary flag. For fixed nonzero \(h\), put \(K=\{m:m(h)=0\}\) and let \(P_K\) project onto its 25 memory labels. The yes event combines bright (route \(|+\rangle\)) with blank (memory in \(K\)). Use all attempted trials in its denominator. An ideal forward write followed by its inverse gives probability one.

For any normalized product state \((a|0\rangle+b|1\rangle)|\psi\rangle\) **at disconnection**, the joint event has probability

\[
 \frac12\|a P_K\psi+b P_KT_{-u}\psi\|^2
 \le\frac12\big(\|P_K\psi\|^2+\|P_{K+u}\psi\|^2\big).
\]

This follows by the triangle inequality and the scalar Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with \(|a|^2+|b|^2=1\). When \(u(h)\ne0\), the cosets are disjoint, giving a sharp limit of \(1/2\). A route-0 input with memory in \(K\) attains it. Convexity extends the bound to any separable mixed state, including shared classical randomness and local quantum states conditioned on that randomness. For any nonzero write, 25 of the 31 projective probe directions meet the condition. The other six have \(K+u=K\). A balanced product route and a uniform memory on a \(T_u\)-orbit in \(K\) then give the event with probability one. The same orbit construction on all of \(G\) gives unit echo brightness for any write if the flag is ignored.

The result concerns entanglement across a specified cut with a trusted inverse and joint measurement. A device emulating the whole circuit with access to settings and outputs can still reproduce the transcript. A different physical source of the same translation also passes. The test identifies neither the proposed compositional origin of \(u\) nor a probability law from prephysical assumptions. The gate, flag and detection acceptance require independent calibration.

## An independent physical measurement of coherent route–memory correlations

Chan and colleagues coupled a photonic early/late time bin to a quantum-dot spin and measured both in three complementary bases. They conditioned on photon–spin readout coincidences. Their acquired file gives the following uncorrected table. Each row contains two outcomes of the photonic analyzer for one spin projection followed by the two outcomes for its opposite projection. In the Z row the photon outcomes are early/late; X and Y use the two photon detector ports in the middle window. [Published paper and methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-023-00717-5) · [Author manuscript and supplement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12844) · [Frozen data and laboratory scripts](https://doi.org/10.17894/ucph.3b3bf28d-1f43-4b81-a03d-c9a9d3029b46)

| Analyzer | First pair | Second pair | Coincidences |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Z | 36, 255 | 160, 69 | 520 |
| X | 237, 46 | 61, 118 | 462 |
| Y | 32, 64 | 105, 23 | 224 |

Applying the authors' phase and port convention, let \(p_j\) be the fraction in the middle two entries of row \(j\), and \(M_j=2p_j-1\). The Bell fidelity estimator is \(F=p_Z/2+|M_X-M_Y|/4\). This gives **0.66047** with propagated standard error **0.01951** using the laboratory script's independent Poisson-count model. Either of the two Bell projectors selected by the sign has squared overlap at most \(1/2\) with every product state. Absolute value thus preserves the population separability bound, though finite-sample maximization can bias an estimator. The paper reports raw \(0.66\pm0.02\) and background-corrected \(0.743\pm0.023\). We reconstructed the raw result. This is evidence for coherence shared by distinct optical and spin degrees of freedom under the authors' analyzer and sampling assumptions. A variable detection efficiency across settings or outcomes can change the reconstruction. The apparatus implements neither the 125-valued carrier nor the joint inverse/flag experiment.
