# A terminal position refinement recovers the reflected history law

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. Mathematical development and verification with OpenAI Codex.

On the signed reflecting Hadamard walk, a single walker can leave a succession of position-region records. Suppose the detector has been calibrated so that its score is proportional to the squared branch norm whenever that norm is at most one half. Resolving a few extra position intervals **only at the final clock** then gives the ordinary joint history law exactly on every sufficiently large walk. An observer can discard the extra position label after detection. This transfers the established single-velocity, elastically reflected trajectory limit to the readout under the assumptions below. ## Which table the detector scores

Use the reflecting wells, sharp localized source and fixed finite sequence of position partitions of the history theorem. Write $t_j/L\to\tau_j$ and take $\tau_d>0$ at the last read. The boundary of each coarse bin has zero mass at its limiting clock. At each clock write the current region coherently to a fresh orthogonal register and retain it. The write acts as the isometry $\psi\mapsto\sum_B |B\rangle\otimes P_B\psi$ and preserves all rail and site coherence within each region. Subsequent prescribed walk steps act on the walker. There is no read, erasure or outcome-dependent change of evolution before the terminal score. The squared norm $w_L(h)$ of each labeled branch is a kinematic quantity. Completeness of the projectors and unitarity of the steps give $\sum_h w_L(h)=1$.

On a launch of type $f$, the hypothesis is a **single** terminal score $f(w_L(h))/\sum_g f(w_L(g))$ on the specified resolved records. For almost every eligible launch, $f(x)=c_f x$ simultaneously on $[0,1/2]$, with $c_f>0$. The calibration must transfer to this preparation and record resolution. The type may be redrawn between launches. Independently weighting every intermediate outcome, or first sampling one path, specifies a different instrument.

## Why three final intervals suffice

For a fixed launch rail state the limiting final position is $Y=F(y+\tau_d V)$, where $F(s)=\operatorname{dist}(s,2\mathbb Z)$ and $V$ has the two-band velocity measure of the walk. Its law is atomless. Each band velocity is a nonconstant analytic function of momentum. The preimage of any given folded position is a finite union of velocity level sets on this bounded range, each of momentum measure zero. Each band weight is integrable against momentum. Choose cuts at the two terciles of $Y$, so each of three intervals $C$ has limiting mass $1/3$. At the final clock refine each original bin $B$ by its intersections with these intervals.

For any such $C$, let $M_L(C)$ be the sum of the kinematic refined-word masses whose final position falls in $C$, summing over **all earlier recorded prefixes**. The finite-word convergence theorem applies to these refined bins. Summing finitely many limits and covering all earlier bins yields $M_L(C)\to\Pr(Y\in C)=1/3$. This uses the terminal marginal *with the earlier writes in place*. No identity with the uninterrupted finite-walk marginal is needed. The strict margin and finite collection of intervals give one $L_0$ beyond which each $M_L(C)<1/2$. Every refined word lies in such a $C$, hence is light enough for the linear calibration. The terminal normalizer is then exactly $c_f$ on each calibrated launch.

For an earlier branch vector $v$ propagated to the last clock, orthogonality gives the finite-size identity

$$
\sum_C\|P_{B\cap C}v\|^2=\|P_Bv\|^2 .
$$

Thus summing the *reported refined frequencies* recovers the ordinary coarse squared-norm history law for each $L\ge L_0$. For a mixed source the same identity follows by linearity. Scoring the coarse words directly could yield a different law if one of them exceeds half. ## Exact wells, unknown rail states and paths

**Every final well can be resolved.** Each final well belongs to one of the three witnessing intervals. Even if only exact final wells are reported, summing their squared norms within an interval gives the same $M_L(C)$ by orthogonality. Thus the growing number of exact last-well labels needs no extra convergence theorem. A more precise read at an *earlier* clock can destroy coherence that would otherwise affect later positions. [A two-clock example](../../Director_G/One_exact_site_record_changes_a_ballistic_history.md)

**Five fixed intervals cover all localized input rail states.** At a common source well, choose the terminal quintiles for the maximally mixed two-rail state $I/2$. For any recorded terminal interval $C$, the sum of the branch effects on the input rails is a positive two-by-two matrix $E_{C,L}$. Every rail state $\rho$ satisfies $\operatorname{tr}(E_{C,L}\rho)\le\operatorname{tr}E_{C,L}=2\operatorname{tr}(E_{C,L}I/2)$. The five reference marginals tend to $1/5$. Eventually all are less than $1/4$ and all five masses are below $1/2$ uniformly in $\rho$. This gives the exact readout conclusion even if $\rho$ varies with $L$; identifying one particular limiting velocity law also requires an appropriate limit for the source.

**A trajectory requires a schedule.** At each fixed mesh level of the reflected-path theorem use the last-clock refinement and then forget it. Increase that level's threshold in $L$ to secure both the half-mass bound and the theorem's word-law approximation. Its diagonal choice of increasingly fine meshes and interpolation therefore gives weak convergence in the uniform path metric to $t\mapsto F(y+tV)$ with one velocity draw for the entire path. The proof is an existence result for these schedules. It does not give a rate for arbitrary growing schedules. ## Three clocks for which the original read already suffices

Start on the right rail at $y=1/4$, recording left/right halves at scaled times $1/2,1,2$. Except on null boundaries the first bit is 1 exactly for $V>1/2$. At $v=1/2$ the sharp-right velocity CDF in the history theorem gives $1/2$ exactly because $a=\arccos(1/\sqrt3)$ and $\arcsin(\sin a/\sqrt2)=\arcsin(1/\sqrt3)=\pi/2-a$. Both first-bit groups split further. For first bit 0, velocities in $(0,1/4)$ and $(1/4,1/2)$ give different middle bits. For first bit 1, velocities in $(1/2,5/8)$ and $(5/8,1/\sqrt2)$ give different last bits. On $0<v<1/\sqrt2$ the sharp-right density $(1+v)/[\pi(1-v^2)\sqrt{1-2v^2}]$ is at least $1/\pi$. Each complete word thus misses a subset of its first-bit group of mass at least $(1/\sqrt2-5/8)/\pi$. In particular every limiting word weighs strictly less than half.

Consequently the original binary bins are sufficient for exact ordinary readout at all sufficiently large finite sizes under the stated calibration. The [exact finite numerators](../../Director_G/results/reflected_histories.json) independently satisfy $2n_h<2^D$ for all eight words at each of the six stored lengths $32,64,128,256,512,1024$, with $2^D$ their common denominator at that length. One-velocity trajectories have zero limiting mass on words $100,101$, since $V>1/2$ puts the second position in the right half as well. A fresh independent velocity drawn for displacement from the original source at each absolute time gives both words positive mass because each required one-clock half has positive marginal mass. This distinguishes the two joint read laws even though all three one-clock marginals agree. A separate exact integer check covers every multiple of four from 4 through 32 wells. At 16 wells the largest word carries $2118536008/4294967296$, close to half. At 4 and 8 wells direct enumeration of every signed microscopic path reproduces all eight entries. A separate balanced terminal bit appended independently and coherently to each word would split its mass in half at **any** finite $L$ if the detector resolves that bit before scoring. This uses an additional prepared degree of freedom. The spatial construction uses the walker's terminal position and the spread supplied by the positive-time, localized launch. The kinematic evolution, coherent records, terminal scalar-score model and transfer of the calibration to the apparatus remain premises. No experimental trajectory frequencies are claimed.
