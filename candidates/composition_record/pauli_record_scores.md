# Calibrating two position scores with local route switches

Working mathematical note. Prepare two parked paths per station. Assume fixed local qubit codes, ordinary effect probabilities, a repeatable four-outcome position read and known local actions of \(I,X,Z,XZ\) up to global phase. For each shot choose one action uniformly and independently at each station. Store the choices with the two positions. For \(P=X,Z\) multiply its usual position score by the sign of \(P\) under this conjugation. The Pauli characters are orthogonal, so the mean signed effect is \(g_P P\) for an arbitrary underlying qubit POVM, even with \(Y\) components. Include misses with zero scores and identity score one. A known real fiducial \((|0\rangle\langle0|+|+\rangle\langle+|)/2\) has both target means \(1/2\). Twice the signed calibration mean determines each gain; retaining the preparation labels allows sharper calibration from the matching pure source. Independent choices across stations then give all four products of the calibrated scores. A common choice at both ends permits hidden components to survive in the correlations. This is a conditional calibration, requiring the route preparations, locality and gate actions to remain valid. [Prior readout twirling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09738)

For one fixed score observable, the four unknown Pauli coefficients are transformed by four distinct characters under Pauli conjugation. The unique four coefficients that project onto a specified one are its character divided by four, by invertibility of the Hadamard matrix. Thus every action is needed for generic exact isolation by signed averaging in this design. Two actions \(I,Z\) leave \(X\) and \(Y\) inseparable. A known real fiducial cannot reveal residual \(Y\). This lower bound concerns Pauli conjugation and generic fixed raw effects, rather than protocols adding independent measurements, trusted reality, or calibrated zero cross-talk.

Write the local gain ratios relative to the calibration estimates as \(g_P/\widehat g_P\). If all four lie in \([1-\eta,1+\eta]\), the difference between the gain-corrected mean and the ideal witness mean is at most \(5\eta+7\eta^2/4\). In the Pauli expansion of the witness, the absolute coefficients of one-body terms sum to \(3/2\); the two-body terms sum to \(7/4\). Each input correlation has magnitude at most one. Thus an incident score of \(-1/8\) excludes separability after gain uncertainty if \(\eta<2/(40+\sqrt{1656})\), about \(2.48\%\), before sampling error on the science run. Finite calibration error and subsequent gain drift contribute jointly: if the calibration mean's simultaneous absolute error is at most \(e_P\), and the science gain differs from the true calibration gain by at most \(d_P\), the mixed-fiducial experiment gives \(|g_P/\widehat g_P-1|\le (2e_P+d_P)/|\widehat g_P|\). Science confidence intervals can be propagated with the same sign and with all four gain intervals; the substituted witness is separately affine in the inverse gains so extremes lie at the 16 box corners for nonzero gains of fixed signs.
