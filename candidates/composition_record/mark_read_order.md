# A detector mark read before or after a walk

**Kirtus Justus**, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada. OpenAI Codex developed the read-order comparison and its identifiability checks.

In the three-tick walk, the occupied wells have squared norms $5/8,1/8,1/8,1/8$. The native remote choice either retains the $5/8$ joint cell or resolves it into $4/8$ and $1/8$, with the three minor cells unchanged. Let $r=3/8$. The scientific outcome $Y$ is the unchanged near tag for the minor cells. In the unsplit setting its probability conditional on the detector mode $F$ is $q_F$. In the split setting it is $r$, provided that each mode's positive scalar score satisfies $f_F(x)=c_Fx$ for all $x\le1/2$. The experiment seeks to resolve $D=q_F-r$ on individual launches. ## What an ordered detector read can tell us

Suppose a meter latches a real number $T$ before the distant setting is chosen. Each launch uses one science read. One version stores $T$. Another chooses a threshold $k$ independently and stores only $B_k={\bf1}_{T\ge k}$. The electronics can apply the comparison to a downstream copy of the latched signal. Keep the original assigned-arm denominators, including trials with $B_k=0$. The science read needs to retain its stated conditional mean given $F,T,k$ in both arms. Loss of hits conditional on the signal would bias the comparison. For each independently assigned threshold, the difference of the *joint*, per-arm frequencies of a positive meter bit and a near hit is
$$
J(k)=E[D\,{\bf1}_{T\ge k}].
$$
Comparing just the two overall near frequencies only measures $E[D]$. A threshold schedule that lets the readout select its own arm requires a separate selection analysis.

If the support of $T$ is ordered into bins, the difference of adjacent tail contrasts is the probability of a bin times its mean $D$. For example, errors of at most $e$ in each tail give error at most $2e$ in each weighted bin. Dividing by a rare bin's probability amplifies this error. The [finite Lean proof](MarkedTail.lean) checks the inversion for arbitrary signed integer weights after clearing common denominators.

For real $T$, zero unconditional contrast and zero $J(k)$ for every rational threshold imply $E[D\mid T]=0$ almost surely. Indeed the finite signed measure $A\mapsto E[D\,{\bf1}_{T\in A}]$ vanishes on a generating family of half-lines and on the whole space. This identifies $D$ itself if $D$ is a function of the latched read, or if $D$ has one sign within almost every value of $T$. Two opposite deviations carried in equal proportions at every $T$ escape all thresholds. Independent sensor calibration, a physical mode switch or a separate bound on unsensed variation is required to make an individual-mode claim.

There is a simple one-bit version for an ordered mode. Suppose that the independently latched signal implements $T=g(q_F)$ in a known interval $[a,b]$, $b>a$, and that a uniformly sampled threshold on this interval is independent of both response and setting. Only the resulting comparator bit $B$ need be stored. If the unconditional arm contrast vanishes, $E[q_F]=r$, and
$$
 E[B Y\mid\mathrm{unsplit}]-E[B Y\mid\mathrm{split}]
   = \operatorname{Cov}(q_F,g(q_F))/(b-a).
$$
For strictly increasing $g$ the right side vanishes only for constant $q_F$. With an independent copy $q'$ the covariance is $\frac12 E[(q_F-q')(g(q_F)-g(q'))]$. If every secant of $g$ has slope at least $\lambda>0$, the measured contrast bounds $\lambda\,\operatorname{Var}(q_F)/(b-a)$ from above. Independent binary read errors with false-positive rate $\alpha$ and false-negative rate $\beta$ multiply the contrast by $1-\alpha-\beta$ under the zero-unconditional-contrast premise. A meter that tracks a control voltage alone does not establish that voltage orders the actual hit probability. Two responses could share the same voltage and have opposite deviations.

## A native one-bit probe and the premise it buys

One can produce a mark with the walk instead of specifying a voltage-response curve. Run the three-tick preparation in an auxiliary read and keep the heavy cell intact. Copy an independent balanced rail bit only on one minor $1/8$ path and resolve it, producing two $1/16$ children. Leave the other minor cells and the heavy cell unchanged. At a given held response the fine minor scores sum to $c_F r$ as before. A hit at one named child has probability $q_F/6$. The auxiliary tag needs its own resolved terminal instrument and one fresh balanced degree of freedom for this particular construction. Simply taking any of the original whole minor wells already gives a mark of propensity $q_F/3$ with the original preparation.

With a shared response for the auxiliary acquisition and the subsequent science read, and conditionally uncorrelated outcome noise in each science arm, their marked joint contrast is $E[q_F(q_F-r)]/6$ for the named child. If the unmarked contrast is zero, this equals $\operatorname{Var}(q_F)/6$. The whole minor well gives the analogous factor $1/3$. A repeatable source, the same physical response across the two acquisitions and the conditional read-noise relation are additional requirements. This construction realizes the scoring dependence with the walk, while exposing precisely where a two-acquisition assumption re-enters. A separately recorded prechoice meter can remove that assumption only if its relation to the science detector is supported. ## A control for prematurely counting a coherent tag as a diagnostic

To see why record timing matters, prepare an independent two-branch label with kinematic masses $s,1-s$ and a normalized walk table with largest cell $m=5/8$. Retain all label–well and near-tag distinctions through one final scalar score. Each resolved cell has mass at most $m\max(s,1-s)$. All lie in the calibrated interval exactly when $1/5\le s\le4/5$. For every calibrated type, the normalization then cancels its slope, the joint frequencies equal the kinematic table entry by entry, and pooling the label gives the ordinary $5/8$ heavy-cell rate. In particular a single free balanced branch at $s=1/2$ suffices even for a type that strongly distorts the direct read. With several conditionally independent coherent label branches, the same bound uses their largest joint branch mass. This is a physical change of the score resolution. It can hide the very fluctuation a diagnostic was meant to detect.

For a concrete control, program a fixed mode with $c_F=1$ below half and either $f_+(5/8)=3/4$ or $f_-(5/8)=21/40$. Linear interpolation through $(1/2,1/2)$, the specified point and $(1,1)$ makes both scores strictly increasing and continuous. Their unmarked direct heavy probabilities are $2/3$ and $7/12$. Now use a balanced tag. Resolve it in an independent apparatus *first* and send the original conditional walk to a separately normalized science detector without disturbing its mode. Under those premises the heavy probabilities remain $2/3$ and $7/12$. Retaining that same tag coherently to the single joint terminal score instead gives $5/8$ in both modes. In the remote split setting the heavy-tag aggregate is also $5/8$ in each mode at either timing. These are predictions for an intervention with a known chosen mode. A fair hidden mixture gives $5/8$ in the pooled direct read under both timings. Thus agreement on pooled records cannot certify that the diagnostic was read independently or that the underlying response was ordinary.

An asymmetric tag gives a second control using the joint record. Prepare it with heavy mass $5/8$ (for example, the heavy-vs-minor bit from a separately prepared copy of the same walk). Its two tag branches must be scored as such when read early. Use the same fair mode for both early normalizations and require its site-hit probability to be unchanged after either tag result. The two heavy hits then have probability $[(2/3)^2+(7/12)^2]/2=113/288$. In the late, jointly scored instrument every product cell has mass at most $25/64<1/2$, so the two heavy hits have probability $25/64$. The gap is $1/576$, although both one-record marginals equal $5/8$. A fresh independent mode between the early acquisitions removes the gap. A balanced tag neutralizes this diagnostic covariance in the displayed modes. The asymmetric test probes persistence through the two normalizations on one launch. It does not demonstrate that persistence in advance.

A programmed mode can validate read order, normalization and meter discrimination for those injected modes. Applying that validation to spontaneously fluctuating modes requires evidence that the injectors and natural fluctuations access the same response family. For the reflecting walk, a final coherent sublabel is useful precisely because its resolved cells stay light. There it gives a specified way to read a classical trajectory, while here a diagnosis of the *unrefined* heavy-cell rule requires a separately latched record.
